# Routes, the register

Every route the programme has taken, live or closed, in one table. A *route* is a mechanism for closing the conjecture, distinct from a *fence post* (a proven negative, [fence-posts.md](findings/fence-posts.md)) and from an *attempt* (a cycle's work, [ATTEMPTS.md](ATTEMPTS.md)). Several routes die to one post; several attempts serve one route.

> **NEXT ROUTE ID: `R60`.** *The mark read `R55` after R55 was taken on 2026-09-28; corrected when R56 was opened.* *R46 is the candidate the [2026-09-27 sweep](reviews/2026-09-27-LIT-step8-sweep.md) named in its table and did not register; the number stays retired.* *Take this number, increment this line, and never reuse one.*

**Routes are numbered R1 upward in the order they were opened**, and the letters they used to carry are kept in the *formerly* column because [ATTEMPTS.md](ATTEMPTS.md), [reviews/INDEX.md](reviews/INDEX.md) and the reviewer reports cite them that way and are append-only. *The letters were a mess: unordered, with a primed variant, and colliding with the strategy letters the write-up used until they were retired.*

**Live and closed routes share this table deliberately.** They are the same objects at different stages, and a register split by status loses rows at the moment a route dies, which is exactly how the closure criteria for A, N, S, D and F were lost once, surviving only in git history until restored here.

**What each row owes.** A live route: its mechanism, what it would prove, and what it still needs. A closed route: what killed it, and **what a successor must clear**, the clearance criterion, which is the row's real value, because a proposal that cannot clear it is the expensive kind that looks new.
