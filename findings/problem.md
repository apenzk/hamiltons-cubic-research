# The problem, the threshold, and the literature

What the conjecture is, what the granted diagonal case actually covers in print, why the diagonal case does not generalise on its own, where $n\ge12$ comes from, the named families, and what the source literature does and does not settle.

## Identity of the conjecture

No single paper states this exact result end-to-end, but it is not folklore either, it traces to a specific unpublished source. It is **Böhm–Wilking's "Conjecture A"** (also referred to as "Conjecture L1" in the surrounding literature), attributed to a PhD thesis by Beitz (Münster, Böhm–Wilking's group), and it sits inside the broader Böhm–Wilking curvature-operator program from [Manifolds with positive curvature operators are space forms](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0606187) (Annals of Math. 167, 2008). It is referenced in passing in a 2023 paper, [Towards Finding the Second Best Einstein Metric in Low Dimensions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.02404), and partial progress toward it appears in [Yiyan Xu, *On Some New Ricci Flow Invariant Curvature Conditions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13633) (2024/2025).

### The diagonal case: located in the literature, and it covers less than assumed

**The assumed "known" diagonal case is Florian Jäger's 2016 Münster PhD thesis**, *An algebraic characterization of the Weyl curvature of $S^m\times S^m$* (advisor Christoph Böhm, second referee Wilking; 99 pages, [full text](https://repositorium.uni-muenster.de/document/miami/820840d1-0252-4bf3-8fc7-853730eee3f5/diss_jaeger_florian.pdf), not on arXiv). Found by a citation chain through Kevin Poljsak's 2022 thesis, which cites it for the diagonal $\#$-formula $(R\#S)_{ij}=\tfrac12\sum_k(r_{ik}s_{jk}+r_{jk}s_{ik})$, matching this repo's own independently derived diagonal formula exactly.

**Its Theorem A, quoted verbatim from the source** (extracted and checked directly, not paraphrased from a search summary):

> "Theorem A. Let $n\ge16$ be an even integer, and let $W\in\langle W\rangle_d$ with $\|W\|=1$ be given such that $W^2+W^\#=\theta W$ for a $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$. Then $\theta=\theta_0(n)$, and $W$ is the normalized Weyl curvature of $S^{\frac n2}\times S^{\frac n2}$."

with $\theta_0(n)=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$, the identical closed form this repo derived independently three ways. Since $P(W)=\theta$ at a critical point and every maximizer is critical, this *is* the diagonal-case maximization result.

**Three ways it covers less than the problem statement assumes**, all consequential:
1. **The threshold is $n\ge16$, not $n\ge12$.** The sentence immediately following Theorem A reads: "The conjecture is that this is also true for all even integers $n\ge12$ and all operators $W\in\langle W\rangle$." So $n=12$ and $n=14$ are open **even in the diagonal case**, precisely the dimensions where the conjecture first bites.
2. **Even $n$ only.** Odd $n$ is not addressed; the analogous $S^m\times S^{m+1}$ statement is explicitly left conjectural.
3. **The proof is long, not short**, roughly 50 pages (§3.1–3.6), by contradiction, via a scalar-curvature "gap phenomenon", chained $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathrm{Ric}^3/\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathrm{Ric}^4$ estimates, and a final appeal to the classification of compact symmetric spaces.

**Reconciling this with the problem statement's "short, elegant" framing.** The person posing the problem describes the diagonal case as solved "kurz und elegant" by an AI, while noting a colleague's doctoral thesis "could only show it in high dimensions". Jäger's $n\ge16$ is plainly that thesis; the short proof is therefore an AI-produced argument that is **not in the literature** and that this repo does not have. So the assumption granted in the problem statement is stronger than anything published, and the gap it papers over ($n=12,14$, and all odd $n$) sits exactly at the threshold under study.

**Jäger's own setup is this repo's vertex matrix.** Quoted: "Those operators $W\in\langle W\rangle_d$ can be expressed as symmetric $n\times n$-matrices $(w_{ij})$ with $0$ on the diagonal via $W(e_i\wedge e_j)=w_{ij}e_i\wedge e_j$ ... The condition that $W$ is Weyl then reduces to $\sum_i w_{ij}=0$ for all $j$." That is exactly the hollow symmetric $\Lambda$ with vanishing row sums used in the $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ bound work recorded in [ATTEMPTS.md](../ATTEMPTS.md), independently rediscovered here, and his starting point too.

**The Beitz thesis was also located, and is not the proof source.** Stine Franziska Beitz, PhD 2018 Münster (advisor Wilking), *Bianchi-convexity and applications to Ricci flow* ([full text](https://repositorium.uni-muenster.de/document/miami/02bdc0a3-53ed-4ee3-a643-7d595df046a2/diss_beitz.pdf); chapters 1–4 as [arXiv:1902.09088](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09088)). It states the conjecture once, as Remark 6.3.15, in the *cone-invariance* formulation ("the cones $\Omega_a$ are invariant under the ODE for $a\in[0,\tfrac n4]$"), not the $P(W)$-maximization formulation, and never mentions sphere products, $\mathbb{CP}^2$, or diagonal Weyl operators. Its own contribution is a local Hessian computation near one boundary point, and *which* boundary point is the thing that matters, recorded here after the thesis was finally transcribed ([notes.md](../references/beitz-2018-bianchi-convexity/notes.md)).

**The cone family in decomposition form, and which end is hard.** The thesis (p. 96) shows $R\in\Omega_a$ is equivalent to

$$\frac{4a-n}4\lVert R_I\rVert^2+a\lVert R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}\rVert^2+\Big(\frac{n-2}4+a\Big)\lVert R_W\rVert^2\le0,$$

which pins the normalisation to $\lVert\mathrm{ric}(R)\rVert^2=\frac{n-1}2\lVert R_I\rVert^2+\frac{n-2}4\lVert R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}\rVert^2$. The two ends are utterly different:

| $a$ | $\Omega_a$ | |
|---|---|---|
| $>\frac n4$ | empty | |
| $=\frac n4$ | $\mathbb R_{>0}\cdot I$, **a single ray** | invariance is trivial, since $\Phi(I)=(n-1)I$ |
| $\to0$ | $\frac{n-2}n\lVert R_W\rVert^2\le\lVert R_I\rVert^2$, with $R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}$ **unconstrained** | the whole content |

**So Beitz's Lemma 6.3.16 covers the degenerate end.** It proves invariance for $a\in[\frac n4-\varepsilon,\frac n4]$, a neighbourhood of the ray, by showing $I$ is a local maximum of $G(R)=dF_R(\Phi(R))$. The open region is $a\to0$, where the cone is at its largest and the constraint is purely a bound on the Weyl part against the scalar part.

**$\theta_0(n)$ IS DERIVED, NOT POSITED, the criterion at $a=0$ produces the target constant exactly.** Take the slice $R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}=0$, so $R=cI+W$. Three literature facts do all the work: *These three are what this derivation DEPENDS ON rather than an index of the shelf: every transcribed statement the record holds is in [INDEX-literature.md](INDEX-literature.md), which a candidate route is screened against first.*

| | |
|---|---|
| [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) Lemma 2.1 | $R+R\#I=(n-1)R_I+\frac{n-2}2R_{\mathrm{Ric}_0}$, with $\langle W\rangle$ in the **kernel**, so $W\#I=-W$ and $\Phi(I,W)=\tfrac12(IW+WI)+W\#I=W-W=0$ |
| [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) eq. (6) | $\mathrm{Ric}(\Phi(R))_{ij}=\sum_k\mathrm{Ric}_{kk}R_{kijk}$, so $\mathrm{Ric}(W)=0\Rightarrow\mathrm{Ric}(\Phi(W))=0$: **$\Phi$ preserves Weyl** |
| [Beitz](../references/beitz-2018-bianchi-convexity/notes.md) p. 96 | $\Omega_0=\{\tfrac{n-2}n\lVert R_W\rVert^2\le\lVert R_I\rVert^2$ **and** $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(R)>0\}$, so $\partial\Omega_0:\ \tfrac{n-2}n\lVert R_W\rVert^2=\lVert R_I\rVert^2$ **on the positive-scalar nappe** |

Hence $\Phi(cI+W)=c^2(n-1)I+\Phi(W)$, and **every cross term in the cubic vanishes**, $\langle W,\Phi(I)\rangle=(n-1)\langle W,I\rangle=0$ and $\langle I,\Phi(W)\rangle=0$ because $\Phi(W)$ is again Weyl. So $\langle R,\Phi(R)\rangle=c^3(n-1)\lVert I\rVert^2+\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$. On the right, $\lVert\mathrm{ric}(I)\rVert^2$ cancels between $F$ and the pairing, and the criterion collapses with **no residue at all** to

$$\langle W,Q(W)\rangle\ \le\ c\,(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2 .$$

Normalising $\lVert W\rVert=1$, the $\partial\Omega_0$ constraint with $\lVert I\rVert^2=\dim\Lambda^2=\frac{n(n-1)}2$ gives $c=\frac1n\sqrt{\frac{2(n-2)}{n-1}}$, **the positive root, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(R)>0$ is what selects it.** *$F$ is even in $R$, so the inequality alone prints a double cone whose lower nappe demands $\langle W,Q(W)\rangle\le-\theta_0(n)$, false at every sphere product: at $n=12$, $R=-c_0I+W_{S^6\times S^6}$ sits on its boundary.* So the bound demanded is

$$c(n-1)=\frac{\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}}n=\theta_0(n).$$

> **The constant is not a guess read off the sphere products, it is what cone invariance at $a=0$ requires.** Verified as squares, $\frac{2(n-2)(n-1)}{n^2}$ on both sides ([b_cone_bridge.py](../scripts/route-b/b_cone_bridge.py)).

**$n\ge12$ IS NECESSARY, NOT MERELY SUFFICIENT, and it is the same polynomial a fifth time.** *Reviewer 90.* The zero-padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ keeps $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$ at every $n$, and the operator $cI+W$ it defines sits on $\partial\Omega_0$, the Weyl tensor itself does not, so the criterion $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$ **fails** there exactly when $\theta_0(n)<\sqrt{3/2}$, i.e. when $n^2-12n+8<0$, i.e. $n\le11$. So $\Omega_0$ is **provably non-invariant** for $4\le n\le11$, Reviewer 92 confirmed the wider range, and notes the witness is Nienhaus's rather than new here. The conjecture's dimension hypothesis is therefore forced by an explicit witness rather than assumed, and the bridge is **sharp at $a=0$**.

**Sharpness at even $n$ only.** *Reviewer 89.* The criterion gives $P(W)\le\theta_0(n)$ at every $n$, but $\theta_0$ is attained by $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ only when $n$ is even. At odd $n$ the best sphere product gives $\frac{2(n-1)(n-3)}{(n-2)(n+1)}<\theta_0(n)^2$, independently reproducing the partition argument's values, $\tfrac{40}{27}$, $\tfrac{120}{77}$, $\tfrac{21}{13}$ at $n=11,13,15$. So this is the Conjecture A **bound**, sharp at even $n$.

**Two inputs the derivation uses and did not name.** *Reviewer 90.* $\Phi$ preserves the first Bianchi identity, without it $\Phi(W)$ could carry a $\Lambda^4$ component and "$\Phi(W)$ is Weyl" would not follow (standard Hamilton fact). And the bridge needs invariance $\Rightarrow G\le0$, which is the first clause of [Beitz](../references/beitz-2018-bianchi-convexity/notes.md) Lemma 2.3.4 read through its proof: the clause as stated assumes every sublevel set of $F$ invariant, and its proof uses only the level set through the point, here $F=\tfrac4{n-2}$, so invariance of $\Omega_0$ alone gives $G\le0$ on $\partial\Omega_0$.

**The symmetry of $T(A,B,C)=\langle A,\Phi(B,C)\rangle$ is a theorem**, [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) eq. (5), and both reviewers re-derived it. It is also **not load-bearing** here: the collapse needs only bilinearity, $\Phi(I,W)=0$ and $\mathrm{scal}(Q(W))=0$.

**The convention hazard.** Beitz and this project use the **same** norm, $\lVert I\rVert^2=\dim\Lambda^2=\frac{n(n-1)}2$, forced by her Remark 1.1.7, $\langle R,S\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(R\circ S)$, and independently by $F(I)=\frac2{n-1}$ at the $\Omega_{n/4}$ endpoint. An earlier version of this section claimed a factor-2 discrepancy; that reading came from a `pdftotext` artefact which drops fraction denominators onto the following line, and it would have invited a reader to "correct" a correct number. **The genuine gap is elsewhere:** Beitz's $\mathrm{ric}$ carries a factor $\tfrac12$ against [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) (her Definition 1.1.10). It cancels here, since $F\langle\mathrm{ric},\mathrm{ric}\,\Phi\rangle$ is invariant under rescaling $\mathrm{ric}$, but it is load-bearing for any figure quoting $\lVert\mathrm{ric}\rVert$ or $\mathrm{scal}$ across the two sources.

**OFF THE SLICE: exactly one new coupling, explicitly identified.** Take the general $R=cI+Y+W$ with $Y=R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}$. [Xu](../references/xu-2024-ricci-flow-invariant-conditions/notes.md) Prop 2.7 plus [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) Lemma 2.2 (which supplies the block Xu's list omits, $(\Phi(Y))_W=\frac1{n-2}(\mathrm{Ric}_0\wedge\mathrm{Ric}_0)_W$) determine every one of the ten distinct triples, the total symmetry of $T(A,B,C)=\langle A,\Phi(B,C)\rangle$ ([BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) eq. (5)) supplying the multiplicities. **Five vanish, five survive, at every $n\ge4$:**

$$\langle R,\Phi(R)\rangle=\underbrace{c^3(n-1)\lVert I\rVert^2+3c\langle I,\Phi(Y)\rangle+\langle Y,\Phi(Y)\rangle}_{W\text{-even}}+\underbrace{3\langle W,\Phi(Y)\rangle+\langle W,Q(W)\rangle}_{W\text{-odd}} .$$

Against the slice, **one** genuinely new coupling appears, the triple $(Y,Y,W)$, equal to $\frac3{n-2}\langle W,\mathrm{Ric}_0\wedge\mathrm{Ric}_0\rangle$. The five that vanish include every $I$–$W$ pairing, by Xu (3).

**The parity argument, and it is two-sided.** The constraint $\frac{n-2}n\lVert W\rVert^2\le\lVert R_I\rVert^2$ depends only on $\lVert W\rVert$, so $cI+Y-W\in\Omega_0$ whenever $cI+Y+W$ is. On the right, $\mathrm{ric}(R)$ is $W$-independent and $F(R)$ is $W$-even, while the only $W$-dependent piece of $\mathrm{ric}(\Phi(R))$ is $2\Phi(Y,W)$, which lands in $\langle\mathrm{Ric}_0\rangle$ by Xu (5) and is $W$-odd. Writing $\mathrm{RHS}_{\rm even}$ and $\mathrm{RHS}_{\rm odd}$ for the $W$-even and $W$-odd parts of $F(R)\langle\mathrm{ric}(R),\mathrm{ric}(\Phi(R))\rangle$, demanding the criterion at both signs therefore gives

$$\big\lvert\,\mathrm{LHS}_{\rm odd}-\mathrm{RHS}_{\rm odd}\,\big\rvert\ \le\ \mathrm{RHS}_{\rm even}-\mathrm{LHS}_{\rm even},\qquad\mathrm{LHS}_{\rm odd}=3\langle W,\Phi(Y)\rangle+\langle W,Q(W)\rangle .$$

**CLASSIFICATION: this expansion is AGREEMENT, not new.** [Xu](../references/xu-2024-ricci-flow-invariant-conditions/notes.md) **Lemma 2.8, eq. (2.34)** is the same identity, term for term:

$$\langle Q(R),R\rangle=\frac n{2(n-1)}\bar\lambda^3+\frac3{2(n-1)}\bar\lambda\lvert\mathrm{Ric}_0\rvert^2+\frac3{n-2}\langle\mathrm{Ric}_0\wedge\mathrm{Ric}_0,W\rangle-\frac2{(n-2)^2}\langle(\mathrm{Ric}_0^2)_0,\mathrm{Ric}_0\rangle+\langle Q(W),W\rangle,$$

matching $(I,I,I)$, $(I,Y,Y)$, $(Y,Y,W)$, $(Y,Y,Y)$, $(W,W,W)$ in that order, **including the coefficient $\frac3{n-2}$**, which was reached here from BW08 Lemma 2.2 rather than copied. Xu attributes it to Huisken's proof of his Theorem 3.3, where it is not stated as an independent lemma. So the block-vanishing derivation is corroboration of a published identity by a second route; the parity argument on top of it is not in Xu.

> **So off the slice the $a=0$ criterion is not Conjecture A alone.** $\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$ enters only as one of three pieces of an odd part that must be bounded on both sides. Setting $Y=0$ kills the new coupling and $\mathrm{RHS}_{\rm odd}$ together and recovers the bridge exactly, the consistency check. What a general-$R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}$ proof still owes is a bound on $\mathrm{LHS}_{\rm odd}-\mathrm{RHS}_{\rm odd}$ ([b_cone_offslice.py](../scripts/route-b/b_cone_offslice.py)).

**Scope, stated plainly.** $a=0$ leaves $R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}$ **unconstrained**, so the full cone conjecture is strictly more than Conjecture A; Conjecture A is its $R_{\mathrm{ric}_0}=0$ slice. What is settled is that the target constant is right, not that the two statements coincide.

**THE BRIDGE BELOW IS PUBLISHED, TWICE, IN SOURCES THIS PROJECT HOLDS.** [Beitz](../references/beitz-2018-bianchi-convexity/notes.md) defines $F=\lVert R\rVert^2/\lVert\mathrm{ric}(R)\rVert^2$ at (6.24), gives $dF_R(S)$ at (6.25), states $\Omega_a=F^{-1}\big((-\infty,\tfrac4{n-2+4a}]\big)$, and opens Lemma 6.3.16 with *"we consider the function $G:R\mapsto dF_R(\Phi(R))$"*. [Xu](../references/xu-2024-ricci-flow-invariant-conditions/notes.md) (3.1)–(3.4) displays the same expansion and calls it "the main task". **An earlier version of this section called the bridge a derivation the project had never made; that was wrong, and the Beitz sentence had been read and quoted in the same session.** It is the third item in this file recorded as new and later found published. The material below is therefore **corroboration**, kept for the explicit block-by-block accounting rather than for priority, and Reviewer 92 adds that it is not even a *second* route: the expansion traces to Huisken 1985 and reaches it the same way. A further convention note from Reviewer 92: [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) defines $P=\tfrac13\langle R,Q(R)\rangle$, a factor $3$ against this project's $P$.

**A convention check the two sources settle between them.** Xu records $F(I)=\frac1{2(n-1)}$ while Beitz's $\Omega_{n/4}$ threshold is $\frac2{n-1}$, a factor $4$, because Xu's $\mathrm{Ric}$ is twice Beitz's $\mathrm{ric}$. This is the hazard Reviewers 89 and 90 identified. The $\theta_0$ derivation is unaffected **only because $\lVert\mathrm{ric}(I)\rVert^2$ cancels** between $F$ and the pairing; any argument that does not cancel it must fix a convention first.

**Why this project's $P$ is that conjecture rather than a relative of it.** Invariance is $G\le0$, and expanding the quotient rule on $F=\lVert R\rVert^2/\lVert\mathrm{ric}(R)\rVert^2$ gives

$$G(R)\le0\iff\underbrace{\langle R,\Phi(R)\rangle}_{P(R)}\le F(R)\,\big\langle\mathrm{ric}(R),\mathrm{ric}(\Phi(R))\big\rangle .$$

$P$ **is** the left-hand side of Böhm–Wilking's criterion. At $a=0$ the constraint lets $\lVert R_W\rVert^2$ run up to $\frac n{n-2}\lVert R_I\rVert^2$, and the pure-Weyl term of $P$ ($\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$, this project's object), is what the criterion needs bounded there. That is the bridge, and it had not been derived: the earlier note that the thesis gives the conjecture "not [in] the $P(W)$-maximization formulation" is right about the wording and wrong about the content. This closes the previously-recorded "Beitz thesis was not locatable" gap with a negative: it is not where the diagonal proof lives.

The algebraic object $\langle W, Q(W)\rangle$ itself (the cubic "Frobenius form" attached to the polarization of Hamilton's $Q = X^2 + X^\#$), is treated in generality (though without this specific maximization conjecture) in [Daniel Fox, *The commutative nonassociative algebra of metric curvature tensors*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04012), which shows the Weyl (Ricci-flat) tensors form a **simple nonassociative subalgebra**, and identifies its idempotents (i.e. the critical points of $P$ on the unit sphere) with points fixed by orthogonal complex/hyper-Kähler-type structures.

A closely analogous, actively-worked sibling program exists for the *curvature operator of the second kind* $\mathring R$ (acting on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$), where products of spheres again serve as extremal/boundary models with explicit dimension thresholds, see [Cao–Gursky–Tran on Nishikawa's conjecture](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.08465) and [Xiaolong Li, New Sphere Theorems under Curvature Operator of the Second Kind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13847).

## Critical-point structure

$Q$ is the diagonal of a symmetric trilinear form $T(X,Y,Z) = \langle X, B(Y,Z)\rangle$, made fully symmetric by the trace identity $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XYZ) = \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(ZYX)$ for self-adjoint operators. So $P(W) = T(W,W,W)$, and Lagrange multipliers on $\{|W|=1\}$ give

$$Q(W) = \lambda W, \qquad \lambda = P(W).$$

For $W$ diagonal on coordinate 2-planes $e_i \wedge e_j$, $Q$ preserves diagonality and reduces to an explicit formula $(Q(W))_{ij,ij} = \lambda_{ij}^2 + \sum_{k \ne i,j} \lambda_{ik}\lambda_{jk}$, turning $P$ into a polynomial in $\binom{n}{2}$ eigenvalues, this is the tractable "diagonal case" already solved.

For the Weyl tensor of $S^p \times S^q$, $O(p)\times O(q)$-equivariance forces $Q(W)$ to share $W$'s three-block structure (pp/qq/pq-type planes), collapsing the critical-point equation to a single scalar ratio condition, confirming it is a genuine critical point, though not by itself which one is the global max.

## Why the diagonal case doesn't trivially generalize

The obvious move (diagonalize $W$ spectrally, invoke the known diagonal result), fails because the two notions of "diagonal" are different objects. The known result requires diagonality **in coordinate 2-planes** $e_i \wedge e_j$ of an orthonormal frame of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (needed because the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ structure constants are only sparse in that basis); a generic spectral eigenbasis of $W$ as an operator on $\Lambda^2\mathbb{R}^n$ is essentially never of that form.

Other standard reduction tools were checked and found insufficient:
- **Sign-flip averaging** onto the coordinate-diagonal locus is norm-decreasing and $P$ is a cubic (neither convex nor concave), so no Jensen-type inequality transfers.
- **Palais's principle of symmetric criticality** correctly proves the sphere-product tensor is critical (large isotropy $O(p)\times O(q)$ forces criticality for free) but gives necessity, not maximality.
- Böhm–Wilking/Brendle–Schoen-style **cone-invariance / maximum-principle** arguments are the closest thing to a viable strategy, but they are dynamical (ODE-invariance under $\dot W = Q(W)$) rather than static, and no one has yet built the static analogue needed here.

## Mechanism behind the $n \geq 12$ threshold

Two independent findings converge on the same picture:

1. **A genuine competitor exists in low dimensions.** For $8 \le n \le 11$, the Weyl tensor of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (isotropy roughly $U(2)\times O(n-4)$) is a competing critical point that *beats* the products-of-spheres tensor, this is stated explicitly as part of the Böhm–Wilking conjecture itself, not merely a proof artifact.
2. **The proof technique also needs $n$ large.** Xu's paper derives the underlying estimate via a Tachibana-trick eigenvalue inequality ($|\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(T^3)| \le \frac{m-2}{\sqrt{m(m-1)}}\|T\|^3$ on the trace-free operator built from $W$) chained through several Hölder/Cauchy–Schwarz steps. The sign of the resulting polynomial obstruction, $(n+1)(n+2)(n-10) - 2$, flips positive only at $n \approx 11$, which is why Xu's own theorem needs a piecewise bound split at $n=10/11$, and why the more general Beitz/Böhm–Wilking conjecture (needing a full parameter range $a \in [0, n/4]$) requires the safer $n \geq 12$.

No explicit numerical/SDP search over small-dimensional Weyl operator spaces was found in the literature; the $n<12$ picture rests on the documented $\mathbb{CP}^2$ counterexample plus the proof-technical obstruction, not on a broader computational survey.

## Named families, and why none of them threatens the conjecture

Multiple independent attempts, cross-checking each other's numerics and formulas, converged on a coherent partial picture. Detail for each item is in [ATTEMPTS.md](../ATTEMPTS.md).

Multiple independent proof attempts across two rounds, cross-checking each other's numerics and formulas along the way, converged on a coherent partial picture:

1. **The exact crossover at even $n$ is $n=6+2\sqrt7\approx11.29$; the two points it compares were constructed three independent ways, and the crossing itself is one forced line of algebra.** Fox's abstract nonassociative-algebra idempotents, a coordinate-level Tachibana-trick computation and a from-scratch explicit-tensor construction supply the balanced sphere-product's $P$-value and the $\mathbb{CP}^2$-type competitor's; once both are in hand, $n^2-12n+8\ge0$ is a rearrangement rather than a fourth derivation. At odd $n$ the constant is $\Theta_n$ and the real crossover is $\frac{13+\sqrt{97}}2\approx11.42$. **The integer threshold $n\ge12$ is reproduced exactly at both parities**, see [Lemma L2](#lemma-l2-exact-dimension-threshold-canonical-statement-and-proof) for the proof.
2. **The sphere-product tensor $W^*=S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ is a genuine local maximum on the full (non-diagonal) tangent space**, not just within the diagonal locus, confirmed by exact Hessian diagonalization (negative semi-definite, zero-eigenspace matching the $O(n)$-orbit dimension exactly) for $n=6,8,9,10,11,\mathbf{12}$. The $n=12$ case (dim $\mathcal W_{12}=1638$) is now fully resolved: 0 positive eigenvalues, exactly 36 zero eigenvalues matching the $O(12)$-orbit dimension, confirmed by two independently-implemented pipelines. Local stability shows no sign of weakening anywhere in this range; the $n\geq12$ cutoff is purely about which of two local maxima has the larger global value, not about $W^*$ losing local maximality.
3. **Multi-factor sphere products ($3{+}$ blocks) are ruled out as competitors.** A direct sweep shows every 3-block or 4-block product strictly underperforms the best 2-block product at every tested $n$ from 8 to 24, with the optimum degenerating toward minimal extra blocks rather than balancing.

#### Lemma L2, exact dimension threshold, canonical statement and proof

Needed to pin down where the known critical points rank. **The threshold is an integer cutoff at $n=12$ and it holds at both parities.** The real crossover of the formally continued formula is a *different* number at each parity, and no integer lies between the two. *Machine-checked at the operators as `L2_operators` in [L2Operators.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2Operators.lean), with the sphere operators identified as the Weyl parts of $S^p\times S^q$ in [L2SphereWeyl.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean).*

**Statement.** Let $n\ge4$ and let $\theta_{\max}^{\rm sp}(n)=\max_{p+q=n}\theta(S^p\times S^q)$. Then $\theta_{\max}^{\rm sp}(n)^2<\tfrac32$ for $4\le n\le11$ and $\theta_{\max}^{\rm sp}(n)^2>\tfrac32$ for $n\ge12$, where $\tfrac32$ is the squared value of the $\mathbb{CP}^2$-type competitor, Fox's $\mathcal S^4(\alpha)$. No dimension sits at equality.

**Proof.** *The competitor is $n$-independent.* $\theta(\mathcal S^4(\alpha))^2=\tfrac32$ at $n=4$ ([Nienhaus](../references/nienhaus-2023-second-best-einstein/notes.md) Thm 4.14(a)), and $\mathfrak{su}(2)_+$ is a subalgebra, so $[\Lambda^+,\Lambda^+]\subseteq\Lambda^+$ and $W^\#$ is literally the same matrix after zero-padding into any $n\ge4$: both $P(W)$ and $\lVert W\rVert$ are unchanged, hence $\theta^2=\tfrac32$ at every $n\ge4$.

*The best sphere product, and why the parities differ.* $\theta(S^p\times S^q)^2=\frac{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)}{(n-2)pq}$ with $p+q=n$ ([Nienhaus](../references/nienhaus-2023-second-best-einstein/notes.md) Prop 2.25, squared). Since $\frac{(p-1)(q-1)}{pq}=1-\frac{n-1}{pq}$ is strictly increasing in $pq$, the maximum over splits is attained at the most balanced one ($p=q=\tfrac n2$ at even $n$, $\{\tfrac{n-1}2,\tfrac{n+1}2\}$ at odd $n$) so the two parities carry different constants and each must be read on its own:
\[
\theta_0(n)^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-2)}{n^2}\quad(n\text{ even}),\qquad\Theta_n^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-3)}{(n-2)(n+1)}\quad(n\text{ odd}).
\]

*Even $n$.* $\theta_0(n)^2-\tfrac32=\frac{n^2-12n+8}{2n^2}$, with roots $6\pm2\sqrt7$. The numerator is $-8$ at $n=10$ and $+8$ at $n=12$.

*Odd $n$.* $\Theta_n^2-\tfrac32=\frac{n^2-13n+18}{2(n-2)(n+1)}$, with roots $\frac{13\pm\sqrt{97}}2$. The numerator is $-4$ at $n=11$, the attained value there is $\Theta_{11}^2=\tfrac{40}{27}$ at $S^5\times S^6$, excess $-\tfrac1{54}$, and $+18$ at $n=13$, where $\Theta_{13}^2=\tfrac{120}{77}$ and the excess is $+\tfrac9{154}$.

*The cutoff.* Both denominators are positive for $n\ge4$ and both numerators are upward parabolas whose smaller roots, $6-2\sqrt7=0.7085$ and $\frac{13-\sqrt{97}}2=1.5756$, lie below $4$, so on the domain each expression is negative exactly below its larger root and positive above it. $7$ and $97$ are non-squares, so neither larger root is an integer, no dimension sits at equality and the crossing is strict on both sides. The two larger roots are $6+2\sqrt7=11.2915$ and $\frac{13+\sqrt{97}}2=11.4244$, differing by $\frac{1+\sqrt{97}-4\sqrt7}2=0.1329$, and **no integer lies in that interval**, so both parities cross between $11$ and $12$ and the integer cutoff is $12$. $\square$

**Two things are exact here and they are different**: the real root of each formally continued formula, which is parity-dependent, and the integer cutoff $12$, which is not.

*Non-circularity, and one self-check.* Both inputs are $n$-uniform closed forms derived without reference to any threshold. The polynomial also certifies the competitor's normalisation: reading the same computation against $\theta^2=\tfrac34$ would give $5n^2-24n+16\ge0$ and a threshold of $n\ge4$, so $n^2-12n+8$ is available only at $\tfrac32$.

*What a consumer may cite.* The statement above, in full, at either parity. The constant at odd $n$ is $\Theta_n$ and **not** $\theta_0(n)$, the two differ by $\theta_0(n)^2-\Theta_n^2=\frac{8(n-1)}{n^2(n+1)(n-2)}$ ([conventions.md](conventions.md)), so a bound read off $\theta_0$ at odd $n$ describes no object.

#### Lemma L47, the dimension lemma for symmetric spaces, canonical statement and proof

**Why it was needed.** For a locally symmetric Einstein space $Q(W)=\lambda W$, so $\theta=\lambda/\lVert W\rVert$ and the cubic becomes a *quadratic* condition on $\lVert W\rVert$. **Statement and argument.** Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\lVert R\rVert^2\ge(n/4)^2/\dim\mathfrak h$, so

$$\dim\mathfrak h\le\frac{3n^2(n-1)}{2(7n-4)}\ \Longrightarrow\ \lVert W\rVert^2\ge\tfrac16 .$$

**That step needs only $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}R\le\dim\mathfrak h$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}R=n/4$**, not isotypy, not $R\succeq0$, and **not the block eigenvalues**. *The rank fact is [Petersen–Stanfield](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11236) **Proposition 2.1**'s image statement; the block cited Prop 2.2, which is a different sentence.* **And the step is sharp rather than merely valid**: by their Theorem C, equality holds on every simple-isotropy space, the type-II groups and $\mathbb{OP}^2$ among them. The implication is exact and reversible in the cleared form $P(n,h)=3n^2(n-1)-2(7n-4)h\ge0$, so all the loss sits at Cauchy–Schwarz.

This covers **every compact irreducible symmetric space of rank $\ge2$ with $W\ne0$**, plus the rank-one $\mathbb{OP}^2$: each family reduces to a polynomial positivity statement with an all-nonnegative-coefficient certificate after a shift, each with a strictly positive constant term ($616$, $48$, $7200$, $114$, $3696$, $2192$, $236$, $84$). **The polynomials and the family-to-constant assignment are in [b_symmetric_dimension_lemma.py](../scripts/route-b/b_symmetric_dimension_lemma.py), transcribed from its tables into [L47Cert.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L47Cert.lean) and kernel-checked there**; three verifiers reproduced the constants as $P$ at each family's corner, two of them all eight. *"First admissible rank" misdescribes two of the eight shifts, $48$ is BDI at $p=q=2$, which is the reducible $S^2\times S^2$, and $84$ is type II at $n=6$, a dimension no compact simple Lie algebra has. Both are conservative, so the certificates stand and the description does not.*

The residue is exactly the compact rank-one spaces. $S^n$ and $\mathbb{RP}^n$ have $W=0$, **which is why $W\ne0$ is a hypothesis and not a remark**: at $S^4$, $R=\tfrac16I$ on all of $\Lambda^2$, so $\lVert W\rVert^2=0<\tfrac16$ and $\theta$ is $0/0$. *This is not [Lemma L46](#lemma-l46-at-a-locally-symmetric-einstein-space-the-weyl-part-is-critical-and-theta-is-quadratic)'s already-booked $\lambda\ne0$, which $S^n$ satisfies at $\lambda=\tfrac12$.* $\mathbb{CP}^m$ and $\mathbb{HP}^m$ have the closed forms $\lVert W\rVert^2=\frac{3m(m-1)}{4(m+1)(2m-1)}$ and $\frac{9m(2m+1)(m-1)}{4(m+2)^2(4m-1)}$, with minima $\tfrac16$ at $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $\tfrac{45}{224}$ at $\mathbb{HP}^2$. #### Lemma L211, a compact Einstein symmetric space has $\lambda>0$, which is where compactness and global symmetry do their work

**Let $M$ be a compact globally symmetric space with $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda g$ and $W\ne0$. Then $\lambda>0$, and every irreducible factor is of compact type.** *This is the step [Lemma L210](#lemma-l210-the-de-rham-reduction-done-at-the-algebra-so-it-costs-no-topology) below turned out to need and no copy of the record had stated*, found by [r918](../reviews/REVIEW_r918.md) and [r919](../reviews/REVIEW_r919.md) independently: L210 removes the Euclidean factor and says nothing about TYPE, while [L47](#lemma-l47-the-dimension-lemma-for-symmetric-spaces-canonical-statement-and-proof)'s census is of compact type. **With this lemma the Theorem's quantifier is covered.**

**The inputs, all four from [Gorodski](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md) and none from outside it.** Corollary 3.1.4, $K(X,Y)=\lambda_i\beta([X,Y],[X,Y])$ on an irreducible factor with $B=\lambda_i\beta|_{\mathfrak p}$; Lemma 2.1.2(b), $\beta|_{\mathfrak k}$ negative definite; Corollary 2.4.7, that the isotropy subgroup of the transvection group of a symmetric space of noncompact type is a **maximal compact** subgroup; and Corollary 5.5.2, that a semisimple Lie algebra is compact exactly when its Killing form is negative definite.

**Argument.**

1. **$\lambda\ne0$, and CARTAN--AMBROSE--HICKS IS NOT WHAT DOES IT** ([r920](../reviews/REVIEW_r920.md), [r921](../reviews/REVIEW_r921.md), independently, against an earlier version of this step and against [L46](#lemma-l46-at-a-locally-symmetric-einstein-space-the-weyl-part-is-critical-and-theta-is-quadratic)'s parenthetical, which carries the same misattribution with no argument). *That theorem does not give $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=0\Rightarrow R=0$.* **The engine is the definiteness of $\beta$ on each irreducible factor**: at $\lambda=0$ every factor would have $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=0$, while Corollary 3.1.4 with Lemma 2.1.2(b) makes an irreducible factor's Ricci definite and so nonzero, so [L210](#lemma-l210-the-de-rham-reduction-done-at-the-algebra-so-it-costs-no-topology)'s decomposition leaves only the Euclidean factor, giving $R=0$ and $W=0$, which the hypothesis excludes.
2. **The type of a factor is the sign of its curvature, and that is the sign of $\lambda$.** On an irreducible factor, $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak k$ and $\beta|_{\mathfrak k}$ is negative definite, so $\beta([X,Y],[X,Y])\le0$ and Corollary 3.1.4 gives $\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}K=-\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}\lambda_i$. And $B=\lambda_i\beta|_{\mathfrak p}$ is an inner product, hence positive definite, so $\lambda_i<0$ exactly when $\beta|_{\mathfrak p}$ is negative definite, which is compact type, and $\lambda_i>0$ exactly when $\beta|_{\mathfrak p}$ is positive definite, which is noncompact type. **So compact type gives $K\ge0$ and noncompact type gives $K\le0$**, and the sign of the Einstein constant follows by tracing.
3. **Suppose $\lambda<0$.** By step 2 every factor is of noncompact type, so $\beta$ is positive definite on $\mathfrak p$, and $\mathfrak p\ne0$ since $\dim M>0$. By [L210](#lemma-l210-the-de-rham-reduction-done-at-the-algebra-so-it-costs-no-topology) there is no Euclidean factor, so $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple **by Corollary 2.1.5**, a fifth citation this step needs and an earlier version did not make. *And the definiteness is across all of $\mathfrak p$ rather than factor by factor because the $\mathfrak p_i$ are mutually $\beta$-orthogonal, which 2.1.3's proof builds them to be.* Then $\beta$ is not negative definite on $\mathfrak g$, so by **Corollary 5.5.2** $\mathfrak g$ is not compact and the transvection group $G$ is noncompact.
4. **The contradiction, and it is where compactness meets global symmetry.** $M$ is globally symmetric, so $G$ acts transitively and $M=G/K$ with $K$ the isotropy subgroup, **compact by Corollary 2.4.7**. Then $G\to G/K=M$ is a principal $K$-bundle with compact fibre over a compact base, so $G$ is compact. That contradicts step 3.
5. **Hence $\lambda>0$**, and by step 2 every factor is of compact type. $\square$

#### Lemma L210, the de Rham reduction, done at the algebra so it costs no topology

**Every locally symmetric space with $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda g$ and $\lambda\ne0$ has curvature tensor that is block-diagonal over a decomposition into irreducible factors, with the mixed blocks exactly zero and no Euclidean factor, and each factor inherits the SAME constant $\lambda$.** *So [Lemma L48](#lemma-l48-the-reducible-case-and-the-symmetric-space-theorem-canonical-statement-and-proof) below, which argues the case of a space that IS a product, together with [L47](#lemma-l47-the-dimension-lemma-for-symmetric-spaces-canonical-statement-and-proof)'s irreducible case, covers every compact Einstein symmetric space.* **This is the step [ROUND_L48](../verification/ROUND_L48.md)'s finding R10 records as having no argument on file and as the one by which the Theorem reaches products at all**, raised by [v191](../verification/VERIFY_v191.md), which also found the word *de Rham* appearing once in all of [findings/](.) and in neither copy of the theorem.

**Conventions and the two inputs, both cited rather than assumed.** A symmetric space carries an orthogonal involutive Lie algebra $(\mathfrak g,s,B)$ with $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak k\oplus\mathfrak p$ the $\pm1$ eigenspaces of $s$ and $\mathfrak p\cong T_xM$. From [Gorodski](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md):

> **Proposition 3.1.2.** Let $M$ be a locally symmetric space, $x\in M$ and $X,Y,Z\in T_xM$. Then $R_x(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]_x$.

> **Proposition 2.1.3 (Decomposition).** Let $(\mathfrak g,s,B)$ be an OIL-algebra. Then there is a decomposition $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak g_r$ into a direct sum of ideals such that each $\mathfrak g_i$ is $s$-invariant, $\mathfrak g_0$ is Euclidean, and $\mathfrak g_i$ is irreducible for $i=1,\dots,r$.

*Euclidean there means $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$.* **Neither statement carries a topological hypothesis**: 3.1.2 asks only that $M$ be LOCALLY symmetric, and 2.1.3's proof is linear algebra on $\mathfrak p$.

**Argument, in four steps.** Write $\mathfrak p_i=\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p$, so $\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak p_i$ by $s$-invariance, and note that distinct ideals of an internal direct sum commute, $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$ for $i\ne j$.

1. **The mixed blocks vanish.** For $X\in\mathfrak p_i$, $Y\in\mathfrak p_j$ with $i\ne j$, $[X,Y]\in[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$, so $R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]=0$ for every $Z$ by 3.1.2.
2. **Each block is preserved.** For $X,Y\in\mathfrak p_i$ and $Z\in\mathfrak p_j$ with $j\ne i$, $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak g_i$ and so $R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]\in[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$. **With step 1 this is exactly L48's *"$R$ is block-diagonal on $\Lambda^2$ and zero on the mixed terms"*, now derived rather than assumed.**
3. **The Euclidean factor goes, and the reason is $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p]=0$ rather than $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$.** [Gorodski](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md)'s **(2.1.4)**, inside the proof of 2.1.3, gives the stronger $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p]=0$. So for $X\in\mathfrak p_0$ and any $Y,Z$, $R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]=0$ by 3.1.2, and $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)$, **which is a trace over all of $\mathfrak p$ and not over $\mathfrak p_0$**, vanishes term by term. Against $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda g$ with $\lambda\ne0$ that forces $\mathfrak p_0=0$, and then $\mathfrak g_0=0$ **by the effectiveness clause of the OIL definition**, that $\mathfrak k$ contains no nontrivial ideal of $\mathfrak g$. *Corollary 2.1.5 is then a restatement of this conclusion rather than independent support for it, since it rests on the same clause.*
4. **The factors inherit the same constant, which is what L48's hypothesis needs.** For $X\in\mathfrak p_i$ the trace defining $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)$ runs over all of $\mathfrak p$, and **step 1 kills every cross term**, so the ambient Ricci at $X$ equals the intrinsic Ricci of the factor $\mathfrak p_i$ at $X$. Hence $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda g$ restricts to $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_i=\lambda g_i$ with the SAME $\lambda$. *This is the hypothesis [v192](../verification/VERIFY_v192.md)'s §7a names as needing the global Einstein condition rather than a per-factor normalisation, since $B=\sum_af(n_a)-f(n)$ is computed at one shared constant.* $\square$

*The $\mathfrak p_i$ are mutually $B$-orthogonal, which 2.1.3's proof builds them to be and which Lemma 2.1.2(c) also delivers, and that is what makes $R$ block-diagonal as an operator on $\Lambda^2$ rather than only on triples.*

### Petersen–Stanfield: what their tables do and do not settle

[Petersen–Stanfield, *Poincaré Polynomials and Curvature Operators of Symmetric Spaces*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11236) (August 2025) computes the curvature operator of every compact irreducible symmetric space. Their **Proposition 2.2** says why: writing $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak h_0\oplus_i\mathfrak h_i$ as centre plus simple factors, *that decomposition is precisely the eigenspace decomposition of $R$*, with eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ on the centre. Their **Theorem B**: the maximum eigenvalue equals the Einstein constant exactly for the Hermitian symmetric spaces.

**Theorem A is an inclusion, not an equivalence.** They say "only the complex Grassmannians **can** have three such eigenvalues"; reading it as "exactly" is wrong, and the $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(2,2)$ row below (two distinct values), is the counterexample.

**Closing the graded enumeration by citation from their tables is unsound.** Graded critical points here are indexed by symmetric *pairs* $(\mathfrak h,\mathfrak k)$ with $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$; they classify symmetric *spaces*. A space's Weyl operator is graded only when its isotropy is a symmetric subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, so their tables do not enumerate what needs enumerating.

#### Lemma L46, at a locally symmetric einstein space the weyl part is critical, and $\theta$ is quadratic

*Verified `PROVEN, hypotheses corrected`, [ROUND_L46](../verification/ROUND_L46.md). The same argument, in four lines, is in [graded-half.md](graded-half.md).*

Let $R$ be the curvature operator of a locally symmetric Einstein space, $\Ric=\lambda g$ with $\lambda$ of either sign, and set $W=R-cI$ with $c=\lambda/(n-1)$. Then $Q(W)=\lambda W$, and when $W\ne0$,
\[
\theta\Big(\tfrac W{\|W\|}\Big)=\frac\lambda{\|W\|},
\]
so $W/\lVert W\rVert$ is a critical point of $P$ on the unit sphere. *The hypothesis is $W\ne0$ and not $\lambda\ne0$: the round sphere has $\lambda=n-1$ and $W=0$.* Neither compactness nor irreducibility nor rank is used. In Petersen--Stanfield's normalisation \cite{ps2025}, where $\lambda=\tfrac12$ for every compact irreducible symmetric space, this reads
\[
\theta^2=\frac1{4\big(\|R\|^2-(\tr R)^2/N\big)},\qquad N=\tbinom n2 ,
\]
using $\lVert W\rVert^2=\lVert R\rVert^2-(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}R)^2/N$ for the orthogonal splitting $R=cI+W$.

**Proof, in three steps.**

**(A) $Q(R)=\lambda R$.** Along a Ricci flow the curvature operator in an Uhlenbeck frame satisfies Hamilton's equation $\partial_tR=\Delta R+2Q(R)$, stated with no compactness hypothesis in [Richard–Seshadri](../references/richard-seshadri-2013-noncoercive-cones/notes.md) (their text, lines 258–264). The family $g(t)=(1-2\lambda t)g$, for $1-2\lambda t>0$, is a Ricci flow written down explicitly, since $\mathrm{Ric}$ is invariant under constant rescaling, and every $g(t)$ is locally symmetric, so $\Delta R=0$. The operator on orthonormal bivectors is $R(t)=R/(1-2\lambda t)$, with derivative $2\lambda R$ at $t=0$. So $2Q(R)=2\lambda R$, at either sign of $\lambda$ and with no existence theorem. *Richard–Seshadri's Lemma 4.1 states the same identity for non-negatively curved Einstein symmetric spaces with $\lambda>0$, which does not cover $H^2\times H^2$.*

**(B) The shift to $W$ is exactly the one that cancels.** $Q(W+cI)=Q(W)+2c\,(W\circ I)+c^2Q(I)$ with $Q(I)=(n-1)I$, so $Q(W)=\lambda W+\lambda cI-c^2(n-1)I-2c(W\circ I)$, and $c=\lambda/(n-1)$ is the choice that kills the remainder once $W\circ I=0$.

**(C) $W\circ I=0$.** $X\circ I=X\#I+X$, and $W\#I=-W$ is [Böhm–Wilking](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) Lemma 2.1, on the record in [isotropy-cap.md](isotropy-cap.md) as the $-2$ eigenvalue of $W\#I=-\tfrac12\sum_\alpha\mathrm{ad}(v_\alpha)W\,\mathrm{ad}(v_\alpha)$. *Consistency: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^\#)=\langle W\#I,W\rangle=-\lVert W\rVert^2$, the record's $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^\#=-1$ at unit norm ([conventions.md](conventions.md)).*

Then $P(W)=\langle W,Q(W)\rangle=\lambda\lVert W\rVert^2$ gives $\theta=\lambda/\lVert W\rVert$ at $W\ne0$. $\square$

#### Lemma L223, the stability of L57: a near-critical diagonal tensor of value near $\Theta_n$ lies near a sphere product

*Gate 2: [r945](../reviews/REVIEW_r945.md) and [r946](../reviews/REVIEW_r946.md) on identical briefs, steps 1 to 3 CONFIRMED by both, the sign convention at zero coordinates and the sign of $B$ repaired on their reports. Derived for [I103](../plan.md) from L57's own chain with its three defects kept. The polynomial cores are kernel-checked in [L223Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L223Statement.lean), including the pairing $\langle Z(v),(v_iv_j)\rangle$ behind L57's norm formula, which with step 2(b)'s projection property derives that formula. All four items are machine-checked on the real objects, `L223_items12`, `L223_item1_upper`, `L223_item1` (both halves of item 1 at one top eigenpair) and `L223_item3` in [L223Items.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L223Items.lean) and `L223_item4` in [L223Item4.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L223Item4.lean).*

**Statement.** *Let $n\ge4$, $S\in\mathcal D_n$ with $\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}^2=1$, and $S\circ S=\lambda S+E$ with $E\in\mathcal D_n$ and $\lambda>\lvert E\rvert$, norms over unordered pairs. Put $\bar\Theta=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$, which is $\Theta_n$ at even $n$, $Z_*^2=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}$, let $\mu$ be the top eigenvalue of $S$ with unit eigenvector $v$, $e_-=\max(0,-v^{\mathsf T}Ev)$ and $\Delta=\bar\Theta-\lambda+e_-/\mu$.*
1. *$\Delta\ge0$, and $\Delta\le\bar\Theta-\lambda+2\sqrt2\lvert E\rvert/(\lambda-\lvert E\rvert)$.*
2. *With $\alpha$ the angle between $S$ and $Z(v)$ and $\eta_1=\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}^2(v_i-v_j)^2-\mu^2/n$: $1-\cos\alpha\le\Delta/\bar\Theta$, $\eta_1\le\mu\Delta$, and $\lvert Z(v)\rvert\ge Z_*(1-\Delta/\bar\Theta)$.*
3. *$\sum_i(v_i^2-\tfrac1n)^2\le\frac{2(n-2)^2}{n(n-1)}\frac\Delta{\bar\Theta}$, so $w$ with $w_i=\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}(v_i)/\sqrt n$, taking $w_i=+1/\sqrt n$ where $v_i=0$, has $\lVert v-w\rVert^2\le\frac{2(n-2)^2}{n-1}\frac\Delta{\bar\Theta}$, and its sign classes of sizes $p,q$ have $\lvert p-q\rvert\le n\lVert v-w\rVert$.*
4. *If $4\sqrt{n-2}\sqrt{\Delta/\bar\Theta}<2(1-\Delta/\bar\Theta)$, then $p,q\ge2$ and $\lvert S-B\rvert\le\big(\sqrt2+\frac{4\sqrt{n-2}}{1-\Delta/\bar\Theta}\big)\sqrt{\Delta/\bar\Theta}$, where $B$ is the unit positive multiple of the projection of $(w_iw_j)$ onto $\mathcal D_n$, constant on the three classes of pairs of the sign partition and a positive multiple of the vertex matrix of $S^p\times S^q$, L57's maximiser at $p=q=\tfrac n2$.*

**Proof.**
1. **Step 1 with its defect.** Pairing the matrix equation with $vv^{\mathsf T}$ as in L57 gives $\lambda\mu+v^{\mathsf T}Ev=\mu^2-\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}^2(v_i-v_j)^2$, and Cauchy--Schwarz with $\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}(v_i-v_j)^2=-\mu$ and $\sum_{i<j}(v_i-v_j)^2=n$ makes $\eta_1\ge0$, so $\lambda=\frac{n-1}n\mu-\eta_1/\mu-v^{\mathsf T}Ev/\mu\le\frac{n-1}n\mu-\eta_1/\mu+e_-/\mu$.
2. **Steps 2 and 3 with theirs.** $\mu=2\langle S,Z(v)\rangle=2\lvert Z(v)\rvert\cos\alpha$. Pairing $Z(v)$ with $(v_iv_j)$, its projection property gives $\lvert Z(v)\rvert^2=\tfrac12(1-\sum v^4)-\frac{\sum v^4}{n-2}+\frac1{2(n-1)(n-2)}=\frac{n^2-3n+3}{2(n-1)(n-2)}-\frac n{2(n-2)}\sum v^4$, using $\sum_{i<j}v_i^2v_j^2=\tfrac12(1-\sum v^4)$, $\sum_{i\ne j}v_i^3v_j=-\sum v^4$ and $\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j=-\tfrac12$. With $\sum v^4=\tfrac1n+\eta_3$, $\eta_3=\sum(v_i^2-\tfrac1n)^2\ge0$, this is $\lvert Z(v)\rvert^2=Z_*^2-\frac n{2(n-2)}\eta_3$. So $r=\lvert Z(v)\rvert/Z_*\in[0,1]$ and $\frac{n-1}n\cdot2Z_*=\bar\Theta$.
3. **The chain.** Together, $\lambda\le\bar\Theta r\cos\alpha-\eta_1/\mu+e_-/\mu$, that is $\bar\Theta(1-r\cos\alpha)+\eta_1/\mu\le\Delta$, and $r,\cos\alpha\in[0,1]$ give items 2's three bounds and $\Delta\ge0$. For item 1's upper bound, $\langle S\circ S,S\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{i\ne j}s_{ij}^3+\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S^3\le2\mu$ because $s_{ij}\le\mu$ (from $(e_i+e_j)^{\mathsf T}S(e_i+e_j)=2s_{ij}$) and every eigenvalue is at most $\mu$, while $\langle S\circ S,S\rangle=\lambda+\langle E,S\rangle\ge\lambda-\lvert E\rvert$. So $\mu\ge(\lambda-\lvert E\rvert)/2$, and $e_-\le\lVert E\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt2\lvert E\rvert$.
4. **The vector.** $\eta_3=\frac{2(n-2)}nZ_*^2(1-r^2)\le\frac{(n-2)^2}{n(n-1)}\cdot2(1-r)$ gives item 3's first bound. $\big\lvert\lvert v_i\rvert-\tfrac1{\sqrt n}\big\rvert\le\sqrt n\,\lvert v_i^2-\tfrac1n\rvert$ gives the second. $\sum w=(p-q)/\sqrt n$ and $\sum v=0$ give $\lvert p-q\rvert/\sqrt n\le\sqrt n\lVert v-w\rVert$.
5. **The sphere product.** For unit $v,w$ the off-diagonal parts satisfy $\lvert(v_iv_j)-(w_iw_j)\rvert\le\lVert v-w\rVert$, and the projection onto $\mathcal D_n$ is non-expansive, so $\lvert Z(v)-\Pi(w_iw_j)\rvert\le\lVert v-w\rVert$. $\Pi(w_iw_j)$ is invariant under the permutations preserving the sign partition, hence constant on the three classes of pairs, hence a multiple of the vertex matrix of $S^p\times S^q$. Item 4's hypothesis makes $\lVert v-w\rVert<\lvert Z(v)\rvert$, so the multiple is nonzero and $p,q\ge2$. Then $\lvert S-B\rvert\le\lvert S-\hat Z(v)\rvert+\lvert\hat Z(v)-B\rvert\le\sqrt{2(1-\cos\alpha)}+2\lVert v-w\rVert/\lvert Z(v)\rvert$, and $2\sqrt{2(n-2)^2/(n-1)}/Z_*=4\sqrt{n-2}$. $\square$

**What it gives.** *A quantitative L57 for near-critical diagonal tensors, radius $O(\sqrt{n\Delta})$ (at odd $n$, $\Delta\ge\bar\Theta-\Theta_n$ keeps it at least of order $1/n$), the $\sqrt n$ matching the $\tfrac1{n-2}$ transverse gap of [L149](second-order.md). Its intended consumer, the small-$\nu$ regime of I104's pair-block stratum, is closed outright by [L224](second-order.md), so it is recorded as a tool.* **No screen is cited as evidence.**
