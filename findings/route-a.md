The degenerate branch $\varphi=0$ is covered: there $W^*$ lies on the orbit, $\max P=\sqrt{3/2}$, and the same contradiction applies. The contradiction is with the *value*, not the location: nothing is assumed about where the sphere product sits.

**Why the bootstrap is needed.** Nienhaus's Theorem 4.17 bounds the analogous quantity using the a priori cap $P\le\theta^*$, available only while $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is the global maximizer, that is, only for $n\le11$, and with his closing estimate identically zero at $\alpha=1/3$, hence borderline by construction. Run at $n=10$ the argument has no contradiction to draw and simply returns $\max P\le\sqrt{3/2}$ (the correct answer there) so it switches over exactly at $n=6+2\sqrt7$ rather than at a fitted constant.

### $\alpha\le1/3$ is structural, and needs no multiplicity-free hypothesis

The tube audit left one gap: $\alpha\le1/3$ is verified at four dimensions but *asserted* for all $n\ge9$ on Theorem 4.14's Schur argument, whose Lemma 4.13 is valid only on **multiplicity-free** isotypic components. That gap is now closed, by an argument that never needs multiplicity-freeness.

**The mechanism.** $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is supported entirely inside $\Lambda^2(\mathbb{R}^4)$, so the whole construction is equivariant for $G=SO(n-4)$ acting on the last $n-4$ coordinates and fixing $\mathbb{R}^4$. Hence $F_u$ commutes with $G$, verified at $3.9\times10^{-15}$ over the generators ([p0_schur_free.py](../scripts/pipelines/p0_schur_free.py)), and decomposing into $G$-isotypic pieces

$$\mathrm{Weyl}_n=\bigoplus_\lambda M_\lambda\otimes V_\lambda\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad F_u=\bigoplus_\lambda F_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V_\lambda},$$

so $\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(F_u)=\bigcup_\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(F_\lambda)$. **Multiplicities are exactly what the spaces $M_\lambda$ absorb**, so Schur applies unconditionally here, this is the structural difference from the thesis's route, which works with the full stabilizer and asks each irreducible subspace to be an eigenspace.

**The multiplicity spaces do not depend on $n$.** Splitting $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ by the $SO(n-4)$ Casimir and restricting $F_u$ to each block:

| $G$-type | $\dim V_\lambda$ ($n{=}10/12/14$) | $\dim M_\lambda$ | $\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(F_\lambda)/\sqrt{3/2}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| trivial | $1/1/1$ | $20/20/20$ | $-1^{\times2},\ -\tfrac16^{\times6},\ 0^{\times6},\ \tfrac13^{\times3},\ \tfrac12^{\times2},\ 1^{\times1}$ |
| vector | $6/8/10$ | $20/20/20$ | $-\tfrac12^{\times4},\ 0^{\times12},\ \tfrac12^{\times4}$ |
| $\Lambda^2$ | $15/28/45$ | $6/6/6$ | $-\tfrac16^{\times2},\ 0^{\times3},\ \tfrac13^{\times1}$ |

Every multiplicity-space dimension and every $F_\lambda$ spectrum is **identical** across all three dimensions, while the $\dim V_\lambda$ grow. Only four blocks carry a nonzero eigenvalue at all, and they are of dimensions $20,20,6,10$, a fixed finite problem. The types occurring are those in $\bigotimes^k\mathbb{R}^{n-4}$ for $k\le4$, since $\mathrm{Weyl}_n\subset S^2(\Lambda^2(\mathbb{R}^n))$ and each $\Lambda^2$ contributes at most two $\mathbb{R}^{n-4}$ factors, so the decomposition is in the stable range once $n-4\ge8$.

**The orbit decomposition, and why $\alpha\le1/3$ follows.** Under $G$, $\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak{so}(4)\oplus(\mathbb{R}^4\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n-4})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n-4)$, and modulo the stabilizer the orbit directions are $\mathfrak{so}(4)/\mathfrak{stab}_4$ (dimension $2$, trivial type) together with $\mathbb{R}^4\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n-4}$ (four copies of the vector type). So

$$T_u(SO(n).u)=2\cdot(\text{trivial})\ \oplus\ 4\cdot(\text{vector}),\qquad \dim=2+4(n-4)=4n-14$$

for **every** $n$, the identity previously verified numerically now has its reason. And the eigenvalue $\tfrac12\sqrt{3/2}$ occurs exactly twice in the trivial block and four times per vector copy, i.e. exactly $2+4(n-4)$ times in total: the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace *is* the orbit tangent space, block by block.

Removing $\mathbb{R}u$ and the orbit therefore leaves, in each block: $\tfrac13$ in the trivial block (top, after deleting the single $1$ and the two $\tfrac12$'s), $0$ in the vector block (after deleting the four $\tfrac12$'s per copy), $\tfrac13$ in the $\Lambda^2$ and $S^2_0$ blocks, and $0$ in the rest. **Hence $\alpha\le1/3$, with equality attained, for every $n$ in the stable range, proved by a fixed finite computation on four multiplicity spaces rather than by an isotypic argument that needs multiplicity-freeness.**

#### Lemma L21, the eigenvalue bound $\alpha\le\tfrac13$, structural at every $n\ge9$

**Statement.** Let $n\ge9$, $m=n-4$, and let $u=W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ padded to $\mathcal W_n$, of unit norm with $\theta^*=P(u)=\sqrt{3/2}$. Decomposing $\mathcal W_n=\bigoplus_\lambda M_\lambda\otimes V_\lambda$ into isotypic pieces gives $F_u=\bigoplus_\lambda F_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V_\lambda}$, since multiplicities are what the spaces $M_\lambda$ absorb. **The step needs the $V_\lambda$ ABSOLUTELY irreducible, not merely irreducible**, because over $\mathbb R$ the correct form is $M_\lambda\otimes_{D_\lambda}V_\lambda$ and a nontrivial $D_\lambda$ admits equivariant operators the display does not name. *It holds here at $m\ge5$, every occurring weight having $\ell(\nu)\le2<m/2$, and it fails at $m=2$ where $SO(2)$ is abelian.* This is the structural difference from the thesis's route, which works with the full stabilizer and asks each irreducible subspace to be multiplicity-free.

## Post F59, three named routes to $c_2$ fail, and why each fails

#### Lemma L167, the competitor's stabiliser meets the mixed block trivially, and the orbit dimension is $4n-14$

### I54, the four $F_\lambda$ have $m$-free entries

**This is the one unproved input of the tube theorem, and the exact evidence for it is a single admissible dimension**: the three exact checks were run at $n=6,8,10$ and the validity range $m\ge5$ excludes the first two. *$M_\lambda(n)$ is a different space at each $n$; equal dimensions license nothing about spectra without an identification intertwining the operators, and none is proposed.* **The discharge wanted**: an $m$-independent basis of each $M_\lambda$, entries as rational functions of $m$ with a degree bound, and rank certificates $\dim\ker(F_\lambda-r\theta^*)$ over $\mathbb Q$. **The hand computation is done, as [Lemma L168](findings/route-a.md), and it PASSES identically in $m$**: the weighted second moment collapses to $\tfrac{(2n-1)(n-1)}6\theta^{*2}=\tfrac{(n-1)(2n-1)}4$, which is [L161](findings/curvature-invariants.md)'s Killing constant, and the first moment vanishes blockwise. *So the closed form is $\lVert T\rVert^2=\tfrac{(n-1)(2n-1)}4\dim\mathcal W_n$, by irreducibility plus one evaluation.* **What remains is the $m$-freeness itself**, two scalar identities cannot pin $56$ eigenvalues, so the discharge below still stands, and a falsifier must now preserve both moments as polynomials in $m$. *The route as filed:* the table forces $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(F_u^2)/\theta^{*2}=4+2m+\tfrac{m(m-1)}{12}+\tfrac{(m-1)(m+2)}4$, while independently $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(F_u^2)=\lVert T\rVert^2/\dim\mathcal W_n$, so a closed form for $\lVert T\rVert^2$ turns $m$-independence into one scalar identity. *Owed by [ROUND_L21](verification/ROUND_L21.md).*
