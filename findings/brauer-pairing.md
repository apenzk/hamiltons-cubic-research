# The Brauer pairing is a monomial in $n$, of degree at most six

## The statement

For two degree-$6$ diagram covariants $P,Q$ of $\mathcal W_n$, the Gram entry is

$$\langle P,Q\rangle \;=\; n^{L}\cdot q_D(W),$$

a **monomial** in $n$ times an $n$-independent contraction, not a general polynomial. $L$ is the number of cycle components of the pairing graph, and

$$L\;\le\;\min(a_P,a_Q)\;\le\;\Big\lfloor \tfrac{f}{2}\Big\rfloor,\qquad f=\lvert\lambda\rvert,$$

with $a_P,a_Q$ the numbers of bare metrics carried by $P$ and $Q$. Since $\lvert\lambda\rvert\in\{0,2,\dots,12\}$ for this certificate, **the ceiling is $n^6$**, attained only in the $16$ $O(12)$ blocks with $\lvert\lambda\rvert=12$. *($19$ is the $SO(12)$ count; three chiral pairs are the difference, and this record's blocks are the $71$ $O(12)$ labels.)*

## Why

Build the pairing graph on the contracted indices. The $24$ Weyl slots have degree $1$; the $f$ free positions have degree $2$. A graph whose vertices have degree $1$ or $2$ is a disjoint union of **paths and cycles**. Each path is an ordinary contraction and contributes to $q_D$. **Each cycle contains no tensor slot at all**, so it closes to $\delta^a{}_a=n$. Cycles alternate $P$-metrics with $Q$-metrics, whence $L\le\min(a_P,a_Q)$.

*This is the same mechanism as the no-free-loop lemma, seen from the other side.* That lemma says a **complete contraction of Weyl factors** has every slot on a tensor and so admits no free loop; here the bare metrics supply exactly the slots that belong to no tensor, and pairing two of them closes the loop. **So the free-index question does not disappear under pairing, it is where the $n$-dependence lives.**

## Verification

Twelve pairings, contracted twice by means sharing no code, float64 through numpy's optimiser at $n=4\ldots10$, and exact integers through an independent greedy path contractor at $n=4,5,6$. The fitted degree equals $L$ in every row. **Two "rotated" rows are the control**: same $a_P,a_Q$, yet the degree drops from $f/2$ to $1$, so the count must be of *cycles*, not of metrics, and a version counting metrics would have passed the other ten rows. Reviewer 238's $n\cdot I_3^2$ is the $f=2$ row, already maximal for its block.

## The bound REQUIRES reducedness, and reducedness is not yet proved

*Reviewer 241.* The bound $L\le\min(a_P,a_Q)$ **fails on unreduced diagrams**, and the witness is explicit at degree 6 with all 24 Weyl slots occupied: with $T=S^3$, $\langle T,T\rangle$ is constant at $20464923860814889/195689447424$ across $n=5..10$, while $\langle (g_{ab}g^{ab})T,\,T\rangle$ is **exactly $n$ times it**, predicted degree $0$, actual degree $1$.

**So reducedness is a genuine precondition of this theorem, not a tidiness condition.** An attempt to discharge it in two lines ([run_a2_rational_data.py](../scripts/runs/run_a2_rational_data.py)) was refuted as non-exhaustive: its two cases are the length-minimal members of two families, and it misses **metric cycles of length $\ge2$** ($g_{ab}g^{bc}\cdots g^{za}=n$, where no single factor carries a self-loop) and **Weyl self-traces routed through a metric chain** ($W_{abxy}g^{ab}=0$). The claim that such loops are "already counted" is also false: $L$ counts cycles of the **pairing** graph, while an internal loop factors out *before* pairing and is a further $n$ on top.

*The normal form to aim at, supplied by 241: every surviving bare metric has both indices free.*

## Two things owed

- **"Reduced" is a precondition, not a theorem.** The derivation assumes no diagram in the spanning set carries a self-loop. Whether the construction produces reduced diagrams is unestablished.
- **The bound is for the *diagram* Gram.** The **block** Gram is *rational*, not polynomial: trace removal introduces $1/n$ and $1/(n-2)$. Plan item A2 needs the rational statement, and the monomial theorem is its numerator half.

## The diagram-realisation theorem, one seed per block

**The covariants reach diagram form through a seed, not through 184 separate expansions.** The construction emits vectors in a weight space, while the pairing theorem above, the padding transfer and the whole $n$-dependence are stated over Brauer diagrams; the gap between the two is what this theorem closes.

Write $f=12$ for the leg count, three Weyl tensors, four legs each.

> **Theorem.** At **every** $n$:
> 1. $M_\lambda=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda, V^{\otimes 12})$ is **irreducible** as a module over the **image** $\nu(B_{12}(n))$, hence generated by **any** nonzero vector;
> 2. so for one nonzero seed $c_0$ per block, $\{\, e\,\nu(D)\,c_0 : D \text{ a Brauer diagram} \,\}$ spans that block's covariant space, $e$ being the projector onto $\mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathrm{Weyl}_n)$.

Step 2 needs $e$ to lie in $\nu(B_{12}(n))$, and it does, as a **linear combination** of diagrams rather than as a single diagram. $e$ is an $O(n)$-equivariant endomorphism of $V^{\otimes 12}$, and the classical **First Fundamental Theorem for $O(n)$**, holding at every $n$, with no threshold, puts every such endomorphism in $\nu(B_{12}(n))$.

**ASWT Theorem 3.17(b) is not the clause that carries this.** That theorem is stated per type: type $B_m$ ($n$ odd) surjects, while type $D_m$ gives only injectivity for $n\ge d$ and isomorphism for $n\ge2d+1$, with §3.5 saying surjectivity *fails* for $\mathfrak{so}_{2m}$. $n=12$ is type $D_6$. **The conclusion survives only because ASWT's group is $SO$ and this programme's is $O$**, and that gap is real rather than bookkeeping: for $SO(4)$, $\dim\mathrm{Inv}(V^{\otimes4})=4$ while $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\nu(D)\varepsilon\}=1$, so the theorem is **false for $SO$ and true for $O$**. So $e\,\nu(D)=\nu(bD)$ and the composite never leaves the diagram algebra. The composition is well defined because $e$ preserves isotypic components, with $e\,M_\lambda=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,\mathrm{Sym}^3\mathrm{Weyl}_n)$ exactly, and **the seed need not lie in the covariant space**, r259 verified the conclusion with a seed $e$ annihilates.

### The theorem is unconditional in $n$, no threshold enters

The theorem above was first filed with semisimplicity of $B_{12}(12)$ as their hypothesis, and Rui's inequality $n\ge f-1$ as *"the binding constraint of the route, clearing by one"*. **Both reviewers refute that.** $M_\lambda$ is a module over the **image** $\nu(B_f(n))$, and the double centraliser makes the image $\bigoplus_\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{End}}(M_\lambda)$ with each $M_\lambda$ irreducible. **The inequality never enters.**

Three hypotheses are used, and all three hold: $V^{\otimes f}$ is a semisimple $O(n)$-module ($O(n)$ reductive in characteristic zero); the $V_\lambda$ are **absolutely** irreducible for $O(n)$; and $\nu(B_f(n))$ is **all** of $\mathop{\mathrm{End}}_{O(n)}(V^{\otimes f})$, which is the FFT and is the load-bearing one. *The **associate/chirality worry is absent**: self-associate $\lambda$ carry no doubling for $O(n)$ (that is an $SO(n)$ phenomenon) and r262 confirms doubling would break $\sum_\lambda m_\lambda(n)\dim V_\lambda=n^f$ at $18$ of $48$ cells.*

Demonstrated below the threshold rather than argued: at $(f,n)=(6,3)$, where $B_6(3)$ is **not** semisimple, one seed still generates all $15$ dimensions of $M_\varnothing$. At three further sub-threshold cells the regular trace form of $B_f(n)$ is degenerate ($58/105$, $745/945$, $706/945$) while the form on the image has rank exactly $\dim\nu$: $35$, $126$, $603$ (r260).

**Where the two criteria do apply**, they measure different objects and share no scale, so ranking them by "slack" was a category error:

| criterion | governs | reads at $n=12$ |
|---|---|---|
| [ASWT 7.1(3)](../references/andersen-stroppel-tubbenhauer-brauer-semisimplicity/notes.md) | semisimplicity of the **abstract** $B_f(n)$ | $n\ge f-1=11$ |
| [Lehrer–Zhang 3.4](../references/lehrer-zhang-sft-orthogonal/notes.md), applied to $B_{12}(n)$ | injectivity of $\nu$ | $n\ge12$, **zero** slack |

Rui's is needed only to identify $M_\lambda$ with the **abstract** cell module $\Delta_\lambda$, which the theorem does not use and only the verification below does.

### Verification

[run_diagram_realisation.py](../scripts/runs/run_diagram_realisation.py), exact rational arithmetic on the up–down tableau counts and floating-point rank on the diagram operators. **Why the Gram rank equals $\dim\nu$**, asked by r261, r262, r263 and r264, and missing until now. The diagram Gram is $[\langle\nu(D),\nu(E)\rangle]$ under the **Frobenius form** $\langle A,B\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{\mathsf T}B)$, and $\langle\nu(D),\nu(E)\rangle=n^{c(D\cup E)}$ where $c$ counts loops. The Frobenius form is **positive definite** on real matrices, so the Gram matrix of a spanning set has rank equal to the dimension of its span, which is $\dim\nu(B_f(n))$. *Positive definiteness is the whole reason; without it a Gram rank bounds the dimension and does not equal it.*

The invariant tested is $\dim\nu(B_f(n))$, which semisimplicity plus the double centraliser forces to equal $\sum_\lambda (\dim\Delta_\lambda)^2$ over the $O(n)$-admissible labels, step 2 written as a number.

Every $(f,n)$ in the semisimple range matches exactly, the two boundary cases $n=f-1$ included: $(3,2)$ at $10$ and $(4,3)$ at $91$, both landing on the prediction.

**The run under-tests its own threshold**, and both reviewers extended it. Reporting *"exactly one sub-threshold failure, $(4,2)$"* was an artefact of starting $n$ at $2$: $(3,1)$ and $(4,1)$ fail too, and $f=5$ adds $(5,1)$, $(5,2)$ at $126$ against $326$ and $(5,3)$ at $603$ against $842$, with the boundary $(5,4)$ landing exactly on $903$. **Five more failures, and three of three below the threshold at $f=5$.**

**The universal identity is $\dim\nu(B_f(n))=\sum_\lambda m_\lambda(n)^2$ over *restricted* up–down tableaux**, which matches the rank at every pair tested ($24$ in the script, and independently $32$ in r259's sweep), including every sub-threshold cell. The cell-module form $\sum_\lambda(\dim\Delta_\lambda)^2$ used above agrees with it **exactly when $n\ge f-1$**, so what the check detects is the identification $M_\lambda\cong\Delta_\lambda$, not anything the theorem needs. At $f=12$ the two part company at every $n\le10$ and agree at every $n\ge11$.

Both reviewers computed $\dim\nu$ by routes sharing nothing with the script above, the diagram Gram rank $[\,n^{c(D\cup D')}\,]$, whose size is independent of $n$, and an exact kernel-partition count, reproducing every published cell.


## The seed is the inclusion padded by cups

The realisation theorem says one nonzero seed per block generates that block, and says nothing about **where a seed comes from**. That gap is what kept obligation (ii) open: if the seed must be found by search, finding a nonzero element of $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$ is the same construction problem the item started with, once per block.

**It need not be searched for.** Let $\lambda$ be a block label, so $\lvert\lambda\rvert\in\{0,2,\dots,12\}$, write $k=(12-\lvert\lambda\rvert)/2$, let $\iota_\lambda:V_\lambda\hookrightarrow V^{\otimes\lvert\lambda\rvert}$ be the inclusion given by Weyl's construction of $V_\lambda$ as the traceless tensors of symmetry type $\lambda$ (classical, unfiled), and let $\mathrm{cup}=\sum_a e_a\otimes e_a\in V\otimes V$ be the invariant metric element.

> **Lemma.** *Let $n\ge1$, and let $\lambda$ be $O(n)$-admissible, $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2\le n$, with $\lvert\lambda\rvert\in\{0,2,\dots,12\}$.* Then $c_0:=\iota_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$, the map $x\mapsto\iota_\lambda(x)\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$, is a **nonzero** element of $M_\lambda=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$.

**Admissibility is what makes $V_\lambda$ nonzero.** For a label that is not admissible, Weyl's construction gives the zero space and no nonzero seed exists, witness $\lambda=(2,2)$ at $n=2,3$ and $\lambda=(2,1,1)$ at $n=3$. **Every label with $\lvert\lambda\rvert\le n$ is admissible**, because $\lambda'_1$ counts the nonzero parts and $\lambda'_2$ the parts of size at least two, so a part of size $p$ contributes at most $p$ to $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2$ ([L65.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L65.lean)). So at $n\ge12$ every block label is admissible, with equality $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2=12$ at $\lambda=(2,2,2,2,2,2)$.

**Proof.** *Equivariance.* $g\cdot\mathrm{cup}=\sum_a ge_a\otimes ge_a$ has coefficient matrix $gg^{\mathsf T}=I$ for every $g\in O(n)$, reflections included, so $\mathrm{cup}$ is invariant, and the tensor product of the equivariant map $\iota_\lambda$ with invariant vectors is equivariant. *Nonvanishing.* For $x\ne0$, $\iota_\lambda(x)\ne0$, and $\mathrm{cup}\ne0$ because $n\ge1$, so $\iota_\lambda(x)\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$ is a tensor product of nonzero vectors and is nonzero. Equivalently, the contraction applying $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ to the two factors of one cup returns $\sum_a\langle e_a,e_a\rangle=n$, so contracting all $k$ cups returns $n^k\iota_\lambda\ne0$. **Nonzero in $M_\lambda$ and nonzero as a map are the same statement**, $M_\lambda$ being a space of maps. $\square$

So every block has a seed in closed form relative to $\iota_\lambda$, and the realisation theorem applies to it. **The seed is $\iota_\lambda$ followed by a padding diagram of $k$ cups.** Where $\lvert\lambda\rvert=12$, $k=0$ and the seed is $\iota_\lambda$ itself, so there the content is Weyl's construction.

### Verification

[run_seed_is_a_diagram.py](../scripts/runs/run_seed_is_a_diagram.py). The nonvanishing identity $\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}\cdot\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}=n^k$ holds at $n=3,4,5,6$ for $k=1,2,3$. A padded seed then generates **exactly** $m_\lambda(n)$ dimensions under the diagram action, compared against the up–down recursion, which knows nothing about seeds or diagrams; three cells are **sub-threshold**, where the abstract algebra is not semisimple and only the image argument is available: $(4,2)$ at $3$, $(5,2)$ at $10$, $(5,3)$ at $15$.

**A test on $\lambda=\varnothing$ and $\lambda=(1)$ alone does not reach a lemma quantified over $\lvert\lambda\rvert$ up to $12$.** $\iota_\lambda$ is built for $(2),(1,1),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)$ and tested $c_0$ **as a map** at ten cells: equivariant for the full $O(n)$ to $3\times10^{-15}$, nonzero, and generating exactly $m_\lambda(n)$ every time, four of them sub-threshold and one at $\lambda=(2)$, $n=2$, $f=6$ generating $15$. The lemma survives being tested properly.

### What stays open

**Obligation (ii) is NOT closed by this.** The chain is: seed $\to$ spanning set of $23!!=316{,}234{,}143{,}225$ elements $\to$ *existence* of a combination. *That count and the $10{,}395$ the restriction collapses are one construction at two sizes: both are perfect-matching counts $(2k-1)!!$ on $2k$ points, at $k=12$ for the $24$ indices a degree-$6$ pairing carries on two sides and $k=6$ once restriction identifies them, so $10{,}395=11!!$ divides $23!!$ ([BrauerCounts.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/BrauerCounts.lean)).* The deliverable asks for **exhibition**, which needs $\dim e\,M_\lambda$ independent members found among $3.16\times10^{11}$ and then a change of basis against the constructed weight-space vectors, none of it done, none of it priced. **The lemma removes the search for a seed and leaves the search for an expansion.**

**Independence on the degree-3 diagonal** (obligation (i)), is untouched, since restriction to $W\otimes W\otimes W$ has a kernel.

#### Lemma L65, the seed is the inclusion padded by cups

*Audited in [ROUND_L65](../verification/ROUND_L65.md): `PROVEN, hypotheses corrected`. The argument is the section "The seed is the inclusion padded by cups", above.*

Let $n\ge1$ and let $\lambda$ be an $O(n)$-admissible label with $\lvert\lambda\rvert\in\{0,2,\dots,12\}$. Write $k=(12-\lvert\lambda\rvert)/2$, let $\iota_\lambda\colon V_\lambda\hookrightarrow V^{\otimes\lvert\lambda\rvert}$ be the inclusion and $\mathrm{cup}=\sum_ae_a\otimes e_a$ the invariant metric element. Then $c_0=\iota_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$ is a nonzero element of $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$, so the seed of every block is given in closed form and need not be searched for. Admissibility is what makes $V_\lambda$ nonzero, and it holds whenever $\lvert\lambda\rvert\le n$.
