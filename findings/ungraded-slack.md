# The ungraded half: the slack gap

The strict side of the graded/ungraded split: the slack $S(W)$, its closed Gram form, what it measures, and the four attacks on the gap that failed. Split out of [graded-half.md](graded-half.md) on 2026-08-31, which had grown to two subjects at 83 KB. The graded enumeration itself stays there.

#### The sharp (II)-side bound is a valid inequality whose separation role is dead

Reviewer 13 rebuilt the four post-Reviewer-12 results from definitions ([review_r13_independent.py](../scripts/lib/review_r13_independent.py), [review_r13_multiblock.py](../scripts/lib/review_r13_multiblock.py), [review_r13_balanced3.py](../scripts/reviewers/r13/review_r13_balanced3.py); full audit in [review_r13_report.md](../agent_logs/review_r13_report.md)) and returned one refutation and three confirmations.

**The sharp (II)-side bound: inequality CONFIRMED, separation REFUTED.** The master identity closes at $\le2\times10^{-14}$ across roughly 380 unique critical points spanning $n=6\ldots24$, every published RHS number reproduces digit-for-digit, Fox equality is exact in all four closed-form constants, and $J_5\ge0$ held everywhere tested. The separation claim it was carrying ($S>0\Rightarrow\mathrm{RHS}<\sqrt{3/2}$), is **false**, and the counterexample is the third-simplest sphere product there is. The balanced family $(S^m)^3$ is zero-defect, ungraded ($S>0$), exactly critical, and has

$$\mathrm{RHS}=\theta\,\frac{4m-1}{3m-1},\qquad \theta^2=\frac{(3m-1)(m-1)}{3m^2},$$

which reaches $1.234230>\sqrt{3/2}$ at $S^8\times S^8\times S^8$ ($n=24$) and tends to $4/3$. The witness is saved as [review_r13_separation_breaker_S8S8S8_n24.npy](../agent_logs/review_r13_separation_breaker_S8S8S8_n24.npy): $\theta^2=161/192$ to twelve digits, criticality residual $2\times10^{-16}$, $\delta^2=0$, and $J_5$ identical across three independent $X_{\mathrm{inv}}$ forms.

**The error that hid it.** An earlier reading asserted that multi-block products give $\mathrm{RHS}=\theta$ because $J_4=J_5=0$. That is wrong: $J_5>0$ at every $k\ge3$ block (measured $1.97$–$5.26$), which contradicts the $0.888$–$1.071$ RHS bullet recorded alongside it. Both statements cannot hold, and the $J_5=0$ one is the false one. **Corrected here.**

**What survives.** The bound remains a valid, Fox-sharp inequality, so it keeps its place as a fence post; what it no longer does is separate. Any repair must be $n$-dependent or $\theta$-aware, the empirical regularity worth exploiting is that in all data, every point with $\mathrm{RHS}\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ has $\theta\le0.916$, far below the range that matters.

**Three confirmations.** (i) The Fox identity $\operatorname{tr}\hat S^4=1/4$ is exact, and for a structural reason rather than a numerical one: $\rho$ is scalar $=1/\sqrt6$ on the $4$-plane by $U(2)$-irreducibility, verified at paddings $n=8,12,16$. On the $\mathfrak{su}(m)$ family the value is $1/(2m)$, so the Tachibana factor leaves $\sqrt{3/2}$ immediately off $m=2$. (ii) The $W^*$ Hessian spectrum is confirmed at $n=6,8,10,12$ and at a held-out $n=16$ recomputed by a second sparse route, all five values and all three claimed multiplicities exact, with the fifth multiplicity identified as $((m-1)(m+2)/2)^2$; the branching derivation is still owed. (iii) The refutation of the growth argument stands, but the table needs real-orthogonal (Frobenius–Schur) accounting: $\mathfrak{su}(9)\times\mathfrak{sp}(11)$ at $n=198$ is of complex type with real dimension $396$ and must be struck.

**Ungraded critical points exist at $n=8,10,12$, constructively.** Zero-padding an operator from $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^6)$ into $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^n)$ preserves Ricci-flatness and the first Bianchi identity automatically, so a padded critical point is an **exact** critical point of the larger problem: residual $6\times10^{-16}$ at $n=8,10,12$, with the slack $S=1.158616$ and $\theta=0.390894929$ carried across unchanged ([p5_continuation.py](../scripts/lib/p5_continuation.py)). So their absence from random-start sweeps at those dimensions is a property of the search, not of the geometry.

**Every named structured family is graded, so the ungraded population has no structured members.** Enumerating all two-block sphere products and the $\mathbb{CP}^m$ family across $n=8\ldots24$ gives 61 critical points, **all with $S=0$** ([p5_structured_regularity.py](../scripts/pipelines/p5_structured_regularity.py)). Since random-start search reaches only a low-$\theta$ sub-population (below), the ungraded points that matter are reachable by neither route, they have no known construction and no search that finds them. That is the sharpest statement of why this half has resisted.

**On the graded population the two-sided regularity fails**, but the bound is not needed there (the graded half is settled by enumeration) so this does not refute the lead where it applies. Of the 61 graded points, 40 have $\mathrm{RHS}\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ with largest $\theta=1.325498$ at $S^{12}\times S^{12}$.

**The multi-block family is now closed in form, and that is the cycle's real result.** For $S^{n_1}\times\dots\times S^{n_k}$,

$$\theta^2=\frac{2(n-1)^2}{(k-1)n^2+\sum_a\frac{(n-n_a)^2}{n_a-1}},\qquad S=\frac{3\theta^2\,e_3(n_1,\dots,n_k)}{(n-1)^2},$$

**proved at every $k$ and every partition** by [Lemma L212](graded-half.md#lemma-l212-the-multi-block-vertex-solution-is-critical-at-every-partition-and-its-second-moment-is-the-norm-identity), whose exact division gives $t=8R$ identically and whose two displays give criticality, where this line first carried it verified symbolically for $k=2,3,4$ with symbolic block sizes and against the full $Q$ at residual $10^{-16}$. Two consequences follow immediately: **$\theta^2<2/(k-1)$ strictly**, so $k\ge3$ forces $\theta<1$ for every partition and every $n$; and **graded $\iff k\le2$**, since $e_3$ vanishes exactly then. Both reproduce every recorded number, $161/192$ at $(S^8)^3$, $253/144$ at $S^{12}\times S^{12}$, all four two-block rationals at $n=12$, $\theta_0(n)^2$, and $\mathrm{RHS}=\theta(4m-1)/(3m-1)$. It also supersedes the sampled enumeration, whose 60 seeds missed the uniform $(6,6,6,6)$ point at $\theta=0.729670$ and the $(12,12)$ coarsening at $n=24$.

**The lead survives but closes nothing, for three separate reasons.** No counterexample exists among roughly 1500 verified critical points, and $1$ is approached strictly from below and never attained. But:

- **The trigger does no filtering.** The computation of $\mathrm{RHS}$ used here had dropped the $\sqrt{1-e^2}$ factor, inflating it, $(6,6,6)$ read $1.322183$ against a true $1.200263$. Under the correct $\mathrm{RHS}$ the fraction of the family with $\mathrm{RHS}\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ falls from $100\%$ to $0.6\%$, and the "14 ungraded triggers" are $2$.
- **Gradedness and $\theta$ are perfectly confounded on this family.** Ungraded means $k\ge3$, and $\theta$ is a function of $k$ alone through $\theta^2<2/(k-1)$; the constant $1$ is exactly $\sqrt{2/(k-1)}$ at $k=3$. So the evidence shows "$k\ge3\Rightarrow\theta\le1$", a **block-count** statement, and cannot distinguish it from the claim about gradedness.
- **The other branch is the ungranted grant, and on this family it is now verified with a floor.** At every witness $\delta^2=0$, $J_4=0$ and the cap vanishes, so $\mathrm{RHS}-\theta=J_5/r^2$ identically. Evaluating that across **505 ungraded multi-block points** at $n=12\ldots24$, over all partitions into $3$, $4$ and $5$ blocks, gives

$$J_5/r^2\in[0.428904,\,0.804619],$$

so $J_5\ge0$ holds throughout with a floor of $0.43$ rather than marginally ([p3_j5_multiblock.py](../scripts/pipelines/p3_j5_multiblock.py)). The values are computed from measured $\mathrm{RHS}$ and $\theta$ at points screened to criticality residual $<10^{-8}$, independently of any closed form. **On the balanced family it is now proven rather than sampled.** There $\mathrm{RHS}=\theta(4m-1)/(3m-1)$, so

$$J_5/r^2=\mathrm{RHS}-\theta=\theta\cdot\frac{m}{3m-1}>0\qquad\text{for every }m,$$

an infinite family closed in form, with $\theta$ matching Reviewer 13's independent expression at difference $0$. Evaluating the same quantity across $147{,}811$ ungraded partitions into $3$ to $6$ blocks up to $n=60$ gives a minimum of $0.044886$ at $(2,2,56)$, the previously recorded $0.154185$ at $(2,2,2,2,2,50)$ came from applying the balanced-only formula at non-integer $m=n/k$, and the correct value there is $0.104311$, and $\mathrm{RHS}$ is **now closed in form for general partitions**: on this family the operator is diagonal in coordinate 2-planes with entries depending only on the blocks the indices fall in, $\delta^2=0$, and $\rho=\mathrm{Ric}(W_-)$ is constant on each block, so each ingredient collapses to sums over block pairs with multiplicities. The assembled expression reproduces the numerically computed $\mathrm{RHS}$ to $4.4\times10^{-16}$ across 105 ungraded points, most of them exactly zero ([p3_rhs_closedform.py](../scripts/pipelines/p3_rhs_closedform.py)). So $J_5/r^2=\mathrm{RHS}-\theta$ is determined by block data alone, with no operator construction, the unbalanced case is computable rather than indicative. **$J_5\ge0$ is proven on the whole family.** Solving the vertex system by hand rather than through the sampling solver gives, for every partition,

$$w_{ab}=-\frac{\theta}{n-1}\quad(a\ne b),\qquad w_{aa}=\frac{\theta\,(n-n_a)}{(n-1)(n_a-1)},$$

an exact critical point at residual $10^{-16}$ against the full structure-constant $Q$. Substituting into the closed forms,

$$\frac{J_5}{r^2}=\frac{\theta\,\big(4n\,e_3+8\,e_4\big)}{(n-1)\sum_a n_a(n-n_a)^2}\ \ge\ 0\quad\text{for every partition, with equality exactly when }k\le2 .$$

That supersedes both the balanced-only proof and the numerical sweep. Gradedness follows from the same expression: $e_3=e_4=0$ exactly when $k\le2$, matching $S=6n_1n_2n_3/D>0$ for $k=3$.

**$\theta$ is globally maximised at balanced blocks**, by strict convexity of $1/(n_a-1)$, and the second-order coefficient is negative in every direction, not only the single direction recorded earlier. The four $k=3$ branches with all off-diagonal entries nonzero are the three two-block coarsenings plus the genuine point, not a second three-block family.

**What remains is algebra, not a sweep**: with both $\theta$ and $\mathrm{RHS}$ closed in form, $J_5\ge0$ on the family is the symbolic inequality $\mathrm{RHS}>\theta$ in the block sizes $n_1,\dots,n_k$. For equal blocks it already reduces to $\theta\,m/(3m-1)>0$, proven for every $m$; the general case is the same statement with $k$ symbols. A finite sweep, however large, proves nothing beyond the range it covers, so it is superseded rather than extended. **This is the grant verified on 505 points**, where it had previously rested on 20, though it remains unproven in general and this family is only one part of the ungraded set.

**The cross-check is resolved, and the answer changes what "census" means here.** Of 106 partitions with a verified critical point, 33 had no solution matching the closed form, but the mismatch is in the solver, not the formula. At $(3,3,3,3)$ the formula gives $\theta=0.638285$ and one run's solutions begin at $0.745356$, while **an earlier run of the same partition returned $0.638285$**. The vertex solver is random-seeded and non-exhaustive, so it samples the solution set rather than enumerating it.

Two consequences. The closed form stands, and it is the only census of the family, the solver is not. And the $J_5$ figures above rest on 505 **solver-found** points, so they are a large sample of the family rather than a census of it; the distinction matters because the earlier claim to a census came from the solver's output, not from the formula.

**Other ungraded constructions all sit below $1$.** Products with $U(m)$ or $Sp(m)$ block structure give new ungraded points at $\theta=0.8367,0.8519,0.8577,0.8700$, and the quaternionic family **decreases**, $\mathbb{HP}^2$ at $1.1155$ graded, then $0.8529$, $0.7454$, $0.6857$ ungraded.

**Random-start search reaches only a low-$\theta$ sub-population, which is why the ceiling has resisted.** At $n=6$, 155 converged critical points (all ungraded), occupy $\theta\in[0.007,0.512]$ and $\mathrm{RHS}\in[1.010,1.153]$. Neither the recorded ceiling $0.900828$ nor the regularity's trigger $\mathrm{RHS}\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ is anywhere in that range, so the surviving lead, every point with $\mathrm{RHS}\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ has $\theta\le0.916$, cannot be tested on it at all ([p5_theta_aware.py](../scripts/pipelines/p5_theta_aware.py)). Everything on file above $\theta=0.512$ arrived from a **structured construction** (the named families, the sphere products, $(S^m)^3$) rather than from search, and the two populations do not meet. That is the same disjointness seen when two methods swept $n=6$ and returned one all-graded and one all-ungraded set, and it means no random sweep is evidence about the high-$\theta$ region.

**The ceiling's behaviour in $n$ is inaccessible by both available methods, for two distinct reasons.** Random-start Levenberg–Marquardt converges nothing at $n=8,10,12$, $0$ of $80$, $0$ of $60$, $0$ of $40$ at $4000$ function evaluations, about thirteen Jacobian builds at dimension $300$, while returning $113$ critical points at $n=6$, where the dimension is $84$. And continuation from padded seeds cannot move: a local ascent in $\theta$ that stays critical and ungraded returns the seed's $\theta=0.390894929$ unchanged to nine digits at $n=8$ and $n=10$, with residuals holding at $10^{-9}$. That is not a tuning failure but the geometry this project already established, **critical points are isolated modulo their orbits**, so there is no path within $\mathrm{Crit}(P)$ to ascend along, and continuation reaches only the orbit it started on.

Raising the ceiling therefore requires finding *different* critical points at higher $n$, which is exactly the search problem that does not scale. The recorded $\theta\le0.900828$ stands on $n=6$ and $8$, and the $0.324$ gap it leaves (what makes this half a loose problem rather than a tight one), is untested above $n=8$ with no method currently able to test it.

**Not checked:** the $J_5\ge0$ grant (still a grant) and the generic ungraded population at $n\ge12$.

#### The subalgebra family: $\theta$ from $\dim\mathfrak h$ alone, and Conjecture A proved on it

**This is the population P5 asked for: one where gradedness varies with no block count present.** Its members are indexed by a subalgebra rather than by a partition, so the confounding that makes the multi-block evidence unreadable (ungraded $\iff k\ge3$, and $\theta$ a function of $k$ alone), cannot arise here.

**The family.** For a subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)=\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ with $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak h^\perp$, $r=\dim\mathfrak h$ and $N=\binom n2$, set

$$W_{\mathfrak h}=c\Big(\Pi_{\mathfrak h}-\tfrac rN\mathrm{Id}\Big),\qquad c=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{r(N-r)}},$$

the unit-norm Casimir operator of $\mathfrak h$. Write $S_{\mathfrak{hhh}}=\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathfrak h}\langle[\varphi_a,\varphi_b],\varphi_c\rangle^2$ over an orthonormal basis, and likewise $S_{\mathfrak{hmm}}$ and $S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}$.

**Theorem.** Let $0<r<N$ and suppose $Q(W_{\mathfrak h})=\theta W_{\mathfrak h}$. Then

$$\theta=\frac2n\sqrt{\frac{rN}{N-r}},\qquad S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}=(N-r)\Big[2(n-2)-\frac{4(n-1)r}{N}\Big],$$

and since $S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}$ is a sum of squares,

$$r\le\frac{n(n-2)}4,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \theta\le\theta_0(n)=\frac{\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}}n,$$

with equality in either place exactly when $S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}=0$, i.e. $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak h$, i.e. $W_{\mathfrak h}$ is $\mathbb Z_2$-graded, i.e. $(\mathfrak{so}(n),\mathfrak h)$ is a symmetric pair. Among symmetric pairs only the balanced split reaches the cap, $\binom p2+\binom q2=n(n-2)/4$ with $p+q=n$ forces $(p-q)^2=0$, and $\mathfrak u(m)\subset\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ has $r=m^2\ne m(m-1)$, so **the maximum over the family is $\theta_0(n)$, attained exactly at the Weyl operator of $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$.** Equality needs $n$ even; for odd $n$ the cap is not an integer and $\theta<\theta_0$ strictly.

**The proof is three identities and one non-negativity.** With $\kappa=2(n-2)$ the Killing constant of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ in this normalisation and $\sigma=1+\kappa/2=n-1$: $\sum_{b,c}c_{abc}^2=\kappa$ for every $a$; $S_{\mathfrak{hhh}}+S_{\mathfrak{hmm}}=\kappa r$ and $2S_{\mathfrak{hmm}}+S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}=\kappa(N-r)$, both because $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]\subseteq\mathfrak h$. Since $W_{\mathfrak h}$ has two eigenvalues, $u=c(1-r/N)>0$ on $\mathfrak h$ and $v=-cr/N<0$ on $\mathfrak m$, the eigen-equation splits into two scalar equations; eliminating $S_{\mathfrak{hhh}}$ and $S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}$ gives $S_{\mathfrak{hmm}}=2\sigma r(N-r)/N$ and $\theta=\sigma ur/(N-r)$, which is the closed form. $\square$

**Criticality is free under three hypotheses**, and they are the practical way to produce members: with $K=N_{O(n)}(\mathfrak h)$ the **full normaliser**, (F1) $\mathfrak h$ and $\mathfrak m$ both $K$-irreducible **and inequivalent as $K$-modules**, (F2) $(\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathbb R^n)^K=\mathbb R\,\mathrm{Id}$, (F3) $(\Lambda^4\mathbb R^n)^K=0$. Then (F2) makes $W_{\mathfrak h}$ Ricci-flat, (F3) makes it Bianchi-free, $Q$ preserves the Weyl space, and the $K$-invariant traceless Weyl operators are $\mathbb R\,W_{\mathfrak h}$, so $Q(W_{\mathfrak h})=\theta W_{\mathfrak h}$. **Taking $K$ connected loses the equality case**: $\mathfrak{so}(4)+\mathfrak{so}(4)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$ then fails (F1), each $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ splitting self-dual $\oplus$ anti-self-dual, and (F3), through the two block volume forms; the $O(4)\times O(4)$ reflections and the block swap restore both.

**All three hypotheses are load-bearing, and what they exclude is the Berger list.** The subalgebras exceeding the cap are $\mathfrak g_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)$ ($r=14$ against $8.75$) and $\mathfrak{spin}(7)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$ ($r=21$ against $12$), each satisfying (F1) and (F2) and failing only (F3); $\mathfrak{so}(n-1)\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)$, failing only (F2); and $\mathfrak u(m)$, $\mathfrak{su}(m)$, $\mathfrak{sp}(m)+\mathfrak{sp}(1)$ and the unbalanced splits, failing (F1). Every one of them carries a parallel $4$-form, so **(F3) says exactly that $\mathfrak h$ is not a special-holonomy algebra**, and the theorem restates as a Lie-theoretic bound: *an irreducible non-special-holonomy subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ has dimension at most $n(n-2)/4$, with equality only for the balanced split.* No statement of it was located in [references/](../references/) or by search.

**Gate 1 and Gate 2 both passed.** [p5_casimir_family.py](../scripts/lib/p5_casimir_family.py) reproduces $\theta_0(n)$ at $n=6,8,10,12,14$ and $0.836660$ for the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ bi-invariant, matches both closed forms against structure-constant sums measured independently, and screens every member for unit norm, Ricci-flatness, $\mathrm{Alt}(W)=0$ and criticality residual, all at $10^{-16}$. Reviewer 25 rebuilt everything from definitions and **confirmed the closed forms, the cap and the equality case**, verifying $\kappa=2(n-2)$ at $n=3\ldots9$, $Q(\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$, and $Q$ preserving the Weyl space to $3.1\times10^{-16}$; it found no counterexample and supplied the normaliser correction, the $0<r<N$ and $\mathfrak h\not\cong\mathfrak m$ hypotheses, and the odd-$n$ strictness ([reviews/INDEX.md](../reviews/INDEX.md), Reviewer 25).

**What it does not reach, stated plainly.** The slack has the closed form $S=\tfrac{r}{nN}[4(N-r)-n^2]$ on the family, and $4(N-r)-n^2\ge0\iff r\le n(n-2)/4$, so **the cap is the graded compression inequality restricted to this family**, a sum-of-squares re-derivation and a cross-check rather than independent leverage. The family also has no near-extremal member other than the extremum: $\theta/\theta_0$ runs $1.000$ at the balanced two-block products and then $0.845,0.730,0.671,0.655,0.612,0.598,0.507,0.471$. And the maximum is unique only within the family, $W_{\mathbb{CP}^3}$ at $n=6$ is critical at residual $5.1\times10^{-16}$ with $\theta=\theta_0(6)$ exactly, tying $S^3\times S^3$ from outside it. Members are the balanced $k$-block products and a handful of isotropy-irreducible points; the unbalanced products, $\mathbb{CP}^m$, $\mathbb{HP}^m$, the complex Grassmannians and every generic ungraded point sit outside.

**Two non-members exceed $\theta_0$ harmlessly**, which is why (F2) matters: $\mathfrak{so}(3)+\mathfrak{so}(5)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$ gives $1.151371$ and $\mathfrak{so}(4)+\mathfrak{so}(6)\subset\mathfrak{so}(10)$ gives $1.203766$, both with $\|\mathrm{Ric}\|\approx1$, so neither is a Weyl operator. (F2) is what places $W_{\mathfrak h}$ in the arena at all.

#### Lemma L4, a generator for critical points, and the Casimir family

**The criterion.** If $K\subseteq SO(n)$ satisfies $\dim\mathcal W_n^K=1$, the $K$-invariant $W$ is a critical point automatically, by equivariance, no per-example computation. **Two hypotheses carry it and both are imports rather than assumptions on $K$**: $O(n)$-equivariance of $Q$, and $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$, **which is [Fox](../references/fox-2019-nonassociative-curvature-algebra/notes.md)'s Lemma 5.7 and no longer a debt**: $\rho(X*Y)$ is a symmetrised expression in the two Ricci tensors and $s(X*Y)=\langle\rho(X),\rho(Y)\rangle$, so $\ker\rho$ is a subalgebra, first Bianchi coming free from $*$ being defined on the algebraic curvature tensors. *Transcribed 2026-09-10, having been asserted as a remark and derived nowhere for as long as seven results consumed it.* **$(\Lambda^4\mathbb R^n)^K=0$ is not among them**: the argument runs verbatim without it, and it is not necessary, $K=U(m)$ has $\omega\wedge\omega\ne0$ while [Lemma L28](nondegeneracy.md) gives $\dim\mathcal W_{2m}^{U(m)}=1$ and criticality. Its real use is one step earlier, making $\dim\mathcal W_n^K=1$ cheap to *verify* on an example; it would become a necessary hypothesis only if the conclusion were restated over Ricci-flat operators rather than over $\mathcal W_n$.

Bi-invariant metrics on compact simple Lie groups and isotropy-irreducible symmetric spaces with $b_4=0$ both qualify, giving critical points at $\theta=0.8367$ ($SU(3)$, $n=8$) and $0.7464$ ($SU(6)/Sp(3)$, $n=14$) among others. **Reading $b_4=0$ as $(\Lambda^4\mathbb R^n)^K=0$ uses Cartan's identification of invariant forms with real cohomology, and that source is not in [references/](../references/)**, the dependency is recorded in [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md) and is unread rather than checked.

**The Casimir family and its closed form.** For a subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with $r=\dim\mathfrak h$, $N=\binom n2$ and $W_{\mathfrak h}$ the unit multiple of $\Pi_{\mathfrak h}-\tfrac rN\mathrm{Id}$ (two eigenvalues by construction), **and satisfying $Q(W_{\mathfrak h})=\theta W_{\mathfrak h}$, which the formula needs and this citation omitted**: at $\mathfrak{so}(5)\subset\mathfrak{so}(6)$, where criticality fails, $P=\tfrac2{15}\sqrt{30}$ against the formula's $\tfrac13\sqrt{30}$, short by exactly two fifths,
$$\theta=\frac2n\sqrt{\frac{rN}{N-r}},$$
**derived in full at [Lemma L23](#lemma-l23-the-conjecture-on-the-subalgebra-family-and-gradedness-as-the-cap-canonical-statement-and-proof) below**, from the two block-averaged trace equations and the Killing relations. *The support is that derivation and not agreement at test points.* **The two-eigenvalue property is a property of this family, not a consequence of the criterion**: the criterion returns an invariant line with no spectral control, and $U(3)$ at $n=6$ has $\dim\mathcal W_6^{U(3)}=1$ with three eigenvalues. **Nothing on file bridges the two**, so a member obtained from the criterion is not thereby a Casimir.

**The maximum, at even $n$.** At fixed $n$, $\theta$ increases with $r$; the largest subalgebra generating a critical point has $r=n(n-2)/4$, attained by $\mathfrak{so}(n/2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n/2)$, **and that this is the only attaining subalgebra is owed**: the two calculations are exact, but they exhaust over Cartan's classification of the symmetric pairs of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, and [references/](../references/) holds no Cartan, Helgason, Berger or Besse entry. *The $n=8$ half is secure by the inner-versus-outer determinant argument in [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md), **not** by the dimension count given here, which the ledger certifies as insufficient at that very dimension*, so the balanced sphere product is itself a member and the family's maximiser. **Both clauses need their quantifiers**: $n(n-2)/4$ is not an integer at odd $n$, and the monotonicity is in $r$ at fixed $n$ alone, across dimensions the family's own members reverse it, $r=8$ giving $0.8367$ at $n=8$ against $r=21$ giving $0.7464$ at $n=14$. [Lemma L23](#lemma-l23-the-conjecture-on-the-subalgebra-family-and-gradedness-as-the-cap-canonical-statement-and-proof) proves the cap and identifies what attains it: exactly the graded members.

**Diagonalizability, stated positively.** $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{so}(n/2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n/2)$ and its complement $\mathfrak m$ are each spanned by *basis* bivectors $e_i\wedge e_j$ (within-block and cross-block respectively) so $\Pi_{\mathfrak h}$ is a $0/1$ diagonal matrix and **every member of the balanced tower is coordinate-diagonal, at every even $n$**, already in the standard frame; $\mathfrak{so}(4)^{\oplus2}\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$ and $\mathfrak{so}(5)^{\oplus2}\subset\mathfrak{so}(10)$ are witnesses past the smallest. #### The slack-gap reformulation, and a literature test case that reinforces it

**The reformulation.** The exact $\theta$ formula proven here reads

$$\theta\operatorname{tr}(W_-)=\|\mathrm{Ric}(W_-)\|^2+6\delta^2-\operatorname{tr}(W_-^2)-S,$$

so the slack enters with a **minus sign**: ungradedness mechanically subtracts from $\theta$. If $S\ge c>0$ on the ungraded critical set, then $\theta$ falls $c/\operatorname{tr}(W_-)$ below the graded ceiling, which the enumeration already bounds. Since the gap to close is $0.324$ wide, *any* positive $c$ suffices, unlike the three closing arguments that died, none of which needed to be sharp either, but all of which were built as sharp inequalities.

**Why this is a better-posed question than an inequality hunt.** $\mathrm{Crit}(P)$ inside the unit sphere of $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ is a compact real algebraic set, so it has **finitely many connected components** (Łojasiewicz; Tarski–Seidenberg). $S$ is a polynomial on it and $\{S=0\}$ is exactly the graded class. **If the graded set is a union of connected components of $\mathrm{Crit}(P)$ (that is, if no continuous path of critical points joins a graded point to an ungraded one) then $S$ is bounded below on the remainder by compactness alone, with no estimate required.** That reduces the crux to a separation statement, and the orbit-rigidity certificates and Łojasiewicz analysis already in this repo were measuring precisely that separation without it being framed as the closing mechanism.

**Jensen's metric: a literature candidate that does not break it.** Petersen–Stanfield end their §2.4 with the curvature operator of **Jensen's second homogeneous Einstein metric on $S^3\times S^3$**, a *non-symmetric* Einstein metric, and one visibly outside the symmetric class, since their Theorem A confines a symmetric space's curvature eigenvalues to $[0,\tfrac12]$ and Jensen's has a negative one. It is therefore the natural candidate for a high-$\theta$ ungraded critical point. Rebuilt here from their presentation ([p3_jensen_s3s3.py](../scripts/pipelines/p3_jensen_s3s3.py)), the spectrum reproduces theirs exactly ($\tfrac7{24}$ with multiplicity $3$, $\tfrac1{12}$ with $7$, $-\tfrac1{24}$ with $5$) as a validation gate. Its Weyl part is then:

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| $\theta$ | $1.095445115=\sqrt{6/5}$ |
| criticality residual | $3.0\times10^{-16}$ |
| $\delta^2$ | $5/48$ |
| slack $S$ | $0$, **and that does not make it graded**: the slack is the sign-graded screen, which [graded-half.md](graded-half.md) marks vacuous wherever $\ker W\ne0$, and this point's Weyl kernel is $7$-dimensional |

**So a non-symmetric homogeneous Einstein metric can have a vanishing slack.** *The stronger reading (that it has a graded Weyl operator), is what the vacuity of the screen at nonzero kernel withholds, and it is why this point is withdrawn as a witness for [Lemma L58](#lemma-l58-the-graded-set-is-strictly-wider-than-the-subalgebra-family).* Gradedness is a condition on the eigenspaces of $W$ carrying a $\mathbb Z_2$-grading, and it is strictly weaker than symmetry of the underlying space. #### Lemma L38, two families in closed form, strictly decreasing, meeting at $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$

**Statement.** For $m\ge2$, the unit Weyl tensor of the $\mathbb{CP}^m$ family has

$$\theta^2=\frac{(m+1)(2m-1)}{3m(m-1)},$$

and the two-eigenvalue point of the symmetric pair $(\mathfrak{su}(m),\mathfrak{so}(m))$ inside $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$, with $p=\dim\mathfrak k=\binom m2$ and $q=\dim\mathfrak m=\binom{m+1}2-1$, has

$$\theta^2=\frac{m(m+1)}{(m-1)(m+2)}=\frac X{X-2},\qquad X=m(m+1).$$
