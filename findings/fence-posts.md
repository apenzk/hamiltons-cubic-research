# Fence posts 1-10, in full

#### Fence post: a hunt that cannot reach the target set is not evidence about the target set

Four 22-hour runs at $n=6,8,10,12$ sampled **2,060,095** screened Weyl tensors looking for a falsifier of $\sup\{\theta:S=0,\text{ non-diagonal}\}\le\sqrt{3/2}$. **Zero were in the target set**, not one was critical (residual $\approx4\times10^{-2}$ against a $10^{-8}$ tolerance, because the sampler took 60 fixed-step gradient steps and never solved), and not one had $S=0$, which is measure-zero for random draws in any case. So the run *could not have found a falsifier however long it ran*, and "no falsifier over two million points" is **vacuous** rather than strong. The recorded maximum $\theta$ pressing to $10^{-5}$ below $\sqrt{3/2}$ at $n=6$ is the global supremum over $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, which was already known, and says nothing about $\{S=0\}$.

*The general form, and it has now cost this project twice:* **before a sample is quoted as evidence, check that the sample can contain the thing being looked for.** A screen applied after the fact does not rescue a population that never met the hypothesis. The related instrument failure is on record too ($\theta$ read off relaxation values, and Lemma L24 read off non-critical points) which is why [weyl_screen.py](../scripts/lib/weyl_screen.py) has `critical_screened`. Here the screen was present and correctly refused every point; what was missing was noticing that it refused *all* of them.

*This is also the standing argument for the method the project actually runs on:* the same question is settled analytically by a bound, and the four runs bought nothing that an inequality would not have given in closed form.

## Post F1
*Reviewer 91.* It read "eigenvalue-only inequalities can never be sharp", which is literally untrue: the target $P\le\theta_0(n)\lVert W\rVert^3$ is itself norm-only and sharp. The correct, narrower statement is about **the split**: at the conjectured maximiser essentially all of $P$'s value sits in $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$, the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$-structure-constant term, which depends on eigenspace *alignment* rather than on the spectrum, while the Tachibana/Hölder estimates bound only $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$, asymptotically negligible there, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)/P=\frac2{(n-1)(n-2)}$ at $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$, proved as an identity rather than scanned. **So a bound that reaches $P$ only through $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$ cannot close the gap.** It says nothing about bounds on $\langle Q(W),W\rangle$ taken whole.

**The witness that proves it, replacing the earlier hand-wave.** *Reviewer 93.* At $n=6$ there are **two diagonal Weyl tensors with identical $\Lambda^2$-spectra** $(1^6,0^3,(-1)^6)$, identical $\lVert W\rVert^2=12$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)=0$, both with trivial stabiliser, and $P$ takes $\{-24,0,+24\}$ across the family. Both reviewers enumerated it exhaustively and exactly: **$300$ such tensors, with counts $60/180/60$**, all at $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)=0$ and $\lVert W\rVert^2=12$. The true spread is **$48$**, not the $24$ first recorded. So $P$ is **provably not a function of the spectrum**, by exhibition rather than by argument. *Note the scope carefully:* this says no *spectral function* determines $P$; it does **not** say a spectral bound cannot be sharp, and at $n=4$ one is, there $P=3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$ identically (post F2c).

## Post F2, SCOPED, and its stated MECHANISM is refuted, though the conclusion survives on better grounds.
*Reviewers 91 and 92, agreeing.* The $1.7$–$1.8\times$ overshoot is real and holds at all $95$ integers $n\in[6,100]$. But the old explanation, "the two extremal eigenvalue profiles do not coincide, the vertex matrix and $W$ cannot both sit at their individual extremes", is **false**, and would misdirect the next attempt into hunting compatible profiles. At the balanced sphere product the vertex bound is **exactly saturated**:

$$\frac{B_\#}{\theta_0}=\frac{n(n-3)}{(n-1)(n-2)}=1-\frac2{(n-1)(n-2)},\qquad\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)}{P}=\frac2{(n-1)(n-2)},$$

the two summing to $1$ identically. **So all of the slack is in the $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$ bound**, which is sharp for arbitrary trace-free operators and is loose on Weyl tensors, the $17\times$/$43\times$ once written here overstates it, the true looseness being at most $2.32\times$/$2.39\times$ (Reviewer 94). Using only *attained* values, any term-by-term route overshoots by at least $f(n)=\frac{n(n-3)}{(n-1)(n-2)}+\frac{n\sqrt3}{6\sqrt{(n-1)(n-2)}}$, unimodal, peaking at $n=11$ and decreasing to the **limit** $1+\frac{\sqrt3}6=1.2886\ldots$, a limit, **not a floor**: $f(4)$, $f(5)$ and $f(6)=\frac{9+\sqrt{15}}{10}$ all lie strictly below it, and $n=6$ is inside the live range, so the bound applies only from $n\ge7$ **Both reviewers refute the figure $1.29$ as an unconditional floor**: exactly, $f(323)>\tfrac{129}{100}>f(324)$. It also presupposes the *unproven* vertex bound, though the conclusion survives on attained values alone. **The term-by-term route stays closed, for that reason.**

2b. **The WHOLE-$\langle Q(W),W\rangle$ route is a different route, and posts 1 and 2 never covered it.** [Xu](../references/xu-2024-ricci-flow-invariant-conditions/notes.md) Lemma 2.14 bounds $\langle Q(W),W\rangle$ without ever splitting it: $\lvert\langle Q(W),W\rangle\rvert\le\sqrt{\frac{(n^2-1)(n-2)}n}\lVert W\rVert^3$, overshooting $\theta_0$ by $\sqrt{n(n+1)/2}$, $\sqrt{10},\sqrt{21},\sqrt{78},\sqrt{210}$ at $n=4,6,12,20$, growing like $n/\sqrt2$. *The route was abandoned here on 2026-08-26 on the strength of posts 1 and 2, which was an error.* **But what it owes is NOT a constant factor**, an earlier version of this line said so, conflating this route with the term-by-term one whose refinement reaches $1.77$. Reviewer 91 localises the loss to a single step, Xu's Rayleigh estimate (2.55), and that step is quantified exactly:

Xu's (2.53) $\sum\langle W(\xi^{pqrs}),\xi^{pqrs}\rangle=-16\langle Q(W),W\rangle$ and (2.54) $\sum\langle\xi^{pqrs},\xi^{pqrs}\rangle=16(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$ are **identities**. Almost all the inequality is (2.55), Reviewer 94 notes Xu's chain also carries a triangle-inequality step, which the $M$-form below deletes for free, and (2.55) applies $\sqrt{(N-1)/N}\,\lVert W\rVert$ to *every* $\xi^{pqrs}$ independently, as if each could simultaneously be $W$'s top eigenvector. Writing $M=\sum\xi\otimes\xi$, a PSD operator with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M=16(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(WM)=-16\langle Q(W),W\rangle$, the conjecture is exactly

$$\big\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W\widehat M)\big\rvert\le\frac{\theta_0(n)\lVert W\rVert}{n-1}=\frac{\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}}{n(n-1)}\lVert W\rVert\ \sim\ \frac{\sqrt2}n\lVert W\rVert,\qquad\widehat M=M/\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M,$$

against the $\approx\lVert W\rVert$ that (2.55) supplies, needed/used is $0.2108$, $0.1124$, $0.0688$, $0.0280$ at $n=6,12,20,50$. **So this route owes a factor growing like $n/\sqrt2$, not a constant**, and closing it means using that $M$ is *quadratic in $W$*, the alignment information (2.55) throws away, which is what post F1 as corrected identifies as the missing ingredient.

**Classification.** This is a reformulation of the kind [target.md](target.md) already covers under "The Rayleigh matrix, exactly", whose verdict stands: such restatements are **Conjecture A rewritten, neither easier nor harder**. What is new here is only the *measurement*, where a published route loses, and how much.

2c. **THE $\sqrt{3/2}$ THRESHOLD IS SHARP AT $n=4$, AND THE REASON IS THE ADJUGATE.** On $\mathrm{Weyl}_4$, $\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{so}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)$ splits $W$ into chiralities $W^\pm$, each a symmetric **trace-free** operator on $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, and on $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ the sharp map $\#$ **is the adjugate**. Since $\langle\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}A,A\rangle=3\det A$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^3)=3\det A$ for trace-free $3\times3$ $A$,

$$\langle W^\#,W\rangle=6\big(\det W^++\det W^-\big)=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((W^+)^3\big)+2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((W^-)^3\big)=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3),$$

*(**Corrected by Reviewers 95 and 96.** This display first read $3(\det W^++\det W^-)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$, **half the truth**, and it contradicted the sentence immediately after it, which correctly quoted $2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$. The cause: on each chirality subalgebra $c^\pm=\mp\sqrt2\,\varepsilon$, so $\#$ restricted there is $2\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}$, **not** $\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}$. Verified on a fully general $\mathrm{Weyl}_4$, all ten parameters.)*

so $\langle Q(W),W\rangle$ is a **constant multiple of $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$, a purely spectral quantity.** Reviewer 94 fixes the constant at $\langle W^\#,W\rangle=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$, hence $\langle Q(W),W\rangle=3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$, confirmed independently on $S^2\times S^2$ and on $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ (whose $\Lambda^2$-spectrum is $(2,-1,-1,0,0,0)$). **The record had no occurrence of "adjugate" anywhere before this.**

**Consequently Lemma 3.5 has a two-line proof** (Okumura's sharp bound at $m=3$ on each chirality, then $a^{3/2}+b^{3/2}\le(a+b)^{3/2}$), giving $\sqrt{3/2}$ with the $\mathbb{CP}^2$ equality case for free. **Huisken's Lagrange enumeration is not load-bearing**, and the moduli count is not the operative obstruction either: the earlier version of this post demoted the correct reason (the $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ splitting) in favour of an incorrect one, and its phrase "at $n=4$ the alignment degrees of freedom are gone" contradicted its own count of $4$.

> **This reframes the crux.** The question is not "spectral versus alignment", at $n=4$ a spectral bound *is* sharp. It is: **for which structures of $T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ does $\langle W^\#,W\rangle=\sum\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ collapse to a function of the spectrum alone?** At $n=4$ the answer is the adjugate. This is the live route.

## Post F24, WITHDRAWN AS A FENCE POST, the implication is true and fences off nothing.
*Reviewers 105 and 106, independently. Recorded here rather than deleted because the reasoning is instructive and the post stood for one commit.*

The statement was: any bound $R$ depending on $W$ only through diagonal information of size $t$, non-increasing in $t$, with $R(2)=\theta_0(n)$, satisfies $R(t)\ge\theta_0(n)$ for all $t<2$. **The implication is correct (it is two lines of logic) and every consequence drawn from it fails.**

- **$R\ge\theta_0$ is non-improvement, not vacuity.** The constant function $R\equiv\theta_0(n)$ satisfies both hypotheses, and *it is Conjecture A*. A bound sitting at $\theta_0$ everywhere is exactly what the project is trying to prove, so "$\ge\theta_0$" cannot be the mark of a useless bound.
- **The sharpest bound of the fenced shape fails the fence's own hypothesis.** $R^\star(t)=\sup\{\theta:\kappa^2=t\}$ is **non-monotone** at $t=2$, so the shape does not even contain its own extremal member.
- **The stated mechanism is unsound.** "Monotonicity is automatic when the diagonal class is a linear space" is false under $\lVert M\rVert_F=1$: lowering $t$ forces off-diagonal mass $1-t/2$ *in* rather than weakening constraints, so the classes are disjoint slices rather than nested.
- **And it would have caught the round's own positive result.** The stability statement $\theta\le\theta_0+O(2-\kappa^2)$ reads $W$ only through $\kappa^2$, is non-increasing in it, and is exact at $\kappa^2=2$, precisely the fenced shape. A fence that excludes the thing the same cycle was recommending is not describing a real obstruction.

**What survives, and it is worth keeping as a positive statement rather than a fence.** The Schur–Horn relaxation *is* strictly loose below $t=2$, both reviewers confirm that independently. The reason is specific and does not generalise: **the balanced sphere product's own $O(n)$-frame orbit sweeps $t\in[0.003,2]$ at $n=12$ with $\theta$ invariant**, so any *single-frame* bound must be $\ge\theta_0$ across that entire interval. That is a fact about one orbit, needs no monotonicity and no linear-space hypothesis, and it explains exactly why the multi-frame family escapes: $\kappa^2\equiv2$ along the orbit.

2g. **THE COLLAPSE HOLDS FOR $n\le5$, AND THE MECHANISM IS LOVELOCK'S IDENTITY.** *Rewritten 2026-08-26. The previous version of this post confined the collapse to $n=4$; Reviewers 97 and 98, on identical briefs, refuted that at $n=5$. What follows replaces it.*

On $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ for $n\le5$, and only there,

$$\beta=\tfrac12\alpha,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \langle W^\#,W\rangle=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \theta=3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3),$$

where $\alpha=W_{tpsq}W_{tpkl}W_{sqkl}$ and $\beta=W_{tspq}W_{tjpl}W_{sjql}$ are the two cubic contractions. So the cubic **is** a function of the $\Lambda^2$-spectrum alone at $n=4$ *and* $n=5$, and is not from $n=6$.

**The mechanism, which covers both dimensions uniformly.** The generalized Kronecker delta on six indices vanishes identically when $n\le5$, and

$$\beta-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}=-\tfrac1{64}\,\delta^{a_1\cdots a_6}_{b_1\cdots b_6}\,W_{a_1a_2}{}^{b_1b_2}W_{a_3a_4}{}^{b_3b_4}W_{a_5a_6}{}^{b_5b_6}.$$

This is Lovelock's dimensionally-dependent identity, obtained by antisymmetrising over $n+1$ indices. The constant $-64$ is independently confirmed at $n=6,7,8,9$ from a direct $720$-term $S_6$ expansion ([run_lovelock_syzygy.py](../scripts/runs/run_lovelock_syzygy.py)). It replaces the $\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{so}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)$ chirality story, which explained $n=4$ and could not reach $n=5$.

**Why the previous version failed, in three ways.** Its collapse table was *correct* (Reviewer 97 reproduced every row, $1/20$, $7/20$, $14/20$, $1/35$, $10/35$, $38/84$) and the conclusion drawn from it was not.

Collapse requires the condition only at the single $\lambda(W)$ that $T(W)$ comes with. Bianchi constrains the frame *relative to* $\lambda$, $54$ dimensions of traceless operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^5$ against $\dim\mathrm{Weyl}_5=35$, so nineteen conditions were discarded. What it proved is that constant $T$ is the only $\lambda$-*uniform* mechanism, which decides no dimension.

Verified spans: the samples behind these statements provably span $\mathrm{Weyl}_4$ (rank $10$) and $\mathrm{Weyl}_5$ (rank $35$) ([run_n5_syzygy.py](../scripts/runs/run_n5_syzygy.py)).

2f. **THE COLLAPSE CRITERION, sufficient only, after repair; the "iff" is REFUTED both ways.** *Reviewers 95 and 96, with explicit witnesses.* In an eigenframe $\theta=\sum\lambda_\alpha^3+3\sum_{\alpha<\beta<\gamma}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma$. - **Necessity is false.** Cross-block mass at a constant value contributes **nothing**: with $T\equiv s$ on all $18$ mixed triples of $\{1,2,3\}\mid\{4,5,6\}$, the mixed total assembles as $s(e_2e_1+e_1e_2)$ and the per-block $e_1=0$ kills it, verified here, identically zero for every $s$. So condition 1 is **not** necessary.

#### The Weyl algebra has no unit, which bars the sharpest extremal-idempotent theorems

#### One line from $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(L_x)\equiv0$

Tkachev's characterisation of minimal algebras, every extremal idempotent satisfies $|c|^2\le\tfrac12|e|^2$, with equality exactly on the Jordan algebras of Clifford type, assumes a **unital** Euclidean metrised algebra, and the conjugation $c\mapsto e-c$ that drives its proof needs the unit outright. The parts of that literature not needing a unit: existence of extremal idempotents in any Euclidean metrised algebra, and the extremal spectral bound $\sigma(L_c|_{c^\perp})\subseteq(-\infty,\tfrac12]$ with $1$ simple, which is this project's Lemma L24. #### The fusion rule is the third-order maximality condition, and it is vacuous along the orbit

*Gate 2, Reviewers 287 and 288.* Krasnov–Tkachev's Proposition 6.7 gives, at an extremal idempotent, the fusion rule $A_c(\tfrac12)A_c(\tfrac12)\subseteq A_c(\tfrac12)^\perp$. **The earlier reading of it here (that it "tracks primitivity"), is refuted.** It is the third-order necessary condition for a local maximum: with $T(z,z,z)$ the cubic's own third variation along a Hessian null direction, the rule is $T\equiv0$ on $A_c(\tfrac12)$ by polarisation. ## Post F31, the sharp target is not freely described, and $n$ cannot be smuggled in as a trace

#### Lemma L64, the transfer mechanism

*Audited in [ROUND_L64](../verification/ROUND_L64.md): `PROVEN, hypotheses corrected`.*

**Setting.** Fix a degree $d\ge1$. Zero-padding embeds $\mathcal W_{n_0}$ isometrically in $\mathcal W_n$ and makes $\{\mathrm{Sym}^d\mathcal W_n^*\}$ a module over the base $\{\mathbb R^n\}$ with the group sequence $\{B_n\}$ of signed permutations. **$k_B(d)$ is that module's presentation degree**, in the sense of Definition 2.7 of [Levin–Chandrasekaran](../references/levin-chandrasekaran-dimension-free/notes.md), and $g(d)\le k_B(d)$ is its generation degree.

**Lemma.** Let $F$ be an $O(n_0)$-invariant form of degree $d$ on $\mathcal W_{n_0}$.

1. **Existence.** For every $n\ge n_0$, $F$ has an $O(n)$-invariant extension to $\mathcal W_n$, a form restricting to $F$ on the padded copy. No hypothesis on $n_0$ enters.
2. **Uniqueness.** When $n\ge n_0\ge k_B(d)$, $F$ has exactly one $B_n$-invariant extension, which is therefore the $O(n)$-invariant extension of part 1.

With $SO$ in place of $O$, part 2 holds as stated, and part 1 holds when $n_0$ is odd or $n_0\ge4d$. In those cases every $SO(n_0)$-invariant form of degree $d$ on $\mathcal W_{n_0}$ is $O(n_0)$-invariant. The first fundamental theorem for $SO(n_0)$ adds to the contractions of step (i) only terms carrying one $\varepsilon$ on $n_0$ of the $4d$ slots, which needs $n_0\le4d$ with $4d-n_0$ even, and at $n_0=4d$ that term antisymmetrises each Weyl factor over all four of its slots and vanishes by $W_{[ijkl]}=0$. Part 2 needs nothing more, because its range lies inside $n_0\ge4d$ by the bound $k_B(d)\ge4d$ below. **Otherwise part 1 fails.** At $n_0=4$ and $d=2$, $F(W)=\sum\varepsilon_{ijkl}W_{abij}W_{abkl}$ is $SO(4)$-invariant, changes sign under $r=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, and equals $2\sum W_{abij}^2$ on a self-dual $W$, so it is nonzero. $\mathrm{blkdiag}(r,-1,I_{n-5})\in SO(n)$ acts on the padded copy as $r$, so $F$ has no $SO(n)$-invariant extension at any $n\ge5$.

**Proof.** (i) Let $\pi_{n_0}$ be the $O(n_0)$-equivariant orthogonal projection of $(\mathbb R^{n_0})^{\otimes4}$ onto $\mathcal W_{n_0}$. The polarisation of $F\circ\pi_{n_0}$ is an $O(n_0)$-invariant multilinear form on $(\mathbb R^{n_0})^{\otimes4d}$, and the first fundamental theorem for $O(n_0)$ writes it as a combination of complete contractions along perfect matchings $\pi$ of the $4d$ slots. On $\mathcal W_{n_0}$ this reads $F=\sum_\pi a_\pi C^{(n_0)}_\pi$, where $C^{(m)}_\pi(W)$ contracts $W^{\otimes d}$ along $\pi$ with every index summed over $[m]$. (ii) Set $G=\sum_\pi a_\pi C^{(n)}_\pi$ on $\mathcal W_n$. Each $C^{(n)}_\pi$ is $O(n)$-invariant. Every summation index sits on a slot of a Weyl factor, so no closed $\delta$-loop occurs, and on a padded tensor every term carrying an index above $n_0$ vanishes. Hence $G$ restricts to $F$, which is part 1. (iii) For $n\ge n_0\ge k_B(d)$, Proposition 2.9 makes restriction from the $B_n$-invariants of $\mathrm{Sym}^d\mathcal W_n^*$ to the $B_{n_0}$-invariants of $\mathrm{Sym}^d\mathcal W_{n_0}^*$ injective. $G$ is $B_n$-invariant and restricts to $F$, so it is the only $B_n$-invariant extension, which is part 2. $\square$

**The hypothesis of part 2 holds for every large enough $n_0$, no value of $k_B(d)$ is known, and at $n_0=12$ the degree-$6$ certificate lies outside its range.**

- **$k_B(d)\ge g(d)\ge4d$.** Let $T\in\mathcal W_4$ have $T_{1234}=1$, $T_{1342}=-1$, $T_{1423}=0$, with their curvature-symmetry images and every other component zero. Its Bianchi sum is $1-1+0=0$, and it is traceless because every Ricci component carries a repeated index. The product of $d$ copies of $T$ on the disjoint blocks $\{4k-3,\dots,4k\}$ of $[4d]$ has a nonzero component at a multi-index with $4d$ distinct values. A signed permutation maps a support of size $m$ to a support of size $m$, so for $m<4d$ no combination of translates of padded elements of $\mathrm{Sym}^d\mathcal W_m$ has such a component. **So $k_B(6)\ge24>12$.**
- **$k_B(d)$ is finite for every $d\ge1$, and no value is on file.** The module sits inside $\{(\mathbb R^n)^{\otimes4d}\}$, generated in degree $4d$ by Example 2.12. Definition A.2 and Example A.3(b) of [Levin–Chandrasekaran](../references/levin-chandrasekaran-dimension-free/notes.md) make a module over $\{\mathbb R^n\}$ with $\{B_n\}$ a module over $\mathrm{FI}_{BC}$, and Theorem A.5 makes every submodule of a finitely generated one finitely generated. So the module and the kernel of a finitely generated free cover of it are both finitely generated, which is a finite presentation degree. Post F32 records that Theorem 2.11's kernel and images clauses give no presentation of $\{\mathcal W_n\}$ inside the curvature tensors.
- **Below the range, uniqueness fails.** $\mathcal W_3=0$, so at $n_0=3$ and $d=2$ both $0$ and $\lvert W\rvert^2$ extend $F=0$.

**The lemma transfers an element and its invariance, and it carries a sign only on the padded copy.** The same mechanism on the module $\{\mathrm{Sym}^2S^n\}$ of quadratic forms on symmetric matrices, presented in degree $4$ by Levin–Chandrasekaran's Example 2.12 and Theorem 2.11, takes $F(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^2-\tfrac14(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X)^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((X-\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X\,I_4)^2\big)\ge0$ on $S^4$ to $G(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^2-\tfrac14(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X)^2$ on $S^5$, where $G(I_5)=5-\tfrac{25}4=-\tfrac54$. **Carrying a nonnegativity certificate from $n_0$ to $n$ needs an argument of its own, and none is on file.** Post F31, above, is the instance at the balanced sphere product.

## Post F34, Cauchy–Schwarz on the sharp term cannot close, and its loss is largest where the conjecture is slack

$\theta=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M^3)+\langle M^\#,M\rangle$ localises the whole difficulty in the sharp term, and the natural relaxation $\lvert\langle M^\#,M\rangle\rvert\le\lVert M^\#\rVert_F$ **cannot reach $\theta_0$.** Three independent reasons, any one sufficient.

**It is the conjecture restated.** $\cos\varphi:=\langle M^\#,M\rangle/\lVert M^\#\rVert_F$ is a definition, so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M^3)+\lVert M^\#\rVert_F\cos\varphi\le\theta_0$ *is* $\theta\le\theta_0$. **The strengthened form is false at every even $n\ge4$**, at the balanced sphere product, with **relative** excess

$$\frac{m}{(2m-1)\big(u+\sqrt{u^2+m^2-m}\big)}>0,\qquad u=m(2m-3),\ m=n/2,$$

asymptotically $\tfrac1{8m^2}=\tfrac1{2n^2}$; the **absolute** excess is $\sqrt{(m-1)/(2m-1)}\big/\big(u+\sqrt{u^2+m^2-m}\big)$. *The distinction was missing and both reviewers flagged it.* *So a successor must be sharp to $O(n^{-2})$ **uniformly in $n$**, not at one dimension.*

Each replaces *bounding $\theta$* with a different statement, and each closes for its own reason.

**Localization at the sphere product.** Nienhaus's $n\le11$ argument does not extend: with $\Gamma_1(\alpha)=(1+\alpha)\sqrt{1-2\alpha}$ (reproducing his own $g_1(1/3)=0$ and $g_2(1/3)=5\sqrt3/9$), the closing estimate vanishes **identically** at $\alpha=1/3$, so any cap $c>1$ breaks it. **The support dichotomy.** Its crux holds, $\ker T$ for $T_{ip}=\sum_{jkl}W_{ijkl}W_{pjkl}$ is exactly the annihilator, so support $s$ forces a padding and $\theta\le M(s)$, but the mechanism proposed for the residual borrows $r$ and $S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}$ from a lemma defining them **only on the subalgebra family**, which every unbalanced sphere product leaves. *A successor must keep every borrowed quantity inside its domain.*

**Cone invariance under $\dot W=Q(W)$.** $\nabla P=3Q(W)$, so the projected ODE is the gradient flow of $P/3$ and the maximizer is its attractor, but the target statement is vacuous: a $Q$-invariant cone $C$ with $C\cap S\subseteq\{P\le\theta_0\}$ is satisfied verbatim by $C=\mathbb R_+W^\ast$, the maximizer's own ray, and yields the conjecture only for $C=\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, where invariance says nothing. *A successor must state a cone condition that is neither vacuous nor the conjecture.* [Beitz's $\Omega_a$ cones](../references/beitz-2018-bianchi-convexity/) are already priced as strictly stronger than Conjecture A.

## Post F66, the maximiser is not a moment-map minimal vector, because no reductive group both preserves $\mathcal W_n$ and acts non-isometrically

**Established 2026-09-13 as the root of [R22](../routes.md), the step 9 route the sweep ranked first, and its argument was corrected at Gate 2** ([r908](../reviews/REVIEW_r908.md), [r909](../reviews/REVIEW_r909.md), which agreed the route is dead and refuted the first argument for it). *The route was to cast $Q(W)=\theta W$ as the minimal-vector condition of a moment map, so the maximiser is the minimal vector of the top Kirwan--Ness stratum and the stratum separates two idempotents of one low-moment fibre. The route is dead, for two reasons neither of which is the moment map being flat on $\mathcal W_n$.*

**First: $Q(W)=\theta W$ is the criticality of a CUBIC on the sphere, categorically not a minimal-vector condition.** A minimal vector is a zero of a moment map, which is QUADRATIC in the vector; $Q(W)=\theta W$ equates a quadratic to a linear term with a degree-zero multiplier, the Lagrange condition of a cubic. The two are conditions of different type, and no identification makes one the other. *The dimension count that a moment-map reading would need, $\mathcal W_n\cong\mathfrak g$, also fails: $\dim\mathcal W_n=n(n+1)(n+2)(n-3)/12$ never equals $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n)=\binom n2$ for $n\ge4$.*

**Second: no reductive group gives $\mathcal W_n$ a non-trivial minimal-vector structure at all.** The isometry group of the definite inner product on $\mathcal W_n$ is COMPACT, and a compact group's minimal-vector theory is trivial, every vector minimal. A NON-compact reductive group such as $GL_n$ does not preserve $\mathcal W_n$: Ricci-flatness is a contraction against the metric, $GL_n$ does not preserve the metric, so a $GL_n$-image of a Weyl tensor is generically not Ricci-flat and $\mathcal W_n$ is not $GL_n$-stable. *There is no group that both fixes the space and acts non-isometrically, which is what a moment-map stratification requires.*

**What was wrong in the first argument, recorded so it is not repeated.** It claimed $GL_n$'s moment map on curvature tensors is the Ricci contraction with $\mathcal W_n$ its zero fibre. The moment map is the QUADRATIC first-slot Gram contraction $A_{ij}=\sum_{pqr}W_{ipqr}W_{jpqr}$, not the linear Ricci tensor, and its zero set is $\{0\}$ for $GL_n$; a Gram matrix vanishes only at $W=0$. Weyl tensors sit in the moment-map zero set only at $n=4$, by the Bel--Robinson identity $W_{ipqr}W_{jpqr}=\tfrac14|W|^2\delta_{ij}$, and the programme is general $n$. *The error was importing Lauret's "Ricci is a moment map" from the setting where the variable is a Lie bracket, and Ricci is quadratic in it, into one where the variable is itself a curvature tensor.*

**What the post SAYS to a successor.** The separator the programme wants cannot be a moment-map or Kirwan--Ness stratum of $\mathcal W_n$: there is no reductive action to stratify it by. *The moment-map idea survives only for the Fox algebra's STRUCTURE TENSOR under $GL(\mathcal W_n)$, where medial and isospectral algebras arise (Route B, [krasnov-tkachev-2022](../references/krasnov-tkachev-2022-medial-isospectral/notes.md)), a genuinely different object. And the separation remains, as [F65](fence-posts.md) also says, a cubic phenomenon that first- and second-order invariants cannot see.*
