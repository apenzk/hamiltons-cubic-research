## Post F21, sectional-curvature machinery cannot deliver $|\lambda_{\min}(W)|$, because at the Kähler points $\lambda_{\min}$ is attained on a non-decomposable bivector that no $2$-plane witnesses

#### Fence post F21: sectional-curvature machinery cannot deliver $|\lambda_{\min}(W)|$, because at the Kähler points $\lambda_{\min}$ is attained on a non-decomposable bivector that no $2$-plane witnesses

The ingredient [b_lambda_weighted_bound.py](../scripts/route-b/b_lambda_weighted_bound.py) needs is control of $|\lambda_{\min}|$ on the band. The natural literature for it is the sharp pointwise Weitzenböck estimates, [Ge arXiv:2607.18216](../references/ge-2026-sharp-weitzenbock-pic2/notes.md), resting on Ni–Wilking's generalised Berger inequality, and the identification is real: **$q_2(W)=-2W$ on Weyl tensors** (verified $10^{-16}$), so their operator is ours. But every estimate in that family is phrased in **sectional** curvature, and on $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ the sectional range is strictly narrower than the spectral range:

| | $K_{\min}$ | $\lambda_{\min}$ | factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{CP}^3$ | $-0.0791$ | $-0.2108$ | $2.7$ |
| $\mathbb{CP}^4$ | $-0.0423$ | $-0.1409$ | $3.3$ |
| $S^6\times S^6$ | $-0.1039$ | $-0.1124$ | $1.08$ |

At the Kähler points the minimising bivector is the Kähler-form direction, which is **non-decomposable**, so no $2$-plane sees it, and those are precisely the points that set the $\sqrt{3/2}$ threshold. *Recorded with the classical inequality itself*, screened at nine points and never violated ([b_berger_inequality.py](../scripts/route-b/b_berger_inequality.py)): $|R(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)|\le\tfrac23(K_{\max}-K_{\min})$ is **EXACTLY tight, ratio $1.0000$, at $\mathbb{CP}^2$, $\mathbb{CP}^3$, $\mathbb{CP}^4$, $\mathbb{CP}^5$ and $\mathbb{HP}^2$**, and exactly $0.7500$ at $S^3\times S^3$, $S^6\times S^6$ and the $C_5$ blow-up. *Exact, by L-BFGS over the Grassmannian and over orthonormal four-frames, both reviewers agreeing.* **The contrast is $0.75$ against $1.00$**, and $\mathbb{CP}^m$ is the classical extremiser of Berger's $\tfrac23$. **And the route is closed quantitatively as well as structurally.** Put a unit bivector in canonical form $\xi=\sum_i\sigma_ie_{2i-1}\wedge e_{2i}$, $\sum\sigma_i^2=1$, at most $r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ terms with the planes mutually orthogonal. Then $\langle W\xi,\xi\rangle=\sum_i\sigma_i^2K(P_i)+\sum_{i\ne j}\sigma_i\sigma_jR(e_{2i-1},e_{2i},e_{2j-1},e_{2j})$, and every off-diagonal term sits on an orthonormal **four**-frame, so Berger applies to it. With $\lVert\sigma\rVert_1\le\sqrt r$,

$$|\lambda_{\min}|\ \le\ |K_{\min}|+\tfrac23(K_{\max}-K_{\min})(r-1).$$

Against what the $\lambda$-weighted bound actually needs ($|\lambda_{\min}|\le\sqrt{3/2}/(n-1)$ at $\Delta=0$), this overshoots by $2.47\times$ ($\mathbb{CP}^3$), $3.62\times$ ($\mathbb{CP}^4$), $8.41\times$ ($S^6\times S^6$), $9.86\times$ ($C_5$), *exact.* **The factor grows with $n$ within each family**, sphere products $3.73\to14.59$ at $n=6\to20$, $\mathbb{CP}^{n/2}$ $2.47\to8.66$ at $n=6\to16$, though not across families, since $C_5$ at $n=10$ exceeds $S^6\times S^6$ at $n=12$. So no sharpening of the constant rescues it. *Strengthening not previously recorded:* $|\lambda_{\min}|/|K_{\min}|=\tfrac{2(m+1)}3=\tfrac{n+2}3$ **exactly** at $\mathbb{CP}^m$, so the blindness grows linearly in $n$.

**Berger's inequality is otherwise absent from this record and is outside the closed class**, since it constrains $R$'s off-diagonal component against the sectional range rather than $T$'s marginals; it is worth keeping for the $\#$ term even though it cannot serve the $|\lambda_{\min}|$ question.

#### Lemma L13, the spectral-moment ceiling, and what it does and does not close

**Statement.** Let $W$ be a critical point of $P$ on the unit sphere of $\mathcal W_n$ with $\theta=P(W)>0$, let $L_W\colon H\mapsto W\circ H$ be the multiplication operator, and let $k_0=\dim(O(n)\cdot W)$. Then

$$\theta^2\Big(1+\frac{k_0}4\Big)\ \le\ \operatorname{tr}(L_W^2)\ =\ \kappa_n,\qquad \kappa_n=\frac{(n-1)(2n-1)}4 .$$

**Proof.** Criticality gives $L_WW=\theta W$. Differentiating $O(n)$-equivariance of $Q$ along the orbit gives $L_Wo_A=\tfrac\theta2 o_A$ for every orbit tangent $o_A$, and the orbit tangent space is orthogonal to $W$. $L_W$ is self-adjoint, so $\operatorname{tr}(L_W^2)$ is the sum of its squared eigenvalues, which is at least $\theta^2+k_0(\theta/2)^2$. $\square$

**The Peirce cap is not used, and could not be.** The step is a **lower** bound on a sum of squares, so a cap (an upper bound on eigenvalues), supplies nothing; what is consumed is the *attainment* of $\theta/2$ on the orbit, which needs no maximality. *The block previously cited the cap as an input, and the cap's Jordan language does not transfer: $\mathcal W_n$ has no unit and its $L_W$ spectrum is not confined to $\{1,\tfrac12,0\}$, at $S^3\times S^3$ the record's own table reads $1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14,0,-\tfrac12$.*

**What the inequality yields, and in which direction.** A larger orbit gives a stronger bound, so $k_0\le\binom n2$ turns the display into $\theta^2\le\frac{2(n-1)(2n-1)}{n^2-n+8}$, which rises to $4$: **the best output the method can conceivably give is $\theta\le2-o(1)$, against a true value below $\sqrt2$.** *That is a ceiling on the method's power, not a bound it delivers at a given point.* **At a point with a small orbit the same inequality is far weaker**, padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $n=12$ is a local maximum with orbit $34$, where it gives only $\theta\le\sqrt{253/38}=2.58$, above the ceiling itself.

**The margin, and what it is a ratio of.** At $n=12$, $\kappa_{12}=\tfrac{253}4$ and $k_0=\binom{12}2=66$ give $\theta\le1.901128$, against $\theta_0(12)=1.236033$ and $\sqrt{3/2}=1.224745$. **The recorded $58.9$ is $(\text{bound}-\theta_0)/(\theta_0-\sqrt{3/2})$**, the overshoot measured in units of the window Conjecture A has to resolve, bracketed at $58.92$, *not a multiplicative excess, which is $1.54\times$.* Asymptotically the same ratio is $3.09$. **The figure is large because $n=12$ is the first dimension above the crossover $6+2\sqrt7=11.29$, where the denominator changes sign**; the ratio falls to $17.9$ at $n=14$ and $3.57$ at $n=100$. *At odd $n$ the denominator must read against $\Theta_n$ rather than $\theta_0(n)$, and the crossover is $\tfrac{13+\sqrt{97}}2$.*

**The ceiling is not the end of the method, and the record has the sharper numbers.** A level-3 bound of $1.7974$ at $n=12$ gives ratio $49.7$, and a level-4 bound of $1.3951$ granting an oracle gives $14.1$. **So "no refinement of it escapes" is false as a claim about the method**; what survives is the source's narrower statement, that no choice of isotropy pushes the level-2 ceiling below $2$.

**$\kappa_n=(n-1)(2n-1)/4$ holds at every $n\ge6$, both parities**, [Lemma L161](#lemma-l161-kappa_nfracn-12n-14-at-every-nge6-both-parities), which reads the constant off any single tensor once Fox's theorem makes it $W$-independent, and evaluates it at a sphere product. *The parity gap this block carried, and the asymptotic ceiling that lived inside it, are closed.*

**What is actually closed, and it is narrower than "the wrong invariants".** For a *known* $L_W$ the moment sequence is a complete invariant for $\theta$ (at a local maximum $\theta=\lambda_{\max}(L_W)$ is simple, and Newton's identities recover it) so the headline read as a statement about moments in general is backwards. **What the method has is not the sequence but three universal identities**: $\operatorname{tr}(L_W)=0$, $\operatorname{tr}(L_W^2)=\kappa_n$ constant in $W$, and the orbit attainment. *The second carries no information beyond the norm, and the record establishes there is no third independent one.* **So the closure is a truncation result: three moments do not separate $\theta$, and the fourth reaches only $14\times$.**
