## Route R2, splitting the critical set

The maximizer is a critical point, so it suffices to control the critical set; the graded compression inequality (proven, equality exactly on the $\mathbb Z_2$-graded class), splits it in two.

### The graded half: the enumeration and what it actually covers

*Literature first, and it paid.* [Petersen–Stanfield, *Poincaré Polynomials and Curvature Operators of Symmetric Spaces*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11236) (August 2025) computes the curvature operator of every compact irreducible symmetric space. **Their Theorem A, Proposition 2.2 and Theorem B are stated in [problem.md](problem.md), under "Petersen–Stanfield: what their tables do and do not settle", which is where that summary is canonical**, this file carried a byte-identical second copy of it, and the readings that go wrong on Theorem A are recorded there too.

**Why that matters here.** This project's graded enumeration ([review_r11_graded_law.py](../scripts/lib/review_r11_graded_law.py)) assumes a graded critical point takes the **two-eigenvalue** form

$$W=\frac{q\,\Pi_{\mathfrak k}-p\,\Pi_{\mathfrak m}}{\sqrt{pq(p+q)}},\qquad p=\dim\mathfrak k,\ q=\dim\mathfrak m,$$

which is scalar on the whole isotropy algebra. Proposition 2.2 says that is the special case $\mathfrak k$ simple (or accidentally degenerate). Whenever $\mathfrak k$ splits, the honest ansatz has one parameter per factor, and the enumeration kept only the diagonal slice $a_1=\dots=a_r$.

**The slice is genuinely partial, and demonstrably so.** [p2_multifactor_graded.py](../scripts/pipelines/p2_multifactor_graded.py) solves criticality on the full three-parameter family $a\,\Pi_{\mathfrak{so}(k)}+b\,\Pi_{\mathfrak{so}(l)}+g\,\Pi_{\mathfrak m}$ for the real Grassmannian pair $\mathfrak{so}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(l)\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)$, imposing Ricci-flatness and unit norm. At $n=12$:

| split | $\theta$ | eigenvalues | matches $\theta_{k,l}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2+10$ | $0.994987437$ | three ($a\ne b$) | $S^2\times S^{10}$ |
| $3+9$ | $1.141798451$ | three | $S^3\times S^9$ |
| $4+8$ | $1.201561484$ | three | $S^4\times S^8$ |
| $5+7$ | $\mathbf{1.228239158}$ | three | $S^5\times S^7$ |
| $6+6$ | $1.236033081$ | **two** ($a=b$) | $S^6\times S^6$ |

Every value reproduces Nienhaus Proposition 2.25 to nine digits, so the solver is right and the family is exactly the sphere products. The structural reading is the point: **only the balanced split is a two-eigenvalue operator.** Every unbalanced sphere product is a genuine three-eigenvalue graded critical point, and $S^5\times S^7$ at $n=12$ sits at $1.228239>\sqrt{3/2}=1.224745$, i.e. **above the threshold the enumeration was built to police, and outside the slice it searched.** The same pattern holds at $n=14$.

**What this does and does not cost.** The enumeration's *conclusion* survives: these points are sphere products, which is the permitted answer, and unbalanced ones sit below the balanced one, as Proposition 2.25 makes obvious. What fails is the *method*'s claim to exhaustiveness. It cannot have ruled out non-sphere-product graded critical points with unequal values across the factors of $\mathfrak k$, because it never looked there. Every symmetric pair with reducible isotropy, the Grassmannians, the Hermitian spaces (centre plus factors, eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ forced on the centre), the Wolf spaces ($\mathfrak{sp}(1)\oplus\mathfrak h'$), needs re-running on the full $(r+1)$-parameter family before the graded half can be called settled.

**Concrete next step.** Petersen–Stanfield tabulate the eigenvalues and multiplicities for all five classes (Lie groups, Grassmannians, simple isotropy, Hermitian, Wolf). For simple isotropy they give $\lambda_{\mathfrak h}=\tfrac14\dim M/\dim\mathfrak h$ in closed form. Running their published data through the criticality equation replaces this project's own Casimir dictionary with a citable one and closes P2 by enumeration rather than by ansatz.

#### Lemma L212, the multi-block vertex solution is critical at every partition, and its second moment is the norm identity

**For every partition $n=\sum_an_a$ with each $n_a\ge2$, the vertex solution is a critical point of $P$, its ordered-pair second moment is $t=8R$ identically, and at unit norm $\theta^2=1/(4R)$.** *Here $R=\tfrac{k-1}8+\tfrac18\big(\sum_a\tfrac1{n_a-1}-\tfrac1{n-1}\big)$ is the multi-block norm identity's right-hand side.* **What this lemma supplies that the chain lacked is CRITICALITY at every partition, which is the one thing [L46](problem.md) was being consumed for**, and the general-$k$ identity, which retires that identity's own debt above $k=4$. *The value $\theta^2=1/(4R)$ and the bound are corroboration: [ungraded-slack.md](ungraded-slack.md) already carries both, for every partition, by a route with no Einstein hypothesis in it. Gate 2 landed, [r922](../reviews/REVIEW_r922.md) and [r923](../reviews/REVIEW_r923.md) on identical briefs.*

**The vertex solution**, defined in [r653](../reviews/REVIEW_r653.md) and carried by [ungraded-slack.md](ungraded-slack.md): the **coordinate-diagonal** Weyl operator whose sectional matrix, at eigenvalue $\theta_g$, is
$$K_{ij}=\frac{\theta_g(n-n_a)}{(n-1)(n_a-1)}\ \text{ for } i\ne j \text{ in one block } a,\qquad K_{ij}=-\frac{\theta_g}{n-1}\ \text{ across blocks.}$$

**$\theta_g$ IS THE $Q$-EIGENVALUE, AND ON THIS FAMILY IT IS ALSO THE EINSTEIN CONSTANT.** *Both readings are needed and they agree. The product metric with $S^{n_a}$ at sectional curvature $c_a$ is Einstein with constant $\lambda$ exactly at $c_a=\lambda/(n_a-1)$, and its Weyl part is this $K$ with $\theta_g=\lambda$. Under the record's tabled $\theta=P(W)/\lVert W\rVert^3$ the multiplier would be scale-INVARIANT, so the scaling step below is entitled to $\theta_g$ only as the eigenvalue* ([r923](../reviews/REVIEW_r923.md)).

1. **$K\mathbf1=0$, which is the well-posedness condition rather than a discharged hypothesis.** A row at $i$ in block $a$ has $n_a-1$ within-block entries and $n-n_a$ across, so its sum is $\tfrac{\theta_g(n-n_a)}{n-1}-\tfrac{\theta_g(n-n_a)}{n-1}=0$. **Every member of $\mathcal W_n$ is Ricci-flat by definition**, so on the diagonal locus, where [L57](problem.md) makes Ricci-flatness exactly $K\mathbf1=0$, this says that $K$ defines a Weyl tensor at all. *It discharges nothing: [L46](problem.md)'s Einstein hypothesis is a condition on a METRIC and is spent on criticality, which is step 2* ([r922](../reviews/REVIEW_r922.md), [r923](../reviews/REVIEW_r923.md), independently).
2. **It is a critical point, at every partition.** On the diagonal locus criticality is $\Phi(K)=\theta_gK$ off the diagonal, with $\Phi(M)=M^{\circ2}+M^2$. Write $u_a$ for the within-block entry and $v=-1/(n-1)$ at $\theta_g=1$. Within block $a$,
$$\Phi_{ij}=(n_a-1)u_a^2+(n-n_a)v^2=\frac{n-n_a}{(n-1)^2}\Big[\frac{n-n_a}{n_a-1}+1\Big]=\frac{n-n_a}{(n-1)(n_a-1)}=u_a,$$
and for $i\in a$, $j\in b$ with $a\ne b$, using $(n_a-1)u_a=\tfrac{n-n_a}{n-1}$,
$$\Phi_{ij}=v\Big[v+\frac{n-n_a}{n-1}+\frac{n-n_b}{n-1}+(n-n_a-n_b)v\Big]=v\cdot\frac{-1+2n-n_a-n_b-n+n_a+n_b}{n-1}=v.$$
*By $Q$'s quadratic homogeneity this gives $Q(W)=\theta_gW$ at every $\theta_g$, and with $\mathcal D_n$'s bijection it is criticality in $\mathcal W_n$.* **This is the step that lets the chain stop needing [L46](problem.md), Richard--Seshadri and the irreducible framing**, and it is what removes *locally symmetric*, *Einstein* and *$\lambda\ne0$.*
3. **$t=8R$ AS AN IDENTITY AT EVERY $k$, WHICH RETIRES THE NORM IDENTITY'S OWN DEBT.** At $\theta_g=1$, summing squares over ordered pairs,
$$t=\sum_{i\ne j}K_{ij}^2=\sum_a\frac{n_a(n-n_a)^2}{(n-1)^2(n_a-1)}+\frac{n^2-\sum_an_a^2}{(n-1)^2}.$$
*The first sum is rational in each $n_a$ and the division is exact: $m(n-m)^2-(n-1)^2$ vanishes at $m=1$, so $(m-1)$ divides it with quotient $m^2+(1-2n)m+(n-1)^2$.* Hence $t(n-1)^2=(n-1)^2\sum_a\tfrac1{n_a-1}+k(n-1)^2+n-n^2$ and, since $\tfrac n{n-1}=1+\tfrac1{n-1}$,
$$t=k-\frac n{n-1}+\sum_a\frac1{n_a-1}=k-1-\frac1{n-1}+\sum_a\frac1{n_a-1}=8R.$$
**And $\lVert W\rVert^2=t/2$ on the diagonal locus**, $t=2\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}W\rVert^2$ being an identity in the pair basis ([conventions.md](conventions.md)), **so the multi-block norm identity holds at every $k\ge2$ and every partition**, where the census above records it verified symbolically to $k=4$ with $(S^2)^5$ the smallest uncovered case. *That case is now covered, and so is every other.*
4. **The scale, with the representative named at each step.** $t$ and $\lVert W\rVert^2$ scale as $\theta_g^2$, so $\lVert W\rVert^2=4R\theta_g^2$ across the family. **Unit norm is $\theta_g=1/(2\sqrt R)$, and there $\theta=\theta_g$ because the configuration is critical**, giving $\theta^2=1/(4R)$. *The identity's own representative sits at $\theta_g=\tfrac12$, where $\lVert W\rVert^2=R$, and step 3's sits at $\theta_g=1$, where $\lVert W\rVert^2=4R$.* $\square$

> **THREE REPRESENTATIVES OF ONE FAMILY ARE IN PLAY, AND WRITING THE CONCLUSION AS $\theta=1/(2\lVert W\rVert)$ NAMES NONE OF THEM** ([r922](../reviews/REVIEW_r922.md), [r923](../reviews/REVIEW_r923.md), independently). *At unit norm $\lVert W\rVert=1$, so that display is satisfiable only when $\lVert W\rVert$ means the $\theta_g=\tfrac12$ representative's norm, and a reader taking it where $\theta$ was computed reads $\theta=\tfrac12$ at every partition. Checked at $(3,3)$: entries $\tfrac3{10}$ and $-\tfrac15$ with $\lVert W\rVert^2=\tfrac9{10}$ at $\theta_g=1$, entries $\tfrac3{20}$ and $-\tfrac1{10}$ with $\lVert W\rVert^2=\tfrac9{40}=R$ at $\theta_g=\tfrac12$, and $\theta=\sqrt{10}/3$ at unit norm.* **So the statement is $\theta^2=1/(4R)$, and $\lVert W\rVert$ appears nowhere in it.**

> **AND $\lambda=\tfrac12$ IS NOT REMOVED, WHICH THIS BLOCK ONCE CLAIMED** ([r922](../reviews/REVIEW_r922.md), [r923](../reviews/REVIEW_r923.md), independently). *[L46](problem.md) reads $\theta=\lambda/\lVert W\rVert$, so the $2$ in $\theta=1/(2\lVert W\rVert)$ IS $1/\lambda$ at $\lambda=\tfrac12$, and $\theta_g=\lambda$ makes the identity's representative that very normalisation.* **What this lemma removes is the other three, and what it replaces them with is step 2.** *The gain is real and it is narrower than the claim: the chain no longer invokes a lemma stated for irreducible symmetric spaces on objects that are products, and the census items that broke it are untouched by it.*

**The bound follows, and it too is prior.** Every $n_a\ge2$ gives $\tfrac1{n_a-1}\ge\tfrac1{n-1}$, so $R>\tfrac{k-1}8$ strictly at $k\ge2$ and $\theta<\sqrt{2/(k-1)}$. *[ungraded-slack.md](ungraded-slack.md) states the same bound strictly, from the same closed form. Confirmation by the right computation, of a conclusion previously reached by the wrong one.

**A false null, caught by a trace check.** The first run reported "no critical point" for all four pairs, the same failure signature as the earlier $\mathfrak u(m)$ episode. The cause was `proj_from_basis` using `np.linalg.qr`, which returns a *full* orthonormal basis regardless of rank, so the $\mathfrak u(m)$ "projector" was the identity ($\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}=28$ against the correct $16$ at $m=4$) and the solver was searching a degenerate family. Replaced with an SVD truncated at the numerical rank; the projectors now give $1/15/16$, $1/24/25$, $1/35/36$ exactly. **Any null result from a projector-based family search must be preceded by a trace check on the projectors.**

#### The two senses of "graded", ordered, and which results are stated in which

**The word carries THREE nested meanings in this record, and every dispute of this cycle turned on the gaps between them.** They are named here once, strongest first, with a witness separating each from the next.

> **(C) PAIR-GRADED.** $W$ is the $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak p)$-invariant Weyl tensor of a symmetric pair. ***The gloss "equivalently sign-graded with $\Delta=0$" is WRONG and is withdrawn***, [Reviewer 318](../reviews/REVIEW_r318.md) shows the two name different sets, and they diverge at the rival maximiser: $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $n=4$ is the unique $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak u(2))$-invariant tensor of $(\mathfrak{so}(4),\mathfrak u(2))$, commutant exactly one-dimensional against $\dim\mathcal W_4=10$, while its kernel carries $3$ of $6$, so it is not sign-graded at all. *The failure is confined to $n=4$ within (C): $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(2,3)$ and $\mathbb{HP}^3$ at $n=12$ both have $\ker W=0$, and the mechanism is exact, $\lambda_{\mathfrak{su}(m)}=c$ iff $m=2$.* This is what [Lemma L8](#conjecture-l8-the-graded-case-reduced-to-a-finite-check-and-settled-across-the-classical-families-canonical-statement-and-proof) and [Lemma L69](#) are about, and the $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak p)$-commutant being one-dimensional makes such a $W$ unique per pair.

> **(A) SIGN-GRADED.** $\mathfrak p=E_+(W)$ and $\mathfrak m=E_-(W)$, these span $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ (equivalently $\ker W=0$) and the bracket relations $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak p$, $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak m$, $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak p$ hold. This is what the defect screen tests, through $S_{\mathfrak{mmm}}$ and $S_{\mathfrak{mpp}}$.
>
> **(B) SLICE-GRADED.** $W$ commutes with $\Lambda^2(\sigma)$ for **some** involution $\sigma$, equivalently $W$ lies in some graded subspace $V$. This is what the C3 route uses. **[Lemma L8](#conjecture-l8-the-graded-case-reduced-to-a-finite-check-and-settled-across-the-classical-families-canonical-statement-and-proof) is sense (C), not (B)**, the correction recorded below this file's labelling table, which this line had not taken up; the distinction decides whether L8's own counterexample bites.
>
> **(B) is itself underspecified, and the two readings differ.** *(B-narrow)*: $\sigma$ is an orthogonal involution of $\mathbb R^n$, $\sigma^2=I$, the coordinate splittings, which is what every slice computation in this record has actually used. *(B-wide)*: $\sigma$ is an involutive **automorphism of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$**, i.e. $\mathrm{Ad}(g)$ with $g^2=\pm I$, which admits $g^2=-I$, a complex structure, and so covers the $\mathfrak u(m)$ gradings. **The involution a sign-graded $W$ defines from its own splitting is an automorphism of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, not automatically one of $\mathbb R^n$**, so *(A)$\Rightarrow$(B) holds for (B-wide) and is open for (B-narrow)*. [Reviewer 317](../reviews/REVIEW_r317.md) reports a witness refuting the narrow form, a perturbation of $W_{\mathbb{CP}^4}$; **not yet reproduced here and not folded in**, $\mathbb{CP}^4$ itself satisfies both readings ($p=2$ works exactly, and so does $\mathrm{Ad}(g)$ for the complex structure), so the witness must be the perturbation rather than the point.

**They do NOT form a chain: (C) and (A) are INCOMPARABLE, and both sit strictly inside (B).** *Reading it as (C)$\subsetneq$(A)$\subsetneq$(B), reasoning that a pair's $\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak m$ are exactly $E_\pm$ with no kernel. **That is false at $\mathbb{CP}^2$**, hypothesis raised by [Reviewer 318](../reviews/REVIEW_r318.md) mid-audit, verified here.*

|---|---|---|---|---|
| $S^3\times S^5$, $S^4\times S^4$ | yes | yes | yes | |

**Each of (C) and (A) implies (B)**, a pair-graded $W$ commutes with its pair's involution, and a sign-graded $W$ is $\pm$-diagonal for the involution its own splitting defines. **Neither implies the other**: $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is pair-graded and not sign-graded, the $n=8$ witness is sign-graded and not pair-graded. *The failure is not generic ($\mathbb{CP}^3$ and $\mathbb{CP}^4$ are sign-graded, with $\ker W=0$), it is specific to $\mathbb{CP}^2$, which is precisely the rival maximiser and the point at $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$ exactly.* **A pair's isotropy algebra is not $E_+(W)$**; they coincide on the subalgebra family, where $W$ is projector-like, and part company when the invariant tensor has a kernel.

**Two consequences the record must carry.**

- **(B) is not a property with a unique witness.** A point carries *several* slice gradings, at least eight at the $n=8$ witness, and every coordinate sphere product lies in **every** coordinate slice, so any quantity defined from $\mathfrak p$ is a function of the *pair* $(W,\sigma)$ and never of $W$ alone. #### Lemma L69, above $\sqrt{3/2}$ the only equivariant graded points are sphere products with both factors $\ge5$, and all of them are nondegenerate

**Scope, stated here rather than downstream.** *Graded* is the **pair** sense ($\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak m$ for a symmetric pair), with $\ker W=0$ and $n\ne8$. **All three are load-bearing**: under the defect-zero reading instead, padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $n=12$ sits at $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$ **exactly**, has $\ker W$ of dimension $63$ out of $66$, and is not a sphere product, a counterexample word for word. *The threshold is non-strict, and what discharges the boundary is that $\mathbb{CP}^2$ lives at $n=4$; no sphere product attains $\sqrt{3/2}$ at any $n$, equality needing $(n+2)\mid36$ with both candidates giving non-square discriminants.*

**What the reduction actually gives, and it is an equivalence.** With $S=\tfrac1{p-1}+\tfrac1{q-1}-\tfrac1{n-1}$, $\lVert W\rVert^2=\tfrac18(1+S)$ and $1+S=\frac{pq(n-2)}{(p-1)(q-1)(n-1)}$, so $\theta^2=\frac{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)}{(n-2)pq}$ (**the closed form derived rather than checked at points**) and $\lVert W\rVert^2\le\tfrac16\iff S\le\tfrac13\iff\theta^2\ge\tfrac32$. *The two routes are one identity, not two computations agreeing.* **The conversion consumes $\theta=1/(2\lVert W\rVert)$ and [Lemma L48](problem.md)'s decomposition, whose Einstein hypothesis is load-bearing and was dropped in transit**, harmless at $k=2$, where the invariant line is one-dimensional, and **undischarged at $k\ge3$**.

**Statement.** For $n\ge12$, every equivariant graded critical point with $\theta\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ is a two-factor sphere product $S^p\times S^q$ with $p,q\ge5$, and every one of those is Morse–Bott nondegenerate with $\lambda_{\max}(L_e\vert_{W^\perp})=\tfrac12$ attained exactly on the orbit tangent.

**Proof.** An equivariant graded point is the Weyl operator of a compact symmetric space, so the recorded classification applies. **That implication is proved here by an invariant count, needing no classification at all**: at every $\mathbb Z_2$-grading of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ the space of $K$-invariant symmetric endomorphisms of $\Lambda^2$ has dimension $3$, of which $\mathrm{Alt}$ and $\mathrm{Ric}$ remove $0$ and $2$, leaving a single line, and the same count runs $4-1-2$ at $p=4$ and $3-1-1$ at $\mathfrak u(m)$, so it is case by case over the gradings, *the block's own three displays give different counts, so "uniform" was the wrong word*. *$p=1$ carries no equivariant point at all.*

## The graded classification: invariant Weyl spaces, the hypotheses Cartan needs, and the $n=8$ triality exception
*From findings/second-order.md. Belongs in graded-half.md because graded-half.md owns the gradedness split, the graded enumeration and the subalgebra family, which is what these four paragraphs classify.*

**Each symmetric pair carries an invariant Weyl space of dimension exactly one.** Checked at every split and at $\mathfrak u(m)$ for $n=6,8,10$ by computing the kernel of the stacked $\mathrm{ad}$-action on the Weyl basis, and reproduced independently by Reviewers 30 and 31. So a graded critical point is not merely associated with a symmetric pair; it **is** the unique invariant operator of that pair, with no room for a second point to hide. **That is refuted by this file's own counterexample above** (a second graded critical point on the same symmetric pair) and holds only for the *equivariant* point of the pair. Two subalgebras that might have widened the list carry a **zero**-dimensional invariant Weyl space and so contribute nothing: $\mathfrak{so}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n-1)$, and $\mathfrak{spin}(7)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$.

**The classification statement, with the hypotheses it actually needs** (Reviewers 30 and 31, identical briefs, agreeing). For a critical point **of the subalgebra family** at $n\ne8$: $\mathfrak h$ is a subalgebra and $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak h^\perp$ is automatically $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak h)$-invariant, so gradedness reduces to $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak h$, which is exactly the slack $S$; $\sigma=(+1,-1)$ is then an involution, $\mathfrak h$ a symmetric subalgebra, and Cartan leaves only $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(q)$ and $\mathfrak u(m)$. **Both hypotheses are load-bearing, and each fails at a specific point.**

**Outside the subalgebra family, slack zero does not imply a $\mathbb Z_2$-grading.** $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, the $n=4$ self-dual operator padded up, and the attractor of an $n=8$ ascent hunt at $379/400$ starts, has $S=0$ and $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\to\mathfrak m$ vanishing at $10^{-32}$, while $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak m]\to\mathfrak h$ and $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]\to\mathfrak m$ leak at $4.0$ ($n=8$) and $4.899$ ($n=10$), re-verified here directly. **At $n=8$, Cartan's equivalence is coarser than the one the argument consumes.** Cartan classifies up to $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{so}(n))$; conjugating a critical point needs $SO(n)$, and $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{so}(8))\supsetneq PO(8)$. #### Lemma L159, the involutions of the compact $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, classified, with the conjugacy notion the record consumes

*Derived in-round on 2026-09-07 to discharge the appeal four findings files were making bare. **Gate 2, two reviewers on identical briefs**, [REVIEW_I40a](../reviews/REVIEW_I40a.md) and [REVIEW_I40b](../reviews/REVIEW_I40b.md), working from the definitions, each building the argument independently and each constructing the same falsifier at $n=8$.*

> **LEMMA L159.** *Let $n\ge3$, $n\ne8$, and let $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ be the **compact** real form. Every involutive automorphism $\sigma$ of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is $\mathrm{Ad}_g$ for some $g\in O(n)$ with $g^2=\pm I$, unique up to sign, and is conjugate under $\mathrm{Ad}(O(n))$ to **exactly one** of*
> - *$\mathrm{Ad}_{g_p}$, $g_p=\mathrm{diag}(-1^p,1^{n-p})$, $0\le p\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, with fixed algebra $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n-p)$ and $\mathfrak g_1\cong\mathbb R^p\otimes\mathbb R^{n-p}$ of dimension $p(n-p)$;*
> - *for even $n$ only, $\mathrm{Ad}_J$ for an orthogonal complex structure $J$, with fixed algebra $\mathfrak u(n/2)$ and $\mathfrak g_1\cong\Lambda^2_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C^{n/2}$ of real dimension $\tfrac n2(\tfrac n2-1)$.*
>
> *The count up to $O(n)$-conjugacy is $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ at odd $n$ and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2$ at even $n$, the identity included, and the isomorphism type of the fixed algebra separates the classes.*

**The three hypotheses, each load-bearing at a named point.** *Compactness* enters at the branch $g^2=-I$, where $J$ orthogonal is what forces $\mathfrak u(m)$ rather than $\mathfrak{gl}(m,\mathbb R)$, and at $\mathrm{Inn}=\mathrm{Ad}(SO(n))$, which needs $\exp$ surjective. *$n\ge3$* is consumed exactly once, in $\ker(\mathrm{Ad}|_{O(n)})=\{\pm1\}$, which needs three distinct indices; at $n=2$ the algebra is abelian and $\mathrm{Aut}=\mathbb R^\times$. *$n\ne8$* is what makes every automorphism preserve the vector representation, so that $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{so}(n))=\mathrm{Ad}(O(n))$.

**The exclusion of $n=8$ is a theorem, not a caution.** Take $\mathfrak{spin}(7)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$ from the octonionic Clifford module, $\dim=21$ (kernel-checked as `finrank_spin7` in [L159Spin7Dim.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L159Spin7Dim.lean)), with $\mathfrak m\cong\mathbb R^7$. Since $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^7\cong\mathfrak{so}(7)$ carries no copy of $\mathfrak m$, the bracket $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]$ lands in $\mathfrak h$ and $\sigma=(+1,-1)$ is a genuine involution. Its fixed algebra acts **irreducibly** on $\mathbb R^8$ (an invariant $k$-plane puts $\mathfrak h$ inside $\mathfrak{so}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(8-k)$, of dimension below $21$ for $2\le k\le6$ and equal to it only at $k\in\{1,7\}$, where $\mathfrak h$ would be the vector stabiliser $\mathfrak{so}(7)$ fixing a line, while $\mathfrak{spin}(7)$ is transitive on $S^7$ and fixes no nonzero vector, kernel-checked as `spin7_no_fixed` in [L159Spin7.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L159Spin7.lean) through $[L_1,L_2]^2=-4$) so it is $O(8)$-conjugate to no $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(q)$ with $p\ge1$, all of which are reducible, to no $\mathfrak{so}(8)$, of dimension $28$, and to no $\mathfrak u(4)$, of dimension $16$. Both reviewers built this object independently. *The abstract-isomorphism form of the statement survives at $n=8$, and the $O(8)$ form does not.*

**And the divergence that bites inside the conjecture's own range is $O(n)$ against $SO(n)$, at every $n$ divisible by four.** On the $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(q)$ family the two agree at every $n$, the $O(n)$-stabiliser $O(p)\times O(q)$ containing elements of determinant $-1$. On $\mathfrak u(n/2)$ they part: writing $n=2m$, the complex structures form two $SO(2m)$-orbits distinguished by the orientation of $v_1,Jv_1,\dots,v_m,Jv_m$, and $\sigma_J=\sigma_{J'}$ exactly when $J'=\pm J$ while $-J$ flips that orientation by $(-1)^m$, so the two orbits fuse at odd $m$ and stay apart at even $m$. **At $n=12$ that is eight $O(12)$-classes against nine $SO(12)$-classes**, the $\mathfrak u(6)$ row splitting; the $SO(n)$ count is $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2$ at $n\equiv2\ (4)$ and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+3$ at $n\equiv0\ (4)$.

**Two things a consumer may not do.** *Separate classes by $\dim\mathfrak g_0$*: at $n=2m$, $\dim(\mathfrak{so}(m-k)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(m+k))=m^2-m+k^2$ equals $\dim\mathfrak u(m)=m^2$ whenever $m$ is a perfect square, first at $n=8$ and then at $n=18$, where $\mathfrak{so}(6)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(12)$ and $\mathfrak u(9)$ both have dimension $81$, so separate by isomorphism type, or by $(\dim\mathfrak g_0,\dim Z(\mathfrak g_0))$. *Substitute $SO(n)$-conjugacy for $O(n)$-conjugacy at $n\equiv0\pmod4$*, which undercounts by one at $n=12$, $16$ and $20$.
