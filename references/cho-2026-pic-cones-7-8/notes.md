# Cho, the coefficient that carries $n=7,8$, transcribed

**J. H. Cho, *Pinching cones for positive isotropic curvature in dimensions seven and eight*, [arXiv:2608.26598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26598).** Page numbers are those of the extraction.

## The coefficient (32) (p. 15)

> The remaining coefficient used in Proposition 3.7 of [8] is positive on both parameter ranges: $\tfrac{nb^2(1-2b)-2(a-b)(1-2b+nb^2)}{b^2}=\tfrac{2+(n-8)b-2(n+2)(n-2)b^2-n(n-2)^2b^3}{2+(n-3)b}>0$. Indeed, the polynomial in parentheses has derivative $-48b(18b+5)<0$ at $n=8$ and $-525b^2-180b-1<0$ at $n=7$. Its endpoint values are, respectively, $\tfrac{128}{81}>0$ and $\tfrac{320000000}{479744163}>0$.

*This is the same polynomial as Brendle's Lemma 3.3, re-verified at $n=7,8$ on a modified parameter range.*

## Remark 4.7 (p. 15)

> The proofs of Lemmas 4.1–4.2 and 4.4–4.5 are direct in both dimensions. For the three inequalities, Chen's original coarse $g,h$-based endpoint reduction works unchanged at $n=8$. At $n=7$ that coarse reduction is insufficient, but the lemma itself still holds: retaining the exact quantities $S_2$ and $S_3$, proving that they increase, and comparing them with the decreasing function $R$ gives a short rigorous replacement.

## What the Weyl part does (from the summary read at the abstract page)

*The curvature operator decomposes orthogonally into three $O(n)$-invariant subspaces (equation 5), and the analysis works with the entire operator; the Weyl component is not estimated separately, and the proof uses only the positivity of the coefficient in (32).* **The classification results are topological, connected sums of quotients of $S^n$ and $S^{n-1}\times\mathbb R$, with no extremal tensor named.**
