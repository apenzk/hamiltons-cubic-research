## Consulted for

The literature search behind [reviews/LIT-invariants.md](../../reviews/LIT-invariants.md): does the standard theory already own the record's tensor $T_{abc}=c_{abc}^2$ and the classification of which of its $\lambda$-moments are determined?

## Statements that matter

- **§2.2 defines $L_{ijk}:=\sum Q([v_\alpha,v_\beta],v_\gamma)^2$**, the record's $T_{abc}$ under another name, with the same total symmetry and the same basis-independence.
- **(3.1) writes the scalar curvature of a diagonal invariant metric as $\sum_i\frac{d_ib_i}{2x_i}-\frac14\sum_{ijk}L_{ijk}\frac{x_k}{x_ix_j}$**, a member of exactly the record's family $\sum\lambda_a^p\lambda_b^q\lambda_c^rT_{abc}$, with $(p,q,r)=(1,-1,-1)$.
- $b_i$ is fixed by $B|_{\mathfrak m_i}=-b_iQ|_{\mathfrak m_i}$ against the Cartan–Killing form; on $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with the record's $Q=-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}$ this is $b_i=2(n-2)$, which is the record's relation (A).

## Verdict

**Names the record's object and supplies the surrounding toolkit; settles none of the record's questions.** The homogeneous-Einstein programme treats $L$ as free parameters describing the group, so it never asks the record's question, which $L$-moments a *spectrum* determines. What transfers is the vocabulary and the Newton-polytope/BKK machinery for Laurent systems in the $x_i$ with $L$ as coefficients.
