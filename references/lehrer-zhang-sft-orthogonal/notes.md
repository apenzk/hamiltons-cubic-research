# Lehrer & Zhang, *The Second Fundamental Theorem of Invariant Theory for the Orthogonal Group*

arXiv:1102.3221 (2011), 21 pages. Stored as [paper.pdf](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.3221).

## Why it is here, and why it should have been here six attempts ago

Section 2 item 1 asks whether a family of `O(n)`-covariants is **linearly independent as functions**. Six attempts at that question were built and refuted (r247, r251, r253, r254, r255, r256) without anyone searching for it. **This paper is the theorem.**

## The statement

Brauer (1937) gives a surjection $\nu:B_r(n)\to\mathrm{End}_{O(V)}(V^{\otimes r})$ from the $r$-string Brauer algebra. The kernel *"has remained elusive"*, and this paper determines it:

> **For $r\ge n+1$, $\ker\nu$ is generated by a single idempotent $E$, given by an explicit formula.**

The complementary half is the sentence the programme actually needs: $\nu$ is injective (equivalently, $B_r(n)$ is semisimple in the relevant sense) precisely in the range where no such $E$ can be formed, the paper notes the semisimplicity condition holds *"only if $r\le n+1$"*.

**So diagram independence is a threshold question in $r$ against $n$, settled in the literature, not something to be discovered by ranking value matrices.** The generator is the antisymmetriser over $n+1$ indices: $\gamma(S,S',\beta)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n+1}}\varepsilon(\pi)\gamma_{D_\pi(S,S',\beta)}$ for disjoint $S,S'\subset[1,2r]$ with $|S|=|S'|=n+1$. **It is the Kronecker-delta antisymmetrisation that must vanish once there are more than $n$ indices to antisymmetrise over**, the same mechanism the record met at $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2\le n$ in the stability theorem, and at the $n=4,5$ collapse $I_2=I_1/2$.

## What it settles for this programme

- **The covariants at degree 3 occupy $12$ legs**, which is $r=6$ for the invariant functionals and $r=6+|\lambda|/2$ for a covariant into the block $\lambda$. *This note previously said $r=12$ here and $r=6$ below, for the same object, un-reconciled.*
- It explains every collapse the record found empirically: r250's $I_2=I_1/2$ at $n=4,5$ and independence at $n\ge6$; r251's measured thresholds *"matchings on $2k$ legs are independent exactly at $n=k$"*; r256's rank-1 universe of two-Weyl contractions.
- **Those measurements were rediscovering the SFT's threshold, one dimension at a time.**

## The injectivity half, read from the body, Theorem 3.4 and Corollary 4.4

> **Theorem 3.4.** *If $r\le n$, the $\gamma_D$ form a **basis** of the space of $O(V)$-invariants on $(V^{\otimes2r})^*$. If $r\ge n+1$, then any linear relation among the functionals $\gamma_D$ is a linear consequence of the relations in Lemma 3.3.*

> *If $r\le n$ the map $\nu:B_r(n)\to\mathrm{End}_{O(V)}(V^{\otimes r})$ is an **isomorphism**. If $r\ge n+1$, $\ker(\nu)$ is generated as an ideal of $B_r(n)$ by $E$.*

**So the threshold is $r$ against $n$, and it is sharp in both directions.** Below it the diagrams are a basis, independence is free. Above it every relation is a consequence of one explicit element.

## What this settles, and the counting that decides it

The programme's covariants are **degree 3 in $W$**, and each Weyl tensor carries $4$ legs, so a covariant into a block of label $\lambda$ is a functional on $V^{\otimes 12}$, that is $2r=12$, hence $\boxed{r=6}$.

**$r=6$ is the count for the $\lambda=\varnothing$ row alone, and it does NOT carry the covariants.** A covariant into the block $\lambda$ is a map $V^{\otimes12}\to V_\lambda$; pairing against $V_\lambda\subset V^{\otimes|\lambda|}$ makes it an invariant functional on $V^{\otimes(12+|\lambda|)}$, so Theorem 3.4 wants $r=6+|\lambda|/2$:

| $\lvert\lambda\rvert$ | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $r=6+\lvert\lambda\rvert/2$ | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | **12** |

At $n=12$ the widest blocks sit at $r=n$, **zero** slack, the same place the injectivity half lands. And at $n=6$ only $77$ of the $160$ labels of $V^{\otimes12}$ are $O(n)$-admissible, so $83$ blocks do not exist there to be independent.

### The theorem does not close the programme's independence question

**Restriction along the degree-3 diagonal has a kernel.** Theorem 3.4 gives independence of the $\gamma_D$ on **twelve free legs**; what the route needs is independence **as functions of one Weyl tensor**, at $W\otimes W\otimes W$. r260 measured the gap: at $n=6$ and $n=7$ the $10{,}395$ diagrams that are a *basis* upstairs restrict to **rank 2**, kernel $10{,}393$. The surviving rank $2$ is r250's two cubic Weyl invariants with $I_2=I_1/2$.

*This also explains the collapses the reviewers measured, which were the other side of the same threshold at lower degree:* r250's $I_2=I_1/2$ at $n=4,5$ with independence from $n\ge6$ is exactly $r=3$ against $n$; r251's *"matchings on $2k$ legs are independent exactly at $n=k$"* is Theorem 3.4 restated, since $2k$ legs means $r=k$.

## The one thing that does not follow

**Diagrams are not the objects the programme holds.** Theorem 3.4 is about $\gamma_D$, the functionals attached to Brauer diagrams. The construction emits *vectors in a weight space*, and whether those are the $\gamma_D$ (or a known linear combination of them), is unestablished. **That gap is the item's whole remaining content**, and it is not a gap the SFT can close.

## Owed before it can be cited as settling the item

- The precise form of the injectivity statement, from the body rather than the abstract, the abstract gives the $r\ge n+1$ half, and the range $r\le n$ needs the complementary lemma read and quoted.
- Whether the covariants at issue are honestly *diagrams in $B_r(n)$*, which is the same open question the record already carries: the construction emits vectors in a weight space, not diagrams.
- The extension to positive characteristic is in the paper and is irrelevant here; the programme works over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb R$.
