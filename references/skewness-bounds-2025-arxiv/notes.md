# De Michele–De Bartolo, transcription

Conventions: $S$ is the sample skewness of $n$ observations, $m_r$ the $r$-th central sample moment, $S=m_3/m_2^{3/2}$.

> **Equation (4)** (p. 2). "Wilkins [18] and Kirby [19] proved that $\lvert S\rvert\le\frac{n-2}{\sqrt{n-1}}$."

**Citations** (p. 8): [18] J. E. J. Wilkins, *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 15(3), 333 (1944), [doi:10.1214/aoms/1177731243](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731243). [19] W. Kirby, *Water Resources Research* 10(2), 220 (1974), [doi:10.1029/WR010i002p00220](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR010i002p00220). The introduction (p. 1) also credits Johnson and Lowe [17] with an upper limit on sample skewness.

**What the record takes.** For centred data $x$ with $\sum x_i^2=1$, $m_2=\tfrac1n$ and $m_3=\tfrac1n\sum x_i^3$, so $S=\sqrt n\sum x_i^3$, and equation (4) reads $\sum x_i^3\le\frac{n-2}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}$, [L225](../../findings/second-order.md)'s vector statement. The source proves nothing itself and states no equality case.
