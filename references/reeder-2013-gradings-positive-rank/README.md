# Reeder, Gradings of positive rank on simple Lie algebras

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Source** | [Gradings of positive rank on simple Lie algebras](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.5765) |
| **Authors** | Mark Reeder, Paul Levy, Jiu-Kang Yu, Benedict Gross |
| **Retrieved** | 2026-09-02 |

## Consulted for

Whether the $\mathbb Z_m$-gradings of a simple Lie algebra are classified, which [SP-3](../../plan.md) needs because a vertex of the $d=3$ pinned polytope is a partial grading ([DERIVE-sp3.md](../../reviews/DERIVE-sp3.md)).

## What it establishes, and what this programme takes from it

- **Torsion automorphisms of a simple Lie algebra are classified by Kac coordinates** on the affine Dynkin diagram; an order-$m$ automorphism gives the $\mathbb Z_m$-grading by its eigenspaces, and Vinberg's $\theta$-group theory describes the action of $\mathfrak g_0$ on $\mathfrak g_1$. [Kochetov's survey](https://www.math.mun.ca/~mikhail/research/publications/gradings_revised_2.pdf) covers gradings on finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras generally.
- **This programme does not need the general machinery.** For $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with $n\ne8$ every order-$3$ automorphism is inner, so the grading is read off an order-$3$ element of $SO(n)$ and its centraliser $SO(p)\times U(q)$ with $n=p+2q$, derived in [DERIVE-sp3.md](../../reviews/DERIVE-sp3.md)'s companion note rather than transcribed from here.

## Verdict

**Confirms the classification exists and is finite; supplies no bound on $\theta$.** Its value to the programme is that the candidate three-eigenvalue multiplicity triples are a known finite list rather than something to search for. **And it says where the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ statement actually lives**: this paper covers $E_6,E_7,E_8$ and states that classical types were done in Levy's *Vinberg's $\theta$-groups in positive characteristic* and in Vinberg's *The Weyl group of a graded Lie algebra*. **Citing this paper for an $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ statement would be wrong**, which is the main thing the reading establishes. Owed if a Levy or Vinberg statement is ever cited as read: those two sources filed with their own notes.
