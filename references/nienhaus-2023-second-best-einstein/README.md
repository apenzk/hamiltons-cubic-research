## Consulted for

The single most load-bearing source. States the conjecture verbatim as **Conjecture A**, and supplies the closed form for every sphere product, the $SO(k)\times SO(l)$ and $\mathrm{Stab}(W_{\mathbb{CP}^2})$ isotypic decompositions, the Hessian at $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, and the localization theorems.

## Statements that matter

- **Conjecture A** (p. 212 of the extraction), the conjecture this project is proving, stated for unit Weyl operators that are **critical points**.
- **Proposition 2.24 / 2.25**, Einstein constant and $\theta_{k,l}$ for every sphere product $S^{k}\times S^{l}$, odd $n$ included.
- **Proposition 4.6**, the $SO(k)\times SO(l)$ decomposition of $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$; hypotheses $k,l\ge5$, $k,l\ne6$.
- **Proposition 4.11** ($n\ge9$), orthogonal decomposition of $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ under the $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ stabilizer.
- **Lemma 4.13**, Schur; valid only on **multiplicity-free** isotypic components. This hypothesis is what the project's own $SO(n-4)$-equivariant argument avoids.
- **Theorem 4.14** ($n\ge9$), the seven eigenvalues (a)–(g) of $F_{W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}}$. Source of $\alpha\le1/3$.
- **Lemma 4.15**, the trace of $F$ vanishes on any $SU(2)_-$-invariant subspace.
- **Theorem 4.17** ($10\le n\le11$), its proof caps $\gamma$ with numerator $c=1$, justified by "$f\le1$ on $[0,\pi]$", i.e. by $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ being the global maximizer. That is available only for $n\le11$.
- **Remark 4.18**, the repair route at $\alpha\approx1/3$, and the author's own admission: *"a strategy on proving the estimate is to estimate $\gamma=\sqrt{2/3}P(W)$ for $W$ as above. Unfortunately, we were not able to do this yet."*
- **Theorem 4.19** ($10\le n\le11$), localization outside a tubular neighbourhood. **Its second case cites Conjecture A by name, so the theorem is conditional on the conjecture itself.**

## Verdict

Read in full. It settles far more than was first assumed, and one item once on this project's open list is its Theorem 4.19. Its $n\le11$ restrictions are a low-dimension obstruction rather than a ceiling, every ingredient of 4.14 holds for $n\ge9$, but 4.17's proof genuinely breaks at $n=12$, where the closing estimate flips sign. Dismissing this source on the strength of a summary cost the project a full round.
