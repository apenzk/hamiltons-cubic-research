## Consulted for

Two of the four facts the record consumes bare from Cartan's classification: **the involutions of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ up to conjugacy**, and **the equivalence relation the classification is stated under**, the $\mathrm{Aut}$-versus-$SO(n)$ distinction that [conventions.md](../../findings/conventions.md) records as the trap.

## Verdict

**It names the equivalence relation as a theorem and then classifies the $\mathfrak{so}$ involutions by explicit orthogonal matrices, the two halves the record needs, in one place.** Theorem 41.5: *"Real forms of $\mathfrak g$ are in bijection with conjugacy classes of involutions $\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak g_c)$."* §41.4 items 3, 5 and 6 then run types $B_n$ and $D_n$, in each case realising $\theta$ as conjugation by a matrix $g$ with $g^2=\pm1$ and stating whether $g$ lies in $SO$ or in $O\smallsetminus SO$: the $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(q)$ pairs with $p,q$ even come from $\det g=+1$, those with $p,q$ odd from $\det g=-1$, and $\mathfrak k=\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb C)$, $\mathfrak u(n)$ in the compact form, from $g^2=-1$.

**Two limits, both material.** The classification is stated for the *complex* $\mathfrak g$ and its real forms, so the compact-form reading of $(\mathfrak{so}(N),\mathfrak k)$ is one translation away from what is printed. And the type-$D_n$ discussion parametrises the outer class by $g\in O(2n)$ with $\det g=-1$ throughout, uniformly in $n$; **$D_4$ is never separated out and triality is not mentioned**, so this source silently treats $\mathrm{Aut}$-conjugacy as $O(2n)$-conjugacy at the one dimension where the record knows they differ.
