## Consulted for

**A pointwise quartic invariant of the Weyl algebra, as a possible input distinct from the two trace identities [F65](../../findings/INDEX-traps.md) exhausted.** The sectional nonassociativity $K(x,y)=(\langle x\circ x,y\circ y\rangle-|x\circ y|^2)/|x\wedge y|^2$ is defined for exactly the class $(\mathcal W_n,\circ,\langle,\rangle)$ belongs to, Euclidean metrised commutative algebras, and section 8 relates it to the spectrum of $L_\circ(e)$ at an idempotent.

## Verdict

**Lemma 8.2 gives an exact identity at every critical point of $P$, and the record did not have it**: with $e=W/\theta$, $\theta^2=4\max_{x\perp e}K(e,x)$, the maximum attained exactly on the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace of $L_e$. *Screened at $S^3\times S^3$ and $S^4\times S^4$ to nine digits.*

**The sufficient route it suggests is FALSE.** *Bounding $K$ over all planes by $\theta_0(n)^2/4$ would prove the conjecture, and at $n=6$ planes through no idempotent reach $K=1.500000$, four times what is needed. The Weyl algebra's sectional nonassociativity also has both signs at the balanced product, $\min\mathrm{Spec}^\perp(L_e)=-\tfrac12$ at $n=6$, so the paper's nonnegativity results (Lemmas 8.5 to 8.7, on square-zero elements, exactness and the cone of squares) do not apply to it.* Theorem 6.3's Böttcher--Wenzel technique for sharp sectional bounds is the field's method and is recorded for its shape.
