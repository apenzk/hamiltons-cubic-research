- **Link**: [arXiv:2606.24023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24023)
- **Identifier**: arXiv:2606.24023v1 [math.RT], 23 Jun 2026.
- **Authors**: L. Darondeau, M. Florence, B. Kolev. A reading of Weyl's *The Classical Groups* alongside Kraft–Procesi and Olver, worked over $\mathbb R$ for $GL(n)$, $SL(n)$, $O(n)$, $SO(n)$.
- **Retrieved**: 2026-08-30 (file date of [paper.pdf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24023)).
- **Consulted for**: what governs restriction of invariants to the diagonal, the operation the record calls the degree-3 diagonal $W\mapsto W\otimes W\otimes W$. The classical name turned out to be **restitution**, understood since Weyl.
- **Verdict**: **it names the operation and settles half the map; it is not a route to obligation (i).** Theorem I.2.1 gives complete polarization with recovery on the diagonal, Remark I.2.2 shows $\mathcal P_d\circ\mathcal R_d$ is the projection onto symmetric multilinear forms, and Remark I.2.4 gives full $G$-equivariance of all of it, block by block. *This folder first claimed those theorems explained the record's rank-2 collapse; [r265](../../reviews/REVIEW_r265.md) and [r266](../../reviews/REVIEW_r266.md) refute that independently, the citations were verbatim, the inference was not.* The measured map is a composite $V^{\otimes12}\to\mathrm{Weyl}_n^{\otimes3}\to$ degree-3 functions of $W$, and restitution governs only the **second** arrow, where the invariant multilinear forms on three independent Weyl slots already have dimension 2 at $n=6,7$, so the non-symmetric complement is $0$-dimensional and Remark I.2.2 accounts for **0 of the $10{,}393$**. **The whole collapse lives in the first arrow**, the passage from $V^{\otimes4}$ to the Weyl subspace with its antisymmetries, pair symmetry, first Bianchi identity and tracelessness, and no polarization statement crosses that gap.
