# Petersen–Stanfield, Poincaré polynomials and curvature operators of symmetric spaces

- **Link**: [arXiv:2508.11236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11236)
- **Identifier**: arXiv:2508.11236v1 [math.DG], August 2025.
- **Authors**: Peter Petersen, James Stanfield.
- **Retrieved**: 2026-09-10, as [petersen-stanfield-2508.11236.pdf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11236), extracted to [petersen-stanfield-2508.11236.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11236).
- **Consulted for**: the $(n,r)$ list of compact irreducible symmetric spaces with simple isotropy, which [graded-half.md](../../findings/graded-half.md) records as the one class of [L4](../../findings/ungraded-slack.md)'s Casimir family left open, found during the [2026-09-10b sweep](../../reviews/2026-09-10b-LIT-step8-sweep.md).
- **Statements that matter**: Theorem A (eigenvalues in $[0,\tfrac12]$, at most three distinct nonzero, rational), Theorem B (largest eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ iff Hermitian symmetric), **Theorem C** (simple isotropy gives ONE nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda=\tfrac14\dim M/\dim H$), and the Section 3.3 table of simple-isotropy spaces.
- **Verdict**: **supplies the one ingredient the record's open class needed.** Theorem C places every simple-isotropy symmetric space in L4's Casimir family at $r=\dim H$, where L4's own inequality $\theta=\tfrac2n\sqrt{rN/(N-r)}\le\theta_0(n)$ already holds, and the Section 3.3 list evaluates them all below $0.9<\sqrt{3/2}$. *It does not cover non-symmetric operators and says nothing about the cubic form itself.*
