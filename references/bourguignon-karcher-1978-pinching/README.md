# Bourguignon–Karcher 1978, Curvature operators: pinching estimates and geometric examples

- Jean-Pierre Bourguignon, Hermann Karcher. Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) **11** (1978), 71–92. [EuDML](https://eudml.org/doc/82010), DOI [10.24033/asens.1340](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.1340). Consulted 2026-08-26 through the citing literature; the paper itself retrieved 2026-09-02 from [Numdam](http://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1978_4_11_1_71_0/), a 1978 scan whose OCR text layer is lossy, the tables in [notes.md](notes.md) were read off the page images.
- Consulted for: the second-kind spectrum of $\mathbb{CP}^n$ with the Fubini–Study metric. **BK's §5.3 table on p. 84 prints $2(n+1)$ on $\mathbb{R}g$, $-4$ at multiplicity $n(n+1)$, and $2$ at multiplicities $n(n-1)$ and $n-1$** (so $-4$ at $n(n+1)$ and $2$ at $n^2-1$ on $S^2_0$), in BK's own $ikjl$ order, the one their §2.2 definition fixes. [Li 2021](../li-2021-nonnegative-second-kind/README.md) §1 and [Li 2022](../li-2022-product-manifolds-second-kind/README.md) quote the same data with the multiplicities unchanged and both signs flipped, $-2$ at $m^2-1$ and $4$ at $m(m+1)$, because Li's $iklj$ order is the negation of BK's.
- **Verdict: the external check on this project's $\mathring W$ implementation and on the relative sign of the two conventions.** [run_bk_crosscheck.py](../../scripts/runs/run_bk_crosscheck.py) reproduces all four rows of the §5.3 table exactly at $n=2,3,4$ in BK's $ikjl$ order, and their negation in Li's $iklj$ order. Each order reproduces its own paper's table, so the 1978 data pins the relative sign rather than selecting a convention; [conventions.md](../../findings/conventions.md) selects Li's $iklj$ for the record on the strength of Li's defining line, quoted there verbatim.

> **Settled 2026-09-02, the verdict above previously had the two orders swapped, and this is a fact about the record's reliability.** It read that Li's $iklj$ order reproduces BK's published pair and that "the flipped order returns $(-4,2)$ and fails". Both halves are backwards: $(-4,2)$ *is* BK's published pair, so the branch recorded as failing was the branch reproducing the source.
>
> **What settled it.** Page 83 gives the operator itself ("$\mathring R_{\mathbb{CP}^n}(h) = -h + \mathrm{trace}(h)\,g - 3\,J\circ h\circ J$") and page 84's §5.3 table gives the eigenvalues, both read off the rendered page images rather than the lossy OCR. An exact integer recomputation from BK's own four-tensor formula on p. 83 confirms that contracting slots 1 and 3, as §2.2 defines $\mathring R$, satisfies that identity and returns $\{2(n+1)\ \text{at}\ 1,\ -4\ \text{at}\ n(n+1),\ 2\ \text{at}\ n^2-1\}$, while the $iklj$ contraction returns exactly the negation, trace eigenvalue $-2(n+1)$ included. BK's own Prop. 4.9 is a second witness: for an Einstein tensor with $\delta\le\bar R\le\Delta$ the traceless eigenvalues obey $k-m\Delta\le\mathring r\le k-m\delta$, here $[-6n+2,\ 2]$, which admits $-4$ and $2$ and excludes $+4$.
>
> **What the wrong claim was measuring.** The docstring and the pass/fail test of [run_bk_crosscheck.py](../../scripts/runs/run_bk_crosscheck.py) hard-coded Li's pair under the label "BK 1978 predicts", so the MATCH it printed compared Li's order against Li's pair, a check that the two conventions differ by a global sign, mislabelled as a check against 1978. The script's arithmetic was right throughout; only its target and its labels were wrong, and both are now corrected.
