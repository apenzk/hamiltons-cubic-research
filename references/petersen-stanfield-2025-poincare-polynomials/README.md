## Consulted for

Computes the curvature operator of every compact irreducible symmetric space. Consulted to test whether the graded enumeration could be closed by citation.

## Statements that matter

- **Theorem A**, eigenvalues lie in $[0,\tfrac12]$; an irreducible space has one, two, or three distinct nonzero eigenvalues, all rational, with three occurring for the complex Grassmannians. **This is an inclusion ("can"), not an equivalence.**
- **Proposition 2.2**, writing $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak h_0\oplus_i\mathfrak h_i$ as centre plus simple factors, that decomposition *is* the eigenspace decomposition of $R$, with eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ on the centre.
- **Theorem B**, the maximum eigenvalue equals the Einstein constant exactly for the Hermitian symmetric spaces.
- **§2.4**, the curvature operator of Jensen's second homogeneous Einstein metric on $S^3\times S^3$, a non-symmetric Einstein metric.
- **§3.2**, the $\mathbb{CP}^m$ curvature-operator eigenvalues, which the project's Hermitian family reproduces exactly.

## Verdict

**Closing the graded enumeration by citation from their tables is unsound.** They classify symmetric *spaces*; graded critical points here are indexed by symmetric *pairs* $(\mathfrak h,\mathfrak k)$ with $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$, and a space's Weyl operator is graded only when its isotropy is a symmetric subalgebra of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$. What it did supply: Proposition 2.2 exposed that the two-eigenvalue ansatz searched only a slice, and Jensen's metric became a test case showing a non-symmetric homogeneous Einstein metric can have a graded Weyl operator.
