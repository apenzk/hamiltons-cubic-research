## Consulted for

The two facts of the Cartan appeal that the classical sources leave open: **triality at $n=8$**, and **the exceptional pairs**. The record derived the $n=8$ separation by hand twice ([LEDGER.md](../../findings/LEDGER.md)) and checks "the twelve exceptional spaces" in [graded-half.md](../../findings/graded-half.md) against a list it cites to nothing.

## Verdict

**Triality is here as an explicit theorem with a construction. The exceptional pairs are here one at a time.**

- **Theorem 1.3.5** (p. 6): the automorphisms $\kappa,\pi,\nu$ of $\mathfrak{so}(8)$, built from octonion multiplication, generate a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(D_4)$ isomorphic to $S_3$, with $\kappa^2=\pi^2=1$, $\nu^3=1$, $\nu=\pi\kappa$. **Theorem 1.3.6** is the infinitesimal triality principle and **Theorem 1.14.2** its group form in $SO(8)$. This is the mechanism [conventions.md](../../findings/conventions.md) warns about, constructed rather than named.
- **The involutions of the five compact exceptional groups**, each with its fixed-point subgroup determined as a group, central quotient included: §1.10, §2.9, §2.11, §3.7, §3.10, §3.11, §3.12, §4.10, §4.11, §4.12, §5.7, §5.8. Twelve involutions, matching the twelve exceptional compact irreducible symmetric spaces.

**What it does not supply is Casimir data.** There are no Casimir eigenvalues, no isotropy-representation decompositions and no dimension tables anywhere in the book, the fourth fact on the record's list is **not** sourced by this folder, or by any source located in this search. The book also asserts exhaustiveness of the involution list in its preface and does not prove it in the text.
