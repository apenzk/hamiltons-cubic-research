## Triality, Theorem 1.3.5, verbatim (p. 6)

$D_4=\mathfrak{so}(8)=\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak C)$ is realised on the Cayley algebra $\mathfrak C$. Yokota defines $\kappa$ by $(\kappa D)x=\overline{D\bar x}$ and $\pi$ by $\pi(G_{ij})=F_{ij}$, where $G_{ij}$ is the standard basis of $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ and $F_{ij}x=\tfrac12 e_i(e_jx)$ is the octonion-multiplication basis; Lemma 1.3.1 gives the change of basis explicitly and Lemma 1.3.2 shows $\kappa,\pi,\nu=\pi\kappa$ are automorphisms.

> "**Theorem 1.3.5.** In the automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(D_4)$ of $D_4$, the subgroup $S_3$ generated by $\kappa$ and $\pi$ is isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_3$ of degree 3. Furthermore, $\kappa,\pi,\nu$ have the following relations.
> $$\kappa^2=1,\quad\pi^2=1,\quad\nu^3=1,\quad\nu=\pi\kappa.$$"

> "**Theorem 1.3.6.** (Principle of infinitesimal triality in $D_4$). For any $D_1\in D_4$, there exist $D_2,D_3\in D_4$ such that $(D_1x)y+x(D_2y)=D_3(xy)$, $x,y\in\mathfrak C$. Also such $D_2,D_3$ are uniquely determined for $D_1$ and we have $D_2=\nu D_1$, $D_3=\pi D_1$."

And its group form (p. 24):

> "**Theorem 1.14.2.** (Principle of triality in $SO(8)$). For any $\alpha_3\in SO(8)$, there exist $\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in SO(8)$ such that $(\alpha_1x)(\alpha_2y)=\alpha_3(xy)$, $x,y\in\mathfrak C$. Moreover, $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ are determined uniquely for $\alpha_3$ up to the sign."

**What this covers and what it leaves.** It gives the record a constructed, order-3 automorphism of $\mathfrak{so}(8)$, the object that makes $\mathrm{Aut}$-conjugacy strictly coarser than $O(8)$-conjugacy there, and the reason the exceptional dimension is $8$ and no other. It does **not** state that some pair of involutions of $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ is $\mathrm{Aut}$-conjugate without being $O(8)$-conjugate; the record's witness ($S^3\times S^5$ against $\mathbb{HP}^2$, both at isotropy dimension $13$), remains the record's own. *So the correct citation is "triality exists and is $S_3$", never "triality separates these two".*

## What the source does not cover

- **Exhaustiveness is asserted in the preface, not proved in the text.** The preface says *"we find all involutive automorphisms $\sigma$ of $G$, and determine the group structures of the fixed points subgroup $G^\sigma$ by $\sigma$. Note that they correspond to classification of all irreducible compact symmetric spaces $G/G^\sigma$ of exceptional type."* Each section constructs its involution and identifies $G^\sigma$; no section proves that the constructed list is complete up to conjugacy. **Cite it for "these twelve are symmetric pairs and here is what the isotropy group is"; cite Cartan's classification, not this book, for "there are no others."**
- **No numerical invariants at all.** No dimensions, no Casimir eigenvalues, no isotropy-representation branchings. The Dimension Lemma's twelve exceptional checks in [graded-half.md](../../findings/graded-half.md) can take their $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak k)$ from here and must take $\dim\mathfrak g$, $\dim\mathfrak k$ from elsewhere.
- **Classical algebras are out of scope** beyond what $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ needs for $\mathfrak g_2$; there is no $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ classification here.
