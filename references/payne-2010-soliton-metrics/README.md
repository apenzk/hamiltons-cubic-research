## Consulted for

Plan item [T6](../../plan.md): what the Jacobi identity forces on the **squares** $T_{abc}=c_{abc}^2$ of structure constants, as opposed to on $c$ itself. This is the one source found that treats the vector of squared structure constants as its primary variable and states the Jacobi condition against it.

## Statements that matter

- **The "structure vector" $[\alpha^2]$** (the vector whose entries are $(\alpha^k_{ij})^2$ over the nonzero structure constants), is Payne's central object. It is the literature's exact analogue of this record's $T$.
- **Theorem 8 / Theorem 1**, the Ricci eigenvalue vector is $-\tfrac12Y^{\mathsf T}[\alpha^2]$, **linear in the squares**, and the nilsoliton condition is the linear system $U[\alpha^2]=-2\beta[1]$ with $U=YY^{\mathsf T}$ the Gram matrix of the diagram.
- **Theorem 13**, the Jacobi condition, by contrast, is Equation (8), $\sum_{s<m}\alpha^s_{ij}\alpha^m_{sk}+\alpha^s_{jk}\alpha^m_{si}+\alpha^s_{ki}\alpha^m_{sj}=0$: **quadratic in $\alpha$ and sign-dependent**, and required only where the Gram matrix has a $-1$ entry.
- **Corollary 14**, if the Gram matrix has **no** $-1$ entries, the Jacobi condition **holds automatically**. Jacobi then constrains nothing beyond the support.
- **The witness (p. 20, and §2 p. 6)**, "*It is possible for different sign choices for the structure constants to give different nilpotent structures ... there exists $\Lambda$ such that there are nonisometric $\mu$ in $\Omega_\Lambda$ with the same structure vector*", realised in dimension six by $\mu_7,\mu_8$ of Will's list; and "*Nonisometric nilmanifolds may have the same Gram matrices, structure vectors, and Dynkin diagrams.*"

## Verdict

**The literature's answer to T6, in the only setting where the question has been asked: the geometric conditions are linear in the squares and the Jacobi identity is not a condition on the squares at all.** Its content splits into a support condition and a residual sign-cancellation system that two different algebras can satisfy with the same $[\alpha^2]$. Payne's setting is nilpotent with a triangular basis rather than $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ in a Weyl eigenframe, so this transfers as a mechanism and not as a theorem, but the mechanism is the sign group, which is present in both.
