## Setting (L282–L302, L346–L372)

$V=\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^n)^*\otimes\mathbb R^n$ is the space of skew bilinear maps, $\mathcal N\subset V$ the nilpotent Lie brackets, $\mathrm{GL}_n$ acts by $g.\mu=g\mu(g^{-1}\cdot,g^{-1}\cdot)$; each $\mu\in\mathcal N$ gives a nilmanifold $(N_\mu,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ and a rank-one solvmanifold $S_\mu$. With $\mathfrak t$ the diagonal matrices, the basis $v_{ijk}=(e'_i\wedge e'_j)\otimes e_k$ ($i<j$) consists of weight vectors, $\pi(\alpha)v_{ijk}=(a_k-a_i-a_j)v_{ijk}=\langle\alpha,\alpha^k_{ij}\rangle v_{ijk}$, with weights $\alpha^k_{ij}=E_{kk}-E_{ii}-E_{jj}$ and $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\operatorname{tr}\alpha\beta^{\mathsf T}$. For $\mu=\sum\mu^k_{ij}v_{ijk}$ the Gram matrix is $U=[\langle\alpha^k_{ij},\alpha^{k'}_{i'j'}\rangle]$ over the support $\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$.

> **Theorem 2.3.** [L1, L2] For $\mu\in\mathcal N$, the following conditions are equivalent: (i) $S_\mu$ is Einstein. (ii) $\mu$ is a critical point of the functional $F:V\to\mathbb R$ defined by $F(\mu)=16\operatorname{tr}R_\mu^2/\lVert\mu\rVert^4$. (iii) $\mu$ is a critical point of $F|_{\mathrm{GL}_n.\mu}$. (iv) $R_\mu\in\mathbb RI\oplus\operatorname{Der}(\mu)$. (v) $R_\mu=c_\mu I+D_\mu$. Under these conditions, the set of critical points of $F$ lying in $\mathrm{GL}_n.\mu$ equals $O(n).\mu$ (up to scaling).

> **Theorem 2.7.** [P, Theorem 1] Assume that $\mu\in\mathcal N$ satisfies $R_\mu\in\mathfrak t$. Then $S_\mu$ is Einstein if and only if $U[(\mu^k_{ij})^2]=\nu[1]$, $\nu\in\mathbb R$, where $[(\mu^k_{ij})^2]$ is meant as a column vector in the same order used in (12) to define $U$, and $[1]$ is the column vector with all entries equal to $1$.

This is [Payne](../payne-2010-soliton-metrics/notes.md) Theorem 1, the record's "linear system in the squares", with $[(\mu^k_{ij})^2]$ the structure vector $[\alpha^2]$ and (L380–L382) "precisely the ones given by the Lagrange method applied to find critical points of the functional $F$".

## The moment map (L318–L332)

> In geometric invariant theory, a moment map for linear reductive Lie group actions over $\mathbb C$ has been defined in [N] and [K]. In our situation, it is an $O(n)$-equivariant map $m:V\smallsetminus\{0\}\to\operatorname{sym}(n)$, defined implicitly by (10) $\langle m(\mu),\alpha\rangle=\tfrac1{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\langle\pi(\alpha)\mu,\mu\rangle$. ... it was proved in [L4] that (11) $m(\mu)=\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu$. ... the functional $F$ defined in Theorem 2.3, (ii) is precisely $F(\mu)=\lVert m(\mu)\rVert^2$.

> **Theorem 2.5.** For $\mu\in\mathcal N$, $S_\mu$ is Einstein if and only if $F|_{\mathrm{GL}_n.\mu}$ attains its minimum value at $\mu$.

## Proposition 3.2, the hull statement (L396–L403, L468–L500, verbatim)

> Given a finite subset $X$ of $\mathfrak t$, we denote by $\mathrm{CH}(X)$ the convex hull of $X$ and by $\mathrm{mcc}(X)$ the minimal convex combination of $X$, that is, the (unique) vector of minimal norm (or closest to the origin) in $\mathrm{CH}(X)$. Each nonzero $\mu\in V$ uniquely determines an element $\beta_\mu\in\mathfrak t$ given by $\beta_\mu=\mathrm{mcc}\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$.

> **Proposition 3.2.** Let $\mu=\sum_{i<j}\mu^k_{ij}v_{ijk}$ be a nonzero element of $V$. (i) $p_{\mathfrak t}\big(\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu\big)=\tfrac2{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\sum_{i<j}(\mu^k_{ij})^2\alpha^k_{ij}\in\mathrm{CH}\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$ and consequently $F(\mu)\ge\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2$. (ii) $F(\mu)\ge\lVert\beta\rVert^2$ for any $\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta$. (iii) If $\mu\in W_\beta$ and $\inf F(\mathrm{GL}_n.\mu)=\lVert\beta\rVert^2$ then $\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta$. (iv) If $\inf F(\mathrm{GL}_n.\mu)=\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2$ then $\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta$, where $\beta$ is the only element in $\mathfrak t^+$ that is conjugate to $\beta_\mu$.

> *Proof.* (i) It follows from (10) and (11) that for any $\alpha\in\mathfrak t$, $\langle\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu,\alpha\rangle=\tfrac1{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\langle\pi(\alpha)\mu,\mu\rangle=\tfrac1{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\langle\sum\mu^k_{ij}\langle\alpha,\alpha^k_{ij}\rangle v_{ijk},\sum\mu^k_{ij}v_{ijk}\rangle=\tfrac2{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\sum(\mu^k_{ij})^2\langle\alpha,\alpha^k_{ij}\rangle=\langle\tfrac2{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\sum(\mu^k_{ij})^2\alpha^k_{ij},\alpha\rangle$. This and the fact that $2\sum_{i<j}(\mu^k_{ij})^2=\lVert\mu\rVert^2$ imply the first part of (i). For the second part it is enough to recall that $\beta_\mu=\mathrm{mcc}\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$ and so $F(\mu)=16\operatorname{tr}R_\mu^2/\lVert\mu\rVert^4=\lVert\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu\rVert^2\ge\lVert p_{\mathfrak t}(\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu)\rVert^2\ge\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2$.

**In the record's language.** The numbers $2(\mu^k_{ij})^2/\lVert\mu\rVert^2$ are a probability measure on the support of the structure vector; the diagonal Ricci vector is its first moment against the points $\alpha^k_{ij}$; an expectation lies in the hull of what it averages; the closest point of the hull is the sharp lower bound for the norm. Every step is the step the record takes in [bounds.md](../../findings/bounds.md) for the row measure $\mu_a=T_{a\bullet\bullet}/2(n-2)$ with the pair-points $(X_{bc},Y_{bc})=(\tfrac{\lambda_b+\lambda_c}2,\lambda_b\lambda_c)$. The three-index symmetry $T_{abc}=T_{bac}$ that the record's rows share is absent here: $(\mu^k_{ij})^2$ is symmetric in $ij$ only, and the moment is one vector in $\mathfrak t$ rather than one per row.

## Proposition 3.3, the linear system is the closest-point condition (L523–L556, verbatim)

> **Proposition 3.3.** Let $\mu=\sum_{i<j}\mu^k_{ij}v_{ijk}$ be a nonzero element of $V$ and consider the matrix $U=[\langle\alpha^k_{ij},\alpha^{k'}_{i'j'}\rangle]$ after fixing an enumeration of the set $\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$. (i) If $[c^k_{ij}]$ is any solution to $U[c^k_{ij}]=\nu[1]$, $\nu\in\mathbb R$, such that $\sum c^k_{ij}=1$ and all $c^k_{ij}\ge0$, then $\beta_\mu=\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}$ and $\nu=\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2$. (ii) For each convex linear combination $\beta_\mu=\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}$, we define a finite set of $\lambda$'s in $V$ associated to $\mu$ by $\lambda=\sum\pm\sqrt{c^k_{ij}}\,v_{ijk}$. If $R_\lambda\in\mathfrak t$ and $\mu$ degenerates to $\lambda$ (i.e. $\lambda\in\overline{\mathrm{GL}_n.\mu}$), then $\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta$, for $\beta$ the only element in $\mathfrak t^+$ conjugate to $\beta_\mu$. (iii) If $\beta_\mu=\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}$ satisfies $c^k_{ij}>0$ for any $\mu^k_{ij}\ne0$, then $[c^k_{ij}]$ is a solution to $U[c^k_{ij}]=\nu[1]$ for $\nu=\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2$.

> *Proof.* (i) It is easy to see that $U[c^k_{ij}]=\nu[1]$ is precisely the linear system from the Lagrange multiplier method applied to find critical points of the functional $[c^k_{ij}]\mapsto\lVert\alpha\rVert^2$, $\alpha=\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}$, restricted to the leaf $\sum c^k_{ij}=1$. Since the set $\{\alpha=\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}:\sum c^k_{ij}=1\}$ is a linear variety there is a unique critical point $\alpha_m$ which is a global minimum, and so $\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}=\alpha_m$ for any solution $[c^k_{ij}]$. If in addition all $c^k_{ij}\ge0$ then $\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}\in\mathrm{CH}\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$ and hence $\alpha_m$ has to be $\beta_\mu$. Moreover, $\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2=\lVert\sum c^k_{ij}\alpha^k_{ij}\rVert^2=\langle U[c^k_{ij}],[c^k_{ij}]\rangle=\nu\sum c^k_{ij}=\nu$.

**For the record.** [Nikolayevsky's Theorem 3](../nikolayevsky-2011-pre-einstein-derivation/notes.md) ("the linear system in the squares has a positive solution iff Einstein nilradical", nice basis) and [Payne's Theorem 1](../payne-2010-soliton-metrics/notes.md) are, by this proposition, the statement that the minimal point of the hull of the weights has a strictly positive convex representation. The record's "two moments pinned" reads, in this frame, as the moment $\sum c\,\alpha$ being prescribed rather than minimised; the hull condition is the same membership either way.

## Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.4, strata and the Einstein points (L430–L445, L577–L590)

> **Theorem 3.1.** [L6] The collection $\{\mathcal S_\beta:\beta\in B\}$ is a $\mathrm{GL}_n$-invariant stratification of $V\smallsetminus\{0\}$: (i) $V\smallsetminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{\beta\in B}\mathcal S_\beta$ (disjoint union). (ii) $\overline{\mathcal S_\beta}\smallsetminus\mathcal S_\beta\subset\bigcup_{\lVert\beta'\rVert>\lVert\beta\rVert}\mathcal S_{\beta'}$ ... (iii) $W_\beta\smallsetminus\{0\}\subset\mathcal S_\beta\cup\bigcup_{\lVert\beta'\rVert>\lVert\beta\rVert}\mathcal S_{\beta'}$. (iv) $\mathcal S_\beta\cap W_\beta=\{\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta:\beta_\mu=\beta\}$.

> **Proposition 3.4.** Let $\mu\in\mathcal N$, $\mu\ne0$. (i) If $S_\mu$ is Einstein then $\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta$ for $\beta$ the only element in $\mathfrak t^+$ conjugate to $\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu$. In such case, the eigenvalue type of $S_\mu$ is a positive scalar multiple of $\beta+\lVert\beta\rVert^2I$. (ii) For $\mu\in\mathcal S_\beta$ the following conditions are equivalent: (a) $S_\mu$ is Einstein. (b) $\tfrac4{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}R_\mu$ is conjugate to $\beta$. (c) $F(\mu)=\lVert\beta\rVert^2$.

Here $\mathcal S_\beta=\{\mu:\beta$ is of maximal norm among $\beta_{g.\mu},\ g\in\mathrm{GL}_n\}$ and $W_\beta=\{\mu:\langle\beta,\alpha^k_{ij}\rangle\ge\lVert\beta\rVert^2$ for all $\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$ (L404–L420). So the hull bound $F\ge\lVert\beta\rVert^2$ is attained exactly at the Einstein points of the stratum: **the extremal points of the hull inequality are the critical points of the functional**, which is the shape the record's conjecture has ($\theta$ bounded by a hull condition, equality at the balanced product).

## What the paper does NOT do

- Nothing on Weyl tensors, on $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with a fixed bracket, or on any quantity of the form $\sum_{bc}T_{abc}\lambda_b\lambda_c$; the weights here are the torus weights $a_k-a_i-a_j$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ on brackets, and the group acts on the bracket while the metric is fixed. In the record the bracket is fixed ($\mathfrak{so}(n)$) and $W$ varies, so the group whose moment map is the record's per-row system is owed before this frame transfers as a theorem.
- One moment vector per algebra, in $\mathfrak t$; the record has one measure per eigenvector $\varphi_a$ with two scalar moments each.
- No inequality of the record's type (an upper bound on $\theta$); the hull bound is a lower bound on $\lVert m\rVert^2$, the direction opposite to the record's.
- Sections 4–5 (7-dimensional 6-step classification; 2-step algebras from graphs, "positive" graphs, Theorem 5.3 and 5.8) are Einstein-nilradical existence results with no bearing on $\mathcal W_n$.
