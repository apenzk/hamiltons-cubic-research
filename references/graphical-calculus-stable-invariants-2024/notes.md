# Weingart, *A Graphical Calculus for Stable Curvature Invariants* (arXiv:2404.16355, 25 Apr 2024)

Stored 2026-08-23. [paper.pdf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16355), [paper.txt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16355) (`pdftotext -layout`, 38 pages). Author: Gregor Weingart.

## What it is

A graphical calculus (trivalent coloured graphs modulo IHX relations) for invariant polynomials on the space $\mathrm{Curv}^-T$ of algebraic curvature tensors. Graphs evaluate to stable curvature invariants; the reduced graph algebra $\mathcal A$ is generated by classes of graphs with connected bivalent black subgraph. Comes with a Maxima implementation.

## Why it matters here

**Conjecture A's functional is one of these invariants.** Lemma 6.2 names it:

> **Lemma 6.2 (Standard Curvature Term Polynomial).** For $R\in\mathrm{Curv}^-T$, the scalar product of $q(R)\star R$ with $R$ equals the stable cubic polynomial
> $$\Theta_3(R):=g_{\Lambda^2T^*\otimes\Lambda^2T^*}\big(q(R)\star R,\ R\big)=\tfrac1{12}\Big[\tfrac16\,\gamma_1-\tfrac23\,\gamma_2\Big]$$
> for two explicit trivalent graphs $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$.

Here $q(R)$ is the **standard Weitzenböck curvature term**, the same operator this project's $Q$ is built from. Section 6's opening states the geometric reading:

> "In a sense the Hitchin–Thorpe inequality deals with the expected value and the variance of sectional curvature, its **third moment** is related to the cubic polynomial $\Theta_3(R):=g(q(R)\star R,R)$ of central importance to this section."

**Checked, and the outcome is split.** $\Theta_3(W)=-2\,\theta(W)$ exactly, at every $n$, with no Ricci correction ([b_theta3_identification.py](../../scripts/b_theta3_identification.py)). *This file first recorded $+32$; that was wrong by a factor of $16$ and a sign, corrected at Gate 2 by [Reviewer 69](../../reviews/REVIEW_r69.md) and [Reviewer 70](../../reviews/REVIEW_r70.md) independently.* But the further reading (that $\theta$ *is* the third moment), is **false**: Weingart writes only that the third moment "is related to" $\Theta_3$, and $S^3\times S^3$ and $\mathbb{CP}^3$ have identical $\theta=1.054093$ with third moments a factor $13$ apart at $264\sigma$ ([b_third_moment_refuted.py](../../scripts/b_third_moment_refuted.py)). On Ricci-flat $W$ the cubic invariants are a $2$-plane; $\theta$ and $\Psi_3$ are different points of it.

## The completeness statement about cubic invariants

> "In consequence the list of generators of the reduced graph algebra $\mathcal A$ up to degree 3 reads: [six graphs]. **Interestingly there exist no further IHX–relations between these generators with connected black subgraphs up to degree 3.** In degree 4 however there exist exactly two independent relations between the 17 isomorphism classes of colored trivalent graphs with 8 vertices."

Degree-2 evaluations given explicitly: $\gamma(R)=-2\kappa$ for the single generator, $8\,g_{\mathrm{Sym}^2T^*}(\mathrm{Ric},\mathrm{Ric})$ and $-24\,g(R,R)$ for the two degree-2 graphs.

This is a **basis statement for the cubic invariants of an algebraic curvature tensor**, which is the class-ceiling question in cleaner form, since it says which cubic quantities exist at all before any of them is bounded.

## Theorem 6.4, and why it is not directly usable

> **Theorem 6.4 (Cubic Curvature Identity for Einstein Manifolds).** For every compact connected Einstein $M^m$, $m\ge3$, scalar curvature $\kappa$:
> $$\int_M\mathrm{pf}_3(R)-\tfrac1{40}\int_M\Psi^\circ_3(R)+\tfrac2{15}\lVert\nabla R\rVert^2=\kappa^3\tfrac{m^2-18m+40}{60m^2}\mathrm{Vol}(M,g)+\kappa\tfrac{3m-104}{30m}\lVert R\rVert^2$$

It is an **integrated** identity, carrying $\lVert\nabla R\rVert^2$ and a volume term, so it says nothing pointwise-algebraic about a single $W\in\mathrm{Weyl}_n$. Recorded so a later cycle does not re-derive it hoping otherwise.


## Sign conventions, the paper is internally inconsistent, and a fence post turns on it

Recorded because Gate 2 found the record had silently picked the wrong branch twice.

| | |
|---|---|
| Lemma 6.2 (line 1848) | $\Theta_3(R):=+\,g(q(R)\star R,R)$ |
| line 2154 | $\Theta_3(R):=-\,g(q(R)\star R,R)$ |
| Definition 6.1 (line ~1805) | carries an explicit $\tfrac14$ prefactor |
| eq (46) | $q(R)\star R=\tfrac12\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{Ric}}R+Q(R)$, sign opposite to Definition 6.1 |

**Definition 6.1 is the reliable branch.** It is self-consistent with both of the paper's explicit representation formulas, $q(R)\star X=\tfrac12\mathrm{Ric}\,X$ on the defining representation, and $q(R)\star=\tfrac12\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{Ric}}-R^{\mathrm{alt}}$ on $\Lambda^2$, and with Hamilton's flow direction, since it gives $Q_{\mathrm{Ham}}=2(W^2+W^\#)$.

**The metric matters too:** $g$ on $\Lambda^2T^*\otimes\Lambda^2T^*$ with $\{dE_\mu\wedge dE_\nu\}_{\mu<\nu}$ orthonormal is $\tfrac14$ of the naive full 4-index contraction, the normalisation that makes $\lVert R\rVert^2=\lVert W\rVert_F^2$ in the pair basis.

**Fence post 19's conclusion flips on the $\Theta_3$ sign**, so any use of this paper must name the branch it is using.


## The degree-by-degree count, what it says about the record's "free data"

*Added 2026-09-01 by [LIT-invariants](../../reviews/LIT-invariants.md).*

Verbatim (paper.txt lines 168–175):

> we can deduce from the table of dimensions presented above that the algebra $\mathcal A^\bullet$ of stable curvature invariants should be a free commutative graded algebra with $1,2,5,15,54,270$ and $1639$ generators respectively of degrees $1,2,3,4,5,6$ and $7$; numbers of generators which coincide up to degree 4 with the number of generators of the reduced graph algebra $\mathcal A^\bullet$ calculated in Section 3 as a direct consequence of Corollary 3.4.

With a free graded-commutative algebra on $g_1,\dots=1,2,5,15$ the *dimensions* of $[\mathrm{Sym}^d(\mathrm{Curv}^-T)^*]^{O(T,g)}$ in the stable range come out as $1,3,8,26$ for $d=1,2,3,4$.

**Why this is the right ceiling for the record's classification.** The record asks which contractions of $T_{abc}$ a critical point's spectrum determines, and answers that the first free one is the degree-4 monomial $\{2,2,0\}$. Weingart's count is the ambient fact that makes such a question non-vacuous: at degree $4$ there are $26$ invariants of an algebraic curvature tensor, while the operator spectrum of $W$ on $\Lambda^2$ supplies only $\operatorname{tr}W^4$ and $(\operatorname{tr}W^2)^2$. **The gap is not one dimension wide; the record has located one specific escape from it, not the whole of it.**

The paper's own degree-3 remark, "there exist no further IHX–relations between these generators with connected black subgraphs up to degree 3. In degree 4 however there exist exactly two independent relations between the 17 isomorphism classes of colored trivalent graphs with 8 vertices", is the same statement graph-side: $17-2=15$ degree-4 generators.
