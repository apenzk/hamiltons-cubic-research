## Consulted for

Whether the operator identity behind the B0 rank-3 theorem, $\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\,\mathrm{ad}(v_\alpha)^2=2W$ on Ricci-flat algebraic curvature operators, is already published.

## Statements that matter

- **Proposition 1.6**, the curvature term of a Lichnerowicz Laplacian in eigenbasis form, $g(\mathrm{Ric}(T),\hat T)=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha|\Xi_\alpha T|^2$. On $T\in\Lambda^2$ the action $\Xi_\alpha T$ is the adjoint action, so this **is** the identity, in the Weitzenböck rather than the curvature-operator form.
- **Proposition 1.11**, **Example 1.10**, the Lichnerowicz Laplacian on algebraic curvature tensors and on $p$-forms, fixing the constant $c$.

## Verdict

**The identity is classical and is cited, not claimed.** It is the Weitzenböck curvature term on 2-forms, $q(R)\omega=\mathrm{Ric}\circ\omega+\omega\circ\mathrm{Ric}-2\mathring R\omega$, which for Ricci-flat $R$ collapses to $q(R)=-2R$ by the first Bianchi identity. What the source does **not** contain is the use made of it here: the splitting of $q(R)$ into the per-eigenvector summands $N_v=2\,v\otimes v-\mathrm{ad}(v)^2$, each **positive semidefinite** with $\ker N_v=v^\perp\cap\mathfrak z(v)$. That splitting is what turns the identity into a constraint on centralisers, and it is new here.
