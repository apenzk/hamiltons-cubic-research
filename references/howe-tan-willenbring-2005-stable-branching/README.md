# Howe, Tan, Willenbring, Stable branching rules for classical symmetric pairs

- **Link**: [arXiv:math/0311159](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311159); Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 357 (2005), 1601–1626
- **Authors**: Roe Howe, Eng-Chye Tan, Jeb F. Willenbring
- **Retrieved**: 2026-08-22
- **Consulted for**: the $U(m)$-isotypic decomposition of $\mathrm{Weyl}_{2m}$, needed to establish nondegeneracy of the $\mathbb{CP}^m$ critical points by Schur's lemma.

## Which statements matter

§2.3.1, the stable branching rule for $GL_n\subset O_{2n}$. Applied at $\lambda=(2,2)$ (the Young diagram of the algebraic curvature tensors), it yields exactly ten $GL(m)$ summands, **all of multiplicity one**, for every $m\ge4$; nine at $m=3$, where one summand has dimension zero.

## Verdict

**This settles multiplicity-freeness for all $m$**, which is the hypothesis Schur needs to make $L_e$ a scalar on each summand, and hence the hypothesis that turns each summand's value into an eigenvalue rather than a Rayleigh lower bound. The count agrees independently with [Tricerri–Vanhecke (1981)](../tricerri-vanhecke-1981-almost-hermitian-curvature/) once the three Ricci-and-scalar modules are removed.

## What it does not cover

The $L_e$ eigenvalues themselves, and the identification of which summand is the $SO(2m)$-orbit tangent. Both are established in this project, the eigenvalues verified at $m=3\ldots12$ and not yet derived in closed form, which is the one step still owed for Observation 28.
