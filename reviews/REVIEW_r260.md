## Claim 1, two algebras, and where the record actually contradicts itself

The brief asks whether $f=12$ and $r=6$ are the same object with incompatible leg counts. They are not.

| question | space | algebra | strings | Lehrer–Zhang threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| are the contractions of three Weyl tensors independent as functionals? | $(V^{\otimes12})^*$ | $B_6(n)$ acting on $V^{\otimes6}$ | $r=6$ | $r\le n$, i.e. $n\ge6$ |
| what acts on $\mathrm{Hom}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$? | $V^{\otimes12}$ | $B_{12}(n)$ acting on $V^{\otimes12}$ | $f=12$ | $f\le n$, i.e. $n\ge12$ |

Lehrer–Zhang's $\nu$ goes $B_r(n)\to\mathrm{End}_{O(V)}(V^{\otimes r})$, $r$ strings, $r$ tensor factors, $2r$ endpoints. A perfect matching of $2r$ points is simultaneously a functional on $V^{\otimes2r}$ and an endomorphism of $V^{\otimes r}$; the two readings differ by a factor of two in the leg count, and the theorem's $f=12$ and the record's $r=6$ sit on opposite sides of it. **The findings table's assertion that these are different statements about different algebras is correct.**

**The record is nevertheless inconsistent, in [notes.md](../references/lehrer-zhang-sft-orthogonal/notes.md) itself.** Line 21: *"The covariants at degree 3 use $r=12$ strings. At $n=12$ that is $r\le n$."* Line 35: *"a covariant into a block of label $\lambda$ is a functional on $V^{\otimes12}$, that is $2r=12$, hence $r=6$."* Same object, same file, two incompatible numbers, un-reconciled. Which is right depends on the sentence: for the *functional* claim the note is making in both places, $r=6$ is correct and $r=12$ is wrong. For the *algebra acting on legs*, $f=12$ is correct, and that is a different sentence, which line 21 was not making.

**And the number that is missing is the sharpest one.** Lehrer–Zhang, applied to the algebra the theorem is actually about, says $\nu:B_{12}(n)\to\mathrm{End}_{O(n)}(V^{\otimes12})$ is an isomorphism iff $12\le n$. That is $n\ge12$: **tighter than Rui's $n\ge11$, from the record's own reference, on the record's own object.** At $n=12$ it clears by **zero**, not by one. It is not needed (claim 3), but the table in [brauer-pairing.md](../findings/brauer-pairing.md) that ranks the two criteria compares Lehrer–Zhang at the *wrong instance* ($r=6$ against $f=12$) and so gets the ordering it reports.

## Claim 2, CONFIRMED, by a route that needs no access to the paper

Script: [c2_semisimple.py](../scripts/reviewers/r260/c2_semisimple.py) / [out_c2_semisimple.txt](../scripts/reviewers/r260/out_c2_semisimple.txt), [out_c2_semisimple_f5.txt](../scripts/reviewers/r260/out_c2_semisimple_f5.txt).

The primary source is paywalled and the note says so, so I did not use it. **Dickson's criterion** decides the question outright: over a field of characteristic $0$, a finite-dimensional algebra is semisimple iff the trace form $(x,y)\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(L_{xy})$ of its regular representation is nondegenerate. I built the Brauer diagram multiplication from scratch, formed that Gram matrix over the diagram basis, and measured its rank modulo three primes near $46{,}000$ (full rank mod $p$ proves full rank over $\mathbb{Q}$).

| $f$ | $\dim B_f$ | semisimple exactly for | rank at $\delta=f-2$ | Rui's $n\ge f-1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | $\delta\ge1$ |, | $\delta\ge1$ ✓ |
| 3 | 15 | $\delta\ge2$ | $7/15$ | $\delta\ge2$ ✓ |
| 4 | 105 | $\delta\ge3$ | $58/105$ | $\delta\ge3$ ✓ |
| 5 | 945 | $\delta\ge4$ | $706/945$ | $\delta\ge4$ ✓ |

Four independent sizes, four sharp thresholds, no exception among positive integers. So:

- **the inequality is $n\ge f-1$, not $n\ge f$**, $B_4(3)$ and $B_5(4)$ are semisimple, both at $\delta=f-1$ exactly;
- **it is stated for $\delta=n$ itself**, not only for generic $\delta$, every $\delta$ above is a specific integer;
- **the claimed sharpness survives**: $\delta=f-2$ fails at every $f$ tested, by a wide margin ($706$ of $945$ at $f=5$), so $n=12$ against $f=12$ genuinely clears Rui's criterion by one.

**One thing the note's "iff" gets wrong, outside its own scope.** $B_3(0)$ and $B_5(0)$ are semisimple (rank $15/15$ and $945/945$) although $0\ge f-1$ fails, while $B_2(0)$ and $B_4(0)$ are not. The note scopes itself to *"$\delta=n$ a positive integer"*, so the programme is untouched; but the displayed statement is written as an unrestricted iff over a char-$0$ field, and at $\delta=0$ with $f$ odd it is false.

## Claim 3, the sharpest one, and the threshold is not what carries it

Script: [c3_image_semisimple.py](../scripts/reviewers/r260/c3_image_semisimple.py) / [out_c3_image_semisimple.txt](../scripts/reviewers/r260/out_c3_image_semisimple.txt).

### The conclusion the route uses is free

$V^{\otimes f}=\bigoplus_\lambda V_\lambda\otimes M_\lambda$ as an $O(n)\times\mathrm{End}_{O(n)}$-bimodule, $O(n)$ being reductive in characteristic $0$. Hence

$$\nu(B_f(n))=\mathrm{End}_{O(n)}(V^{\otimes f})=\bigoplus_\lambda\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb C}(M_\lambda),$$

a product of full matrix algebras **at every $n$**, whose simple modules are exactly the $M_\lambda$. So $M_\lambda$ is irreducible over the image, therefore over $B_f(n)$ acting through $\nu$, therefore generated by any nonzero vector, **with no hypothesis on $n$ whatsoever**. The theorem invokes semisimplicity of $B_{12}(12)$ for this, and semisimplicity of $B_{12}(12)$ is not what supplies it.

### The witness

At three $(f,n)$ below Rui's threshold, $B_f(n)$ is *not* semisimple and the multiplicity spaces are *still* irreducible. I measured this by the same Dickson argument applied on the module $U=V^{\otimes f}$: an element of $\mathrm{rad}(A)$ is nilpotent and stays nilpotent after multiplication, so it has trace $0$ in **every** representation; a nondegenerate $\mathrm{tr}_U(xy)$ therefore forces $\mathrm{rad}=0$. Computed combinatorially, $D\!\cdot\!E=n^L F$ and $\mathrm{tr}(\nu(F))=n^{\#\text{components of }F\text{ closed up}}$, with no matrices at all.

| $(f,n)$ | Rui | rank of $B_f(n)$'s regular trace form | rank of $\mathrm{tr}_U(xy)$ | $\dim\nu(B_f(n))$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $(4,2)$ | fails | $58$ of $105$ | $35$ | $35$ |
| $(5,2)$ | fails | $745$ of $945$ | $126$ | $126$ |
| $(5,3)$ | fails | $706$ of $945$ | $603$ | $603$ |
| $(3,2)$ | holds | $15$ of $15$ | $10$ | $10$ |
| $(5,4)$ | holds | $945$ of $945$ | $903$ | $903$ |

**In every cell the rank on the image equals $\dim\nu(B_f(n))$ exactly** (right two columns, the last computed independently in claim 5). So the image is semisimple at every $n$ tested, including all three where the algebra is not, and the theorem's *"hence generated by ANY nonzero vector"* holds there too.

### The brief's proposed alternative is also wrong

The brief suggests injectivity of $\nu$ (which needs $n\ge f$), is what is really required. It is not. If $B_f(n)$ is semisimple then every quotient of it is semisimple and equals $\bigoplus_{\lambda\in S}\mathrm{End}(\Delta_\lambda)$ for a subset $S$; matching against $\bigoplus_\lambda\mathrm{End}(M_\lambda)$ gives $M_\lambda\cong\Delta_\lambda$ for exactly the admissible $\lambda$, with no injectivity used. The data confirms it: at $(4,3)$ and $(5,4)$ ($n=f-1$, where $\ker\nu\ne0$), the prediction lands exactly, $91$ and $903$.

### So the honest thresholds are three, and the route needs none of them

| statement | threshold at $f=12$ |
|---|---|
| $M_\lambda$ irreducible, generated by any nonzero vector, **what step (iii) consumes** | none |
| $M_\lambda\cong\Delta_\lambda$ as $B_{12}(n)$-modules for admissible $\lambda$ | $n\ge11$ (Rui) |
| $\nu$ injective, so $\Delta_\lambda$ survives for **every** $\lambda$ | $n\ge12$ (Lehrer–Zhang) |

The theorem's stated $n\ge11$ is honest for the middle row, which is the row it states. It is **not** honest as *"the binding constraint of the whole route"*: the route consumes the top row, and the top row is unconditional. The one-dimension margin the commit headlines is a margin on a statement the argument does not need.

## Claim 4, the projector is fine; the attribution and the deliverable are not

**Surjectivity is unconditional, and it is the FFT.** Brauer's map $B_f(n)\to\mathrm{End}_{O(n)}(V^{\otimes f})$ is onto for every $n\ge1$, the classical First Fundamental Theorem for $O(n)$. The file calls it *"the SURJECTIVITY half of the SFT"*; the Second Fundamental Theorem is precisely the statement about $\ker\nu$, which is what Lehrer–Zhang's paper is for, and the record's own note gets this right (*"Brauer (1937) gives a surjection $\nu$"*). The fact is right; the name is wrong, and it matters here because the file's whole point is that this half carries no threshold, which is true of the FFT and false of the SFT.

**$e$ is an equivariant idempotent.** $\mathrm{Weyl}_n\subset V^{\otimes4}$ is $O(n)$-stable, permuting the three four-leg groups commutes with the diagonal $O(n)$-action, so $\mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathrm{Weyl}_n)\subset V^{\otimes12}$ is an $O(n)$-submodule and the orthogonal projector onto it (with respect to any invariant inner product) lies in $\mathrm{End}_{O(n)}(V^{\otimes12})=\nu(B_{12}(n))$. So $e\,\nu(D)=\nu(bD)$ and the composite stays in the algebra. **Confirmed.**

**The spanning consequence is correct, and correctly reasoned.** $e$ acts on $M_\lambda=\mathrm{Hom}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$ by post-composition, so the composition is well defined; $\mathrm{Hom}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,\mathrm{Sym}^3\mathrm{Weyl}_n)=eM_\lambda$ is the block's covariant space; and for any nonzero $c_0\in M_\lambda$, $\nu(B)c_0=M_\lambda$ gives $\{e\,\nu(D)c_0\}$ spanning $eM_\lambda$. **The seed need not lie in the covariant space**, the argument needs only $c_0\ne0$ in $M_\lambda$, and the brief's worry is answered in the affirmative.

**What it does not buy.** [plan.md](../plan.md) item 1's deliverable is *"each of the 184 constructed covariants is exhibited as an explicit combination of Brauer diagrams"*. The theorem delivers *covariant $=$ (diagram algebra element) applied to a seed*, and the seed is a vector in a weight space with no diagram expression, the item's standing gap, restated in the same commit. Three consequences the commit message does not carry:

- **The reduction is $184\to71$, not to zero.** One seed per block over $71$ blocks; blocks of multiplicity $1$ save nothing. $113$ expansions avoided out of $184$, and $71$ remain.
- **"Spans" is existence, not a construction.** The diagram set has $23!!=316{,}234{,}143{,}225$ elements and $\nu$ is injective at $n=12$, so the image has that same dimension. Nothing in the theorem bounds how many $D$ must be tried or gives the coefficients.
- **The cost is unestimated and plausibly worse.** $\nu(D)c_0$ is computed in $V^{\otimes12}$, of dimension $12^{12}=8.9\times10^{12}$ at $n=12$, whereas the $33{,}177{,}600$-term figure it is being compared against (r258) is a pairing inside the weight space. The claim that this is cheaper than $184$ expansions is asserted, not shown.

### (a) "Theorem 3.4 now SUPPLIES that hypothesis outright", REFUTED, with a witness

[r245](REVIEW_r245.md) claim 1(iii) states the hypothesis precisely: independence of *"the covariant family the constraint data is built on"* **as functions**, at every $n\ge12$, objects evaluated at $W\otimes W\otimes W$ for one Weyl tensor $W$. Theorem 3.4 gives independence of the $\gamma_D$ as functionals on **twelve free legs**. Passing from one to the other is restriction along the degree-3 diagonal $\mathrm{Weyl}_n\to V^{\otimes12}$, and restriction is a linear map with a kernel.

I measured that kernel at the programme's own degree and at the smallest $n$ where Theorem 3.4 applies. Random Weyl tensors built from an antisymmetrised random tensor, Bianchi-projected and trace-removed (residuals $\le2\times10^{-15}$ on all four symmetries, checked in the output), $25$ samples:

| object | legs each | $r$ | $n$ | diagrams (a **basis** upstairs) | rank after restriction | kernel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weyl tensor | 4 | 6 | 6 | $10{,}395$ | **2** | $10{,}393$ |
| Weyl tensor | 4 | 6 | 7 | $10{,}395$ | **2** | $10{,}393$ |
| symmetric traceless | 2 | 3 | 3–12 | $15$ | **1** | $14$ |

**A basis of $10{,}395$ functionals becomes a rank-2 family the moment the three tensor slots are filled with the same $W$.** Theorem 3.4 is true and says nothing about what survives that map. (The rank $2$ is a cross-check on the record: it is r250's two cubic Weyl invariants with $I_2=I_1/2$ resolved, independent from $n\ge6$.)

The audit's own docstring concedes the mechanism three paragraphs later (*"the construction emits VECTORS IN A WEIGHT SPACE"*) while the A2″ paragraph asserts that a theorem about $\gamma_D$ supplies the hypothesis. Those two sentences are in the same file and cannot both hold.
