## The statement and what it was for

The [audit note](2026-09-28-I121-MG-audit.md)'s steps 1 to 3 (Gate 2 [r1818](REVIEW_r1818.md), [r1819](REVIEW_r1819.md)) put the maximiser of $T(G)=\langle G^\#,G\rangle$ over positive unit $G$ on $\mathfrak{so}(m)$ at a positive $G$ with $G^\#=\lambda G$ and full range on one simple subalgebra $\mathfrak h$. **Step 4 as stated**: such a $G$ is scalar. With it, the Lie table gives $\sup T=\max\{\tfrac2{\sqrt3},\tfrac{m-2}{\sqrt{\binom m2}}\}$.

## Three attempts, each ending at a lower bound on $\lambda$

Write $G=\sum_ks_kA_kA_k^{\mathsf T}$ with $A_k$ orthonormal, $c_{ijk}=\langle[A_i,A_j],A_k\rangle$, and $C=\sum_{ij}c_{ijk}^2$ the Casimir row. The equation is the row system $\sum_{ij}s_is_jc_{ijk}^2=2\lambda s_k$.

1. **Muirhead on the rows.** Weighting row $k$ by $s_k^{p-1}$ and symmetrising gives $C\frac{\sum s}N\le2\lambda\le C\frac{\sum s^2}{\sum s}$, the two ends meeting only at scalar $s$. *Both follow from the hypergraph data $c^2$ alone.*
2. **Perron–Frobenius.** $\Phi_G(X)=\sum_is_i\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}_{A_i}X\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}_{A_i}^{\mathsf T}$ is self-adjoint and completely positive with $\Phi_G(G)=2\lambda G$, so $2\lambda$ is its spectral radius, and convexity of $G\mapsto\lambda_{\max}(\Phi_G)$ with the adjoint representation's irreducibility gives the same lower bound.
3. **The second-order condition at a maximiser**, along the component of $I$ orthogonal to $G$: $\lambda\ge Ct/(N+t^2)$ with $t=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}G$ at $\lVert G\rVert=1$. Another lower bound.

**The obstacle they share**: every one reads only $c^2$ and the Casimir rows, and step 4 needs an upper bound on $\lambda$ at a non-scalar $G$.

## Step 4 is false: $\mathfrak g_2\supset\mathfrak{su}(3)$ (derived, checked)

- **Two weights on an isotropy-irreducible pair.** For a simple $\mathfrak k\subset\mathfrak h$ with $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak k^\perp$ irreducible under $\mathfrak k$ and not isomorphic to its adjoint (automatic when $d_{\mathfrak m}\ne d_{\mathfrak k}$), and $G=aP_{\mathfrak k}+bP_{\mathfrak m}$, the rows are constant on each part by Schur, in all three real types. Put $\kappa$ for $\mathfrak k$'s Casimir on itself in $\mathfrak h$'s metric, so $\kappa/C=h^\vee_{\mathfrak k}/(j\,h^\vee_{\mathfrak h})$, $j$ the Dynkin index. Counting triples with one index in $\mathfrak k$ and two in $\mathfrak m$ gives $c_1=2d_{\mathfrak k}(C-\kappa)/d_{\mathfrak m}$ for the mixed pairs landing in $\mathfrak m$ and $c_2=C-c_1$ for $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]_{\mathfrak m}$. The $\mathfrak m$ row fixes $2\lambda=ac_1+bc_2$, and the $\mathfrak k$ row then reads, in $r=b/a$, $$(C-\kappa)r^2-c_2r+(\kappa-c_1)=0,$$ with the scalar root $r=1$ and a second root $r_2=\frac{\kappa-c_1}{C-\kappa}$. **So a positive non-scalar solution exists exactly when $\frac\kappa C>\frac{2d_{\mathfrak k}}{d_{\mathfrak h}+d_{\mathfrak k}}$ and $\frac\kappa C\ne\frac12+\frac{d_{\mathfrak k}}{2d_{\mathfrak h}}$**, the latter being the double root $r_2=1$ (and $C>\kappa$ holds whenever $\mathfrak m\ne0$). A symmetric pair has $c_2=0$ and $r_2=-1$.
- **The boundary and a crossing.** $\mathfrak g_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)$ has $\kappa/C=\tfrac45=\tfrac{2\cdot14}{21+14}$ exactly, so $r_2=0$ (the $\mathfrak g_2$-supported solution). **$\mathfrak{su}(3)\subset\mathfrak g_2$ (long roots, $j=1$) has $\kappa/C=\tfrac34>\tfrac{16}{22}$**, $c_1=\tfrac23C$, $c_2=\tfrac13C$, and $r_2=\tfrac13$: **$G=P_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}+\tfrac13P_{\mathfrak m}$ is a positive definite, non-scalar sharp-eigenoperator on the simple algebra $\mathfrak g_2$**, $\lambda=\tfrac{28}9$ for this unnormalised $G$ in the record's units ($I^\#I=5I$ on $\mathfrak{so}(7)$).
- **Checked by construction** ([screen_I122_g2_su3.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I122_g2_su3.py), seconds): $\mathfrak g_2$ as the stabiliser of the 3-form in $\mathfrak{so}(7)$ (dimension $14$), $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ as the stabiliser of $e_7$ in it (dimension $8$), and the record's $\#$ from $\mathfrak{so}(7)$'s structure constants. The residual $\lVert G^\#G-\lambda G\rVert$ is $5\times10^{-15}$ at $r=\tfrac13$ and $9\times10^{-15}$ at $r=1$, against $0.05$ to $1.3$ elsewhere on the family.
- **Its value is below the scalar's**: at unit norm, $\langle G^\#,G\rangle/\lVert G\rVert^3=1.0568$ at $r=\tfrac13$ against $1.0690=\tfrac4{\sqrt{14}}$ at $r=1$, the table's $\mathfrak g_2$ value. Along the family $\langle G^\#G,G\rangle=24+24r^2+8r^3$ and $\lVert G\rVert^2=8+6r^2$, so $T'\propto-r(r-\tfrac13)(r-1)$: $r=\tfrac13$ is the family's minimum and $r=1$ its maximum. Within $\mathfrak g_2$ the Hessian orthogonal to $G$ at $r=\tfrac13$ has one positive direction ($I-tG$), six null (the orbit $G_2/SU(3)$) and $97$ negative ([r1877](REVIEW_r1877.md)): a saddle.
- **Families where the scalar is the only solution**, by hand: a symmetric pair with two weights, $\mathfrak u(2)\subset\mathfrak{su}(3)$ with three weights ($\mathfrak{su}(2)$, $\mathfrak u(1)$, $\mathbb C^2$), and $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ with weights constant on the Cartan and on each root plane.
- **A screen of the full critical set is uninformative** ([screen_I122_step4_falsifier.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I122_step4_falsifier.py)): least squares on $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ to $\mathfrak{so}(7)$, in $G=hh^{\mathsf T}$ and in $G=e^S$ with the residual scaled by $1/\lambda$, converges only to rank-deficient solutions, several at the scalar values of proper subalgebras ($\lambda=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, $\tfrac2{\sqrt6}$, $\tfrac3{\sqrt{10}}$, and $\tfrac1{5\sqrt3}$ at the principal $\mathfrak{so}(3)\subset\mathfrak{so}(5)$), and never to the scalar itself: this optimiser reached no full-range solution, so the screen decides nothing about them.

## What I122 now needs

**The maximality form of step 4**: on a simple $\mathfrak h$, no positive full-range $G$ has $T(G)$ above the largest value the table gives for $\mathfrak h$ and its simple subalgebras. The $\mathfrak g_2$ solution satisfies it. *A proof must use maximality, second order or global, since the critical equation admits non-scalar full-range solutions.* R55's abandon condition fired as written. I122's conjectured supremum stands.

**It reduces to one inequality, through the second-order condition.** At a maximiser of $T$ on the unit sphere with full range on $\mathfrak h$, the Hessian gives $\langle G^\#X,X\rangle\le\tfrac\lambda2\lVert X\rVert^2$ for $X\perp G$. Along $X=I_{\mathfrak h}-tG$, $t=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}G$ at $\lVert G\rVert=1$ (so $\lambda$ is the unit-norm eigenvalue, the value $T$), with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G^\#I)=\tfrac12Ct$ and $\langle G^\#I,G\rangle=\lambda t$, this reads $$\lambda\ \ge\ \frac{Ct}{N+t^2},\qquad N=\dim\mathfrak h,$$ with equality at the scalar $G=I/\sqrt N$. **So step 4's maximality form follows from the reverse inequality $\lambda(N+t^2)\le Ct$ at every full-range positive sharp-eigenoperator, with equality only at the scalar.** The $\mathfrak g_2$ saddle satisfies it strictly ($C=\kappa_{\mathfrak g_2}=8$ in the record's units, $t=3.397$, $1.0568<1.064$), so the second-order test along $I$ rejects it. *Unproved in general.* **On the two-weight family it is a theorem** ([r1877](REVIEW_r1877.md)): along $G=P_{\mathfrak k}+rP_{\mathfrak m}$, $T'(r)$ is a positive multiple of $-r(C-\kappa)(r-1)(r-r_2)$, and the family's tangent at a solution is proportional to $I-tG$, so the inequality holds strictly at every two-weight solution with $0<r_2<1$ and fails at every one with $r_2>1$, which needs $\kappa/C>\tfrac12+\tfrac{d_{\mathfrak k}}{2\dim\mathfrak h}$. *On that family, the inequality turns on whether the solution weights $\mathfrak m$ above $\mathfrak k$.*

## Toward $\lambda(N+t^2)\le Ct$ (derived, unreviewed)

- **The path to the scalar.** Along $G+\varepsilon I$, $T(G+\varepsilon I)=\lambda+3\lambda t\varepsilon+\tfrac32Ct\varepsilon^2+\tfrac12CN\varepsilon^3$ and $\lVert G+\varepsilon I\rVert^2=1+2t\varepsilon+N\varepsilon^2$, so $f(\varepsilon)=T/\lVert\cdot\rVert^3$ has $f'\propto\varepsilon(A+\varepsilon B)$ with $A=3\big(Ct-\lambda(N+t^2)\big)$ and $B=\tfrac32C(N+t^2)-6\lambda tN$, the cubic and quartic coefficients cancelling exactly. **The target is $A\ge0$**, and $f(\infty)=\tfrac C{2\sqrt N}$ is the scalar's value. By AM–GM $\tfrac{Ct}{N+t^2}\le\tfrac C{2\sqrt N}$, so the target would give every full-range sharp-eigenoperator a value at most the scalar's.
- **The Casimir on $S^2\mathfrak h$.** $\Phi_I(X)=-\sum_i\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}_{A_i}X\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}_{A_i}$ equals $C-\tfrac12\Omega_{S^2\mathfrak h}$, and $\langle G,\Phi_IG\rangle=s^{\mathsf T}Rs=2\lambda t$. Splitting $G=\tfrac tNI+G_\perp$, the target is equivalent to $\langle G_\perp,\Phi_IG_\perp\rangle\le\frac{Ct^2}{N+t^2}\lVert G_\perp\rVert^2$. On $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ the nontrivial components of $S^2\mathfrak h$ have Casimir $2n$ ($S^2_0\mathbb R^n$), $4n-4$ (Weyl) and $4n-16$ ($\Lambda^4$) against the adjoint's $2n-4$, so $\Phi_I\le\tfrac C2$ off the trivial component from $n=6$, **which gives the target near the scalar only**, where $\tfrac{t^2}{N+t^2}\to\tfrac12$.
- **The obstacle**: at small $t$ the target needs $G_\perp$ to avoid the low-Casimir components, and only the critical equation can say so. *A proof uses the rows and the $S^2\mathfrak h$ decomposition together.*
