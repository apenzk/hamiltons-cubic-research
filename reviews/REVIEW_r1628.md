## Checks, and the object that would break each claim

- **The normalisation.** The breaking object is a position $(a,b,c,d)$ with $R_{abcd}\ne0$ at which the right side does not return $R_{abcd}$. The proof multiplies out to $X\cdot(\text{diag indicator}+\tfrac12 N_P+\tfrac1{24}N_F)$ with $N_P$, $N_F$ the counts of ordered pairs and ordered distinct four-tuples whose part is supported there. At $X\ne0$, $a\ne b$ and $c\ne d$ (by `cd_self12`, `cd_self34`), so $\lvert\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\}\rvert\in\{2,1,0\}$, which is the three-way split at [L120Span.lean:326](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L326), [333](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L333), [336](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L336). Diagonal: indicator $1$, $N_P=0$ by `pairForm_shape.1`, $N_F=0$ since a diagonal position is not `D4`. Four-distinct: indicator $0$, $N_P=0$ by `pairForm_shape.2`, $N_F=24$. One-shared: indicator $0$, $N_F=0$ by `fourForm_shape`, $N_P=2$. Each sums to $1$. At $X=0$ both sides are $0$ whatever the counts. The split is exhaustive.
- **The $24$, by hand.** `FourForm i j k l` lists the six ordered pairings $ij|kl$, $kl|ij$, $ik|jl$, $jl|ik$, $il|jk$, $jk|il$, each in both orientations of each pair, which is every ordered partition of $\{i,j,k,l\}$ into two pairs. So for a four-distinct $(a,b,c,d)$ every ordering $(i,j,k,l)$ of $\{a,b,c,d\}$ satisfies it, and a tuple containing an index outside $\{a,b,c,d\}$ cannot, giving $4!=24$. That is `fourForm_iff_perm`, and `count_24` evaluates the restricted sum.
- **The $2$, by hand.** At $\{a,b\}=\{u,y\}$, $\{c,d\}=\{v,y\}$ with $u,v,y$ distinct, `PairForm j k` requires $\{a,b\}$, $\{c,d\}$ to be $\{j,y'\}$, $\{k,y'\}$ in some order with $y'\notin\{j,k\}$. The shared index forces $y'=y$ and then $\{j,k\}=\{u,v\}$, two ordered pairs. `pairForm_iff_one` proves this in both directions.
- **The one-shared witness, all four subcases** ([L120Span.lean:339](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L339)–[351](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L351)). $a=c$: $(u,v,y)=(b,d,a)$, with $b\ne d$ else the position is diagonal, $d\ne a$ else $c=d$. $a=d$: $(b,c,a)$, $b\ne c$ else diagonal by the swapped branch. $b=c$: $(a,d,b)$. $b=d$: $(a,c,b)$. Each orientation disjunct matches the chosen $u,y$ ($a=y\wedge b=u$ in the first two, $a=u\wedge b=y$ in the last two) and the $c,d$ disjunct likewise. With none of the four equalities and $a\ne b$, $c\ne d$ the position is `D4`, excluded. Correct in all four.
- **Coordinates of each part.** The breaking object is a pair part that disagrees with $R$ at some `PairForm` position other than $(j,y,k,y)$. `coord_pairPart` routes through `pairForm_coord_zero` applied to $R-\mathrm{pairPart}$, which is Weyl and vanishes at every $(j,y,k,y)$ (by `coord_pairS_diag` at $y\notin\{j,k\}$ and by skewness at $y\in\{j,k\}$), so it vanishes on every `PairForm` position. Off `PairForm`, `iso_coord_form` with `pairS_iso` puts every nonzero coordinate of the part inside `PairForm`. `coord_fourPart` is the same pattern with `fourForm_coord_zero` at the three pairings and `four_coord_form`. The sign $y=-R_{ikjl}$ matches L120Four's `four_isotypic` and the Bianchi relation $x+y+z=0$ follows from `cd_bianchi` after `cd_sw34`. `coord_diagPart` uses `coord_diagS` and `cd_sw34` at $(a,b,b,a)$. All three correct.
- **Faithfulness to L120 (gap 1).** The breaking object is a part that lies outside its record summand. `pairPart R j k` is `pairLin j k c ∘ skewProj` on the Ricci cut, which `pairS_iso` places in the $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$-isotypic subspace (and `pairS_iso_signMat` at the full group). `fourPart` is `fourS i j k l x y z` on the Bianchi cut, placed by `fourS_iso`. `diagPart R` is `diagS (diagWeight R)`, placed in the fixed subspace by `diagS_flip` with `diagWeight_isWeight`. The record's $V_{jk}$ and $V_{ijkl}$ are indexed by unordered sets, and the ordered sums in `weyl_span` land in them because the characters are symmetric in their indices. So the spanning claim is faithful. *The gap*: none of these three memberships is stated in L120Span.lean, and `weyl_span`'s type names only operators, so the phrase "the three summands span" at [L120Span.lean:20](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L20) is a declaration plus three one-line compositions left to the reader. `coord_pairPart` already builds the pair membership internally ([L120Span.lean:95](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L95)).
- **Directness (gap 2).** The header at [L120Span.lean:22](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Span.lean#L22) says the sum is direct "because the three kinds of part are supported on disjoint sets of positions". The argument is correct once complete, but it needs three facts, and the sentence names one. (i) Every member of each summand vanishes off its own positions: `iso_coord_form`, `four_coord_form`, and the flip-fixed reduction behind `diag_isotypic`. (ii) The supports of distinct summands are disjoint, *within* each family as well as across the three kinds: distinct unordered pairs have disjoint `PairForm` sets and distinct four-sets disjoint `FourForm` sets. `pairForm_iff_one` and `fourForm_iff_perm` prove exactly the within-family half, and `pairForm_shape`, `fourForm_shape` the across-kinds half. (iii) A Weyl operator vanishing at every coordinate is zero (`cd_ext`). Then a vanishing sum of summand members has each member's coordinates zero on its own support, hence everywhere, hence the member is zero. The header's "argued here and has no declaration" is accurate.
- **n small.** The breaking object is $n<4$, where `D4` is never satisfied, or $n<3$. `weyl_span` carries no bound on $n$, and at small $n$ the four-set sum is empty and the identity still holds position by position. No hidden hypothesis.
- **r1627 fixes (note 3).** The L120Diag header now points to the dimension theorems. `diagSummand_finrank_closed` states $2\dim\operatorname{range}(\mathrm{weightOp})=n(n-3)$ at $n\ge3$ in natural numbers, where $n-3$ truncates harmlessly under the hypothesis. *Cosmetic*: the L120Diag header states $n(n-3)/2$ without the $n\ge3$ that both cited theorems carry.
- **INDEX row.** The new clause matches the file. The stated debt, directness as a declaration, is the only piece of the displayed decomposition not now in Lean, given the identification files and this one. "statement: not typed" stays accurate since no single declaration states the displayed equation with $\oplus$.
