## A. The object and the projection

**The curvature operator.** [WeylReal.lean:39](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L39) sets $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X^\top Y)$, so $\langle\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{ij}\rangle=1$ for $i\ne j$ (two entries of modulus one, halved). [Families.lean:147](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Families.lean#L147) defines `diagOp w` as the Hadamard product, and `diagOp_phi` gives $\mathrm{diagOp}(w)\varphi_{ab}=w_{ab}\varphi_{ab}$. The unit sphere's curvature operator is the identity on $\Lambda^2$ under the convention in which $\mathrm{Ric}>0$, and the product's is the identity on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^p\oplus\Lambda^2\mathbb R^q$ and zero on mixed bivectors. That is `diagOp (sectProd S)`.

**The sign.** [WeylReal.lean:106](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L106) has $\mathrm{Ric}(R)_{ab}=\sum_c\langle R\varphi_{ac},\varphi_{bc}\rangle$. At $a\in S$ this is $\sum_c\mathrm{sectProd}_{ac}\cdot\langle\varphi_{ac},\varphi_{ac}\rangle=\#\{c\in S,c\ne a\}=p-1$. So sectional curvature $+1$ on in-block pairs gives $\mathrm{Ric}=(p-1)g$ on the first factor and $(q-1)g$ on the second, as the brief requires. Off-diagonal $\mathrm{Ric}_{ab}$ vanishes because $\langle\varphi_{ac},\varphi_{bc}\rangle=0$ for $a\ne b$.

**The Weyl part is the orthogonal projection.** On algebraic curvature operators, $\mathcal R=\mathcal W_n\oplus(\mathrm{Sym}^2\odot g)$ and the two summands are orthogonal under the trace pairing, which `innerOp` is up to the positive factor fixed by the ordered-pair doubling. So the Weyl part of $R$ is its `innerOp`-projection onto $\mathcal W_n$. *The opposite position*: a different normalisation of `innerOp` could tilt the complement. It cannot, since any positive multiple of an inner product has the same orthogonal complements.

**$\mathcal W_n^\perp$ on the diagonal locus.** Symmetric hollow weights form a space of dimension $\binom n2$, the zero-row-sum ones have codimension $n$ (the row-sum map is onto for $n\ge3$), and $h\mapsto(h_a+h_b)$ is injective for $n\ge3$. So the weights $h_a+h_b$ span exactly the $n$-dimensional complement of the Weyl weights inside the diagonal locus, and they are the diagonal Kulkarni–Nomizu products $h\odot g$ with $h$ diagonal.

**Is orthogonality to linear Ricci-flat operators enough?** Yes. `sphereProduct_weylPart` gives $A=W+K$ with $W=\mathrm{diagOp}(c\cdot\mathrm{sphereWeight}\,S)$ Weyl and $K=\mathrm{diagOp}(\mathrm{knWeight}\,h)$ orthogonal to every linear Ricci-flat $R$, a set containing every linear Weyl operator. If $A=W'+K'$ is the orthogonal decomposition, then $W-W'=K'-K$ lies in $\mathcal W_n$ and is orthogonal to $\mathcal W_n$, so its `innerOp` norm is zero and $W=W'$ on $\Lambda^2$. The characterisation needs only these two properties, so the first summand being Weyl is exactly what closes it.

**Gap A1 (minor): the identification is prose.** *Location*: the docstring at [L2SphereWeyl.lean:106-110](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean#L106) and the conjunct at [L2SphereWeyl.lean:113](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean#L113), echoed in the L2 row at [lean/INDEX.md:624](../lean/INDEX.md#L624). *The failure*: no theorem states that $W$ is the Weyl projection of `diagOp (sectProd S)`, and no definition of "Weyl part" exists for one to state it against. The uniqueness argument above, which needs `innerOp` definite on skew-preserving linear maps, lives only in the word "so". The conjunct is `IsWeyl`, while the record's $\mathcal W_n$ is [FoxProduct.lean:110](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/FoxProduct.lean#L110)'s `IsWeylOp = IsLinOp ∧ IsWeyl`, so the first summand's membership in the space the orthogonality quantifies over is also left to inspection. *The opposite position*: `diagOp` is visibly linear, and "Weyl plus orthogonal to Weyl is the projection" is textbook linear algebra that every reader supplies. That is true, and it is why the gap is minor. It survives because the row says **PROVED** and the step that turns the theorem into the claim is outside the kernel. A uniqueness lemma, or the row saying "gives the decomposition whose first summand is, by uniqueness, the Weyl part", would close it.

**Radii (checked, no gap).** The Lean proves the unit-radius case, and the record's family is "$S^p\times S^q$". With curvatures $K_1,K_2>0$ the same computation gives $\langle R,\mathrm{diagOp}(\mathrm{sphereWeight})\rangle=\tfrac12pq(K_1+K_2)>0$ and the diagonal Weyl weights invariant under $O(p)\times O(q)$ form a line, so the Weyl part is a positive multiple of `sphereWeight` at every radius pair, and the unit case loses nothing for $\theta$. This is a hand computation, recorded as a check.

## B. The constants

**From the split.** On in-block pairs of $S$: $1=c\,\tfrac q{p-1}+2h_S$. On in-block pairs of $S^c$: $1=c\,\tfrac p{q-1}+2h_T$. On cross pairs: $0=-c+h_S+h_T$. Substituting the first two into the third gives $c\,(1+\tfrac12(\tfrac q{p-1}+\tfrac p{q-1}))=1$, which is `cW`, and $h_S=\tfrac12(1-c\tfrac q{p-1})$, $h_T=\tfrac12(1-c\tfrac p{q-1})$, which is `hW` ([L2SphereWeyl.lean:73-79](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean#L73)).

**From the projection formula, independently.** $\lVert\mathrm{diagOp}(\mathrm{sphereWeight})\rVert^2=\tfrac12\bigl[p(p-1)\tfrac{q^2}{(p-1)^2}+q(q-1)\tfrac{p^2}{(q-1)^2}+2pq\bigr]=pq\bigl[1+\tfrac12(\tfrac q{p-1}+\tfrac p{q-1})\bigr]$ and $\langle\mathrm{diagOp}(\mathrm{sectProd}),\mathrm{diagOp}(\mathrm{sphereWeight})\rangle=\tfrac12[p(p-1)\tfrac q{p-1}+q(q-1)\tfrac p{q-1}]=pq$. The ratio is `cW` exactly.

**Against the Schouten tensor.** $P=\tfrac1{n-2}(\mathrm{Ric}-\tfrac{\mathrm{Scal}}{2(n-1)}g)$ should equal $h$. At $(3,3)$: $n=6$, $\mathrm{Scal}=12$, $P=\tfrac14(2-\tfrac65)=\tfrac15$ on both blocks, and the file gives $c=2/5$, $h=\tfrac12(1-\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac32)=\tfrac15$. At $(2,3)$: $n=5$, $\mathrm{Scal}=8$, $P=\tfrac13(1-1)=0$ on $S$ and $\tfrac13(2-1)=\tfrac13$ off it, and the file gives $c=\tfrac13$, $h_S=0$, $h_T=\tfrac13$.

**The record's normalisation at $p=q=3$.** The Weyl part is $c\cdot\mathrm{sphereWeight}$ with entries $\tfrac25\cdot\tfrac32=\tfrac35$ in-block and $-\tfrac25$ across. [Families.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Families.lean#L300)'s `sphereWeight` docstring gives the record's balanced weight as $1/(p(p-1))=\tfrac16$ inside and $-1/(pq)=-\tfrac19$ across. The ratios are $\tfrac35/\tfrac16=\tfrac{18}5$ and $\tfrac25/\tfrac19=\tfrac{18}5$, so the true Weyl part is $\tfrac{18}5$ times the record's $W_{S^3\times S^3}$, a positive factor, and $c>0$ at every admissible $(p,q)$ (`cW_pos`) keeps the sign that $\theta$ does see. The scratch file's `norm_num` of $c(3,3)=2/5$ closed.

## C. Proofs

**`innerOp_knWeight`** ([L2SphereWeyl.lean:42](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean#L42)). `innerOp` unfolds to $\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\langle k_{ij}\varphi_{ij},R\varphi_{ij}\rangle$ through `diagOp_phi`, needing only symmetry of `knWeight` (`hsym`). *The $i=j$ terms*: `knWeight` is $0$ there while $h_i+h_i$ need not be, and the proof's `e` handles it by $\varphi_{ii}=0$, which makes $\langle R\varphi_{ii},\varphi_{ii}\rangle=0$ through the second argument alone, so $0=h_i\cdot0+h_i\cdot0$ with no claim about $R(0)$. *The $\varphi_{ji}$ symmetry*: `hswap` rewrites by `phi_swap` and pulls the sign out of $R$ with `hneg`, then out of the transpose, so $\langle R\varphi_{ji},\varphi_{ji}\rangle=\langle R\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{ij}\rangle$, and the two halves $\sum_ih_i\sum_jT_{ij}$ and $\sum_jh_j\sum_iT_{ij}$ agree, cancelling the $\tfrac12$. *`IsLinOp`*: used once, `hR.2 (-1) Z`, for oddness. Oddness is genuinely needed, since a non-odd $R$ breaks `hswap`. The result is $\sum_ah_a\mathrm{Ric}(R)_{aa}$, matching [WeylReal.lean:106](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L106).

**`sectProd_split`** ([L2SphereWeyl.lean:90](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean#L90)). Diagonal: all three weights are hollow. In $S$: $1=c\tfrac q{p-1}+2h_S$, true by the definition of $h_S$ alone. Cross, both orders: $0=-c+h_S+h_T$, which is `hc` rearranged, and `linarith` uses `hc`. Out of $S$: $1=c\tfrac p{q-1}+2h_T$, by definition. `hc` itself is `field_simp` after both fractions are shown positive, which needs $p,q\ge2$.

**`sphereProduct_weylPart`** ([L2SphereWeyl.lean:111](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2SphereWeyl.lean#L111)). The first conjunct is `sectProd_split` pushed through `diagOp` entrywise. The Weyl conjunct is `diagOp_isWeyl` of `sphereWeight_isWeight` scaled. The last is `innerOp_knWeight` with $\mathrm{Ric}=0$. Nothing is assumed that the statement does not carry.
