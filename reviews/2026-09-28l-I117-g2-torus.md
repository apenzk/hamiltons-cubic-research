*Working note for [I117](../plan.md), route [R48](../routes.md) (U$_{\mathrm s}'$ on the torus family of every compact simple $\mathfrak h$), by the manager's decision of 2026-09-28k. $\mathfrak g_2$ is one of the families R48 lists as remaining ([R48 note](2026-09-27-R48-torus-families.md), Open). Nothing here is numbered. The family, its product table, the stabiliser types and both exact solves passed Gate 2 ([r1884](REVIEW_r1884.md), [r1885](REVIEW_r1885.md), every item CONFIRMED, corrections folded); the antisymmetric factorisation is unreviewed.*

## What H asks of $\mathfrak g_2$

By [L270](../findings/second-order.md) (3), statement H at $n\ge9$ needs, of a stable positive-definite $\#$-idempotent $C$ on a simple $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$, only $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$, which U$_{\mathrm s}'$'s $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge d/c^2$ implies through [L256](../findings/second-order.md). On $\mathfrak g_2$ (index one in $\mathfrak{so}(7)$), $d=14$ and $c=4$, so $d/c^2=\tfrac78$, against $N/(n-2)^2=\tfrac{36}{49}=0.735$ at $n=9$ and less above.

## The family

A maximal torus of $G_2$ has rank two. The operators on $\mathfrak g_2$ commuting with it are a symmetric $2\times2$ block on the Cartan $\mathfrak t$ and one scalar on each of the six positive root planes: **a nine-dimensional family, closed under $\#$ by equivariance.**

## The family's algebra, exactly (derived)

Write $C=A+\sum_\alpha w_\alpha P_\alpha$ over the six positive root planes, $A$ on the Cartan, and fix $\mathfrak g_2$'s roots with $\lvert\alpha\rvert^2=2$ long and $\tfrac23$ short in the record's metric. Then $A^\#A=0$ (the Cartan is abelian), $A^\#P_\alpha=\tfrac12(\alpha^{\mathsf T}A\alpha)P_\alpha$, $P_\alpha^\#P_\alpha=\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ on the Cartan, and a mixed product $P_\alpha^\#P_\beta$ is $\tfrac12P_\gamma$ on each root $\gamma=\pm\alpha\pm\beta$, except that two short roots give $\tfrac23$ on the short one. **By hand** ([r1885](REVIEW_r1885.md)): in an orthonormal basis $(P_\alpha^\#P_\beta)$ on $V_\gamma$ is $S/4$, $S$ the sum of squared structure constants over the three planes and totally symmetric in them. The Casimir rows ($2c=8$) give $S_{LL}+S_{SS}=4$ onto a long root and $2S_{SL}+S_{SSS}=\tfrac{20}3$ onto a short one, $\mathfrak{su}(3)$'s $c=3$ gives $S_{LL}=2$, and total symmetry then gives $S_{SL}=2$, $S_{SSS}=\tfrac83$: the coefficients $\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac23$. The table reproduces L263's blocks. So $C^\#C=C$ is $A=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha^2\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ together with, for each positive root $\gamma$, $$w_\gamma\Big(1-\sum_\alpha w_\alpha^2(\alpha\cdot\gamma)^2\Big)=\sum_{\{\alpha,\beta\}}2\,\mathrm{coef}^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\,w_\alpha w_\beta ,$$ six cubic equations with rational coefficients ($(\alpha\cdot\gamma)^2\in\{4,1,0\}$ among long and mixed pairs, $\{\tfrac49,\tfrac19\}$ among short). $A$ is positive definite whenever the weights are nonzero, since the roots span the Cartan. **Positive-definite torus-invariant idempotents are the positive solutions $w\in\mathbb R_{>0}^6$.**

- **On the Weyl-invariant weights** (long $x$, short $y$) the system is $x(1-6x^2-2y^2)=x^2+y^2$ and $1-2x^2-\tfrac23y^2=2x+\tfrac43y$, whose positive solutions are exactly $(\tfrac14,\tfrac14)$, which is $I/4$, and $(\tfrac9{28},\tfrac3{28})$, which is L263's. *Solved exactly; the other two roots have $x=-\tfrac12$.*
- The $SO(4)$ idempotent and the fourth screened one break the Weyl symmetry, so the full list needs the six-variable system ([solve_I117_g2_torus.py](../scripts/misc/solve_I117_g2_torus.py)). **Its ideal is zero-dimensional with $409$ complex solutions counted with multiplicity** (Singular through Sage, $1.2$ s). Eliminating to one variable and isolating real roots did not finish in the bounded time ($20$ and $10$ minutes), so **the exact enumeration is a long run and waits**. *What it would decide: whether the four screened members are all the positive ones, which with their norms and stabilities settles U$_{\mathrm s}$ on this family.*
- **What an analytic route needs instead.** U$_{\mathrm s}'$ asks only that no member is lighter than $I/4$. With $y_L=\sum_{\text{long}}w^2$ and $y_S=\sum_{\text{short}}w^2$, $\lVert C\rVert^2=\lVert A\rVert^2+2(y_L+y_S)\ge\tfrac12(2y_L+\tfrac23y_S)^2+2(y_L+y_S)$, equality at $I/4$, so a lower bound on the root masses $y_L,y_S$ from the six equations would do, as [L270](../findings/second-order.md) (4) does on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$'s torus with $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge4y^2/m+2y$.

## Exact on every symmetric type (derived and solved)

- **The stabiliser types.** $G_2$'s Weyl group acts on the six root planes through $S_3$, its longest element $-1$ fixing every plane, and it permutes the three long and the three short planes compatibly, each long root orthogonal to one short root. So a solution's stabiliser on the planes is $S_3$, a reflection (fixing one orthogonal long–short pair and swapping the other two pairs), or trivial; a three-cycle is transitive on each triple and forces the uniform weights.
- **$S_3$**: the Weyl-invariant section above, two positive solutions.
- **A reflection** ([solve_I117_g2_torus_reflection.sage](../scripts/misc/solve_I117_g2_torus_reflection.sage), seconds): with $x$ on the fixed long plane, $y$ on the fixed short one, $z$ and $u$ on the swapped pairs, the system is four cubics with $53$ complex solutions, $31$ real, and **exactly four positive ones**:

| $(x,z,u,y)$ | $\lVert C\rVert^2$ | identified |
|---|---|---|
| $(\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14)$ | $\tfrac78$ | $I/4$ |
| $(\tfrac9{28},\tfrac9{28},\tfrac3{28},\tfrac3{28})$ | $\tfrac{351}{392}$ | L263's $SU(3)$ |
| $(\tfrac{11}{29},\sqrt{\tfrac{77}{1682}},\sqrt{\tfrac{77}{1682}},\tfrac5{29})$ | $\tfrac{746}{841}$ | L263's $SO(4)$ |
| $(0.338753,0.311979,0.109157,0.105290)$, $x$ of degree $6$ | $0.895440$ | new, the screen's fourth |

- **So every positive-definite torus-invariant idempotent on $\mathfrak g_2$ with a nontrivial stabiliser is Weyl-conjugate to one of these four** (the $SO(4)$ point and the new one have three images each), each with $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tfrac78$, equality only at $I/4$. **U$_{\mathrm s}'$ holds exactly on them.** The trivial-stabiliser type is empty on a $3300$-start screen of the six-weight system, and its exact exclusion is the full system's enumeration, which waits.
- *Stability* (the screen's top eigenvalues $\tfrac5{12}$, $0.566$, $\tfrac{15}{28}$, $0.536$) separates $I/4$ from the other three, which U$_{\mathrm s}'$ does not need.

## Toward the trivial-stabiliser type (derived, unreviewed)

For the reflection fixing $(P_0,P_5)$, write $w_{1,4}=p\pm a$ and $w_{2,3}=q\pm b$. The antisymmetric combinations of the system factor as $$E_1-E_4=-2\big(\alpha_1a+Kb\big),\qquad E_2-E_3=-\tfrac29\big(9Ka+\beta_2b\big),\qquad K=2pq+w_5>0,$$ with $\alpha_1=5a^2+b^2+11p^2+q^2+w_0^2-w_0+w_5^2-1$ and $\beta_2=9a^2+5b^2+9p^2+11q^2+9w_0^2-9w_0+w_5^2-12w_5-9$. **So a solution asymmetric under this reflection has $\alpha_1\beta_2=9K^2$, and one with trivial stabiliser satisfies that for each of the three reflections.** *Excluding positive solutions of the three together is the next step for the generic type.*

## $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$'s torus system (derived at every $k$, Gate 2 [r1888](REVIEW_r1888.md), [r1889](REVIEW_r1889.md))

In $\mathfrak{so}(4k)$'s metric the long roots $2\varepsilon_i$ have $\lvert\alpha\rvert^2=2$ and the short $\varepsilon_i\pm\varepsilon_j$ have $1$. The table has the $\mathfrak g_2$ shape, $A^\#A=0$, $A^\#P_\alpha=\tfrac12(\alpha^{\mathsf T}A\alpha)P_\alpha$, $P_\alpha^\#P_\alpha=\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}$, with mixed coefficients $\tfrac14$ for short plus short onto short and $\tfrac12$ otherwise ([screen_I117_sp3_torus.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I117_sp3_torus.py)'s construction). With long weights $u_i$, short weights $p_{ij}$ on $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ and $q_{ij}$ on $\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j$, and in Cartan coordinates $\varepsilon_i=f_i/\sqrt2$, $$a_i=A_{ii}=2u_i^2+\tfrac12\sum_j(p_{ij}^2+q_{ij}^2),\qquad A_{ij}=\tfrac12(q_{ij}^2-p_{ij}^2),$$ and $C^\#C=C$ reads $$u_i(1-2a_i)=\sum_jp_{ij}q_{ij},\quad p_{ij}\big(1-X^-_{ij}\big)=(u_i+u_j)q_{ij}+\tfrac12\sum_l(p_{il}p_{jl}+q_{il}q_{jl}),\quad q_{ij}\big(1-X^+_{ij}\big)=(u_i+u_j)p_{ij}+\tfrac12\sum_l(p_{il}q_{jl}+q_{il}p_{jl}),$$ with $X^\pm_{ij}=\tfrac12(a_i+a_j)\pm\tfrac12(q_{ij}^2-p_{ij}^2)$. **Without the $u$'s this is $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$'s torus system up to the rescaling $(p,q)=2(U,V)$** ([L271](../findings/second-order.md), two coupled copies exchanged by $P$ and $Q$), a formal identification, since at $u=0$ the long rows force $\sum_jp_{ij}q_{ij}=0$, which no positive point satisfies. *Both readers derived the coefficients at every $k$: each triple of roots lives in a block $\mathfrak{sp}(3)$ whose embedding keeps the metric, and $I^\#I=cI$ read row by row fixes $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac14$.* *The conjecture is that its only positive solution is uniform ($I/c$), and a proof would add the long-root rows to L271's machinery.*

- **The quadratic sums split in $s=p+q$ and $d=p-q$** (derived, the identities checked at a random point; the two halves still meet through $m_{ij}$, the $\tfrac12d^2$ and $\tfrac12s^2$ terms and the long rows): with $m_{ij}=\tfrac12(a_i+a_j)$ and $a_i=2u_i^2+\tfrac14\sum_j(s_{ij}^2+d_{ij}^2)$, $$s_{ij}\big(1-m_{ij}-\tfrac12d_{ij}^2\big)=(u_i+u_j)s_{ij}+\tfrac12\sum_ls_{il}s_{jl},\qquad d_{ij}\big(1-m_{ij}-\tfrac12s_{ij}^2\big)=-(u_i+u_j)d_{ij}+\tfrac12\sum_ld_{il}d_{jl},$$ and $u_i(1-2a_i)=\tfrac14\sum_j(s_{ij}^2-d_{ij}^2)$; positive definiteness is $s>\lvert d\rvert$ and $u>0$. **$d=0$ is an invariant sector holding the uniform point** ($u=\tfrac1c$, $s=\tfrac2c$, $c=k+1$). *So the uniqueness conjecture splits: the $d$-sector vanishes at every positive solution, and on $d=0$ an $\mathfrak{so}(k)$-like system with vertex weights $u_i$ has only the uniform solution.*
- **The $d$-sector's trace identity.** Weighting the $d$-rows by $d_{ij}$ and summing over ordered pairs, $\sum_{ij}B_{ij}d_{ij}^2=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}D^3$ with $B_{ij}=1-m_{ij}-\tfrac12s_{ij}^2+u_i+u_j$. So a nonzero $D$ with every $B_{ij}\ge\beta>0$ has $\lambda_{\max}(D)\ge2\beta$, while $\lambda_{\max}(D)\le\max_i\sum_j\lvert d_{ij}\rvert<\max_i\sum_js_{ij}$. **Hence $D=0$ wherever $2\min B\ge\max_i\sum_js_{ij}$.** At the uniform point $B=1+\tfrac1c-\tfrac2{c^2}>1$ and the row sums are $\tfrac{2(k-1)}{k+1}<2$, so the $d$-sector vanishes on the open set of solutions where $2\min B>\max_i\sum_js_{ij}$, which contains $I/c$ with margin $\tfrac{6c-4}{c^2}$, shrinking like $\tfrac6k$. *With the theorem below, $I/c$ is an isolated positive solution.* *Globally the crude bounds ($a_i\le\tfrac12$ from the long rows) give neither side, so this is the open step of the $d$-half.*

## On the sector $p=q$, $I/c$ is the only positive-definite idempotent of $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$'s torus family (derived, Gate 2 [r1888](REVIEW_r1888.md), [r1889](REVIEW_r1889.md) CONFIRMED on every step, the Lean proof checked against the claim and the prose by [r1891](REVIEW_r1891.md) and [r1892](REVIEW_r1892.md), CONFIRMED, numbered [L285](../findings/second-order.md))

**Statement.** *Every positive solution of $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$'s torus system with $p_{ij}=q_{ij}$ for all $i\ne j$ is the uniform one, $u_i=\tfrac1{k+1}$ and $p_{ij}=q_{ij}=\tfrac1{k+1}$, which is $I/c$.*

**Proof.** *Put $s=p+q=2p$ and $U=\operatorname{diag}(u)$, and let $S$ be the symmetric matrix of the $s_{ij}$ with zero diagonal.*
1. **The long rows give $a_i=u_i$.** With $d=0$, $u_i(1-2a_i)=\tfrac14r_i$ and $a_i=2u_i^2+\tfrac14r_i$, $r_i=\sum_js_{ij}^2$, give $u_i-2u_ia_i=a_i-2u_i^2$, which is $(u_i-a_i)(1+2u_i)=0$, so $a_i=u_i$ since $u_i>0$, and $r_i=4u_i(1-2u_i)$. *Since $r_i\ge0$, $u_i\le\tfrac12$, strict for $k\ge2$ (at $k=1$ the sum is empty and $u=\tfrac12$).*
2. **The $s$-rows are one matrix equation.** With $m_{ij}=\tfrac12(u_i+u_j)$ they read $(S^2)_{ij}=s_{ij}\big(2-3(u_i+u_j)\big)$ off the diagonal, and step 1 is $(S^2)_{ii}=4u_i(1-2u_i)$, so $$S^2=2S-3(US+SU)+4U-8U^2 .$$
3. **Completing the square**, $T=S+3U-I$ satisfies $T^2=(I-U)^2$, a diagonal matrix (checked symbolically).
4. **All $u_i$ are equal.** $T$ commutes with $T^2$, so $T_{ij}\big((1-u_i)^2-(1-u_j)^2\big)=0$, and $T_{ij}=s_{ij}>0$ off the diagonal with $u_i+u_j\ne2$ gives $u_i=u_j$.
5. **The columns of $M=S+2uI$ are positive eigenvectors of $S$.** With every $u_i=u$, step 2 reads $S^2=(2-6u)S+4u(1-2u)I$, so $SM=(2-4u)M$, while $M_{ii}=2u>0$ and $M_{ij}=s_{ij}>0$.
6. **Two positive eigenvectors of $S$ for one eigenvalue are proportional.** For $Sx=\lambda x$ and $Sy=\lambda y$ with $y>0$, let $t=\min_ix_i/y_i$, attained at $m$. Then $z=x-ty\ge0$ and $z_m=0$, so $0=\lambda z_m=\sum_{l\ne m}s_{ml}z_l$ with every $s_{ml}>0$ forces $z=0$. *(The elementary step of Perron–Frobenius.)*
7. **So $M=2uJ$, and the diagonal fixes $u$.** Columns $i$ and $j$ of $M$ are proportional, $M_{\cdot j}=tM_{\cdot i}$: row $i$ gives $M_{ij}=2ut$, and row $j$ with symmetry gives $2u=tM_{ij}=2ut^2$, so $t^2=1$, and $t>0$ since $M_{ij}=2ut>0$, so $t=1$ and $s_{ij}=2u$. The diagonal of $SM=(2-4u)M$ then reads $4u^2(k-1)=2u(2-4u)$, and dividing by $2u>0$ gives $u=\tfrac1{k+1}$ and $s=\tfrac2{k+1}$. $\square$

**What it gives.** *On the equal-weight sector the uniqueness conjecture is proved at every $k$. What remains is that every positive solution lies in that sector, which is the $d$-half above, proved near $I/c$ by the trace identity.* **Lean**: the statement from the long rows and the $p$-rows as `equal_weight_torus` in [SpTorusEqualWeight.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/SpTorusEqualWeight.lean), steps 1 and 2 in its body, and steps 3 to 7 as `equal_weight_unique` from the matrix equation of step 2 with $S$ symmetric, zero diagonal, positive off the diagonal and $0<u_i<1$, with `square_completion`, `u_equal` and `eigvec_proportional` beneath it. The derivation of the displayed rows from $C^\#C=C$ is prose ([r1888](REVIEW_r1888.md), [r1889](REVIEW_r1889.md)).

## The whole system as two square completions (derived, in Lean, Gate 2 [r1893](REVIEW_r1893.md), [r1894](REVIEW_r1894.md))

**Statement.** *With $S=P+Q$, $D=P-Q$, $U=\operatorname{diag}(u)$ and $A_d=\operatorname{diag}(a)$, the definition of $a_i$, the long rows, the $p$-rows and the $q$-rows together are equivalent to*
$$(S+A_d+2U-I)^2=\operatorname{diag}(1-2u_i+a_i^2)-S\circ D\circ D,\qquad(D+A_d-2U-I)^2=\operatorname{diag}(1+2u_i+a_i^2)-D\circ S\circ S,$$
*with $\circ$ the entrywise product, whose diagonals vanish.* **Proof.** *Expand the squares: the diagonal of the first is $-2$ times the sum of the $a_i$ definition and the long row, off it $-2$ times the sum of the $p$- and $q$-rows, and the second takes the differences in place of the sums.* The two combinations are invertible, so the identities give back every row. $\square$ **Lean**: `plus_block` and `minus_block` in [SpTorusBlocks.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/SpTorusBlocks.lean), from the rows as the structure `TorusRows`. The converse is prose.

- **At $D=0$ the first is [L285](../findings/second-order.md)'s square completion** ($a=u$ by step 1), and the second's diagonal is step 1 itself. *The rows are the weight graph of the defining representation: the $2k\times2k$ matrix $\hat W=\begin{pmatrix}P+A_d&Q+2U\\Q+2U&P+A_d\end{pmatrix}$ on the weights $\pm\varepsilon_i$, every off-diagonal entry positive, splits into these two blocks, and the rows read $(\hat W^2)_{xy}=f_{xy}\hat W_{xy}$ with $f=1$ on the long edges, $2(1\pm A_{ij})$ on the short ones and $2+a_i$ on the diagonal, which is the definition of $a_i$.*
- **The diagonals are the row sums**: $\sum_js_{ij}^2=2(a_i+u_i)(1-2u_i)$ and $\sum_jd_{ij}^2=2(a_i-u_i)(1+2u_i)$, so $a_i\ge u_i$ with equality exactly when row $i$ of $D$ vanishes.
- **The $d$-sector in this form.** Off the diagonal the second identity is $(D^2)_{ij}=d_{ij}(g_i+g_j-s_{ij}^2)$ with $g_i=1+2u_i-a_i$, and the first is $(S^2)_{ij}=s_{ij}(h_i+h_j-d_{ij}^2)$ with $h_i=1-2u_i-a_i=g_i-4u_i$. Three attempts at $D=0$ globally, each stopping in the same regime:
  1. *The trace identity with Perron–Frobenius* (the section above): for $D\ne0$, $\lambda_{\max}(D)\ge\min_{i\ne j}(g_i+g_j-s_{ij}^2)$ against $\lambda_{\max}(D)<\rho(S)$, and the first identity at $S$'s Perron vector $v$ gives $\sum_i\big((\rho(S)-h_i)^2-(1-2u_i+a_i^2)\big)v_i^2=-v^{\mathsf T}(S\circ D\circ D)v\le0$, so $\rho(S)\le\max_i\big(h_i+\sqrt{1-2u_i+a_i^2}\big)$. The two bounds separate at $I/c$ with margin of order $\tfrac6c$ and cross when $u_i$ is small against $a_i$ and $s_{ij}^2$.
  2. *The spectrum of $D$* (sketch): off the diagonal $D^2$ is $D$ times a factor near $2$ and $D^2$'s diagonal is $O(\lVert D\rVert^2)$, so the eigenvalues cluster near $0$ and near $2$ while $\operatorname{tr}D=0$. This excludes $D\ne0$ when the factor's spread and the row sums are small, which is the neighbourhood of $I/c$ again.
  3. *The square root* (derived, unreviewed, not in Lean). $Z=G-D$, $G=\operatorname{diag}(g)$, squares to $X=\operatorname{diag}(1+2u_i+a_i^2)-D\circ S\circ S$. With $R=\operatorname{diag}\sqrt{1+2u_i+a_i^2}$ and $E=Z-R$, $Z^2-R^2=RE+EZ$, and $E\mapsto RE+EZ$ is self-adjoint for the Frobenius product with eigenvalues $r_i+z_j$. So when $\lambda_{\min}(Z)>-\min_ir_i$, $\lVert D\rVert_F\le\lVert E\rVert_F\le\lVert D\circ S\circ S\rVert_F/(\min_ir_i+\lambda_{\min}(Z))\le\max_{ij}s_{ij}^2\,\lVert D\rVert_F/(\min_ir_i+\lambda_{\min}(Z))$, the off-diagonal part of $E$ being $-D$. For $k\ge2$ the long rows give $a_i<\tfrac12$, and $\sum_js_{ij}^2=2(a_i+u_i)(1-2u_i)>0$ gives $u_i<\tfrac12$, so $\sum_js_{ij}^2<1-4u_i^2<1<\min_ir_i$. **Hence $D=0$ at every positive solution with $\lambda_{\min}(G-D)>\max_{ij}s_{ij}^2-\min_i\sqrt{1+2u_i+a_i^2}$, a negative threshold, in particular wherever $D\preceq\operatorname{diag}(1+2u_i-a_i)$.** Since $r_i^2-g_i^2=\sum_jd_{ij}^2\ge0$, on uniform data this needs less of $\lambda_{\max}(D)$ than attempt 1 (off uniform data neither threshold dominates), and against $\rho(S)$ it runs short in the same regime.
- **At $k=2$ the $d$-sector vanishes, so U holds on $\mathfrak{sp}(2)$'s torus family, and on $\mathfrak{so}(5)$'s, the same algebra** (derived, in Lean, Gate 2 [r1909](REVIEW_r1909.md), CONFIRMED; new to the record, L279 to L281 starting at $\mathfrak{so}(15)$). With one pair the sums over a third index are empty, so the $p$- and $q$-rows are $p(1-X^-)=(u_0+u_1)q$ and $q(1-X^+)=(u_0+u_1)p$, and their difference is $(p-q)\big(1-m+u_0+u_1-\tfrac12(p+q)^2\big)=0$. The long rows give $a_i<\tfrac12$, $\tfrac12(p^2+q^2)<\tfrac12-2u_0^2$ and $pq<u_0$, so the bracket exceeds $u_1+2u_0^2>0$ and $p=q$; [L285](../findings/second-order.md) then gives $u=p=q=\tfrac13$. **The only positive-definite torus-invariant idempotent of $\mathfrak{sp}(2)\cong\mathfrak{so}(5)$ is $I/3$.** **Lean**: `sp2_unique` in [Sp2Torus.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/Sp2Torus.lean), from `TorusRows` at $k=2$. *From $k=3$ the $d$-rows carry $(D^2)_{ij}=\sum_ld_{il}d_{lj}\ne0$ and this argument stops.*
- **So the open regime is $a_i\gg u_i$**: there $\sum_jd_{ij}^2$ nearly equals $\sum_js_{ij}^2$, so on most pairs one of $p_{ij},q_{ij}$ is much smaller than the other, and the solution sits near a face $q=0$ or $p=0$ of the positive cone, where the system degenerates to $\mathfrak u(k)$'s. *A global proof has to control solutions near those faces, and none of the three bounds reaches them.* The Hermitian pair $\mathfrak{sp}(k)\supset\mathfrak u(k)$ contributes no two-weight solution there: $C=\alpha P_{\mathfrak u(k)}+\beta P_{\mathfrak p}$ has diagonal Cartan part, while an idempotent has $A_{ij}=\tfrac12(\beta^2-\alpha^2)$, so $\alpha^2=\beta^2$, and $\alpha=\beta$ at a positive point.

## H on a simple subalgebra asks only for a lower bound on the trace (derived, the scalar core in Lean, Gate 2 [r1896](REVIEW_r1896.md), [r1897](REVIEW_r1897.md))

**Statement.** *Let $\mathfrak h$ be compact simple with $I^\#I=cI$, $d=\dim\mathfrak h$, and $\gamma^*$ the largest of [L265](../findings/second-order.md)'s scalars $c-\mathrm{Cas}_V/4$ over the nontrivial irreducible components $V\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak h$. Every $\#$-idempotent $C$ on $\mathfrak h$ with $\tau=\operatorname{tr}C$ has $\gamma^*\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tau-(c-\gamma^*)\tau^2/d$, and $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tau^2/d$. When $\gamma^*>0$, which holds on every compact simple algebra but $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (there $\gamma^*=-c/2$ and the pairing forces $\tau=d/c$), the first is a lower bound on the norm.* **Proof.** *Pair $C^\#C=C$ with $I$: by symmetry of $F$, $\tau=\langle C,I^\#C\rangle=c\tau^2/d+\sum_V\gamma_V\lVert C_V\rVert^2\le c\tau^2/d+\gamma^*\big(\lVert C\rVert^2-\tau^2/d\big)$, since $I^\#$ acts on $V$ by $\gamma_V$. The second bound is Cauchy–Schwarz.* $\square$ *It is [L259](../findings/second-order.md) (3)'s pairing with $\mathfrak h$'s own scalars, with the inequality taken at the highest nontrivial scalar where L259 eliminates the lowest, which on $\mathfrak g_2$ reaches $\tfrac{36}{49}$ only from $\tau\approx3.40$. It refines [L260](../findings/second-order.md) (4)'s neighbour $\tau\le c\lVert C\rVert^2$, the relative bound whose absolute version L260's closing sentence names as missing ([the class-ceiling note](2026-09-26-I73-class-ceiling.md), batch 33). What it adds is the threshold each algebra needs.* **Lean**: `norm_ge_of_pairing` and `g2_threshold` in [TraceThreshold.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TraceThreshold.lean), the pairing identity entering as a hypothesis as in L259's `idem_norm_ge`.

- **$\mathfrak g_2$** ($c=4$, $d=14$, scalars $\tfrac53$ on $\mathbf{27}$ and $-1$ on $\mathbf{77}$): $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tfrac35\tau-\tfrac1{10}\tau^2$, with equality exactly when $C_{\mathbf{77}}=0$, as at $I/4$ and L263's $SU(3)$ point. **So every $\#$-idempotent on $\mathfrak g_2$ with $\operatorname{tr}C\ge\tfrac{12}7$ has $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tfrac{36}{49}$**: the parabola covers $[\tfrac{12}7,\tfrac{30}7]$ and $\tau^2/14$ the rest. $\tfrac{36}{49}$ is H's $N/(n-2)^2$ at $n=9$, which binds: $\mathfrak g_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)$ keeps the metric and $N/(n-2)^2$ falls with $n$, and a representation of larger Dynkin index $j$ scales the metric by $j$, the idempotent by $j$ and so asks $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/((n-2)^2j^2)$ in this metric. $I/4$ has trace $\tfrac72$, and the other three known points have traces $3.21$ to $3.37$.
- **$\mathfrak{sp}(k)$** ($c=k+1$, $d=k(2k+1)$, and $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak{sp}(k)=[0]\oplus[\omega_2]\oplus[2\omega_2]\oplus[4\omega_1]$ with scalars $k+1$, $\tfrac{k+2}2$, $\tfrac12$, $-1$): $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tfrac2{k+2}\big(\tau-\tfrac{\tau^2}{2(2k+1)}\big)$. In $\mathfrak{so}(4k)$'s metric (padded to $n=9$ at $k=2$) a trace sufficient for H is the smaller root of this bound against $N/(n-2)^2$ (Cauchy–Schwarz covers everything above the larger root, since $\tau_+\ge2k+1$): $\tau_-=1.79,1.91,2.08,2.29,2.53$ at $k=2,\dots,6$, against $d/c=\tfrac{10}3,\tfrac{21}4,\tfrac{36}5,\tfrac{55}6,\tfrac{78}7$, a fraction of $I/c$'s trace falling from $0.54$ to $0.23$.
- **What remains on the torus families is a trace bound.** $\operatorname{tr}C=\operatorname{tr}A+2\sum_\alpha w_\alpha$, and $\operatorname{tr}C\ge\tau_-$ at the stable positive-definite solutions suffices for H there. On $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$ the weight graph gives $\hat W^2\ge\hat W$ entrywise (every factor $f$ is at least $1$, since $p_{ij}^2+q_{ij}^2<1$ makes $\lvert A_{ij}\rvert<\tfrac12$), so its Perron root is at least $1$ and $\operatorname{tr}C$, the sum of $\hat W$'s row sums over one sign, is at least $1$: short of $\tau_-$, because it bounds only the largest row. *And a trace bound has to use positive definiteness*: on $\mathfrak g_2$'s family $w=\tfrac12$ on one long root and $0$ elsewhere solves every row, the long root's $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ idempotent, with trace $\tfrac32<\tfrac{12}7$ (and $\lVert C\rVert^2=\tfrac34$, H holding there on the subalgebra), and its linearisation has no kernel on the family, so it is isolated in the torus family (on all of $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak g_2$ the kernel is the $G_2$-orbit's tangent). ## An absolute trace floor, and H at every embedding of index $j$ with $j^2\ge\tfrac34h^\vee$, $\mathfrak f_4$, $\mathfrak e_6$, $\mathfrak e_7$, $\mathfrak e_8$ among them (derived, in Lean, Gate 2 [r1910](REVIEW_r1910.md), [r1911](REVIEW_r1911.md), numbered [L287](../findings/second-order.md))

**Statement.** *(1) Every nonzero positive-semidefinite $\#$-idempotent $C$ on a compact simple $\mathfrak h$, in the metric where $I^\#I=cI$, has $\operatorname{tr}C\ge h^\vee/c$ and $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge h^\vee/c^2$. (2) In an embedding $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)$ of Dynkin index $j$ (the adjoint's being $h^\vee$), $c=h^\vee/j$ and $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge j^2/h^\vee$. So where $j^2\ge\tfrac34h^\vee$ and $n\ge9$, every positive-definite $\#$-idempotent on $\mathfrak h$ has $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$, and H asks nothing of $\mathfrak h$, on or off the torus. This covers $\mathfrak f_4,\mathfrak e_6,\mathfrak e_7,\mathfrak e_8$ in every embedding, $\mathfrak g_2$ in every embedding with $j\ge2$, and every $\mathfrak h$ in its adjoint embedding.*

**Proof.** *(1) Write $C=\sum_i\lambda_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$, $\lambda_i\ge0$ on an orthonormal eigenbasis of $\mathfrak h$. Since $(vv^{\mathsf T})^\#(ww^{\mathsf T})=\tfrac12[v,w][v,w]^{\mathsf T}$, the diagonal of $C^\#C=C$ at $u_m$ is $\lambda_m=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}c_{ijm}\lambda_i\lambda_j$ with $c_{ijm}=\langle[u_i,u_j],u_m\rangle^2$, and $\sum_ic_{ijm}=\lVert[u_j,u_m]\rVert^2$ by invariance. Take $\lambda_m$ the largest, positive as $C\ne0$: $\lambda_i\le\lambda_m$ gives $2\le\sum_j\lambda_j\lVert[u_j,u_m]\rVert^2\le K\operatorname{tr}C$ with $K=\max_{\lvert x\rvert=\lvert y\rvert=1}\lVert[x,y]\rVert^2=\max_{\lvert x\rvert=1}\lVert\mathrm{ad}_x\rVert^2=\lvert\theta\rvert^2$, the longest root's squared length (every $x$ is conjugate into a Cartan, where $\mathrm{ad}_x$ has eigenvalues $\pm i\alpha(x)$). With $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{ad}_x^{\mathsf T}\mathrm{ad}_x)=2c\lvert x\rvert^2$ ([L260](../findings/second-order.md)'s $\sum_{i,j}c_{ijk}=2c$) and $\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\alpha(x)^2=h^\vee\lvert\theta\rvert^2\lvert x\rvert^2$, $\lvert\theta\rvert^2=2c/h^\vee$. Pairing $C^\#C=C$ with $I$ gives $\operatorname{tr}C=\langle I^\#C,C\rangle\le c\lVert C\rVert^2$, since $I^\#$ acts on $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak h$ by [L265](../findings/second-order.md)'s scalars $c-\mathrm{Cas}_V/4\le c$, so $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge h^\vee/c^2$. (2) Through a real representation of index $j$, $c=h^\vee/j$, so $h^\vee/c^2=j^2/h^\vee\ge\tfrac34$, while $N/(n-2)^2<\tfrac34$ at $n\ge9$. The index is additive over summands, so every embedding of $\mathfrak h$ has $j$ at least its smallest nontrivial real representation's: $\mathfrak f_4$'s $26$ ($j=3$, $h^\vee=9$), $\mathfrak e_6$'s $27\oplus\overline{27}$ ($6$, $12$), $\mathfrak e_7$'s quaternionic $56$ taken twice ($12$, $18$), $\mathfrak e_8$'s $248$ ($30$, $30$). $\mathfrak g_2$'s $7$ is its only representation of index $1$ against $h^\vee=4$, so every embedding other than $7$ plus trivial summands has $j\ge2$ and passes. The adjoint has $j=h^\vee$.* $\square$ **Lean**: `trace_floor`, `trace_ge`, `norm_floor`, `index_form`, `requirement_lt`, `index_H`, `exceptional_pass` in [ExceptionalH.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/ExceptionalH.lean). The eigenbasis identity, $\lvert\theta\rvert^2=2c/h^\vee$, the pairing $\operatorname{tr}C\le c\lVert C\rVert^2$ and the indices enter as hypotheses.

- **The floor is weak for U and strong for H.** $h^\vee/c$ against $I/c$'s $d/c$ is a fraction $h^\vee/d$ ($\tfrac2{7}$ on $\mathfrak g_2$, about $\tfrac1{2k}$ on $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$), so it is no substitute for the absolute lower bound $\operatorname{tr}C\ge d/c$ that U needs ([L260](../findings/second-order.md) (4)'s obstacle). But H needs far less on the heavy algebras.
- **Index one is where it stops.** $\mathfrak g_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)$ has $j^2/h^\vee=\tfrac14$ (its torus family is [L286](../findings/second-order.md)), and the standard $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$, $\mathfrak{su}(k)$, $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ have $j=1$ against $h^\vee=k+1,k,k-2$. Doubling an embedding multiplies $j^2$ by four, so $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$ on $\mathbb H^k\oplus\mathbb H^k$ passes for $k\le4$.
- **A support restriction.** With [L266](../findings/second-order.md) (2), the floor caps the complementary Casimirs of a proper simple support: $\mathrm{Cas}_{\mathfrak h}(V)\le dc/h^\vee$ for every $\mathfrak h$-invariant irreducible $V\subseteq\mathfrak h^\perp$ ([r1911](REVIEW_r1911.md)).
- **Gate 2.** [r1910](REVIEW_r1910.md) and [r1911](REVIEW_r1911.md), independently, CONFIRMED both parts and found that every $\mathfrak g_2$ embedding with $j\ge2$ passes, which the statement now carries. [r1911](REVIEW_r1911.md) gave the direct route through $\operatorname{tr}C\le c\lVert C\rVert^2$, which replaced an assembly through [L286](../findings/second-order.md) (1)'s pairing bound, its concavity in the trace and [L256](../findings/second-order.md).

## H asks nothing more of $\mathfrak g_2$'s torus family: every positive solution has trace at least $2$ (derived, in Lean, Gate 2 [r1899](REVIEW_r1899.md), [r1900](REVIEW_r1900.md), numbered [L286](../findings/second-order.md))

**Statement.** *Every positive-definite torus-invariant idempotent $C$ of $\mathfrak g_2$ has $\operatorname{tr}C\ge2$, hence $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tfrac45>\tfrac{36}{49}$ by the section above: the concave parabola is at least $\tfrac45$ on $[2,4]$, its value at both ends, and $\tau^2/14>\tfrac45$ beyond.* **Proof.** *Write $x_i$ on the long roots $L_i$ and $y_i$ on the short roots $S_i$, with $L_i\perp S_i$. The short rows are $y_i(1-\sigma_i)=x_ky_j+x_jy_k+\tfrac43y_jy_k$ with $\sigma_i=x_j^2+x_k^2+\tfrac49y_i^2+\tfrac19(y_j^2+y_k^2)$ (the two long roots not orthogonal to $S_i$ meet it with $(\alpha\cdot\gamma)^2=1$, and short roots with $\tfrac49$ and $\tfrac19$). Take $i$ with $y_i$ the largest short weight. Then $x_ky_j+x_jy_k+\tfrac43y_jy_k\le y_i\big(x_j+x_k+\tfrac23(y_j+y_k)\big)$, so dividing by $y_i>0$,*
$$G_i:=x_j+x_k+\tfrac23(y_j+y_k)+\sigma_i\ \ge\ 1 .$$
*Now $\operatorname{tr}C=\operatorname{tr}A+2\sum_\alpha w_\alpha=\sum_\alpha\big(2w_\alpha+\lvert\alpha\rvert^2w_\alpha^2\big)$, and each weight's term is at least twice its part of $G_i$: $2x+2x^2$ against $x+x^2$ for $x_j,x_k$; $2y+\tfrac23y^2$ against $\tfrac23y+\tfrac19y^2$ for $y_j,y_k$; $2y_i+\tfrac23y_i^2$ against $\tfrac49y_i^2$, which needs $y_i\le9$ and holds since $\tfrac49y_i^2<1$ (the row's right side is positive). So $\operatorname{tr}C\ge2G_i\ge2$.* $\square$ **Lean**: `row_bound`, `weight_lt`, `trace_of_row` and `g2_torus_trace` in [G2TorusTrace.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/G2TorusTrace.lean) from the three short rows, and `g2_torus_H` closing on `g2_threshold`.

- **It uses the short rows only, and positivity of the short weights.** The long-root $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ point, with every short weight $0$, has trace $\tfrac32$ and falls outside it, as it must.
- **What it settles.** With $\tfrac{36}{49}$ binding for $\mathfrak g_2$ at every $n\ge9$, no positive-definite torus idempotent of $\mathfrak g_2$ is light enough to matter to H, whatever its stabiliser: **the trivial-stabiliser type needs no enumeration for H.** U$_{\mathrm s}$ in its sharp form ($\lVert C\rVert^2\ge\tfrac78$) stays open there, and H does not ask it.
- **On $\mathfrak{sp}(k)$ the same argument runs on the $p$- and $q$-rows**, which are linear in $(p,q)$ with coefficients $u_i+u_j$ and the other short weights. The largest short weight's row gives $G\ge1$ with $G=(u_i+u_j)+\tfrac14\sum_{l\ne i,j}(p_{il}+p_{jl}+q_{il}+q_{jl})+X^\mp_{ij}$, where $X^-_{ij}=u_i^2+u_j^2+p_{ij}^2+\tfrac14\sum_{l\ne i,j}(p_{il}^2+p_{jl}^2+q_{il}^2+q_{jl}^2)$ and $X^+_{ij}$ has $q_{ij}^2$ in place of $p_{ij}^2$, every weight again carries at least twice its part in the trace, and $\operatorname{tr}C\ge2$. **That exceeds $\tau_-$ at $k=2,3$ ($1.79$, $1.91$), so H asks nothing more of $\mathfrak{sp}(2)$'s and $\mathfrak{sp}(3)$'s torus families**, and falls short from $k=4$ ($\tau_-=2.08$), where one row is not enough. *The trace bound is `sp_trace_ge_two` in [SpTorusTrace.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/SpTorusTrace.lean); the threshold comparison is prose.*
