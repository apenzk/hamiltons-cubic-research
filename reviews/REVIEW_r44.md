### Reviewers 44 and 45: the padded degenerate point, and what it does not prove

**They agree on all six verdicts, including refuting the cycle's headline.** Own implementations in [review_r44_core.py](scripts/review_r44_core.py) with nine drivers and [review_r45_core.py](scripts/review_r45_core.py) with five; nothing under review imported. Reports in [review_r44_report.md](agent_logs/review_r44_report.md) and [review_r45_report.md](agent_logs/review_r45_report.md).

**P1–P4 CONFIRMED, and criticality upgraded from measurement to theorem.** The padded operator is *already inside* $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ before any projection, Ricci $5.6\times10^{-17}$, first Bianchi **exactly $0.0$**, unit norm, so the projector question that the brief worried about is moot: nothing is removed and no normalisation can be wrong. And $Q$ commutes with padding bit for bit, $\max|Q_n(\mathrm{pad}\,W)-\mathrm{pad}\,Q_6(W)|=0.0$ at $n=8,10,12,14$, because $[\Lambda^2\mathbb R^6,\Lambda^2\mathbb R^6]\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^6$ forces $W^\#$ back into the block. **So any critical point pads to a critical point with the same $\theta$ and the same $S$**, a general statement, where the brief offered four measurements. $\theta=\sqrt{10}/3$ exactly, $S$ is exactly $0$ (the $10^{-28}$ figures were an artefact of building $\mathfrak m$ from numerical eigenvectors), and the excess is $9$ at $n=6\ldots14$ including odd $n$, lying wholly inside the padded block (block mass $1.000000000000$) as the Weyl projection of $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^3)\otimes\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^3)$.

**P5 REFUTED by both, for the same reason: it is a non-sequitur.** Degeneracy removes the *sufficient* condition for local isolation; it does not produce a nearby $S>0$ critical point, which is what non-openness requires. A degenerate orbit can still be isolated at third order, and that is exactly what happens here. Both reviewers measured it independently: the third-order scalar $\Psi_1=-0.592927061$ is the same nonzero constant at $n=6,8,10$, reproducing this project's own $n=6$ value from independent code; and gauge-free correctors pushed off the excess directions, 48 transverse starts in one audit, 262 pushed to $t=0.4$ in the other, **all return to the starting orbit**, with $S\le2.4\times10^{-23}$ and $\theta$ unchanged to twelve digits. **So $\{S=0\}$ appears open at precisely the point offered as the reason it is not.** What the degeneracy costs route B is the clean mechanism, not the conclusion.

**P6 PLAUSIBLE, with the margin thinner than claimed and the counterexample set larger.** Padding does not preserve nondegeneracy in general: the whole $S^2\times S^k$ family is nondegenerate natively and degenerate at every larger $n$, with excess growing linearly ($4(n-4)$ for $S^2\times S^2$, reaching $40$ at $n=14$). Block mass separates the two mechanisms cleanly, $S^3\times S^3$'s excess is $100\%$ inside the block, $S^2\times S^k$'s is $100\%$ outside. The good news is real though: padding does **not** degenerate balanced products with $k\ge4$, and $S^5\times S^5$ padded $10\to12$ at $\theta=1.2$ stays nondegenerate. Had that failed, $\theta_0(n-2)$ would sit degenerately just under $\theta_0(n)$ and the window would close. Instead the degenerate families sit frozen while $\theta_0(n)\uparrow\sqrt2$.

**One disagreement, settled against the reviewer who raised the tighter bound.** One audit reported every degenerate $S=0$ point at $\theta\le\sqrt{10}/3=1.054093$; the other reported the record's highest at $\sqrt{6/5}=1.095445$. Checked here directly: the record's own table has the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ Kähler point at $n=6$, $\theta=1.095445$, nullity/orbit $16/11$, excess $5$. The higher value is correct, giving a safe window of $0.050$, $0.105$, $0.141$ at $n=8,10,12$, widening with $n$, and matching the second audit's figures.

**The cycle rediscovered an existing finding.** [findings/INDEX.md](../findings/INDEX.md) already records "every graded critical point is Morse–Bott nondegenerate" as **false, proven over $\mathbb Q$**, with a table of degenerate graded points whose $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ Kähler row, $n=8$, $\theta=1.054093$, excess $9$, *is* the padded $S^3\times S^3$ point this cycle presented as new. One audit confirmed they share the full $W$- and $L_e$-spectra, while noting they are different orbits, since padding to $n=10$ gives orbit $33$ against $37$. The genuinely new content is the padding theorem and the third-order isolation, not the degeneracy.
