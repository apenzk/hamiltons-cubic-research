## 2. Target 2, the equation on association schemes: unwritten, and one page of analysis says what it is

No paper poses $S\circ S+S^2=\theta S$ on a Bose–Mesner algebra; OpenAlex full-text and title searches on the relevant vocabulary return nothing, and DGK's acknowledgement is the only trace of the idea. What the equation becomes there is short enough to record here.

Let $A_0=I,A_1,\dots,A_d$ be a symmetric $d$-class association scheme on $n$ points with valencies $k_i$ and intersection numbers $A_iA_l=\sum_jp^j_{il}A_j$. A hollow $S$ in the Bose–Mesner algebra is $S=\sum_{i=1}^dx_iA_i$, and:

- $SJ=0$ reads $\sum_ik_ix_i=0$;
- $S\circ S=\sum_ix_i^2A_i$ because the $A_i$ are disjoint $0/1$ matrices;
- $S^2=\sum_{i,l}x_ix_l\sum_jp^j_{il}A_j$, whose $A_0$ coefficient is $\sum_ik_ix_i^2$ (as $p^0_{il}=k_i\delta_{il}$), which is DGK's diagonal $D=\big(\sum_kS_{ik}^2\big)I$ automatically.

So DGK's equation (1) on the scheme is the system
$$x_j^2+\sum_{i,l=1}^dp^j_{il}\,x_ix_l=\theta x_j\quad(j=1,\dots,d),\qquad\sum_{i=1}^dk_ix_i=0,$$
$d$ quadratics in $d$ unknowns and $\theta$, with one linear constraint and one scale. At $d=2$ this is DGK Theorem 5.1: $x_1=z+y$, $x_2=y$, the row-sum constraint is $y/z=k/(1-n)$, and the two quadratics collapse to $\theta=\lambda-\mu+1$ at $z=1$. For $d\ge3$ the system is projectively zero-dimensional for generic intersection numbers, so **each scheme carries finitely many diagonal critical points up to scale, computable from its intersection numbers alone, PLAUSIBLE as a generic statement, and a theorem for $d=2$.** DGK's Example 6.4 is the $d=3$ case of the scheme of $\mathbb Z/\ell\times\mathbb Z/m$ with classes "same row", "same column", "neither", and its three solutions are the three points of that zero-dimensional system. This is the analytical content of target 2; the record can use it as the frame for any future diagonal family without a computation.

## 4. Target 4, critical points of $Q$: one paper poses the problem, one proves the lemma

**[Bhattacharya 2013](../references/bhattacharya-2013-curvature-ode/notes.md)** studies the zero set of $\tilde Q(R)=Q(R)-\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(R)\rVert^2R$ on $\{s=1\}$. For $R=cI+W$ Einstein, $\tilde Q(R)=0$ is exactly $Q(W)=\tfrac1nW$ at $c=1/(n(n-1))$, so an Einstein zero of $\tilde Q$ is a $\theta$-critical Weyl tensor with $\theta\lVert W\rVert=1/n$. The paper states on p. 3 that the higher-dimensional classification is open, classifies $n=4$ (Proposition 4.5: $S^4$, $S^2\times S^2$, $\mathbb{CP}^2$, i.e. $\theta$-critical points $0$, $W_{S^2\times S^2}$ at $\sqrt3/2$, $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $\sqrt{3/2}$, matching the record's $n=4$ table), proves the converse of Lemma L46 on closed Einstein manifolds (Proposition 4.1), settles the $W=0$ sector in every dimension (Theorem 4.6), and shows products of symmetric Einstein factors are critical iff Einstein (Proposition 4.2). It states outright why its method stops at $n=4$: above $n=4$ an eigenbasis of $R$ need not diagonalise $Q(R)$. Zero citations; DGK do not cite it.

**[Richard–Seshadri 2013](../references/richard-seshadri-2013-noncoercive-cones/notes.md)** proves Lemma L46 as Lemma 4.1 and, in Proposition 4.3, writes the ODE solution through $R=cI+W$ critical explicitly: the Weyl part moves along the ray $W/(1-\theta t)$. That is the mechanism behind DGK's sentence that their solutions are "obstructions to an evolution towards constant positive sectional curvature", which DGK assert without proof. Corollary 0.3 then says every invariant cone finer than $\{s\ge0\}$ has each symmetric-space $W$ on its boundary, consistent with the cone reading of the conjecture in [problem.md](../findings/problem.md).

**Checked and set aside.** Gururaja–Maity–Seshadri ([1101.5884](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.5884)) is about Wilking's cones $C(S)$ (contained in the isotropic-curvature cone, with a connected-sum/convergence dichotomy) and has no critical-point content beyond what [wilking-lie-algebraic-cones](../references/wilking-lie-algebraic-cones/) already gives. Ni–Wu ([math/0607356](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607356)) is a global splitting theorem for nonnegative curvature operator. No arXiv abstract combines "algebraic curvature operator", "Ricci flow" and "eigenvector"; Fox's nonassociative-algebra paper, already filed, is the only hit for "Weyl tensor" + "Ricci flow" + "algebraic".

## 5. Target 5, the hull condition has a name

**In the Lie-algebra literature it is moment-map convexity.** [Lauret–Will](../references/lauret-will-2006-einstein-solvmanifolds-moment-map/notes.md) Proposition 3.2(i): for any bracket $\mu=\sum\mu^k_{ij}v_{ijk}$, the diagonal part of the normalised Ricci operator is $\tfrac2{\lVert\mu\rVert^2}\sum(\mu^k_{ij})^2\alpha^k_{ij}$, a convex combination of the torus weights $\alpha^k_{ij}=E_{kk}-E_{ii}-E_{jj}$ with the squared structure constants as weights, hence in $\mathrm{CH}\{\alpha^k_{ij}:\mu^k_{ij}\ne0\}$; the closest point $\beta_\mu$ of that hull gives the sharp bound $\lVert m(\mu)\rVert^2\ge\lVert\beta_\mu\rVert^2$, attained exactly at the Einstein points (Proposition 3.4). Proposition 3.3 identifies Payne's linear system $U[c]=\nu[1]$, which the record holds from [Payne](../references/payne-2010-soliton-metrics/notes.md) and [Nikolayevsky](../references/nikolayevsky-2011-pre-einstein-derivation/notes.md), as the Lagrange condition for that closest point. This is Kirwan–Ness convexity, proved over $\mathbb R$ by Marian. The record's row measure $\mu_a=T_{a\bullet\bullet}/2(n-2)$ with the pinned pair $(\mathbb E[X],\mathbb E[Y])$ ([r337](REVIEW_r337.md), [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md)) is the same construction with one measure per eigenvector and two scalar moments; the difference is that Lauret's group acts on the bracket at a fixed metric while the record fixes $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ and varies $W$, so the group action whose moment map is the record's per-row system is owed before a theorem from this frame is cited as covering $\mathcal W_n$.

**In classical analysis it is the truncated moment problem.** "Two moments pinned, a third bounded, extremal measures atomic on the hull boundary" is the Chebyshev–Markov inequality over a Tchebycheff system (Karlin–Studden, *Tchebycheff Systems*, 1966, Chs. II–IV; Krein–Nudelman, *The Markov Moment Problem*, 1977). The $\Pi$-bound's parabola-and-chord argument in [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) is the two-point case of it. Pointer only: neither book is on arXiv and no folder was filed.

**What this changes for I24.** Nothing in grade. It supplies the name and a template: the hull condition is sharp for the relaxed problem (every hull point is some probability measure), so what cuts further is realisability of the measure by $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ brackets, the same split [LIT-spectra](LIT-spectra.md) F2 found in the nilsoliton analogue.
