## 1. Item 1, the subalgebra and the rotations

The proof step by step.

- **"The fixed space of $\ker(\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k)$."** $\Gamma$ is elementary abelian of exponent $2$, so the characters trivial on $H=\ker(\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k)$ are exactly the annihilator of $H$, which is $\{1,\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k\}$. By L120 (Given 1) the $\Gamma$-isotypic summands of $\mathcal W_n$ carry pairwise distinct characters under the full sign group, the trivial one on $\mathcal D_n$ and $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$ on $V_{jk}$. So $\mathrm{Fix}(H)=\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$. This follows from L120 and needs the full sign group, which the lemma inherits from Given 1.
- **The four-set witness.** $Q\setminus\{j,k\}$ has at least two elements. A single flip at $i\notin\{j,k\}$ has $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k=1$, so it lies in $H$, and $\chi_Q=-1$ on it because $i\in Q$. Follows.
- **The pair witness.** $\{a,b\}\ne\{j,k\}$ and both have two elements, so $\{a,b\}\setminus\{j,k\}$ is nonempty. The flip at an index $i$ of it lies in $H$ and gives $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b=-1$, since exactly one of $a,b$ equals $i$. Follows. The alternative witness $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$ with $c\notin\{a,b,j,k\}$ is correct when $a\notin\{j,k\}$ and is redundant, the single flip always sufficing.
- **Subalgebra.** $Q$ is $O(n)$-equivariant, so its polarisation is $\Gamma$-equivariant, and the fixed space of any subgroup is closed under $\circ$. The proof leaves this implicit. It is standard and follows.
- **Rotations.** $H=\{\varepsilon:\varepsilon_j=\varepsilon_k\}$ is generated by the single flips outside $\{j,k\}$ and the double flip at $j,k$, which is $-I$ on the $(j,k)$-plane. A rotation $g$ in that plane commutes with each generator, so $h\cdot(g\cdot W)=g\cdot(h\cdot W)=g\cdot W$ for $W\in\mathrm{Fix}(H)$. The proof names the generators without saying they generate $H$. They do, so it follows.

A consequence worth recording beside the item: because $Q(\mathcal S_{jk})\subset\mathcal S_{jk}$, a critical point of $P$ restricted to the sphere of $\mathcal S_{jk}$ satisfies $Q(W)=\theta W$ in $\mathcal W_n$ outright, so items 3 and 5 hold whichever notion of critical point the reader brings.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 2. Item 2, $\nu\circ\nu=G_x$

**The sharp formula and its range.** The written formula $(R^\#)_{ab,ab}=\sum_{c,c'}[R_{ac,ac'}R_{bc,bc'}-R_{ac,bc'}R_{bc,ac'}]$ does not state the range of $c,c'$. The range is immaterial: if $c\in\{a,b\}$ or $c'\in\{a,b\}$, one factor of each term carries a repeated index ($R_{aa,\cdot}$, $R_{bb,\cdot}$, $R_{\cdot,aa}$ or $R_{\cdot,bb}$) and vanishes. I derived the formula from the Lie-algebraic definition $R^\#=\mathrm{ad}\circ(R\wedge R)\circ\mathrm{ad}^*$ to check its shape and sign. With $E_{pq}=e_pe_q^T-e_qe_p^T$ one has $[E_{ac},E_{cb}]=E_{ab}$ uniformly in $c\notin\{a,b\}$, so $\mathrm{ad}^*e_{ab}$ is proportional to $\sum_ce_{ac}\wedge e_{cb}$. Pairing with $\langle X\wedge Y,Z\wedge V\rangle=\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,V\rangle-\langle X,V\rangle\langle Y,Z\rangle$ gives $\sum_{c,c'}[R_{ac,ac'}R_{cb,c'b}-R_{ac,c'b}R_{cb,ac'}]$, and the orientation flips $R_{cb,c'b}=R_{bc,bc'}$, $R_{ac,c'b}R_{cb,ac'}=R_{ac,bc'}R_{bc,ac'}$ give the record's formula up to one overall constant. On a diagonal tensor the first term survives only at $c=c'$ and the second never (it needs $\{a,c\}=\{b,c'\}$ with $c\ne b$), giving $\sum_cK_{ac}K_{cb}$. That matches Given 2's diagonal product at $A=B$, which fixes the constant at $1$. The step follows.

**The entries of $\nu$.** $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2\lVert x\rVert^2$ fixes the convention $\nu_{ja,ka}=\nu_{ka,ja}=x_a$ and no other nonzero entry. The nonzero entries pair $\{j,\ell\}$ with $\{k,\ell\}$, $\ell\notin\{j,k\}$, and the value is unchanged by reversing both orientations.

- $(\nu^2)_{ja,ja}=\nu_{ja,ka}^2=x_a^2$, likewise at $ka$. $\nu^2$ is zero at $jk$ and $ab$ and off the diagonal among these. Follows.
- $(\nu^\#)_{ab,ab}$: the first term needs $\{a,c\},\{a,c'\}$ to be $\{\ell,j\},\{\ell,k\}$, so $\ell=a$ and $\{c,c'\}=\{j,k\}$ in either order, each order giving $\nu_{aj,ak}\nu_{bj,bk}=x_ax_b$. The second term needs $\{a,c\},\{b,c'\}$ to share their $\ell$, so $c=c'=\ell$ and $\{a,b\}=\{j,k\}$, impossible. Total $2x_ax_b$. Follows.
- $(\nu^\#)_{jk,jk}$: the first term has factors $\nu_{jc,jc'}$ pairing two sets that share $j$, never of the form $\{\ell,j\},\{\ell,k\}$, so it vanishes. The second term needs $c=c'=a$ and gives $-\nu_{ja,ka}\nu_{ka,ja}=-x_a^2$. Total $-\lVert x\rVert^2$. Follows, and this is the sign that matters.
- $(\nu^\#)_{ja,ja}$: the first term vanishes as above. In the second, $\nu_{jc,ac'}\ne0$ forces $c=a$, $c'=k$, and then the partner $\nu_{aa,jk}$ vanishes. Zero, likewise at $ka$. Follows.
- **Off-diagonal entries.** $\nu\otimes\nu$ carries the character $(\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k)^2=1$, $Q$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, and an entry $e_{pq}\odot e_{rs}$ has trivial character only if each index occurs an even number of times, which forces $\{p,q\}=\{r,s\}$. Follows.

**Two independent checks on the sign of $K_{jk}$.** First, $Q$ maps $\mathcal W_n$ into itself (Given 2, L209), so $G_x$ must have zero row sums. Row $j$ is $K_{jk}+\sum_aK_{ja}=-\lVert x\rVert^2+\lVert x\rVert^2=0$, and row $a$ is $2x_a^2+2x_a\sum_{b\ne a}x_b=0$ by $\sum x=0$. With the opposite sign on either term of the sharp formula, row $j$ would be $2\lVert x\rVert^2$ or row $a$ would be $4x_a^2$. Second, by total symmetry $\langle M,G_x\rangle=\langle L_M\nu,\nu\rangle$ for diagonal $M$, and Lemma L122 gives the pair block as $\tfrac12[\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(M_{ji}+M_{ki})-M_{jk}\mathrm{Id}+M^{(jk)}]$ in the basis $f_i$ of norm $\sqrt2$, so $\langle L_M\nu,\nu\rangle=\sum_ax_a^2(M_{ja}+M_{ka})-M_{jk}\lVert x\rVert^2+\sum_{a\ne b}M_{ab}x_ax_b$. Reading off coefficients returns exactly $K_{ja}=K_{ka}=x_a^2$, $K_{jk}=-\lVert x\rVert^2$, $K_{ab}=2x_ax_b$. So item 2 is L122's bracket read as a representer, and the record should cite L122 there.

**Norm over unordered pairs.** $2(n-2)$ entries $x_a^2$ give $2\sum x^4$, the $jk$ entry gives $\lVert x\rVert^4$, and $\sum_{a<b}4x_a^2x_b^2=2(\lVert x\rVert^4-\sum x^4)$. The total is $3\lVert x\rVert^4$, and $\lVert G_x\rVert=\sqrt3\lVert x\rVert^2=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. Follows, and the Lean `G_norm_sq` covers the polynomial identity.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 3. Item 3, the pinning and the odd equation

- **"$V_{jk}$'s equation is $L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$."** $Q(D+\nu)=D\circ D+2D\circ\nu+G_x$. By L120 at the $\Gamma$-fixed $D$, $D\circ D\in\mathcal D_n$ and $D\circ\nu\in V_{jk}$, and $G_x\in\mathcal D_n$ by item 2. Projecting $Q(W)=\theta W$ onto $V_{jk}$ gives $2D\circ\nu=\theta\nu$. Follows from L120 and item 2, which the proof uses without naming L120.
- **"$\langle L_D\nu,\nu\rangle=\langle D,\nu\circ\nu\rangle$."** Total symmetry of $\langle X\circ Y,Z\rangle$, which the record uses throughout (L214). Follows, giving $\langle D,G_x\rangle=\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, and division by $\lVert G_x\rVert=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2\ne0$ gives $\theta/\sqrt3$. The unit hypothesis is unused beyond fixing what $\theta$ means.
- **"The $\mathcal D_n$ equation is $D\circ D+G_x=\theta D$."** The complementary projection. Follows.
- **"$G_x$ is swap-even."** $\sigma$ acts on a diagonal tensor by permuting the indices of $K$ (the sign of $\sigma e_{jk}=-e_{jk}$ cancels in a diagonal entry), and $G_x$ is invariant under $j\leftrightarrow k$. Follows. $\sigma$ preserves $\mathcal D_n$, so $D^\pm=\tfrac12(D\pm\sigma D)\in\mathcal D_n$, and the odd part has $K_{ka}=-K_{ja}$, $K_{jk}=K_{ab}=0$, as stated.
- **"$(D\circ D)^-=2D^+\circ D^-$."** $\sigma$-equivariance of $\circ$ makes $D^+\circ D^+$ and $D^-\circ D^-$ even and $D^+\circ D^-$ odd. Taking odd parts of the $\mathcal D_n$ equation gives $2D^+\circ D^-=\theta D^-$. Follows.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 4. Item 4, the odd action of $L_{G_x}$

- **The identification.** An odd $Y\in\mathcal D_n$ has $Y_{ja}=y_a$, $Y_{ka}=-y_a$ and no other entry. Its Ricci rows are row $j$: $\sum_ay_a$, row $k$: $-\sum_ay_a$, row $a$: $y_a-y_a=0$. So the odd part is exactly $\{y:\sum y=0\}$, of dimension $n-3$. Follows.
- **$(G\circ Y)_{ja}$.** $G_{ja}Y_{ja}=x_a^2y_a$. In $\tfrac12\sum_c(G_{jc}Y_{ca}+Y_{jc}G_{ca})$ the index $c=k$ gives $G_{jk}Y_{ka}+Y_{jk}G_{ka}=(-\lVert x\rVert^2)(-y_a)+0=\lVert x\rVert^2y_a$, and $c=b\notin\{j,k,a\}$ gives $G_{jb}Y_{ba}+Y_{jb}G_{ba}=0+y_b\cdot2x_bx_a$, summing to $2x_a(\langle x,y\rangle-x_ay_a)$. The stated line and its simplification to $\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2y_a+x_a\langle x,y\rangle$ follow.
- **The even part vanishes.** $G$ is even and $Y$ odd, so $G\circ Y$ is odd by equivariance. I also checked the $ab$ entry directly: $\tfrac12(G_{aj}Y_{jb}+G_{ak}Y_{kb}+Y_{aj}G_{jb}+Y_{ak}G_{kb})=\tfrac12(x_a^2y_b-x_a^2y_b+y_ax_b^2-y_ax_b^2)=0$. Follows.
- **Invariance of the target.** $x\perp\mathbf1$ makes $y\mapsto x\langle x,y\rangle$ preserve $\{y:\sum y=0\}$. Implicit and correct.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 5. Item 5, the commutators and the minimising angle

- **"$\Lambda^2\xi$ sends $e_{ja}\mapsto e_{ka}$, $e_{ka}\mapsto-e_{ja}$."** This is the convention $\xi e_j=e_k$, $\xi e_k=-e_j$, and it also kills $e_{jk}$ and $e_{ab}$. The opposite convention for $E_{jk}$ flips the sign of $\varphi$ and changes no conclusion. Follows.
- **"$\xi\cdot W=[\Lambda^2\xi,W]$."** The derivative of conjugation. Follows. Write $A=\Lambda^2\xi$, which is antisymmetric.
- **$[A,D]$.** $[A,D]e_{ja}=K_{ja}e_{ka}-K_{ka}e_{ka}$ and $[A,D]e_{ka}=-K_{ka}e_{ja}+K_{ja}e_{ja}$, so the entries are $K_{ja}-K_{ka}$ at $(ka,ja)$ and $(ja,ka)$. Their sum is $(\text{row }j-K_{jk})-(\text{row }k-K_{jk})=0$, so the element is in $V_{jk}$. Follows.
- **$[A,\nu]$.** $[A,\nu]e_{ja}=A(x_ae_{ka})-\nu(e_{ka})=-2x_ae_{ja}$ and $[A,\nu]e_{ka}=A(x_ae_{ja})+\nu(e_{ja})=2x_ae_{ka}$. Diagonal with $\mp2x_a$, zero row sums, norm$^2$ $8\lVert x\rVert^2=4\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. Follows. It is the odd element with $y=-2x$.
- **$\xi^2$ preserves each summand.** The two computations hold for every $D\in\mathcal D_n$ and every $\nu\in V_{jk}$, so $\mathrm{ad}_A$ maps $\mathcal D_n\to V_{jk}$ and $V_{jk}\to\mathcal D_n$, and $\mathrm{ad}_A^2$ preserves both. The $V_{jk}$-part of $e^{\varphi\,\mathrm{ad}_A}W$ is the sum of the odd powers on $D$ and the even powers on $\nu$, which is the stated expansion. Follows.
- **The sign.** For symmetric $X,Y$ and antisymmetric $A$, $[A,X]$ is symmetric and $\langle X,[A,Y]\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XAY)-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XYA)=-\langle[A,X],Y\rangle$. So $\langle\nu,\xi^2\cdot\nu\rangle=-\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2$, and the $\varphi^2$ coefficient $\lVert\xi\cdot D\rVert^2-\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2$ follows.
- **"A minimum gives both conditions."** A minimum over the circle exists by compactness and is interior. Re-basing at the minimiser (item 1 keeps the rotated tensor in $\mathcal S_{jk}$), $f'(0)=0$ gives $2\sum_ax_a(K_{ja}-K_{ka})=0$, and $f''(0)\ge0$ gives $4\lVert\nu\rVert^2\le2\sum_a(K_{ja}-K_{ka})^2$. Both follow, with $K$ and $x$ those of the rotated tensor. Criticality is unused here.

**Repair, notational.** $\langle x,K_{j\cdot}-K_{k\cdot}\rangle$ and $\sum_a(K_{ka}-K_{ja})^2$ must run over $a\notin\{j,k\}$. Read over all $a$, the rows contribute $K_{jk}$ and $-K_{jk}$ at the extra indices and the sum gains $2K_{jk}^2$. The statement fixes the range only through the definition of $\nu$ and should repeat it. The item also rests on the prior fact that the orbit direction is a $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvector ([diagonal-half.md](../findings/diagonal-half.md) line 151, [fence-posts.md](../findings/fence-posts.md) line 492), which the note cites as F37 and the lemma does not.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 6. Item 6, the pairing

- **"Pair $Q(D)=\theta D$ with $G_x$."** $\theta\langle D,G_x\rangle=\langle D\circ D,G_x\rangle$. Follows.
- **"Use total symmetry."** $\langle D\circ D,G_x\rangle=\langle G_x\circ D,D\rangle=\langle L_{G_x}D,D\rangle$. Follows.

**Repair, notational.** The statement writes $\langle K,G_x\rangle$, a matrix against a tensor. The identity proved is for $\langle D,G_x\rangle$, which equals $\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}(G_x)_{ab}$ over unordered pairs. Over ordered pairs the left side doubles, which is the factor of two [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) exists to catch, so the statement should say "over unordered pairs" or write $\langle D,G_x\rangle$. The hypotheses "unit" on $D$ and on $x$ are unused: the identity is homogeneous of degree two in $x$ and needs only $Q(D)=\theta D$, and $x\perp\mathbf1$ is used only to place $G_x$ in $\mathcal D_n$.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 7. Scope, unstated hypotheses and the "What it gives" paragraph

**Hypotheses.**

- **$n\ge5$ is used nowhere.** At $n=4$, $V_{jk}$ is one-dimensional, the single-flip witnesses of item 1 still exist, and every computation of items 2 to 6 goes through. The restriction is harmless and should be labelled as the far-region's range rather than a hypothesis of the identities.
- **Used and unstated:** L120's block preservation at $\Gamma$-fixed $D$ (item 3), the $O(n)$-equivariance of $\circ$ (items 1, 3, 4), total symmetry of the trilinear form (items 3, 6), and the sharp formula's normalisation against Given 2 (item 2). All are on the record, and none is in question.
- **Prior art.** Item 2's $G_x$ is the representer of L122's pair-block bracket (section 2), and item 3's pinning is L122's quadratic form paired with $\nu$. Item 5's $\xi\cdot D$ is the orbit direction already known to be a $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvector (section 5). The lemma cites neither, and should.

**The "What it gives" paragraph claims more than the items and more than its linked note.** It says the pair-block stratum "reduces, with these identities, to one open inequality", the gap bound at diagonal critical points above $\sqrt{3/2}$. The linked [pair-block note](2026-09-14-I104-pair-block.md) says otherwise in its own text.

- It records that the chain with constants closes only for $\lVert\nu\rVert^2\ge0.2$, and that by screen with sampled even constants: "Owed for a lemma: the even constants derived rather than sampled, and a bridge for $0<\lVert\nu\rVert^2<0.2$."
- It says the small-$\nu$ regime needs the gap "with $\gamma$ explicit, stable under the perturbation the chain allows", and "the owed step is that gap on the pair block, uniformly over the diagonal critical points above $\sqrt{3/2}$, and its stability". The critical points in that regime are nearly diagonal, and the gap at exactly diagonal critical points reaches them only through that stability.
- Its final section is titled "The pair-block gap, reduced by hand to one inequality on the diagonal locus". What reduces to one inequality is the gap, and the gap is one input to the stratum.

So the sentence conflates "the gap reduces to one inequality on $D$" with "the stratum reduces to one inequality". The correct statement is that items 4 and 6 turn the pair-block gap off the orbit direction into $Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2\le\theta^2-2\gamma\theta$ for unit $x\perp\mathbf1$, $x\perp y$, and that the stratum further needs the gap's stability, the bridge and derived even constants. Two smaller defects in the same paragraph: $\gamma$ and "the pair-block form" are undefined in the lemma, and the passage from item 6 to a bound on the even form uses $\langle L_{G_x}D^+,D^-\rangle=0$, which follows from parity as in item 3 but is not among the items. The reduction itself checks by hand: for unit $x$, $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2$, the orbit direction is an eigenvector of the pair block so its orthogonal complement is invariant, and item 4 gives the odd contribution $\lVert x\rVert^2\lVert y\rVert^2+2\langle x,y\rangle^2=\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2+2\langle x,y\rangle^2$.

**Verdict: REFUTED**, for the "What it gives" sentence only. The items' scope is sound.
