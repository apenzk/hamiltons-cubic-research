## 2. The refusal's REASON, against the code that reads logs

The message asserts a causal claim about a supervisor, so I checked it against both readers rather than against the commit.

- **[loop_stop.py](../.claude/hooks/loop_stop.py).** `_agents_running` collects reviewer logs by four name patterns, a fifth and sixth, and a content test on the first line, then skips any log older than `max_age = 2700` seconds and returns `True` for the first warm log whose last six non-blank lines contain no match of `[\s\[\]0-9:.\-TZ]*(DONE|FAILED)\b`. Appending `FAILED closed by ...` makes that match succeed, so the log is passed over and the hook stops treating that reviewer as in flight. The docstring at [loop_stop.py:452](../.claude/hooks/loop_stop.py#L452) names the consequence in the other direction: an unclosed warm log SILENCES the hook, which is why the tool closes logs at all.
- **[reviewer_watchdog.sh](../scripts/misc/reviewer_watchdog.sh).** For each watched log it runs `tail -n 1 "$log" | grep -qE '^(DONE|FAILED)' && continue`, so a closed log is not counted in `alive`. When every watched log is closed the script prints `WATCHDOG: every watched reviewer has finished.` and exits 0. This is the stronger of the two: the watchdog does not merely stop blocking, it asserts completion in words.
- **So the guard is right for the right reason.** A reader who wanted to fault the message could only object that loop_stop's effect is "stops blocking" rather than "is told it finished", and the watchdog closes that gap by saying it outright.
- **One nuance the message leaves out.** Both readers look at the END of the log, and the tool appends there, so the corruption is maximally visible to exactly the two things that read logs. There is no third reader that would notice the line is the tool's.

## 3. Is a staleness test wrong in principle, or was the implementation wrong?

**Wrong in principle, and for a sharper reason than the commit gives.** The commit argues from the frequency of long gaps. The argument that actually holds is about bounds and asymmetry.

- **No sound threshold exists.** A legitimate gap has no upper bound derivable from anything except the log corpus, and the corpus is what you would calibrate the threshold on, so any threshold is fitted to history and refuted by the next longer computation. [loop_stop.py:343](../.claude/hooks/loop_stop.py#L343) has already been through this once: its window sits "just past the worst measured gap (39 min)", and my measurement below finds a reviewer log with a genuine 135.7-minute gap, so that comment is stale and the 45-minute window is not the generous bound it claims to be.
- **The two errors are asymmetric.** A false "dead" verdict corrupts a live log silently, tells both readers a working reviewer has finished, and folds the round without it. A false "alive" verdict costs a delayed salvage with the log intact. A guard whose failures are silent in one direction and loud in the other must be set so that it can only fail loudly, and a threshold large enough for that (say three hours) is no longer a guard, because by then the caller has waited long enough to assert death anyway. The assertion is both cheaper and more accurate.
- **Staleness is still the right instrument in the right place, and it is already there.** [reviewer_watchdog.sh](../scripts/misc/reviewer_watchdog.sh) takes `STALE` from its caller, measures the age, and PRINTS a warning that names both readings ("Treat it as lost, or resume it with SendMessage"). It modifies nothing, so a wrong threshold there costs a false alarm. The division in the tree is correct: the watchdog measures and notifies, the salvage requires an assertion.
- **The measurement, over the logs rather than over the claim.** I parsed leading timestamps in every log in [agent_logs/](../agent_logs/) whose name matches a reviewer convention, excluding watchdogs and the three reviewers on other briefs, and took consecutive differences, discarding any apparent gap above six hours as a stamp artefact and folding clock-only stamps over midnight. Of 411 logs with at least three stamped lines: 38 (9.2%) have a maximum gap above 900 seconds, the first attempt's window; 16 (3.9%) above 1800 seconds; 12 (2.9%) above the hook's 2700. The worst are [r330-lemma71-uncond.log](../agent_logs/r330-lemma71-uncond.log) at 135.7 minutes, [r489.log](../agent_logs/r489.log) at 122.0, [review_r45_padded.log](../agent_logs/review_r45_padded.log) and [review_r19.log](../agent_logs/review_r19.log) at 121.0, and [r297-orbit-mass.log](../agent_logs/r297-orbit-mass.log) at 91.0. I spot-checked the raw stamps in three of them, and the gaps are real rather than parse artefacts: r489 runs `01:48:32` to `03:50:00`, r330 runs `19:19` to `21:35`, r297 runs `2026-09-01T00:08:19` to `01:39:25`.
- **So the skill's claim is supported and the commit's gloss is not.** [SKILL.md:78](../.claude/skills/research-loop/SKILL.md) says measured gaps "run past half an hour whenever an agent starts a long computation", which the numbers bear out. The commit's "which is the normal case rather than the edge" does not: the normal case is a maximum gap under fifteen minutes, in 90.8% of reviewer logs. The correction strengthens nothing and weakens nothing in the design, since a 3.9% silent-corruption rate on a tool run at the moment work stops being recoverable is already disqualifying, but the commit's reason as written is false and the conclusion needs the bound argument instead.

## 4. The overwrite refusal, and what it does not fix

- **It fires on both kinds.** On a PARTIAL: `r999: reviews/REVIEW_r999.md already exists as a PARTIAL, not overwritten, log closed`. On a real report: `r998: ... already exists as a PARTIAL, not overwritten, log closed`, the report left byte-identical. The `kind` label is decided by `'PARTIAL' in head` over the first 400 characters, so a genuine report whose standfirst says "PARTIAL CONFIRMATION" is MISLABELLED as a PARTIAL. The old bug that label came from is gone, since both branches now refuse, so the cost is a message that invites a caller to delete a real report and re-run.
- **THE DEFECT THE REFUSAL WAS FOR SURVIVES ON THE REFUSAL PATH.** The commit's reason for refusing is that "the first run appended a closing line to the log, so the next PARTIAL carries the tool's own output as though the reviewer had logged it". Refusing the overwrite stops the second PARTIAL, and `_close_log` still runs on that branch, so each invocation appends one more line. I ran the same reviewer three times and counted three `FAILED closed by` lines in its log. The vector is therefore intact rather than closed: delete the PARTIAL, for a re-launched reviewer on the same number or for any other reason, and the next salvage folds all of them into the report as the reviewer's words. The corruption lives in the unconditional close, not in the overwrite.
- **Worse on a reviewer that closed itself.** A log ending `DONE r996 verdict CONFIRMED, report committed abc1234`, with a real report beside it, comes back with the tool's `FAILED closed by ...` appended below the `DONE`. The watchdog reads only the tail, so it now reads `FAILED` for a reviewer that succeeded. loop_stop reads six lines and still sees the `DONE`, so the two readers disagree about the same log, which is the state hardest to diagnose later.
- **The fix is one test.** Close only when the last six non-blank lines carry no anchored `DONE|FAILED`, which is the same predicate [loop_stop.py:490](../.claude/hooks/loop_stop.py#L490) already applies. That preserves the property the closing exists for, since an unclosed corpse still gets closed, and makes repeat runs idempotent.
- **The legitimate case in the brief exists, and the tool's answer to it is right.** A reviewer that filed a real report, kept working and was then stopped has log lines the report lacks. The tool refuses, closes the log and says so. Nothing is destroyed, because the log is the durable trace and it survives untouched apart from the closing line, so the extra work is unpublished rather than lost. That case is not rare: of the reviewer logs on disk whose number has a non-PARTIAL report, 147 have no protocol closing line in their last six non-blank lines, so refuse-and-close is the tool's COMMON path rather than an edge. What the message should add is the log path it just closed, since the caller's next move is to read those lines and the message names only the report.

## 7. Is the theorem's `Id` the object the claim means?

**Yes, and the difference off the skew subspace is invisible to `Ric` by construction.** `Ric R a b = ∑ c, inner2 (R (phi a c)) (phi b c)` at [WeylReal.lean:106](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L106), so `R` is applied to `phi a c` and to nothing else.

- **Typed, not argued.** `r905_Ric_congr_on_phi`, from `simp only [Ric, h]`: if `R (phi a c) = S (phi a c)` for all `a c`, then `Ric R a b = Ric S a b`. Composed with `Ric_id_eq` this gives `r905_Ric_of_agrees_with_id`: any `R` that is the identity on the `phi` family, whatever it does off it, has `Ric R a b = (n-1)·[a = b]`. Both compile with axioms `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.
- **So the statement is what is wanted.** Had the intended `Id` been the identity on bivectors extended by zero, or the orthogonal projection onto the skew subspace, it would agree with `id` on every `phi a c` and the theorem would be the same statement about it. An operator can differ from `id` on symmetric matrices only, and `Ric` cannot see that.
- **The insensitivity extends past `Ric`, which matters for the pin.** `sharpQF` at [WeylReal.lean:102](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L102) feeds `R` the argument `phi i j` and then `br η (R ...)`, and the bracket of two skew matrices is skew, so every consumer in this layer applies `R` to skew arguments only. The choice of `id` over a projection is therefore harmless throughout, not only in `Ric_id_eq`.
- **The prose's `Id` and the layer's `Id` are the same object here.** The block takes `R = id` to be the constant-curvature tensor `R_abcd = δ_ac δ_bd - δ_ad δ_bc`, and [inner2_phi_phi](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Families.lean#L84) gives exactly that expression for `⟨φ_ij, φ_kl⟩`, so the identity operator on bivectors IS that tensor under the record's own pairing. The ambiguity the commit's third bullet names is resolved by the Gram matrix rather than by a choice.
- **The arithmetic agrees with the prose.** `∑_c (δ_ab - [c = b ∧ a = c])` is `n - 1` at `a = b` and `0` otherwise, which is the block's `∑_a R_abad = (n-1)δ_bd`. The commit's "the two agree" is correct in sign and in factor.

## 8. Does it pin the sign, and is `Q(Id) = (n-1)Id` carried at general `n`?

**The sign is pinned, and the second half of the pin is carried at general `n` over the reals, in a file neither the commit nor the block cites.**

- **What the brief pointed at is indeed only `n = 4`.** [sharpQF_one_basis](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Sharp.lean#L64) reads `sharpQF 1 (Pi.single a 1) = 2` for `a : Fin 6`, over the rationals, by `native_decide`. It is six DIAGONAL values of `Id#` in the pair basis, so on its own it does not even give `Id# = 2·Id` at `n = 4`, since `sharpMat`'s off-diagonal entries come from a polarisation the file never evaluates. And it trusts the compiler, as [Sharp.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Sharp.lean) declares.
- **The general statement IS carried.** [sharpOp_id](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/StructureConstants.lean#L217) proves `sharpOp (fun Z => Z) X = ((n : ℝ) - 2) • X` for every skew `X`, every `ι`, over the reals. Its chain is `sum_inner2_conj` for `∑ ⟨A φ B, φ⟩`, then `sum_inner2_ad2` for the Killing contraction `2(n-2) tr(XY)`, then `sharpQF_id`, `sharpBil_id` and `skew_expand`. I printed the axioms: `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` for `sharpOp_id` and for `sum_inner2_ad2`, so no `sorry` and no compiler trust anywhere in it.
- **And `Q(Id) = (n-1)Id` is three lines from it, which I typed.** `r905_Qop_id`: for skew `X`, `Qop (fun Z => Z) X = ((n : ℝ) - 1) • X`, by `simp only [Qop, compOp, sharpOp_id X hX]` and one `add_smul`. It compiles with axioms `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`, and `r905_Qop_id_phi` specialises it to the `phi a c` that `Ric` feeds. So both halves of the pin are available over the reals at every `n` in the record's own layer, and the `+` reading of `Ric(Q(R))_ab = ∑ R_acbd Ric(R)_cd` at `R = Id` reads `(n-1)²δ` on both sides from typed statements alone.
- **The citation is what is wrong, not the claim.** The commit's third bullet argues from "`sharpQF 1 = n-2`", the `n = 4` rational anchor. [curvature-invariants.md:171](../findings/curvature-invariants.md) rests the second half on "a `Q(id) = (n-1)id` that [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) carries twice and calls a pin", and [conventions.md:75](../findings/conventions.md) calls `id# = (n-2)id` a check to be performed rather than a theorem held. The [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) row for L208 writes "with `Q(Id) = (n-1)Id`" as a given. Three places treat as assumed what one file proves. The repair is a citation and, if wanted, the three-line theorem beside `Ric_id_eq`.
- **A screen at `n = 4`, reported as a screen.** With the record's own rational machinery I checked by `native_decide` that `sharpMat 1 a b = 2·[a = b]` and `Qop 1 a b = 3·[a = b]` over all 36 entries, so the off-diagonal half that [sharpQF_one_basis](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Sharp.lean#L64) omits is true there. This trusts the compiler and establishes nothing on its own. It is here only to say that the gap at `n = 4` is documentary rather than a hidden failure.
