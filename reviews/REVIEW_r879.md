# REVIEW r879: the $\kappa=1$ case is not closed by the quoted three lines

**Reviewer r879, cold read, 2026-09-12. Verdict: GAP, with the statement rescued at $n=4$ and the case shown non-vacuous at every even $n$.** The three-line block proves a conditional whose hypothesis it never states: that the positive eigenspace $A$ admits an orthonormal basis of *decomposable* bivectors. That hypothesis is exactly the "coordinate configuration" restriction, it does not follow from isotropy, and I exhibit a closed-form $A$ at $n=4$ that satisfies every hypothesis the block states and contradicts its conclusion, failing only first Bianchi. Since first Bianchi is what rules that object out, **any valid proof must use first Bianchi, and the quoted argument uses it nowhere.** I then close $n=4$ by hand, leaving $n\ge6$ open.

## Conventions, quoted from the brief

- $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ is realised as skew matrices, $\varphi_{ab}=E_{ba}-E_{ab}$ a UNIT bivector, $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$.
- $\lVert R\rVert^2=\tfrac14\sum_{ijkl}R_{ijkl}^2$ on four-index tensors.
- $(\omega\wedge\eta)_{ijkl}=\omega_{ij}\eta_{kl}-\omega_{ik}\eta_{jl}+\omega_{il}\eta_{jk}+\omega_{jk}\eta_{il}-\omega_{jl}\eta_{ik}+\omega_{kl}\eta_{ij}$, so $(\omega\wedge\omega)_{ijkl}=2(\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}-\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}+\omega_{il}\omega_{jk})$, twice the Pfaffian.
- $\mathcal W_n$: symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ that are first-Bianchi, Ricci-flat and trace-free. $u\wedge v=vu^{\mathsf T}-uv^{\mathsf T}$.

Everything below is exact. I ran no search, no sweep and no numerical experiment: the brief's analysis-only footing was respected, and every object named here is written down in closed form.

## 1. The three-line theorem, derived from scratch

**Step 1, isotropy, holds and is basis-free.** Let $W=\lambda_+\Pi_A+\lambda_-\Pi_{A^\perp}$ with $\dim A=a$, $N=\binom n2$. Given 3 reads $\lambda_+\sum_{\alpha\in A}M_\alpha+\lambda_-\sum_{\beta\in A^\perp}M_\beta=0$, and given 1 gives $\sum_{\alpha\in A}M_\alpha+\sum_{\beta\in A^\perp}M_\beta=\Theta(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}_n$. Eliminating the second sum, $(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)\,\Theta(A)=-\lambda_-(n-1)\mathrm{Id}_n$, and since $\lambda_+\ne\lambda_-$, $\Theta(A)=\kappa\,\mathrm{Id}_n$. Taking traces, $2a=\kappa n$, so $\kappa=2a/n$ and at $a=n/2$, $\kappa=1$: $\sum_{\alpha\in A}M_\alpha=\mathrm{Id}_n$. Correct, and it needs nothing about decomposability, since $\Theta$ is basis-independent by given 1.

**Step 2, the multiplication, is where the argument breaks.** For decomposable $\omega_\alpha$, multiply $\sum_\beta\Pi_\beta=\mathrm{Id}$ by $\Pi_\alpha$ and use $\Pi_\alpha^2=\Pi_\alpha$: $\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=\Pi_\alpha-\Pi_\alpha^2=0$. Correct. For a general unit $\omega_\alpha$ one gets $\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}M_\alpha M_\beta=M_\alpha-M_\alpha^2$, and $M_\alpha^2\neq M_\alpha$, so nothing vanishes.

**The trace identity is correct.** For any orthogonal projections, $\lVert\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta\rVert_F^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\beta\Pi_\alpha^{\mathsf T}\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\beta\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta^2)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)$, using idempotence twice and symmetry once. So $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)\ge0$, the trace of step 2 is a sum of nonnegative terms equal to zero, every term is zero, and $\lVert\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta\rVert_F=0$ gives $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=0$, that is, mutually orthogonal planes. Nonnegativity is enough, and the Frobenius norm is what upgrades "trace zero" to "product zero". This part of the block is airtight.

**How far the general case gets, exactly.** For a unit skew $\omega$ with singular values $s_1,s_1,s_2,s_2,\dots$ one has $\sum_i s_i^2=1$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^2=2\sum_i s_i^4\le2(\sum_i s_i^2)^2=2$, with equality **iff** exactly one $s_i$ is nonzero, that is iff $\omega$ is decomposable. Taking the trace of $M_\alpha-M_\alpha^2=\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}M_\alpha M_\beta$ and summing over $\alpha$ gives the exact identity

$$\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_\alpha M_\beta)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\bigl(2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_\alpha^2\bigr)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}4\sum_{i<j}s_i^{(\alpha)2}s_j^{(\alpha)2}\;\ge\;0 .$$

So at $\kappa=1$ the total pairwise plane overlap equals the total non-decomposability of the chosen eigenbasis, term by term. This is a trade-off, not a forcing: the argument recovers mutual orthogonality precisely when the deficiency is zero, and says nothing otherwise.

**A witness that step 2 is genuinely basis-dependent, not merely presented loosely.** Take $n=4$ and $A=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\varphi_{12},\varphi_{34}\}$, a legitimate $\kappa=1$ eigenspace (see section 4). In the orthonormal basis $\{\varphi_{12},\varphi_{34}\}$ the conclusion holds: $\Pi_{12}\Pi_{34}=0$. In the equally legitimate orthonormal basis $\{\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\varphi_{12}+\varphi_{34}),\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\varphi_{12}-\varphi_{34})\}$ one has $M_1=M_2=\tfrac12\mathrm{Id}_4$, so $M_1M_2=\tfrac14\mathrm{Id}_4\ne0$ while $M_1+M_2=\mathrm{Id}_4$ still holds. Same $W$, same $A$, same isotropy relation, and line 2's conclusion is false. The block's chain therefore depends on a choice it never makes, and the existence of the good choice is the content of the case.

## 2. From mutual orthogonality to a frame

Granted $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=0$ for $\alpha\ne\beta$, the planes are pairwise orthogonal subspaces, so $\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha$ is the orthogonal projection onto $P_1\oplus\dots\oplus P_{n/2}$, an orthogonal direct sum of dimension $2\cdot n/2=n$. Since that projection is $\mathrm{Id}_n$, the sum is all of $\mathbb R^n$, and an orthonormal basis $\{f_{2\alpha-1},f_{2\alpha}\}$ of each $P_\alpha$ concatenates to an orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$. Correct, and the dimension count is a consequence rather than an input, which is what the block's closing remark is getting at.

**Sign and normalisation.** By given 2 the unit decomposables whose plane is $P_\alpha$ are exactly $\pm f_{2\alpha-1}\wedge f_{2\alpha}$, so $\omega_\alpha=\varepsilon_\alpha f_{2\alpha-1}\wedge f_{2\alpha}$ with $\varepsilon_\alpha=\pm1$. Two reasons the sign is harmless: swapping $f_{2\alpha-1}\leftrightarrow f_{2\alpha}$ flips it, so the frame can be chosen to make every $\varepsilon_\alpha=+1$, and in any case $W$ depends on the eigenbasis only through $\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha$, which is invariant under $\omega_\alpha\mapsto-\omega_\alpha$. The normalisation is the one the whole block needs: $\lVert\varphi_{ab}\rVert=1$ under $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$, and $\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_P$ holds for exactly this convention. Under the other common normalisation $u\wedge v$ would have norm $\sqrt2$ and $\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}=2\Pi_P$, which would move $\kappa$ by a factor of two and break the arithmetic $\kappa=2a/n$. The block is consistent with the brief's convention throughout.

## 3. From $\Pi_A$ to $W$, and the two meanings of $\Pi$

$W$ symmetric with exactly two eigenvalues on the orthogonal complementary eigenspaces $A$ and $A^\perp$ of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ is $W=\lambda_+\Pi_A+\lambda_-(\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^2}-\Pi_A)=\lambda_-\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^2}+(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)\Pi_A$ by the spectral theorem. Correct, with **$\Pi_A$ the projection in $\Lambda^2$, of rank $a$**, whereas the $\Pi_\alpha$ of lines 1 and 2 are projections in $\mathbb R^n$, of rank $2$. The block uses the same letter for both inside one sentence, and a reader who carries the $\mathbb R^n$ meaning into "determined by $\Pi_A$ alone" gets nonsense, since $\sum_\alpha\Pi_{P_\alpha}=\mathrm{Id}_n$ is the same for every configuration and determines nothing. The intended reading, $\Pi_A=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha$ in $\Lambda^2$, is right.

Diagonality then follows: with $\omega_\alpha=f_{2\alpha-1}\wedge f_{2\alpha}$ the $n/2$ vectors $\omega_\alpha$ are themselves members of the coordinate bivector basis $\{f_i\wedge f_j\}_{i<j}$ of $\Lambda^2$ induced by the frame, so $\Pi_A$ is the diagonal $0/1$ matrix in that basis, and $\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^2}$ is diagonal in every basis. Hence $W$ is diagonal in the induced coordinate basis, with $\lambda_+$ on the $n/2$ selected coordinate bivectors and $\lambda_-$ on the other $n(n-2)/2$. Two footnotes. First, "determined by $\Pi_A$ alone" is true only up to the scalars, and those are pinned: Ricci-flatness and trace-freeness both reduce to the single relation $\lambda_++(n-2)\lambda_-=0$, so $W$ is determined by $A$ up to positive scale. Second, the conclusion is a classification *within* the hypotheses, so it does not by itself show any such $W$ exists. That is section 4.

## 3b. The missing hypothesis, and a lemma that supplies what the block omits

The block's own closing remark says that no dimension count, divisibility or rank argument enters. True, and it should have been read as a warning rather than a boast, because **no first-Bianchi argument enters either**, and I now show one must.

**Lemma (first Bianchi at two eigenvalues).** With $b(R)_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl}+R_{iklj}+R_{iljk}$, a direct expansion gives $b(\omega\otimes\omega)_{ijkl}=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}+\omega_{ik}\omega_{lj}+\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}-\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}+\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}=\tfrac12(\omega\wedge\omega)_{ijkl}$ in the brief's wedge convention. So for $W=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\,\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha$, first Bianchi is exactly $\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\,\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha=0$ in $\Lambda^4\mathbb R^n$. Put $\Xi(A):=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha=2\,b(\Pi_A)$, which is basis-independent because $\Pi_A$ is. Evaluating on the coordinate basis, every $f_i\wedge f_j$ is decomposable, so $\Xi(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)=0$ and therefore $\Xi(A^\perp)=-\Xi(A)$. At two eigenvalues first Bianchi becomes $\lambda_+\Xi(A)+\lambda_-\Xi(A^\perp)=(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)\Xi(A)=0$, hence

$$\textbf{first Bianchi} \iff \Xi(A)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha=0 .$$

Two corollaries. If $A$ has an orthonormal basis of decomposables then $\Xi(A)=0$ automatically, so Bianchi is free in the coordinate world, which is why the block never missed it. And the two-eigenvalue problem is exactly: **find $A$ with $\Theta(A)=\tfrac{2a}{n}\mathrm{Id}_n$ and $\Xi(A)=0$.** The quoted block uses the first condition and drops the second.

**Counterexample to the block's chain from its stated hypotheses.** Take $n=4$ and any $2$-dimensional $A\subset\Lambda^+$, for instance $A=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\bigl\{\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\varphi_{12}+\varphi_{34}),\ \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\varphi_{13}+\varphi_{42})\bigr\}$. For every unit self-dual $u$ one has $uu^{\mathsf T}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Id}_4$, because $SO(4)$ is transitive on the unit sphere of $\Lambda^+$ and $\tfrac12\mathrm{Id}$ is the only invariant, so $\Theta(A)=\mathrm{Id}_4$ and $\kappa=1$ exactly as at $a=n/2=2$. Setting $\lambda_+=2$, $\lambda_-=-1$, the operator $W=2\Pi_A-\Pi_{A^\perp}$ is symmetric, trace-free ($2\cdot2+4\cdot(-1)=0$) and Ricci-flat in the sense of given 3 ($2\,\mathrm{Id}_4+(-1)(3\,\mathrm{Id}_4-\mathrm{Id}_4)=0$). **Yet $A$ contains no decomposable vector whatever**, since a self-dual $\omega$ has $\omega\wedge\omega=\lVert\omega\rVert^2\mathrm{vol}\ne0$, so there are no planes, no mutually orthogonal planes, and no frame in which $W$ is coordinate-diagonal. The only hypothesis this object violates is first Bianchi: $\Xi(A)=2c\,\mathrm{vol}\ne0$ with $c>0$. The block's conclusion therefore does not follow from the hypotheses the block invokes, and the gap is not cosmetic: it is the whole difficulty of the case.

**What the block does prove.** *Let $W\in\mathcal W_n$ have two eigenvalues with $a=n/2$, and suppose $A$ admits an orthonormal basis of decomposable bivectors. Then their planes are mutually orthogonal, they sum to $\mathbb R^n$, and $W$ is coordinate-diagonal in the induced frame with $\lambda_++(n-2)\lambda_-=0$.* That is correct, clean and worth keeping. It is a statement about coordinate configurations, and given 4's own aside ("$n\mid2a$ is necessary for a *coordinate* configuration") shows the record knows the distinction elsewhere.

## 4. The case is not vacuous, at $n=4$, at $n=6$, and at every even $n$

Take the frame $f_1,\dots,f_n$ and $A=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{f_1\wedge f_2,\ f_3\wedge f_4,\ \dots,\ f_{n-1}\wedge f_n\}$, so $a=n/2$. Then $\Theta(A)=\Pi_{12}+\Pi_{34}+\dots=\mathrm{Id}_n$, so $\kappa=1$. Ricci-flatness reads $\lambda_+\mathrm{Id}+\lambda_-\bigl((n-1)\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{Id}\bigr)=0$, that is $\lambda_++(n-2)\lambda_-=0$, and trace-freeness reads $\tfrac n2\lambda_++\tfrac{n(n-2)}2\lambda_-=0$, the same relation. First Bianchi holds by the lemma of section 3b, since every basis vector is decomposable and $\Xi(A)=0$. So

$$W_n:=(n-2)\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n/2}\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha-\Pi_{A^\perp}\in\mathcal W_n,\qquad \lambda_+=n-2,\ \lambda_-=-1,$$

is a genuine two-eigenvalue algebraic Weyl tensor with $a=n/2$, for every even $n\ge4$. At $n=4$ it is $\lambda_+=2$ on $\{\varphi_{12},\varphi_{34}\}$ and $\lambda_-=-1$ on the remaining four coordinate bivectors, and as a cross-check it commutes with the Hodge star ($*\varphi_{12}=\varphi_{34}$, so $A$ is $*$-invariant) with $W^+=W^-=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(2,-1,-1)$, each trace-free, which is the classical shape of a Weyl tensor in dimension four. At $n=6$ it is $\lambda_+=4$ on three mutually orthogonal coordinate bivectors and $\lambda_-=-1$ on the other twelve.

**So the theorem is not vacuous, and it is sharp in the sense that its conclusion is attained.** More: at $n=4$ the conclusion is also *true*, which I verify by classifying the case outright, so the defect found in section 3b is a defect of proof and not of statement.

**Full classification at $n=4$, $\kappa=1$, by hand.** Let $\dim A=2$ with $\Theta(A)=\mathrm{Id}_4$ and $\Xi(A)=0$. Since $\omega\wedge\omega=(\lVert\omega^+\rVert^2-\lVert\omega^-\rVert^2)c\,\mathrm{vol}$ for $\omega=\omega^++\omega^-$, the Bianchi condition is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_AP^+)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_AP^-)=1$, where $P^\pm$ project onto $\Lambda^\pm$. Choose the orthonormal basis of $A$ adapted to the splitting by the CS decomposition: $\omega_i=\cos t_i\,u_i^++\sin t_i\,u_i^-$ with $\{u_i^+\}$ orthonormal in $\Lambda^+$ and $\{u_i^-\}$ orthonormal in $\Lambda^-$ wherever the corresponding cosine or sine is nonzero. Bianchi gives $\cos^2t_1+\cos^2t_2=\sin^2t_1+\sin^2t_2$, that is $\cos2t_1+\cos2t_2=0$, so $t_2=\tfrac\pi2-t_1$ up to the symmetries. Now $u^\pm(u^\pm)^{\mathsf T}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Id}_4$ and the cross term contributes the traceless symmetric $Y_i:=\mathop{\mathrm{sym}}\bigl(2u_i^+(u_i^-)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)$, so $\Theta(A)=\mathrm{Id}_4+\tfrac12\sin2t_1\,(Y_1+Y_2)$ after using $\cos t_2\sin t_2=\cos t_1\sin t_1$. Hence either $\sin2t_1=0$ or $Y_1=-Y_2$. The second is impossible: under the standard isomorphism $\Lambda^+\otimes\Lambda^-\cong S^2_0(\mathbb R^4)$ the relation $Y_1=-Y_2$ forces $u_2^+=\pm u_1^+$, contradicting $u_1^+\perp u_2^+$. So $\sin 2t_1=0$, giving either $t_1=t_2=\tfrac\pi4$, in which case both $\omega_i$ have $\lVert\omega^+\rVert=\lVert\omega^-\rVert$ and are decomposable, or $\{t_1,t_2\}=\{0,\tfrac\pi2\}$, in which case $A=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{u^+,v^-\}$ and the vectors $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(u^+\pm v^-)$ form an orthonormal basis of $A$ by decomposables. Either way $A$ has a decomposable orthonormal basis, section 1 applies, and $A$ is $O(4)$-equivalent to $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\varphi_{12},\varphi_{34}\}$. **$n=4$ is closed affirmatively, and the proof uses Bianchi twice.**

At $n\ge6$ I could not close the gap and did not find a counterexample. The one structured family I could rule out in closed form is the quaternion-like one: three pairwise $\Lambda^2$-orthogonal complex-structure forms $\omega_{J_\alpha}/\sqrt3$ on $\mathbb R^6$ all have $M=\tfrac13\mathrm{Id}_6$, hence $\Theta(A)=\mathrm{Id}_6$, and $*(\omega_J\wedge\omega_J)\propto\omega_J$ turns $\Xi(A)=0$ into $J_1+J_2+J_3=0$. Squaring $J_3=-(J_1+J_2)$ against $J_i^2=-\mathrm{Id}$ gives $J_1J_2+J_2J_1=\mathrm{Id}$, whose trace is $2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(J_1J_2)=6$, while $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality demands $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(J_1J_2)=0$. Contradiction, so that family is empty.

## 5. The $n=6$ enumeration

The block for this is not quoted in my brief, so I audit the arithmetic rather than its wording. At $n=6$, $N=\binom62=15$. Given 7 allows $a=n/2=3$ or $a\ge n/2+2=5$, and the same for $15-a$, so $15-a=3$ or $15-a\ge5$: together $a\in\{3\}\cup[5,10]\cup\{12\}$. Integrality of $\kappa=2a/n=a/3$ holds for a coordinate configuration, since $\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha$ then has integer diagonal in the frame, so $3\mid a$, leaving $a\in\{3,6,9,12\}$. The swap of given 7 identifies $a\leftrightarrow15-a$, that is $3\leftrightarrow12$ and $6\leftrightarrow9$, and negates $W$. **So up to sign exactly $a=3$ and $a=6$ survive, as claimed.** Two caveats a reader should carry:

- $a=9$ and $a=12$ are admitted by the counts and are omitted only by the swap. If the record's chain lists $\{3,6\}$ without invoking the swap in the same breath, it is under-reporting.
- $3\mid a$ is a *coordinate* restriction. Non-coordinate configurations only need $\Theta(A)=(a/3)\mathrm{Id}_6$ with $a/3$ real, so $a=5$ ($\kappa=5/3$), $a=7$, $a=8$ and $a=10$ are not excluded by anything above. They are excluded only once one knows every $A$ has a decomposable orthonormal basis, which is the same gap as section 3b. The $n=6$ enumeration is therefore complete relative to the coordinate hypothesis and incomplete without it.

## 6. The overlap identity at $a=6$, $\kappa=2$

Here $\kappa=2a/n=12/6=2$, so $\sum_{\alpha\in A}\Pi_\alpha=2\,\mathrm{Id}_6$ over a decomposable orthonormal basis. Multiply by $\Pi_\alpha$ and take the trace: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Pi_\alpha+\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Pi_\alpha=4$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Pi_\alpha=2$, so

$$\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}t_{\alpha\beta}=2\quad\text{for every }\alpha,\qquad t_{\alpha\beta}:=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)\in[0,1] .$$

**Correct, each plane's total overlap is exactly $2$.** The range $[0,1]$ per term is given 6, which I re-derived: $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality of two unit decomposables means $\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2=0$ for the principal angles, so one angle is right, and $t=\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_2$ collapses to the squared cosine of the other. The consistency check $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\bigl((\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha)^2\bigr)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(4\,\mathrm{Id}_6)=24$ against $6\cdot2+6\cdot2=24$ holds. Note the same inheritance as everywhere: the identity is about a decomposable basis.

## 7. The coordinate solutions at $a=6$

A coordinate configuration at $a=6$ assigns to each $\alpha$ a coordinate $2$-plane $\{i,j\}$, that is an edge of a simple graph $G$ on six vertices, with $t_{\alpha\beta}=1$ when the edges share a vertex and $0$ when they are disjoint, by given 6. Then $\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha=2\,\mathrm{Id}_6$ says every vertex has degree exactly $2$, so $G$ is $2$-regular, and the edge count is automatically $\tfrac12\sum_v\deg v=6=a$, consistent. Conversely each edge of a $2$-regular graph meets exactly one further edge at each endpoint, so its total overlap is $2$, matching section 6, and this holds inside a triangle as well as along a long cycle. **So the coordinate solutions are exactly the $2$-regular simple graphs on six vertices**, that is disjoint unions of cycles of length at least three covering all six vertices: $C_6$ and $C_3+C_3$, and nothing else ($C_4+C_2$ and $C_5+C_1$ fail simplicity and regularity respectively). Two up to isomorphism, as claimed.

Both are genuine: distinct coordinate bivectors are $\Lambda^2$-orthonormal by construction, so $\Lambda^2$-orthonormality is free; $\Theta=2\,\mathrm{Id}_6$ holds by $2$-regularity; first Bianchi holds by section 3b since every $\omega_\alpha$ is decomposable; and Ricci-flatness with trace-freeness both reduce to $2\lambda_++3\lambda_-=0$, realised by $(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)=(3,-2)$, with $6\cdot3+9\cdot(-2)=0$. They are inequivalent: the Gram pattern of $t$ is the adjacency of the line graph, and $L(C_6)=C_6$ while $L(C_3+C_3)=C_3+C_3$, which are non-isomorphic.

## 8. The symmetric ansatz $t_{\alpha\beta}=2/5$, the item that would settle $n=6$

**The Gram spectrum is as claimed.** The Gram matrix of the six $\Pi_\alpha$ in the Frobenius inner product is $T=2I_6+\tfrac25(J-I_6)=\tfrac85I_6+\tfrac25J$, since $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Pi_\alpha^2=2$. Its eigenvalues are $\tfrac85+\tfrac25\cdot6=4$ on $\mathbf 1$ and $\tfrac85$ with multiplicity five. **Correct: $4$ and $8/5^{(5)}$, positive definite, so no semidefiniteness obstruction exists and the six $\Pi_\alpha$ would be linearly independent in the $21$-dimensional $S^2(\mathbb R^6)$.** The row sums confirm section 6: $5\cdot\tfrac25=2$.

Two things sharpen the picture before the realisation question. First, **the ansatz implies isotropy**: with $S_\alpha:=\Pi_\alpha-\tfrac13\mathrm{Id}_6$, which is trace-free, the Gram matrix becomes $G=\tfrac4{15}(6I_6-J)$, whose kernel contains $\mathbf 1$, so $\lVert\sum_\alpha S_\alpha\rVert_F^2=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}G\mathbf 1=0$ and $\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha=2\,\mathrm{Id}_6$ for free. The $S_\alpha$ are a regular simplex of six points spanning a five-dimensional subspace of $S^2_0(\mathbb R^6)$, each with spectrum $\{\tfrac23^{(2)},-\tfrac13^{(4)}\}$ and satisfying $S_\alpha^2=\tfrac13S_\alpha+\tfrac29\mathrm{Id}$. Second, **the moment identities do not obstruct**: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Theta^2)=24$ is satisfied identically, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Theta^3)=48$ forces $\sum_{\text{distinct}}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta\Pi_\gamma)=0$ over the $120$ ordered triples, equivalently $\sum_{\text{distinct}}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S_\alpha S_\beta S_\gamma)=\tfrac{16}3$, which the expansion of $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\bigl((\sum_\alpha S_\alpha)^3\bigr)=0$ reproduces exactly ($\tfrac83-8+\tfrac{16}3=0$, using $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S^3=\tfrac49$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S_\alpha^2S_\gamma)=-\tfrac4{45}$). Third moments are therefore silent.

**A necessary local structure, and it is satisfiable.** With one right principal angle per pair, $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta\Pi_\alpha=\tfrac25x_{\alpha\beta}x_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathsf T}$ for a unit $x_{\alpha\beta}\in P_\alpha$. Summing over $\beta\ne\alpha$ and using $\Pi_\alpha(2\,\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_\alpha)\Pi_\alpha=\Pi_\alpha$ gives $\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}x_{\alpha\beta}x_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathsf T}=\tfrac52\Pi_\alpha$: **five unit vectors in each $2$-plane forming a tight frame of constant $5/2$**, realised by five equiangular directions at $36^\circ$. No obstruction there either, only a hint of pentagonal symmetry.

**The realisation, and where it fails.** Positive semidefiniteness is genuinely insufficient, and I can say so concretely rather than in principle, because the Gram data *is* realisable by planes. Let $a_1,\dots,a_6$ be unit vectors along the six five-fold axes of the icosahedron in $\mathbb R^3$, so that $\langle a_\alpha,a_\beta\rangle^2=\tfrac15$ for $\alpha\ne\beta$ and $\sum_\alpha a_\alpha a_\alpha^{\mathsf T}=2\,\mathrm{Id}_3$ by transitivity and trace. On $\mathbb R^6=\mathbb R^3\oplus\mathbb R^3$ put

$$\Pi_\alpha:=a_\alpha a_\alpha^{\mathsf T}\oplus a'_\alpha a'^{\mathsf T}_\alpha,\qquad a'_\alpha:=a_{\sigma(\alpha)}\ \text{for any permutation }\sigma .$$

Each $\Pi_\alpha$ is a rank-two projection, $\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha=2\,\mathrm{Id}_3\oplus2\,\mathrm{Id}_3=2\,\mathrm{Id}_6$ exactly, and $t_{\alpha\beta}=\langle a_\alpha,a_\beta\rangle^2+\langle a'_\alpha,a'_\beta\rangle^2=\tfrac15+\tfrac15=\tfrac25$ exactly. **Every numerical datum of the ansatz is met.** And it is not an eigenbasis: the matrix of inner products between the adapted bases is $\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\langle a_\alpha,a_\beta\rangle,\langle a'_\alpha,a'_\beta\rangle)$, so both principal angles equal $\arccos(1/\sqrt5)$, the pair is **isoclinic**, and $\langle\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta\rangle=\pm\tfrac15\ne0$ for all fifteen pairs. No relabelling or sign choice repairs this, since each factor is $\pm1/\sqrt5$ and the product never vanishes. The moral is exact: $t$ and $\Theta$ see only $\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_2$, while an eigenbasis needs $\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2=0$, and the Gram matrix cannot express that.

**What I could and could not settle.** A $t\equiv2/5$ eigen-configuration must be non-isoclinic in every pair, with angles $(\tfrac\pi2,\arccos\sqrt{2/5})$. I looked for a symmetry-based realisation and found none: if a group acts transitively on the six planes with the pentagonal frame of $x_{\alpha\beta}$ as an orbit, the natural candidate is $A_5\cong PSL(2,5)$ on the six points of $PG(1,5)$ with point stabiliser $D_5$, and in a six-dimensional real representation the $D_5$-invariant $2$-planes are only these: the $2_1$ or $2_2$ isotypic piece inside a single summand, which confines all six planes to a proper subspace and kills $\Theta=2\,\mathrm{Id}_6$; the two-dimensional trivial isotypic piece of $1\oplus5$, which puts $\mathbf 1$ in every plane and gives $t=1$; and, in $3\oplus3'$, the span of the two axis lines, which is precisely the icosahedral realisation just refuted. **So no transitively $A_5$-symmetric solution exists, and the symmetric ansatz is unrealised, though not yet proved impossible.** Deciding it needs either a genuine obstruction coupling $t$ to $\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2$, or a search over configurations with one right angle per pair. **The search is computation, and under the analysis-only footing I have not opened it. What a run would decide: whether a non-coordinate two-eigenvalue Weyl tensor exists at $n=6$, $a=6$. A hit would refute the coordinate-only picture of the whole two-eigenvalue case, a clean miss would only narrow the ansatz.**

## 9. The complex-line exclusion

By given 8, two complex lines in $\mathbb C^m=\mathbb R^{2m}$ have equal principal angles $\theta_1=\theta_2=\theta$. Then $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality of their unit bivectors, $\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2=\cos^2\theta=0$, forces $\theta=\tfrac\pi2$ in both angles, so the planes are fully orthogonal and $t_{\alpha\beta}=\cos^2\theta_1+\cos^2\theta_2=0$. **The argument is correct, and what it excludes is precise: no set of pairwise $\Lambda^2$-orthogonal complex lines of a fixed complex structure can carry any nonzero overlap at all.** Consequences and limits:

- It kills $\kappa=2$ outright for all-complex-line configurations at $n=6$, since the total overlap per plane would be $0$ rather than $2$.
- It does **not** kill $\kappa=1$: three pairwise orthogonal complex lines in $\mathbb C^3$ give $\Pi_1+\Pi_2+\Pi_3=\mathrm{Id}_6$, which is the coordinate solution of section 4 and no new object.
- On **mixed** configurations it says only that any two planes that happen to be complex lines of one and the same complex structure are fully orthogonal, contributing $t=0$ to the row sums. A configuration containing, say, two complex lines of $J$ and four planes that are not $J$-invariant is untouched, and so is one whose planes are complex lines of *different* complex structures. If the record's block claims more than this, it overreaches.
- The isoclinic mechanism is the general fact here and complex lines are one instance of it. My section 8 realisation is the other: isoclinic pairs are exactly those where $t$ is achieved with $\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2\ne0$, hence never orthogonal unless $t=0$.

## Verdict

**GAP.** The quoted three-line block is a correct proof of a coordinate-restricted theorem and an incorrect proof of the theorem it announces.

- **Sound as written**: the isotropy step, the identity $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\lVert\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta\rVert_F^2$, the nonnegativity argument, the passage to a frame, the sign discussion, and the reduction of $W$ to $\lambda_-\mathrm{Id}+(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)\Pi_A$.
- **Unsound as written**: the tacit promotion of $\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ to an idempotent. A $2$-plane inside $\Lambda^+$ at $n=4$ satisfies every hypothesis the block cites, has $\kappa=1$, and contains no decomposable vector, so the conclusion fails for it. The missing ingredient is first Bianchi, equivalent at two eigenvalues to $\Xi(A)=\sum_\alpha\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha=0$, which the block never invokes.
- **Recommended action**: restate the result with "$A$ admits an orthonormal basis of decomposables" as an explicit hypothesis, add the $\Xi(A)=0$ lemma to the record, mark $\kappa=1$ as **closed at $n=4$ only** and reopen the general even $n$ as the question "does $\Theta(A)=\mathrm{Id}_n$ with $\dim A=n/2$ and $\Xi(A)=0$ force a decomposable orthonormal basis?", and keep the non-vacuity witness $W_n$ so the case is never suspected of being empty. The claim "no dimension count, no divisibility and no rank argument enters, which is why it holds at every even $n$" should be withdrawn: nothing in the quoted chain distinguishes even $n$ beyond $a=n/2$ being an integer, and the chain's reach is limited by Bianchi, not by counts.
- **Items 4 and 8, the two the brief flags**: the theorem is **not vacuous** at every even $n$. The symmetric ansatz is **not realised**, its Gram spectrum being exactly $4$ and $8/5^{(5)}$, positive definite and therefore no obstacle, with the only closed-form realisation I could build (icosahedral) failing the one condition the Gram matrix cannot see.
