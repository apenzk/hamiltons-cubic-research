## 1. `Majorizes` and Schur's theorem

For $v:N\to\mathbb R$ and $k\le\lvert N\rvert$ write $s_k(v)$ for the sum of its $k$ largest entries. Every $k$-subset sum is at most $s_k(v)$, and some $k$-subset attains it, so $s_k(v)=\max_{\lvert S\rvert=k}\sum_Sv$. If $s_k(y)\le s_k(x)$, then for any $S$ of size $k$ the top-$k$ set $T$ of $x$ gives $\sum_Sy\le s_k(y)\le s_k(x)=\sum_Tx$. Conversely, applying the clause at the $S$ attaining $s_k(y)$ gives $s_k(y)\le\sum_Tx\le s_k(x)$. So `Majorizes x y` is $\sum x=\sum y$ and $s_k(y)\le s_k(x)$ for all $k$, which is $x\succ y$.

`schur_majorizes` then reads $\lambda(A)\succ\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}A$, Schur's theorem. The proof writes $A_{ii}=\sum_xU_{ix}^2\lambda_x$ from Mathlib's `spectral_theorem` and shows $U_{ix}^2$ has unit row and column sums from $UU^*=U^*U=1$. It is the route the record's L52 block names ("$K_i=\sum_j\lvert U_{ij}\rvert^2\mu_j$ with $(\lvert U_{ij}\rvert^2)$ doubly stochastic"), and only Schur's half is proved, which is all the record uses. The docstring's remark that Mathlib names Schur-Horn only in a docstring of `Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.Spectrum` is accurate at the pinned Mathlib: that is the only hit, and the majorization hits are the asymptotics tactic and `Convex/Birkhoff.lean`.

**Where the layer could now use it.** `rigidity_at_t_two`'s `hschur_phi : Majorizes mu dphi` is `L54_mu_majorizes` at `mu := eigenvalues of Mpair R` and `dphi := Kmat` on pairs, and a corollary instantiating it would type L54 (4)'s $\mu$ half with one hypothesis fewer. L52Statement's `hDmu` and `hKmu` and DoublyStochastic's `IsDoublyStochastic P` with the representation $K=P\mu$ are the three facts `schur_majorizes` proves internally as `hdiag`, `hrow` and `hcol`. Exporting them as a lemma, "for real symmetric $A$ there is a doubly stochastic $P$ with $A_{ii}=\sum_jP_{ij}\lambda_j$", would discharge both files' Schur input. *Observed and outside this review: the working tree carries an uncommitted edit to SchurMajor.lean that states such an existential with `DoublyStochastic.IsDoublyStochastic`. This review covers the committed text only.*

## 2. `Mpair` and $\mu$

The record's item 2 reads $\mu\succ d_\varphi$ with $d_\varphi$ "the diagonal entries of $M$ in the pair basis $\{e_a\wedge e_b\}$". `phi a b = single b a 1 - single a b 1` and `inner2 X Y = ½ tr(XᵀY)`, so `inner2 (phi a b) (phi c d)` is $\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}$, and on $a<b$, $c<d$ the second term never fires. The $\binom n2$ vectors are orthonormal in a space of that dimension, hence a basis of the skew matrices, which is $\Lambda^2$ in the layer. In it $M$ has matrix entries $\langle M\varphi_q,\varphi_p\rangle$ = `Mpair R q p` = `Mpair R p q`, so the eigenvalues of `Mpair R`, counted with multiplicity by Mathlib, are $\mu$.

The normalisation agrees with r1411's: $\sum\mu^2$ is the squared Frobenius norm of `Mpair R`, which is $\sum_{a<b,c<d}W_{abcd}^2=\tfrac14\sum_{abcd}W_{abcd}^2$ = `normSq R` by `innerOp_R4`, the record's $\lVert M\rVert_F^2$. The statement is homogeneous, so the record's $\lVert M\rVert_F=1$ is not needed.

The one step left informal is this identification: no lemma says that `Mpair R`'s eigenvalues are the eigenvalues of $R$ on skew matrices. It is the meaning of "$M$ in the pair basis" and it is correct, and a lemma stating it would let the layer's other spectral statements about $R$ meet this one.
