**One line.** L204's bound, its sharpness and its ruling for $\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}$ all stand as written, the single mathematical defect being the rival bound's rank criterion, which is stated as sufficient and is only necessary.

## 1. The eigen-expansion and the $\tfrac16$

**The factor is right, and the convention that makes it right is the record's own.** For a unit bivector $\omega$ the operator $\omega\otimes\omega$ has matrix entries $\langle\varphi_{ij},(\omega\otimes\omega)\varphi_{kl}\rangle=\langle\omega,\varphi_{ij}\rangle\langle\omega,\varphi_{kl}\rangle$, and $\langle\omega,\varphi_{ij}\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\omega^{\mathsf T}\varphi_{ij})=-\omega_{ij}$, so the four-index form is $(\omega\otimes\omega)_{ijkl}=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$ with the two signs cancelling. The record's $\tfrac14$ then gives $\lVert\omega\otimes\omega\rVert^2=\tfrac14(\sum_{ij}\omega_{ij}^2)^2=\lVert\omega\rVert^4$, which is the operator Frobenius norm, so the convention is coherent at the rank-one generator.

Applying the Bianchi sum, $\mathfrak b(\omega\otimes\omega)_{ijkl}=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}+\omega_{ik}\omega_{lj}+\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}$. I checked by hand that this is totally antisymmetric in all four indices, the three adjacent transpositions each producing a sign, using skewness of $\omega$ and nothing else. Since $\mathfrak b=3\,\mathrm{id}$ on $\Lambda^4$ (the map $(j,k,l)\mapsto(k,l,j)$ being an even permutation) and $\mathfrak b$ is self-adjoint in $\tfrac14\sum S_{ijkl}T_{ijkl}$, $\tfrac13\mathfrak b$ is a self-adjoint idempotent, hence the orthogonal projection onto $\Lambda^4$. So $\mathrm{Alt}(A)=\tfrac13\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha\,\mathfrak b(\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha)$ for any symmetric $A$ with orthonormal eigenbasis, by linearity of the four-index correspondence.

**The $\tfrac16$ is then exactly the record's wedge normalisation.** [SelfDual.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/SelfDual.lean)'s `wedgeSelf_apply` reads $(\omega\wedge\omega)_{abcd}=2(\omega_{ab}\omega_{cd}-\omega_{ac}\omega_{bd}+\omega_{ad}\omega_{bc})=2\,\mathfrak b(\omega\otimes\omega)_{abcd}$, the doubling being the self-wedge's, so $\tfrac13\mathfrak b=\tfrac16\,(\cdot)\wedge(\cdot)$ and the block's $\mathrm{Alt}(A)=\tfrac16\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha\,\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha$ is correct. The $\tfrac16$ and the polynomial identity of item 3 are true together under this doubled reading and under no other: dropping the $2$ would force $\tfrac13$ in the expansion and divide the identity's right side by $4$, which is presumably the history behind the withdrawn $\tfrac13$ the block records. The final constant survives either bookkeeping, since $\tfrac13\sqrt{3(m-1)/m}=\tfrac16\sqrt{12(m-1)/m}$, so the convention is a presentation question rather than a live risk to the number.

**One correction to the prose.** The clause "$\tfrac13\mathfrak b$ being idempotent with the norms splitting $\tfrac16+\tfrac56=1$" attributes to the projection a ratio that belongs to one witness. At the balanced $u$ with $m=2$, $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(u\otimes u)\rVert^2=\tfrac16$ against $\lVert u\otimes u\rVert^2=1$, which is the split quoted. At the decomposable $\varphi_{12}\otimes\varphi_{12}$ the split is $0+1$, since $\varphi_{12}\wedge\varphi_{12}=0$. The projection has no fixed split, so the sentence should say that the split is $\tfrac16+\tfrac56$ at the extremal balanced bivector, where it does confirm the $\tfrac16$ by exhibiting a projection-consistent ratio.

## 2. Where positivity is spent

**Positivity is spent at exactly one step and nowhere else.** The expansion $A=\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha\,\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha$ needs the spectral theorem for a symmetric operator, so it holds for any sign of $\mu_\alpha$. The triangle inequality gives $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(A)\rVert\le\tfrac16\sum_\alpha|\mu_\alpha|\,\lVert\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha\rVert$, and the single step that needs $\mu_\alpha\ge0$ is the replacement of $\sum_\alpha|\mu_\alpha|$ by $\operatorname{tr}A$. For a general symmetric $A$ the correct conclusion is $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(A)\rVert\le\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}\,\lVert A\rVert_{\text{nuclear}}$, and the recorded form is that at a positive spectrum.

**The witness verifies.** At $n=4$ take $s_1=(\varphi_{12}+\varphi_{34})/\sqrt2$ and $a_1=(\varphi_{12}-\varphi_{34})/\sqrt2$, both unit. Their $\Lambda^4$ images are opposite: $\mathfrak b(s_1\otimes s_1)_{1234}=s_{12}s_{34}=\tfrac12$ and $\mathfrak b(a_1\otimes a_1)_{1234}=-\tfrac12$. So for $A=s_1\otimes s_1-a_1\otimes a_1$, which has $\operatorname{tr}A=0$, the tensor $\mathrm{Alt}(A)$ has $(1234)$ component $\tfrac13$, giving $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(A)\rVert^2=\tfrac14\cdot4!\cdot\tfrac19=\tfrac23$. The screen returns $\tfrac23$ exactly. The inequality would read $\sqrt{2/3}\le0$, so the hypothesis is load-bearing as claimed, and the failure is confined to the nuclear-norm step.

## 3. The polynomial identity

**Derived from the component definition, and skewness alone suffices.** Write $T=\mathfrak b(\omega\otimes\omega)$ with $T_{ijkl}=a+b+c$, $a=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$, $b=\omega_{ik}\omega_{lj}$, $c=\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}$, and $N=\sum_{ij}\omega_{ij}^2=2\lVert\omega\rVert^2$. Each square sums to $N^2$ after relabelling, so the diagonal part is $3N^2$. Each cross term is a trace:

- $\sum_{ijkl}ab=\sum_{ijkl}\omega_{ij}\omega_{ik}\omega_{kl}\omega_{lj}=\sum_{ij}\omega_{ij}(\omega^3)_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(\omega^{\mathsf T}\omega^3)=-\operatorname{tr}(\omega^4)$.
- $\sum_{ijkl}ac=\sum\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}=-\sum\omega_{ij}\omega_{jk}\omega_{kl}\omega_{li}=-\operatorname{tr}(\omega^4)$, the sign coming from $\omega_{il}=-\omega_{li}$.
- $\sum_{ijkl}bc=\sum\omega_{ik}\omega_{il}\omega_{lj}\omega_{jk}=-\operatorname{tr}(\omega^4)$, by the same single sign flip.

Hence $\sum_{ijkl}T_{ijkl}^2=3N^2-6\operatorname{tr}(\omega^4)=12\lVert\omega\rVert^4-6\operatorname{tr}(\omega^4)$, and since $\omega\wedge\omega=2T$ the record's norm gives $\lVert\omega\wedge\omega\rVert^2=\tfrac14\cdot4\sum T_{ijkl}^2=12\lVert\omega\rVert^4-6\operatorname{tr}(\omega^4)$. The only property of $\omega$ used is $\omega^{\mathsf T}=-\omega$, so the identity is a polynomial identity in the entries valid at every $n$ and needs no rank, no normal form and no positivity. **Both stated ends check by hand.** For $\varphi_{12}$: $\lVert\omega\rVert^2=1$ and $\omega^2=-\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0,\dots)$ so $\operatorname{tr}\omega^4=2$, giving $12-12=0$, as it must since a decomposable bivector has vanishing self-wedge. For $(\varphi_{12}+\varphi_{34})/\sqrt2$: $\omega^2=-\tfrac12 I_4$ so $\operatorname{tr}\omega^4=1$, giving $12-6=6$, which is $12(m-1)/m$ at $m=2$. The screen reproduces the identity exactly on three random integer skew matrices at each of $n=4,\dots,9$ and both ends exactly.

## 4. The rank step and the Cauchy-Schwarz

**$\omega^2$ is symmetric negative semidefinite.** $(\omega^2)^{\mathsf T}=(\omega^{\mathsf T})^2=\omega^2$, and $\langle\omega^2x,x\rangle=-\langle\omega x,\omega x\rangle\le0$.

**The rank is at most $2m$ at every $n$, and the cheap reason matters for Lean.** At even $n$, $2m=n$ and the claim is trivial. At odd $n$, $\det\omega=\det\omega^{\mathsf T}=(-1)^n\det\omega=-\det\omega$ forces $\det\omega=0$, so $\operatorname{rank}(\omega^2)\le\operatorname{rank}\omega\le n-1=2m$. The eigenvalue pairing of the normal form is therefore never needed for this step, which strengthens the block's claim about the Lean reach in item 11.

**The Cauchy-Schwarz is exact, with no off-by-two.** Let $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k$ be the nonzero eigenvalues of $\omega^2$, so $k\le2m$. Then $\sum_j\lambda_j=\operatorname{tr}\omega^2=-2\lVert\omega\rVert^2$, because $\lVert\omega\rVert^2=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\omega^{\mathsf T}\omega)=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\omega^2$, and $\operatorname{tr}\omega^4=\sum_j\lambda_j^2$ since the zero eigenvalues contribute nothing. Cauchy-Schwarz against the all-ones vector of length $k$ gives $\sum_j\lambda_j^2\ge(\sum_j\lambda_j)^2/k=4\lVert\omega\rVert^4/k\ge4\lVert\omega\rVert^4/(2m)=2\lVert\omega\rVert^4/m$. **The $4$ comes from squaring $\operatorname{tr}\omega^2=-2\lVert\omega\rVert^2$ and the halving comes from dividing by $2m$, and the two combine to exactly the stated $2/m$.** This is where an off-by-two would live, and the bookkeeping is right: the $m$ in the conclusion is the count of blocks while the $2m$ in the hypothesis is the count of eigenvalues.

## 5. The combination

**It is a maximum, not merely a bound.** Items 3 and 4 give $\lVert\omega\wedge\omega\rVert^2=12-6\operatorname{tr}\omega^4\le12-12/m=12(m-1)/m$ at $\lVert\omega\rVert=1$. Equality needs $k=2m$ and all $\lambda_j$ equal, which the balanced $\omega=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i\le m}\varphi_{2i-1,2i}$ supplies at every $n\ge2$: there $\omega^2$ has the eigenvalue $-1/m$ with multiplicity $2m$, so $\operatorname{tr}\omega^4=2/m$ and the bound is met. At odd $n$ the one remaining eigenvalue is $0$ and contributes to neither side. So $\max_{\lVert\omega\rVert=1}\lVert\omega\wedge\omega\rVert=\sqrt{12(m-1)/m}$, attained.

**The arithmetic closes.** $\tfrac16\sqrt{12(m-1)/m}=\sqrt{12(m-1)/(36m)}=\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}$ identically. The screen confirms $\lVert\omega\wedge\omega\rVert^2=12(m-1)/m$ and $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(u\otimes u)\rVert^2=(m-1)/(3m)$ exactly as rationals at $n=4,\dots,9$, reproducing the block's $2.449490$, $2.828427$, $3$, $3$ as $\sqrt6$, $\sqrt8$, $3$, $3$.

## 6. The canonical-form route

**The $24$ is right and so is its decomposition.** In canonical form with $\sum_i a_i^2=1$ the only nonzero entries are $\omega_{2i-1,2i}=-a_i$, so a term of $\mathfrak b(\omega\otimes\omega)_{ijkl}$ survives only when the index set is a union of two whole blocks, which gives the $\binom m2$ sets and kills every set touching an unpaired index, so an odd dimension supplies nothing. On the set $\{2i-1,2i,2j-1,2j\}$ exactly one of the three terms survives, giving $\mathfrak b=a_ia_j$ and $(\omega\wedge\omega)=2a_ia_j$. Each such set carries $4!$ signed orderings, so $\lVert\omega\wedge\omega\rVert^2=\tfrac14\cdot4!\cdot\sum_{i<j}(2a_ia_j)^2=24\sum_{i<j}a_i^2a_j^2$, and the block's reading of $24$ as $\tfrac14\cdot4!\cdot2^2$ is the correct account of where the three factors come from.

**The maximisation is right.** With $t_i=a_i^2$ and $\sum t_i=1$, $\sum_{i<j}t_it_j=\tfrac12(1-\sum t_i^2)$, maximal when $\sum t_i^2$ is minimal, which is $1/m$ at $t_i=1/m$. That gives $24\cdot\tfrac12(1-1/m)=12(m-1)/m$, agreeing with item 5 identically rather than numerically. **The reduction is legitimate.** Every real skew matrix is $O(n)$-conjugate to that canonical form, $\lVert\omega\rVert$ is conjugation-invariant, and $\mathfrak b$ commutes with the index-wise action of $O(n)$ on four-index tensors while $\sum T_{ijkl}^2$ is invariant under it, so both sides are $O(n)$-invariant. The second route needs none of this, which is its advantage.

## 7. The ruling

**The ordering is exact and the equality case is exactly the even dimensions.** $\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}\le\sqrt{(n-2)/(3n)}$ iff $1-1/m\le1-2/n$ iff $n\ge2m$. Since $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, $n\ge 2m$ always, with equality iff $n$ is even. At odd $n=2m+1$ the inequality is strict. **At $n=9$**: $m=4$ gives $(m-1)/(3m)=\tfrac14$ and the constant $\tfrac12$, against $(9-2)/27=7/27$ and $\sqrt{7/27}=0.5091751$. **At $n=3$**: $m=1$ gives the constant $0$, which is correct for the strongest possible reason, since $\Lambda^4\mathbb R^3=0$ makes $\mathrm{Alt}(A)=0$ for every $A$, while the $n$-form gives $\sqrt{1/9}=\tfrac13>0$. One presentational slip: the block writes "the $n$-form misses at $1/9$", which is the value of $(n-2)/(3n)$ rather than of the constant, the constant being $\tfrac13$, and the adjacent clause quotes $\tfrac12$ at $n=9$ as a constant, so two different quantities appear in the same sentence.

**Sharpness holds, and that is what settles the ruling.** At $A=u\otimes u$ with the balanced unit $u$, $A$ is positive semidefinite of rank one with $\operatorname{tr}A=\lVert u\rVert^2=1$, and $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(A)\rVert=\tfrac16\lVert u\wedge u\rVert=\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}$ by item 5. So the constant is attained, at every $n\ge4$ and in particular at odd $n$. The screen returns equality as an exact rational at $n=4,\dots,9$. **Attainment is the load-bearing half**: since the supremum over positive semidefinite $A$ equals $\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}$ and is reached, no smaller constant works, and at odd $n$ the strictly larger $\sqrt{(n-2)/(3n)}$ is therefore provably non-optimal rather than merely larger. The ruling for $\sqrt{(m-1)/(3m)}$ is correct, and the identification of $\sqrt{(n-2)/(3n)}$ as its even-$n$ specialisation is correct.

## 8. The $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ attainment

**All three properties follow from one fact about unit self-dual forms.** Take $s_1=(\varphi_{12}+\varphi_{34})/\sqrt2$, $s_2=(\varphi_{13}+\varphi_{42})/\sqrt2$, $s_3=(\varphi_{14}+\varphi_{23})/\sqrt2$, orthonormal in $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$, each satisfying $s_a^2=-\tfrac12 I_4$, which the screen confirms. Write $W=\sum_a\mu_a\,s_a\otimes s_a$ with $\mu=(2,-1,-1)$.

- **Trace-free**: $\operatorname{tr}W=\sum_a\mu_a=0$.
- **Ricci-flat**: $\mathrm{Ric}_{jl}=\sum_iW_{ijil}=\sum_a\mu_a(s_a^{\mathsf T}s_a)_{jl}=\sum_a\mu_a(-s_a^2)_{jl}=\tfrac12\delta_{jl}\sum_a\mu_a=0$. So at $n=4$, for an operator supported on $\Lambda^+$ whose eigen-bivectors are complex structures, Ricci-flatness is precisely operator trace-freeness.
- **Bianchi**: each unit $s_a$ has the same $\Lambda^4$ image, $\mathfrak b(s_a\otimes s_a)_{1234}=\tfrac12$ for all three, so $\mathrm{Alt}(W)_{1234}=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac12\sum_a\mu_a=0$, and at $n=4$ that single component is all of $\Lambda^4$. **This is the exact point the block's closing clause makes**, that Bianchi at $n=4$ reduces to trace-freeness on this family, so balance and Bianchi are compatible rather than opposed, the reason being that the three self-dual forms share one $\Lambda^4$ image.

**The number is exact.** $W^2=\sum_a\mu_a^2\,s_a\otimes s_a$ with weights $(4,1,1)$, so $\operatorname{tr}(W^2)=6$ and $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)_{1234}=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac12\cdot6=1$, giving $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)\rVert^2=\tfrac14\cdot4!=6$. So $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)\rVert=\sqrt6=\sqrt{1/6}\cdot6=\sqrt{1/6}\operatorname{tr}(W^2)$, and $\sqrt{1/6}$ is the constant at $m=2$. The screen returns $6$ for both $\operatorname{tr}(W^2)$ and $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)\rVert^2$ exactly. Equality at a genuine Weyl tensor holds, so the bound is sharp at the obstruction a $\Lambda^4$-vanishing theorem would have to defeat, which is what the route is told and it is told correctly.

## 9. The rival bound and the crossover

**The rival bound is correct.** $\mathrm{Alt}$ is an orthogonal projection in the record's norm by item 1, so $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(A)\rVert\le\lVert A\rVert$, and for a symmetric operator $A$ the record's $\tfrac14$ makes $\lVert A\rVert^2=\sum_{i<j,k<l}A_{ijkl}^2=\operatorname{tr}(A^2)$. Hence $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(A)\rVert\le\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}A^2}$ with no hypothesis beyond symmetry.

**The crossover criterion is wrong in the direction the block asserts.** Solving it directly, the rival is strictly better exactly when $\operatorname{tr}A^2<\frac{m-1}{3m}(\operatorname{tr}A)^2$, that is when the participation ratio $(\operatorname{tr}A)^2/\operatorname{tr}A^2$ exceeds $3m/(m-1)$. For positive semidefinite $A$ of rank $r$, $\operatorname{tr}A^2\ge(\operatorname{tr}A)^2/r$, so $r>3m/(m-1)$ is **necessary**: at $r\le3m/(m-1)$ the rival cannot win. It is not sufficient. **Counterexample, exact**: at $n=6$, $m=3$, threshold $3m/(m-1)=\tfrac92$, take $A=\mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac1{10},\tfrac1{10},\tfrac1{10},\tfrac1{10},\tfrac1{10})$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^6$, of rank $6>\tfrac92$. Then $\operatorname{tr}A=\tfrac32$ and $\operatorname{tr}A^2=\tfrac{21}{20}$, while $\frac{m-1}{3m}(\operatorname{tr}A)^2=\tfrac29\cdot\tfrac94=\tfrac12$, and $\tfrac{21}{20}>\tfrac12$, so the recorded bound is the better estimate at rank $6$. The rank criterion holds for a flat spectrum, where $\operatorname{tr}A^2=(\operatorname{tr}A)^2/r$ and the comparison is $1/r$ against $(m-1)/(3m)$: at $m=3$ a flat rank $5$ wins and a flat rank $4$ loses, matching the threshold $\tfrac92$. **The repair is one word plus a clause**: rank exceeding $3m/(m-1)$ is necessary, and the criterion that decides is the participation ratio. Two consequences worth recording: at $m=2$ the threshold is $6=\dim\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$, so at $n=4$ the rival never strictly wins, and the advice that a consumer "must check which applies" remains correct with the corrected test.

## 11. The Lean statement

**The three gap-free theorems are non-vacuous and say what the block claims.** `altConst_le_altConstN` gives the ordering at every $n\ge2$ by splitting on parity, `altConst_eq_altConstN_of_even` gives equality at even $n$, and `altConst_lt_altConstN_n9` gives the strict separation at $n=9$. Both `hn : 2 ≤ n` hypotheses are load-bearing, supplying $k\ge1$ through `omega` in each branch, and the constants are the intended ones with `altConst (n / 2)` using natural division for $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. At $n=2$ both sides are $0$, which is a true instance rather than a degenerate one, since $\Lambda^4\mathbb R^2=0$.

**`alt_le_altConst_trace` types the second half of the chain and assumes the first.** Its hypotheses repair the two defects the docstring names, the family being indexed by `Fin (n * (n - 1) / 2)` and each member constrained to unit norm, and an orthonormal family of that cardinality in a space of that dimension is automatically a basis, so quantifying over arbitrary nonnegative weights is equivalent to quantifying over positive semidefinite $A$ rather than wider. The gap is elsewhere: `hT` **defines** $T$ as $\tfrac16\sum_a\mu_a\,\mathrm{wedgeSelf}(\omega_a)$, so the statement never asserts that $T$ is $\mathrm{Alt}$ of the four-index form of $A$. Item 1's derivation, which is where the $\tfrac16$ and the projection property live, is therefore assumed by the type rather than proved, and a reader of the Lean file alone gets the triangle inequality plus the maximisation. Recording that explicitly is the honest description of what is owed, alongside the `sorry`.

**The second derivation does move the remaining obligation within reach, and by more than the block claims.** The identity $\lVert\omega\wedge\omega\rVert^2=12\lVert\omega\rVert^4-6\operatorname{tr}\omega^4$ is a finite sum manipulation over `Fin n` whose three cross terms are traces of matrix products, which this layer can express with `Matrix.trace` and `Finset.sum` reindexing. The Cauchy-Schwarz half needs the spectrum of the symmetric $\omega^2$, available through `Matrix.IsHermitian.spectral_theorem`, and the rank bound $\operatorname{rank}\omega^2\le2\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, which by item 4 needs only $\det\omega=0$ at odd $n$ from $\det\omega^{\mathsf T}=(-1)^n\det\omega$. **No eigenvalue pairing and no real normal form is required**, so the claim that the route is reachable where the normal form is not is correct, and the cheapest path to the rank step is the determinant rather than the spectrum.

## 12. What would break it

**Three objects carry the lemma, and each was attacked.**

- **The identity's $-6$.** It is $2\times$ three cross terms, each independently equal to $-\operatorname{tr}\omega^4$. A single sign error in one of them would move the maximum. I derived all three separately by reduction to $\operatorname{tr}(\omega^{\mathsf T}\omega^3)$ rather than by symmetry from one, and screened the identity exactly at $n=4,\dots,9$ on integer skew matrices, where a coefficient error would show as a rational mismatch rather than a rounding one.
- **$\det\omega=0$ at odd $n$.** The entire odd-$n$ half of the ruling rests on it. If a skew $\omega$ at odd $n$ could have $\operatorname{rank}\omega^2=2m+1$, the odd-$n$ constant would rise and the $n$-form could be recovered. The determinant argument settles it at every odd $n$.
- **The $\tfrac13$ in $\mathrm{Alt}=\tfrac13\mathfrak b$ together with the norm's $\tfrac14$.** A different normalisation of either rescales the constant directly. I pinned both against the record's own definitions, the $\tfrac14$ by checking that it makes $\lVert\omega\otimes\omega\rVert=\lVert\omega\rVert^2$ and the $\tfrac13$ by checking that $\mathfrak b$ is $3\,\mathrm{id}$ on $\Lambda^4$ and self-adjoint.

**Where I looked for a falsifier and found one.** I tried to beat the balanced witness with unbalanced and with higher-rank bivectors, and the maximisation is strict, the flat spectrum being the unique maximiser up to the block structure. I tried the bound without positivity and found the block's own witness gives $\sqrt{2/3}$ against $0$.
