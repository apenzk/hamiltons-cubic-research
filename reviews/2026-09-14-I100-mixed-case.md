# I100's mixed case after L220, 2026-09-14, on Opus

**Written while [L220](../findings/second-order.md) is in Gate 2** ([r939](REVIEW_r939.md), [r940](REVIEW_r940.md)), so nothing here edits the block, the plan or the I100 derivation that the reviewers may read. Folded after the round.

## 1. Disjoint four-set blocks: L220's chain goes through unchanged

**Claim.** *Let $Q_1,\dots,Q_m$ be pairwise disjoint four-sets and $\mathcal S=\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{Q_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{Q_m}$. Every unit critical point $W=D+\sum_i\nu_i$ of $P$ in $\mathcal S$ with some $\nu_i\ne0$ and $\theta>0$ has $\theta\le\sqrt{3/2}$.*

**Why the chain survives.** $\mathcal S$ is the fixed space of $\bigcap_i\ker\chi_{Q_i}$, whose trivial characters among L120's are $1$ and the $\chi_{Q_i}$: a product $\chi_{Q_i}\chi_{Q_j}$ is the character of an eight-element set, which no block of $\mathcal W_n$ carries. So $\mathcal S$ is a subalgebra, **$\nu_i\circ\nu_j=0$ for $i\ne j$**, and $\nu_i\circ\nu_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$. Fix $i$ with $\nu_i\ne0$ and write $D=q_i+r_i$, $r_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$.

- **$L_{r_i}\nu_i=0$**: for $\nu'\in V_{Q_i}$, $\langle r_i\circ\nu_i,\nu'\rangle=\langle r_i,\nu_i\circ\nu'\rangle=0$. So $\lVert q_i\rVert^2=\theta^2/3$ exactly as in L220's step 5.
- **The pairing with $q_i$** picks up $\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle=0$ for $j\ne i$, since $\Pi_{Q_j}\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$ by disjoint support. So $P(q_i)+\langle L_{q_i}r_i,r_i\rangle+\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu_i\rVert^2=\tfrac{\theta^3}3$, L220's step 6 verbatim.
- **Steps 7 and 8** are statements about $q_i$ and any $r\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$, so they hold, and step 9 runs with $\lVert r_i\rVert^2=1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-\sum_j\lVert\nu_j\rVert^2\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-\lVert\nu_i\rVert^2$, **which only helps**. $\square$

*Screened already*: restricted ascent on $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{\{0..3\}}\oplus V_{\{4..7\}}$ at $n=8$ and $12$ found every critical point with a four-set component at $\sqrt{3/2}$ with the other component zero. **So L220 extends to any family of disjoint four-sets as a corollary**, to be stated in the block at the fold.

## 2. Where the chain stops: blocks that share indices, and pair blocks

- **Two four-sets sharing two indices**: $\chi_{Q_1}\chi_{Q_2}=\chi_{Q_1\triangle Q_2}$ is a four-set character, so the generated subalgebra contains $V_{Q_1\triangle Q_2}$ and $\nu_1\circ\nu_2\ne0$ in general. The $\mathcal D_n$ equation gains cross terms and step 4's $L_r\nu=0$ loses its reason.
- **Two four-sets sharing three indices**: the product is a pair character, so pair blocks enter.
- **A pair block $V_{jk}$**: $L_D$ acts on it through the whole rows $j$ and $k$ of $D$ ([L122](../findings/second-order.md)), so no plane $\Pi$ of $\mathcal D_n$ carries the analogue of step 4, and the orbit directions $\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D$ live inside it ([F37](../findings/fence-posts.md)).

**So I100's mixed case after L220 is: some block pair with overlapping support, or a pair block.** *The first is a finite list of subalgebras up to $S_n$ (the index pattern of two overlapping four-sets), each with its own critical equations, and L220's method (an eigenvalue condition pinning a norm) is the thing to try on it first.*

## 3. Two four-sets sharing two indices: the equations, and a screen

**Setting.** $Q_1=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $Q_2=\{1,2,5,6\}$, $Q_3=Q_1\triangle Q_2=\{3,4,5,6\}$. The fixed space of $\ker\chi_{Q_1}\cap\ker\chi_{Q_2}$ is $\mathcal S=\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{Q_1}\oplus V_{Q_2}\oplus V_{Q_3}$, closed because the trivial characters form $\{1,\chi_{Q_1},\chi_{Q_2},\chi_{Q_3}\}$. For $W=D+\nu_1+\nu_2+\nu_3$ criticality splits by character into
$$D\circ D+\textstyle\sum_i\nu_i\circ\nu_i=\theta D,\qquad 2D\circ\nu_i+2\nu_j\circ\nu_k=\theta\nu_i\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).$$
**Pairing the second with $\nu_i$**, with $\tau=\langle\nu_1\circ\nu_2,\nu_3\rangle$ symmetric in the three and $\langle D\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\langle q_i\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle$ for $q_i$ the projection of $D$ on $\Pi_{Q_i}$ (L220's step 4 argument, unchanged):
$$\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu_i\rVert^2-\tau,\qquad\text{so}\qquad\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q_i\rVert\ \ge\ \tfrac\theta2-\frac{\tau}{\lVert\nu_i\rVert^2}\ \text{ for each }\nu_i\ne0 .$$
*The eigenvalue condition becomes an inequality with a common correction $\tau$, and the three planes $\Pi_{Q_i}$ are no longer orthogonal (they share the pairs $\{1,2\}$, $\{3,4\}$, $\{5,6\}$), which is where L220's arithmetic will need a new estimate.* The pairing of the $\mathcal D_n$ equation with $D$ and $P(W)=\theta$ are consistent identities, $P(D)=\theta(t-\tfrac N2)+3\tau$, and give nothing new.

**Screen** (restricted ascent, 80 starts at $n=8$ and $n=12$). *Every converged critical point with a nonzero block component has exactly one component, at $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=0.5$ and $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$, which is L220's padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ on one of the three four-sets; no critical point with two or three components appeared.* **So the overlapping case is plausibly L220 again, with $\tau$ and the overlap of the planes as the two things a proof must control.** *Ascent reaches maxima only, so a saddle with several components is not excluded by this screen.*

**Saddles screened too** (least squares on the critical equation restricted to $\mathcal S$, 400 starts at $n=8$, residual below $10^{-8}$). *This finds critical points of every index, not only maxima: over a hundred distinct critical values with two or three nonzero block components, and the largest $\lvert\theta\rvert$ among them is $0.657$, against $\sqrt{3/2}=1.2247$ for the single-component points.* **So at $n=8$ no critical point of any index with overlapping four-set components comes near $\sqrt{3/2}$**, and the multi-component critical set looks like a large low-lying family rather than a competitor. *A screen at one $n$, which licenses the derivation and proves nothing.*

## 4. The overlapping case with one of the three components zero: $\theta^2\le\tfrac65$

**Claim.** *In §3's setting, a unit critical point with $\nu_3=0$, $\nu_1,\nu_2\ne0$ and $\theta>0$ has $\theta^2\le\tfrac65$.*

**Proof.** The $\chi_{Q_3}$ equation with $\nu_3=0$ reads $2\nu_1\circ\nu_2=0$, so $\tau=0$ and the pinning is exact: $\lVert q_i\rVert^2=\theta^2/3$ for $i=1,2$. Pair the $\mathcal D_n$ equation with $q_1$ and write $r_1=D-q_1\perp\Pi_{Q_1}$:
$$P(q_1)+\langle L_{q_1}r_1,r_1\rangle+\tfrac\theta2N_1+\langle\nu_2\circ\nu_2,q_1\rangle=\tfrac{\theta^3}3 .$$
The new term is bounded by two facts. **$\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$** on $V_Q$, from the pattern $\rho^2(\cos2\varphi,\cos(2\varphi\mp\tfrac{4\pi}3))$ with $\rho^2=\tfrac12\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. **And $\lvert\langle\hat q_1,\hat q_2\rangle\rvert\le\tfrac13$** for unit patterns on $Q_1$ and $Q_2$, which share only the pair $\{1,2\}$, whose entry has square at most $\tfrac13$ under $x+y+z=0$, $2(x^2+y^2+z^2)=1$. So $\lvert\langle\nu_2\circ\nu_2,q_1\rangle\rvert\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2N_2\cdot\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac\theta{\sqrt3}=\tfrac\theta6N_2$. With L220's steps 7 and 8 and $\lVert r_1\rVert^2=1-N_1-N_2-\tfrac{\theta^2}3$:
$$\tfrac5{18}\theta^2\le\tfrac13+\tfrac16N_1-\tfrac16N_2,$$
and the same with $1\leftrightarrow2$. Adding the two gives $\tfrac59\theta^2\le\tfrac23$. $\square$

**What remains of the overlapping case is all three components nonzero**, where $\tau\ne0$ turns the pinning into the inequality of §3 and $\lVert q_i\rVert$ is no longer fixed. *Unreviewed, and to be briefed with the L220 corollaries after that round.*

## 5. The product constant between overlapping blocks, screened

*For unit $\nu_1\in V_{Q_1}$, $\nu_2\in V_{Q_2}$ with $\lvert Q_1\cap Q_2\rvert=2$, a grid over both blocks' angles at $n=8$ gives $\max\lVert\nu_1\circ\nu_2\rVert=\tfrac12$, and $\max\lvert\tau\rvert=\tfrac12$ over unit triples including $\nu_3\in V_{Q_1\triangle Q_2}$.* **So $\lvert\tau\rvert\le\tfrac12\sqrt{N_1N_2N_3}$ by screen**, and for the largest component the pinning of §3 reads $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q_i\rVert\ge\tfrac\theta2-\tfrac12\sqrt{N_i}$. *That is the estimate the three-component case needs, and $\tfrac12$ is to be derived from the pairing-basis products before it is used.*

**The three-component case with $\tau\le0$ closes by the same sum as §4.** *If $\tau\le0$, each pinning gives $\lVert q_i\rVert\ge\theta/\sqrt3$, and with the lossy coupling bound $\kappa_i\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\lVert q_i\rVert(t-\lVert q_i\rVert^2)$ and the cross terms bounded as in §4, dividing by $\lVert q_i\rVert$ gives $\theta u-\tfrac{u^2}{2\sqrt3}\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}(1-\tfrac N2+N_i)$ with $u=\lVert q_i\rVert$. Its left side increases on $[0,1]$, so $u\ge\theta/\sqrt3$ gives $\tfrac56\theta^2\le1-\tfrac N2+N_i$, and summing over the three blocks $\tfrac52\theta^2\le3-\tfrac N2$, so $\theta^2\le\tfrac65$.* **What remains is $\tau>0$**, where the pinning loses up to $\tfrac12\sqrt{N_i}$.
