## R5 first, because it is the question the brief calls most important

**The identity is, by construction, the linear combination $y^A(\text{A}_i)+y^B(\text{B}_i)+y^C(\text{C}_i)$ of three relations that hold at every $\theta$-critical Weyl tensor. It follows from them at every Weyl tensor, at every spectrum, at every $d$, for every $\varphi$. It cannot fail, and the check was very nearly vacuous.**

Here is the one line. $T$ is totally symmetric, so $\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{a\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta=\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{a\beta\gamma}\tfrac{\lambda_\beta+\lambda_\gamma}2$, and therefore for any quadratic $\varphi(x)=y^A+y^Bx+y^Cx^2$ with polarisation $\psi(x,y)=y^A+y^B\tfrac{x+y}2+y^Cxy$, $\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{a\beta\gamma}\,\psi(\lambda_\beta,\lambda_\gamma)=y^A\!\sum T+y^B\!\sum T\lambda_\beta+y^C\!\sum T\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma=y^A\cdot2(n-2)+y^B\cdot(-2\lambda_a)+y^C\cdot2\lambda_a(\theta-\lambda_a)$, which is exactly $y^A(\text{A})+y^B(\text{B})+y^C(\text{C})$ as those are stated at [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md). (A) and (B) are [Böhm–Wilking](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/notes.md) Lemma 2.1 and hold at every Weyl tensor; (C) is criticality. So the object being verified is a tautology on the criticality locus, and $S^3\times S^4$, $S^3\times S^5$ and $S^4\times S^5$ are on that locus.

**What the check therefore could not have caught, which is everything that does work in this section.** It cannot test the annihilation, because the annihilated coefficients are multiplied by exactly $0$ and contribute exactly $0$ whether or not the record's account of them is right, a claim of two surviving types, or of four, evaluates numerically to the same number. It cannot test R2's forbidding logic, R3's sign reasoning, R4's counts, or the ten-variable reduction. It does not even test the reduced orbit counts $2\mu_j\mu_k$ and $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$ unless the left side was assembled from them rather than from a raw sum over $\beta,\gamma$, and no script was committed with the claim, so which of the two was done is not recoverable from the record.

**The single failure mode it retains, and it is three lines wide.** The record replaced the assembled right-hand side by a hand-simplified closed form. From $\varphi(x)=(x-\nu_r)^2$, i.e. $(y^A,y^B,y^C)=(\nu_r^2,-2\nu_r,1)$, the assembled right-hand side is $2\big[(n-2)\nu_r^2+2\nu_i\nu_r-\nu_i^2+\nu_i\theta\big]$, which at $r=i$ collapses by $2(n-2)+4-2=2(n-1)$ to $2\nu_i\big[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta\big]$. An error in that collapse is the only thing the numbers could have exposed, and a symbolic evaluation settles it exactly, in less space than the check took to describe.

**And the three points chosen are the weakest available witnesses even for that.** Four separate degeneracies, each verified analytically and then screened.

## R1, CONFIRMED, and the family is the extremal one rather than merely a convenient one

**$\psi$ is the correct polarisation, and it is forced.** Against (A$_i$), (B$_i$), (C$_i$) as [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) states them, the accumulated coefficient on a mixed type $\{i,j,k\}$, $j\ne k$, is $2\mu_j\mu_ky^A+\mu_j\mu_k(\nu_j+\nu_k)y^B+2\mu_j\mu_k\nu_j\nu_ky^C=2\mu_j\mu_k\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)$, and on a repeated type $\{i,j,j\}$ it is $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)\big[y^A+y^B\nu_j+y^C\nu_j^2\big]=\mu_j(\mu_j-1)\varphi(\nu_j)=\mu_j(\mu_j-1)\psi(\nu_j,\nu_j)$. The $\tfrac12$ in $\psi$ is exactly the $(\nu_j+\nu_k)$-against-$2$ mismatch [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) already flags as the two orders of a mixed pair. So "orbit count times $\psi$" is right in both cases.

**Three types survive in every one of the nine cases, with no exception.** $\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)=(\nu_j-\nu_r)(\nu_k-\nu_r)$ vanishes iff $j=r$ or $k=r$, so the surviving types in row $i$ are $\{i,j,k\}$ with the unordered pair $\{j,k\}$ drawn from $\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{r\}$, three pairs, hence three types, independently of $i$ and of whether $r=i$. Writing them out gives nine distinct three-subsets: $\{122,123,133\}$, $\{111,113,133\}$, $\{111,112,122\}$ for row $1$; $\{222,223,233\}$, $\{112,123,233\}$, $\{112,122,222\}$ for row $2$; $\{223,233,333\}$, $\{113,133,333\}$, $\{113,123,223\}$ for row $3$. No row-root combination degenerates, and the three named in the record's table are the $r=i$ cases.

**One hypothesis is load-bearing and unstated in the section.** The count of three needs every surviving orbit count nonzero, i.e. $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)\ne0$ for the repeated types, which is $m_j\ge3$ for $j\ne i$ and $m_i\ge4$ never being required only because the surviving repeated types never have $j=i$ when $r=i$. [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s admissibility caveat says the ten-variable count itself holds only when every $m_i\ge3$, so the whole section inherits that, and the section does not say so.

**The upgrade: these are the maximum-annihilation multipliers, and there are exactly $d$ of them per row.** Write $X=(\nu_j+\nu_k)/2$, $Y=\nu_j\nu_k$; then $\psi=y^A+y^BX+y^CY$ is a line, the pair-point $P_{jk}$ is $(X,Y)$, and $P_{jj}=(\nu_j,\nu_j^2)$ lies on $Y=X^2$. The double root $(x-\nu_r)^2$ is the coefficient triple $(\nu_r^2,-2\nu_r,1)$, which is the **tangent to that parabola at $\nu_r$**, and $P_{jk}$ lies on it iff $(\nu_j-\nu_r)(\nu_k-\nu_r)=0$, so it meets exactly the $d$ pair-points containing $r$. That is [post F43](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)(i)'s own statement that a line meets at most $d$ pair-points if it is a tangent and at most $2$ otherwise. **So the double-root family is the unique maximum-annihilation family, the first family's $(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$ is a generic secant hitting $2$, and the three double roots span the whole three-dimensional multiplier space at each row**, the determinant of $(\nu_r^2,-2\nu_r,1)_{r=1,2,3}$ is $-2$ times a Vandermonde. Every per-row identity at $d=3$, the first family included, is a combination of these nine. That is the sharpest thing in the section and it is not stated there.

## R2, CONFIRMED, logic and count

**The logic is sound and the direction is right.** The left side is a sum over all ten types with the seven non-surviving coefficients equal to exactly zero, so it is a sum over the three surviving types alone; if all three $t$ vanish the left side is zero and the right side must be too. Contrapositive: a nonzero right-hand side means no feasible point has all three of those $t$ zero, hence no vertex whose zero set contains that three-subset.

**Seven is right.** A four-subset of the ten types containing a fixed three-subset is that three-subset plus one of the remaining $10-3=7$ types.

**And the monotone form is what makes this safe against the degeneracy [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) flags.** "No vertex zero set contains $S$" removes every four-subset containing $S$ regardless of whether the vertex has four zeros or more, so this cut (unlike the Jacobi enumeration's "exact vanishing set"), does not lose the degenerate vertices. Worth recording, because it is the one respect in which this family is better founded than the one it is being compared to.

## R3, REFUTED as stated: the second of the two is not unconditional

**The right-hand sides are right.** For row $i$ and root $r$ the assembled right-hand side is $2\big[(n-2)\nu_r^2+2\nu_i\nu_r-\nu_i^2+\nu_i\theta\big]$; at $r=i$ this is $2\nu_i\big[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta\big]$ and at $r\ne i$ it stays genuinely quadratic in $\nu$ and affine in $\theta$, so "three clean, six quadratic" is correct. The screen reproduces all nine at all three sphere products to $\le4\times10^{-12}$.

**The first is unconditional.** $\nu_1>0$ at any nonzero traceless $W$, and with $\theta>0$ the bracket is positive, so $2\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_1+\theta]>0$ needs no hypothesis beyond $\theta>0$. The three-subset $\{122,123,133\}$ is genuinely forbidden.

**The third is right.** $\nu_3<0$, so the product vanishes iff $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert$, which is the $\Pi$-bound of [Lemma L107](../findings/bounds.md) attained. Correct as recorded.

**The second is not unconditional, and this record has already been corrected on exactly this point once.** $2\nu_2[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]$ vanishes when $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$, which requires $\nu_2<0$, and $\nu_2<0$ is entirely available at $d=3$. Tracelessness forces $\nu_1>0>\nu_3$ and says nothing about the middle value; kernel-freeness excludes only $\nu_2=0$; and [Lemma L35](../findings/stratum-two-negative.md) does not help, because it caps $\theta$ only when at most **two** eigenvalues counted with multiplicity are negative, whereas $\nu_2<0$ at $d=3$ with every $m_i\ge3$ gives at least six. When $\nu_2<0$ the degeneracy sits at $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert<(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert$, strictly inside the $\Pi$-bound, so it is not excluded by $\Pi$-strictness either. **[DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) records this as error of fact (ii) against the first family ("'Vanishing exactly at $\Pi$-saturation' is false when $\nu_2<0$") and the double-root section walks into it from the other direction.** The correct statement is that the second identity is unconditional *under $\nu_2>0$*, which is the same hypothesis the $179$ already carries.

## R4, the numbers are right, the attribution is wrong, and the honest reading is worse than recorded

**Recount, done by hand and then screened.** $\binom{10}4=210$. The six first-family four-subsets with right-hand side $\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_j+\theta]$ give $204$. The §G3 pair cut removes every four-subset containing both $\{123\}$ and $\{133\}$; $28$ contain both, of which $3$ were already among the six, so $204-25=179$. Adding the two unconditional three-subsets gives $173$; adding all nine gives $130$; adding the three quadratic first-family four-subsets on top gives $128$. **Every figure in the record reproduces.**

**But $\{122,123,133\}$ cuts nothing, and it is the only genuinely unconditional one.** Every one of its seven four-subsets contains both $123$ and $133$, so all seven were already removed by the §G3 pair cut. Applied alone to the $179$ it returns $179$. The whole $179\to173$ step is done by $\{112,123,233\}$: of its seven four-subsets one contains $\{123,133\}$ and was already gone, and the other six are new. Confirmed both by hand and by the screen, $(1,1)$ alone gives $179$, $(2,2)$ alone gives $173$.

> **So the sentence "This time the two doing the unconditional work are the proved ones, which is the difference worth recording" is false in both halves.** Only one of the two does any work, and it is the one whose right-hand side is *not* shown nonzero without $\nu_2>0$. The pattern the section claims to have escaped (the proved identities cutting nothing and the cut resting on the unproved ones), is the pattern it reproduces, one identity narrower.

**The $\nu_2>0$ hypothesis is invisible in the record and it is doing all the work.** The $179$ itself needs it, since the §G3 pair cut is derived at $\nu_1>\nu_2>0>\nu_3$; the double-root step needs it; and [plan.md](../plan.md) states "the proved bound is $210\to204\to179\to173$ supports" with no hypotheses attached at all. Dropping $\nu_2>0$ and keeping only kernel-freeness, $\theta>0$ and $\Pi$-strictness, the chain I get is $210\to206$ (four of the six first-family identities survive) $\to199$ (the $\nu_1$ double root, which now *does* cut seven) $\to192$ (the $\nu_3$ double root under $\Pi$-strict). **$192$, not $173$, is the figure that carries no sign hypothesis on $\nu_2$.**

**The control does survive $173$, and it does not survive $130$.** $\{113,123,223,333\}$ contains neither unconditional three-subset and does not contain $\{123,133\}$, so it survives $204$, $179$ and $173$, the record's claim is correct. It is killed by the all-nine cut, and specifically by the $(3,3)$ identity $\{113,123,223\}$, whose right-hand side vanishes exactly at $\Pi$ attained. That is internally consistent, since the $130$ is asserted only under $\Pi$-strict, and a symmetric pair attains $\Pi$. **But it is worth saying out loud what that means: the hypothesis needed to reach $130$ excludes every sphere product, which the screen confirms attains $\Pi$ exactly at $S^3\times S^4$, $S^3\times S^5$ and $S^4\times S^5$ alike.** The route to $130$ is a route on the complement of the family the conjecture is about.
