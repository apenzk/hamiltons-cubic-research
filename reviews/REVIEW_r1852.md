## 1. The Kneser coordinates

On $Q=\{1<2<3<4\}$ with $a=W_{12,34}$, $b=W_{13,24}$, $c=W_{14,23}$, Bianchi reads $a-b+c=0$. The brief's definition gives, in sorted orientation, $X_{(12|34)}=\tfrac12(b+c)$, $X_{(13|24)}=\tfrac12(W_{12,34}+W_{14,32})=\tfrac12(a-c)$, $X_{(14|23)}=\tfrac12(W_{12,43}+W_{13,42})=-\tfrac12(a+b)$. These sum to zero identically, before Bianchi is used. With Bianchi, $X_{(13|24)}-X_{(14|23)}=\tfrac32a$, $X_{(12|34)}-X_{(14|23)}=\tfrac32b$, $X_{(12|34)}-X_{(13|24)}=\tfrac32c$, so each $J_\pi$ is $\tfrac23$ of a signed difference of the other two $X$'s. The map $(b,c)\mapsto(X_{(12|34)},X_{(13|24)})$ has determinant $-\tfrac34$, so the $X$'s determine $\nu_Q$ and hence $B_f$. CONFIRMED.

- Correction (wording): $X_{ef}$ is invariant under swapping $e_0\leftrightarrow e_1$, $f_0\leftrightarrow f_1$ and $e\leftrightarrow f$ (pair symmetry of $W$ and two antisymmetries), so the qualifier "in the sorted-edge orientation" is vacuous for the zero sum. The orientation matters only for the signs in the $J$ formulas.

## 2. Cliques and the constant kappa_n

A clique of $KG(n,2)$ is a set of pairwise disjoint edges, that is a matching, and its triangles are rectangles. A maximum clique has $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edges, so at even $n$ it is a perfect matching (and every maximal clique is maximum, since two uncovered vertices would extend it). At odd $n$ the maximum cliques are near-perfect matchings, which the programme does not use. $\kappa_n$ is finite because every Kneser edge lies in some perfect matching, so the constraint $\max_ME(M)\le1$ bounds every $X$. The bound $24T\le24\kappa_nt^3$ follows by homogeneity (cubic against quadratic) from the matching inequality, which holds in a globally maximal frame only, as stated. CONFIRMED at even $n$.

## 4. The CP^m calibration

- **Spectrum.** With curvature operator $2(m+1)$, $2$, $0$ on $\langle\omega\rangle$, $\mathfrak{su}(m)$, $\mathfrak u(m)^\perp$ and $\kappa=\operatorname{scal}/(n(n-1))=2(m+1)/(2m-1)$ (trace check: $2(m+1)+2(m^2-1)=2m(m+1)=\operatorname{scal}/2$), the Weyl eigenvalues are $\tfrac2{2m-1}\cdot\{2(m^2-1),\,m-2,\,-(m+1)\}$ with multiplicities $1$, $m^2-1$, $m^2-m$. At $m=2$ this is $(2,0,0,0,-1,-1)$ up to scale, the stated multiset. CONFIRMED.
- **Criticality and theta, built as operators** ([review_r1852_cpm_Q.py](../scripts/reviewers/review_r1852_cpm_Q.py), $Q=W^2+W^\#$ with $W^\#$ from the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ structure constants, normalised so $I^\#=(n-2)I$): at $m=3,4,6$ the residual $\lVert Q(W)-\theta W\rVert$ is below $4\cdot10^{-16}$, $\theta$ equals $\sqrt{(m+1)(2m-1)/(3m(m-1))}$ to six places, the spectrum is as above, and $\operatorname{tr}W^3=0.544614$, $0.572827$, $0.580604$. CONFIRMED.
- **The table** ([review_r1852_cpm.py](../scripts/reviewers/review_r1852_cpm.py)): $\theta$, $\operatorname{tr}W^3$, $\theta\lambda_{\max}$, $E=\theta-\tfrac32\operatorname{tr}W^3$ and $\varepsilon_n$ reproduce at every row $m=5$ to $8$. $E(\mathbb{CP}^6)=0.054056>\varepsilon_{12}=0.022473$. CONFIRMED.
- **Upgrade: $\tfrac3{n+2}$ is an analytic lower bound at every $m$.** In a unitary frame $(e_1,Je_1,\dots)$ the Weyl diagonal is $4-\kappa$ on the $m$ holomorphic planes and $1-\kappa$ on the other $2m(m-1)$, so the diagonal mass is $\tfrac{18m(m-1)(2m-1)}{(2m-1)^2}$ against $\lVert W\rVert^2=\tfrac{12m(m^2-1)(2m-1)}{(2m-1)^2}$, a ratio of $\tfrac3{2(m+1)}=\tfrac3{n+2}$ (reproduced numerically as $0.2143$ at $m=6$). So $t_{\max}(\mathbb{CP}^m)^2\ge\tfrac3{n+2}$ at every $m$. Equality is measured at $m=4,5$ only.
- **Correction to "down to $t^2=\tfrac3{14}$".** $\mathbb{CP}^6$ blocks the reduction on the region $t_{\max}^2\le\tau$ for every $\tau\ge t_{\max}(\mathbb{CP}^6)^2$. If $t_{\max}(\mathbb{CP}^6)^2$ exceeded $\tfrac3{14}$ the blocked range would start higher. Correct statement: "down to $t_{\max}(\mathbb{CP}^6)^2$, which is at least $\tfrac3{14}$ (unitary frame) and equals it if the $m=4,5$ pattern holds". The logical point (the sufficient condition $E<\varepsilon_{12}$ fails at a critical point of value $0.925<\theta_0$) is exact and unaffected.
- **Correction to "the loss is L198's step, $\theta\lambda_{\max}=0.78$".** The step is $\theta\operatorname{tr}W^3\le1$, via $\theta\lambda_k^3\le\lambda_k^2$ termwise. At $\mathbb{CP}^6$, $\theta\operatorname{tr}W^3=0.537$, a slack of $0.463$, split by eigenspace as $0.157$ (top eigenvalue, the part $\theta\lambda_{\max}=0.78$ measures), $0.077$ ($\mathfrak{su}(6)$) and $0.229$ (the negative eigenspace, where $\theta\lambda^3<0$ is compared with $\lambda^2>0$). The attribution to L198's step is right, and the cited number accounts for a third of the loss. Most of the loss sits on the negative eigenvalues.

## 5. Large E and good frames

- **The $t^2$ lower bound is valid.** The ascent multiplies orthogonal matrices by $\exp$ of skew generators and evaluates $t^2$ exactly in that frame, so each value is attained and bounds $t_{\max}^2$ from below. Reproduced at $n=8$, $c=\tfrac12$: $P=0.5253$, $E=0.3924$, $t^2=0.5408$, $0.5421$, $0.5420$, $0.5421$ over the four starts. CONFIRMED.
- **Calibrations.** The balanced product ($S^m\times S^m$, eigenvalues $\propto m$ on $m(m-1)$ and $-(m-1)$ on $m^2$) has $\operatorname{tr}W^3=1/\sqrt{m^2(m-1)(2m-1)}$ at unit norm, so $E=\theta-O(n^{-2})\approx\theta_0$ at $t_{\max}=1$, given that its value is $\theta_0$. The $k$-sum heuristic ($E\approx\theta_0/\sqrt k$, $t_{\max}\gtrsim1/\sqrt k$) is sound to leading order when pairwise overlaps are $O(n^{-2})$ and triple cross terms carry random signs. PLAUSIBLE as a heuristic.
- **Overreach 1: the tensors built fail the matching family in their own frame.** The screen builds $W=B_f$ with $t^2=0$ in its own frame and $\max_ME(M)=0.063$ (n = 8). The matching inequality $E(M)\le t^2$ then fails in that frame. The configurations that "pass the matching family" are $A+B_f$ with a diagonal $A$ of mass $t^2\ge\max_ME(M)$, and the screen built none. Correction: "the capped four-set parts, taken alone, have frames with half their mass on the diagonal". Adding $A$ of mass $0.063$ moves $E$ and $t_{\max}^2$ by amounts the screen does not measure.
- **Overreach 2: the tensors are not critical points.** The candidate is a statement about critical points. The screens exhibit two non-critical tensors (the first start at each $n$) with large $E$ and large $t_{\max}^2$, which is consistent with the candidate and bears on it no further. "What would prove the node is the statement these screens support" should read "the statement these screens are consistent with". Likewise "the configurations that pass ... are near-diagonal tensors" generalises from two tensors.
- **Overreach 3: "near-diagonal".** A frame with $t^2\ge0.54$ (n = 8) or $0.45$ (n = 10) has half its mass on the diagonal, which the table says and "near-diagonal" overstates.
- **Missing constraint on $\tau$.** At $n=12$, $\mathbb{CP}^6$ is a critical point with $E>\varepsilon_{12}$, so the frame-free candidate can hold only with $\tau\le t_{\max}(\mathbb{CP}^6)^2$ (at least $\tfrac3{14}$). At $n\le10$, $\varepsilon_n<0$ and $\mathbb{CP}^4$, $\mathbb{CP}^5$ have $E>\varepsilon_n$, so there too $\tau$ is capped by their $t_{\max}^2$. The section should state this, since it fixes the largest region the candidate can clear.
- **Table column.** "cap $\max_ME(M)$" lists the realised $0.063$ at $n=8$ but the cap $0.05$ at $n=10$, where the realised value is $0.0437$. Correction: list one or the other.

## 6. The block equations

- **Product coordinates.** $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=4X_\pi^2+3J_\pi^2$ is a quadratic form in the linear coordinates $(X_\pi,J_\pi)$ of the two-dimensional $V_Q$ (invertible from $(b,c)$), so it polarises to $\langle u,v\rangle=4X_uX_v+3J_uJ_v$. The element with coordinates $(X_1X_2,\tfrac12J_1J_2)$ relative to the pairing $(e_2|e_3)$ of $Q_{23}$ pairs with every $\nu_3$ as $4X_1X_2X_3+\tfrac32J_1J_2J_3=\tau$, and $\nu_1\circ\nu_2\in V_{Q_{23}}$ by r1846. Nondegeneracy fixes the element. The product $J_1J_2$ changes sign with the orientations of $e_2,e_3$ only, as $J_3$ does, so the coordinates are orientation-consistent. CONFIRMED.
- **The $\chi_Q$ component.** $Q(A+B_f)=A\circ A+2A\circ B_f+B_f\circ B_f$. $A\circ A$ is diagonal (trivial character, the trivial-character entries of a symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2$ being the diagonal ones). $A\circ\nu_{Q'}$ carries $\chi_{Q'}$, which leaves $2A\circ\nu_Q$. In $B_f\circ B_f$, $\chi_{Q_1}\chi_{Q_2}=\chi_Q$ needs $Q_1\triangle Q_2=Q$, so $\lvert Q_1\cap Q_2\rvert=2$, $Q_1=e\cup g$, $Q_2=f\cup g$ with $(e|f)$ a pairing of $Q$ and $g$ disjoint from $Q$. Ordered pairs give the factor $2$ over unordered pairings. The characters are those of the full sign group $\{\pm1\}^n\subset O(n)$, which separates $Q$ from $Q^c$ at $n=8$. The component lies in $V_Q$ because $Q(W)$ is an algebraic curvature tensor and its Ricci part has no disjoint-index entries. CONFIRMED.
- Notes: $L_A$ is used without definition (it is $A\circ\cdot$ restricted to $V_Q$). Summing the three pairings' terms needs the change of coordinates between pairings (section 1), which the phrase "each term a vector along its own pairing's axes" leaves implicit.
