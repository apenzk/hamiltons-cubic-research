## A. L126 at a sphere product

**The record** ([second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) lines 1487 to 1492): *"a four-set block meets [the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace of $L_e$] exactly when $\Sigma_{ijkl}=\theta^2$ ... At $S^p\times S^q$, $n=p+q$, the nonzero four-set blocks are the $(2,2)$-splits, with the common value $\mu_{p,q}=\frac{n-2}{2(p-1)(q-1)}$, and $\mu_{p,q}=\tfrac12\iff\frac1{p-1}+\frac1{q-1}=1$."* L121 (line 989): *"Let $A=M_{ij}+M_{kl}$, $B=M_{ik}+M_{jl}$, $C=M_{il}+M_{jk}$ ... Spectrum $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA}$."*

**The split and the pairs.** `hi : i ∈ T`, `hj : j ∈ T`, `hk : k ∉ T`, `hl : l ∉ T` with `i ≠ j`, `k ≠ l` is a $(2,2)$-split, the cross distinctness being automatic. The `let`s give $A=w_{ij}+w_{kl}$, $B=w_{ik}+w_{jl}$, $C=w_{il}+w_{jk}$, the record's letters with $M_{ab}=w_{ab}$, since `diagOp w` sends $\varphi_{ab}$ to $w_{ab}\varphi_{ab}$ at symmetric $w$.

**The threshold.** $L_M$ on the four-set space has eigenvalues $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma$ and $L_e=L_M/\theta$ at unit $M$, so $\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma/\theta=\tfrac12$ is exactly $\Sigma=\theta^2$, which is `L126_threshold`'s left side. By hand: $A=t(\tfrac q{p-1}+\tfrac p{q-1})$, $B=C=-2t$, so $\Sigma=(A-B)^2=t^2\big(\tfrac{(n-1)(n-2)}{(p-1)(q-1)}\big)^2$. The unit norm gives $t^2=\tfrac{2(p-1)(q-1)}{pq(n-1)(n-2)}$ and $\theta^2=\tfrac{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)}{(n-2)pq}$, so $\Sigma/\theta^2=\big(\tfrac{n-2}{(p-1)(q-1)}\big)^2$, which is `fourSet_sigma`.

**The value of $\theta$ and the norm.** `theta` is $P/\lVert R\rVert^3$ and `theta_sphereProduct_value` sets it to `thetaSplit p q` $=\sqrt{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)/((n-2)pq)}$, Nienhaus's $\theta_{p,q}$ ($10/9=\theta_0(6)^2$ at $(3,3)$), with no `sorry` below it. `normSq` is `innerOp` over ordered pairs with the $\tfrac12$ in `inner2`. The proof's `ht2` shows `normSq = 1` is $\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}w_{ab}^2=\sum_{a<b}w_{ab}^2=1$, the record's unit norm on $\Lambda^2$. This matters because $\Sigma$ is quadratic in $w$ and $\theta$ is scale-free.

**Hypotheses.** `hp`, `hq` ($p,q\ge2$) are the record's scope and are used for the divisions. `ht` ($t>0$) fixes the sign that `theta_sphereProduct_value` needs. `hn` fixes the scale. None is vacuous: the witness in the table meets them all. At $(3,3)$ both sides of the iff are true ($\tfrac12+\tfrac12=1$). At $T=\{0,1\}$, $n=6$ ($p=2$, $q=4$) both are false, and $\mu_{2,4}=\tfrac23$ is the record's value.

**Not in the Lean, and not claimed.** That the non-$(2,2)$ four-sets give $\Sigma=0$ (true by hand: a $(3,1)$ or $(4,0)$ split has $A=B=C$), and that $\tfrac12G$ is the four-set block of $L_M$ (the L121 (statement) row declares it untyped).

## B. L146's dominance

**The record** ([second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) lines 1926 to 1932): *"$\alpha\,h_a$ on $e_a\wedge e_b$, $a\in B_1$, $b\in B_2$; $-\beta(h_a+h_{a'})$ on $e_a\wedge e_{a'}$ ... Tracelessness forces $\max_{a\ne a'}\lvert h_a+h_{a'}\rvert\ge\tfrac{m-2}{m-1}\max_a\lvert h_a\rvert$, sharp at the spike ... while $\beta/\alpha=\tfrac m{m-2}$ covers that ratio with room"*, and *"at $m=5$, $h=k=(1,0,0,0,-1)$ gives $\tfrac34$ against the formula's $\tfrac58$, and at $m=6$, $h=k=(1,-\tfrac15,\dots)$ gives $\tfrac45$ against $\tfrac{12}{25}$ ... the cross branch caps at $\sqrt{m-2}/m<1/\sqrt{m-1}$."*

**`pair_ge` implies the max bound.** Apply it at an $a$ maximising $\lvert h_a\rvert$: some $b\ne a$ has $\lvert h_a+h_b\rvert\ge\tfrac{m-2}{m-1}\max\lvert h\rvert$, so the maximum over pairs is at least that. The proof picks the partner maximising $\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}(h_a)(h_a+h_b)$ against the mean $\tfrac{m-2}{m-1}h_a$ and is sound. The statement at every $a$ is stronger than the record's. Witness: $h=(3,-1,-1,-1)$, $a=0$, $b=1$, equality $2=\tfrac23\cdot3$.

**$\alpha$ and $\beta$, re-derived by hand.** $T=h\owedge g_q$ is diagonal with value $h_a$ on cross planes and $0$ in both blocks, $\mathrm{Ric}(T)=m\,h$ on $B_1$ and $0$ on $B_2$, scalar $0$. For traceless diagonal $A$, $A\owedge g$ has value $A_a+A_b$ on $e_a\wedge e_b$ and Ricci $(n-2)A$. So the Weyl part is $T-\tfrac1{n-2}\mathrm{Ric}\owedge g$, with value $h_a(1-\tfrac m{2m-2})=\tfrac{m-2}{2(m-1)}h_a$ on cross planes, $-\tfrac m{2(m-1)}(h_a+h_{a'})$ in $B_1$ and $0$ in $B_2$. **Both constants are right.** The second copy $k$ contributes $\alpha k_b$ on cross planes, so the joint cross branch is $\alpha(h_a+k_b)$, as the record says.

**`inblock_dominates`.** $\beta\cdot\tfrac{m-2}{m-1}=\tfrac m{m-1}\alpha\ge\alpha$ is correct. With `pair_ge` it gives $\beta\max_{a\ne a'}\lvert h_a+h_{a'}\rvert\ge\alpha\max_a\lvert h_a\rvert$, the in-block branch beating the single-copy cross branch. The composition is two lines and is not a Lean theorem, a gap in the word "proved" on the row, not in any statement.

**`spike_sharp`.** It states $\max(\lvert(m-1)-1\rvert,\lvert-2\rvert)=\tfrac{m-2}{m-1}\lvert m-1\rvert$ at $m\ge4$, two reals. The docstring's *"at the spike $h=(m-1,-1,\dots,-1)$ every pair sum is at most ..."* is true (the pair sums are $m-2$ and $-2$, the top entry $m-1$) but the vector is not built. $m\ge4$ is needed: at $m=3$ the spike $(2,-1,-1)$ has pair maximum $2$ against top entry $2$, ratio $1\ne\tfrac12$. The record's "sharp at the spike" carries no $m$, and its hypotheses box sets $m\ge5$, so nothing is contradicted.

**The integer scalings.** At $m=5$, $\alpha=\tfrac38$, $\beta=\tfrac58$: $8\alpha(h_a+k_b)=3(h_a+h_b)$ with maximum modulus $6$ at $a=b=0$, so $\tfrac68=\tfrac34$. $8\beta(h_a+h_{a'})=5(h_a+h_{a'})$ with maximum $5$ over $a\ne a'$, so $\tfrac58$. At $m=6$, $\alpha=\tfrac25$, $\beta=\tfrac35$, and $h=h'/5$ with $h'=(5,-1,\dots,-1)$: $25\alpha(h_a+k_b)=2(h'_a+h'_b)$, maximum $20$, so $\tfrac45$. $25\beta(h_a+h_{a'})=3(h'_a+h'_{a'})$, maximum $12$, so $\tfrac{12}{25}$. `cross5` and `cross6` allow $a=b$, correct since $a\in B_1$ and $b\in B_2$ index different blocks. `inblock5` and `inblock6` require $a\ne b$, correct. `traceless` checks both vectors.

**Is "$\tfrac34$ against $\tfrac58$" what these compare?** Yes. At $h=k$ the display's $\beta\max_{a\ne a'}\lvert h_a+h_{a'}\rvert$ is unambiguous and equals the $B_2$ in-block value too. The cross value is attained on a diagonal operator, so it is an eigenvalue modulus, and $\lVert H\rVert_{op}\ge\tfrac34>\tfrac58$. The display is homogeneous and the hypotheses box says "at every norm", so the unnormalised comparison is the right one. That the branch values are eigenvalues of the Weyl image is quoted, not derived, which the L146 row declares owed.

**`cross_cap`.** $\sqrt{m-2}/m<1/\sqrt{m-1}$ at $m\ge3$ is proved. That $\sqrt{m-2}/m$ is the cross branch's cap at unit Frobenius norm is not typed. By hand, $\lVert H\rVert_F^2=\tfrac{m(m-2)}{2(m-1)}(\lVert h\rVert^2+\lVert k\rVert^2)$ (the cross terms vanish by tracelessness), $\max\lvert h_a\rvert\le\sqrt{(m-1)/m}\,\lVert h\rVert$, and Cauchy–Schwarz give $\alpha(\lvert h_a\rvert+\lvert k_b\rvert)\le\sqrt{m-2}/m$, attained at two spikes. So the record's number is right.

## C. L123 by averaging

**The record** ([second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) line 1444): *"Let $F$ be an orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$, $\Gamma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\pm1)\}\subset O(n)$ its full sign group, and $X\in\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ a vector lying in a single $\Gamma$-character space. If $R$ has zero diagonal in the frame $F$, then $\langle X,R\circ X\rangle=0$."* The proof identifies the trivial character space with the diagonal entries.

**Zero diagonal.** `hR0 : ∀ i j, inner2 (R (phi i j)) (phi i j) = 0` is $R_{\alpha\alpha}=0$ in the $\varphi$ basis, and $\varphi_\alpha\odot\varphi_\alpha$ spans the trivial character space. The $i=j$ instances are vacuous since $\varphi_{ii}=0$. The frame form $\langle R(O\varphi_{ij}O^{\mathsf T}),O\varphi_{ij}O^{\mathsf T}\rangle=0$ is zero diagonal on the frame bivectors $f_i\wedge f_j$, $f_i=Oe_i$.

**The character hypothesis.** `hX : act (signMat ε) X = c ε • X` for every sign vector, with $c(\varepsilon)^2=1$, is "$g\cdot X=\chi_X(g)X$". $c$ need not be a homomorphism, which is weaker than the record. `hc` is demanded at every $\varepsilon:\iota\to\mathbb R$, not only at sign vectors. It is satisfiable (define $c$ as $1$ off the sign vectors), so this is a usability point only. **`hX` is an equation of maps on all of $\mathrm{Mat}_n$**, as the file's docstring says. For the canonical extension $X\circ\mathop{\mathrm{skew}}$ it is equivalent to the $\Lambda^2$ condition, because conjugation by an orthogonal $g$ commutes with the skew part, and `innerOp` and `foxTri` read their arguments only on the $\varphi$ family. That bridge is not typed, the same gap r1441 recorded for `L120_block`.

**The added hypotheses.** `hRs` and `hXs` (both preserve skew matrices) hold for every element of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ read as an operator on $\Lambda^2$. Symmetry of $R$ and $X$ is not assumed, a generalisation.

**The averaging proof.** `sum_four_sign`: at $k\ne l$ with $Y_{kl}=Y_{lk}=0$, when $k$ occurs in $\{a,c\}$ zero or two times its multiplicity in $\{k,l,a,c\}$ is odd and flipping it negates each term. When it occurs once, the exclusion of $(a,c)\in\{(k,l),(l,k)\}$ puts $l$ outside $\{a,c\}$, so $l$ has multiplicity one and flipping it negates. Both branches are exhaustive and the case $a=c$ is covered. `avg_phi_zero` reduces the $(a,c)$ entry of $\sum_g g\cdot R(\varphi_{kl})$ to exactly that sum, using `signConj_phi` and $Y=R\varphi_{kl}$ skew. `Lpair_act`: `innerOp_act` gives $\langle gX,g(R\circ X)\rangle=\langle X,R\circ X\rangle$, `foxProd_act` gives $g(R\circ X)=(gR)\circ(gX)$, and $gX=cX$ with bilinearity gives $c^2\langle X,(gR)\circ X\rangle$. `L123` then has $2^n\langle X,R\circ X\rangle=\langle X,A\circ X\rangle$ with $A=\sum_g g\cdot R$ zero on bivectors, and `foxTri_congr_on_skew` replaces $A$ by $0$. All sound.

**`L123_frame`.** $O\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\varepsilon)O^{\mathsf T}$ is the sign flip in the frame of $O$'s columns, the record's $\Gamma$ for frame $F$. The proof conjugates by $O^{\mathsf T}$ and applies `L123`. `hO'` follows from `hO` for square matrices and is harmless.

**Witness.** $n=4$, $X=\varphi_{12}\odot\varphi_{34}$ (character $\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3\varepsilon_4$), $R=\varphi_{12}\odot\varphi_{13}$, both composed with the skew part. $R$ has zero diagonal, both preserve $\Lambda^2$, `hX` holds with $c=\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3\varepsilon_4$ on sign vectors and $1$ elsewhere. For the frame form, $O$ the rotation by $\pi/4$ in the $(1,2)$-plane with $R$, $X$ conjugated by $O$.
