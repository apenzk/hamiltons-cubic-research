## The subalgebra, and why the claim must be stated up to rotation

**$\mathcal S_{jk}=\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$ is a subalgebra**, the fixed space of $\ker(\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k)$: a four-set character $\chi_Q$ is $-1$ on the flip at an index of $Q\setminus\{j,k\}$, and a pair character $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b\ne\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$ is $-1$ on the flip at an index of $\{a,b\}\setminus\{j,k\}$ or on $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$ with $c\notin\{a,b,j,k\}$, each lying in the kernel. $\dim\mathcal S_{jk}=\tfrac{n(n-3)}2+n-3$, confirmed at $n=8,12$.

**Unlike $\mathcal S_Q$, it contains maximisers with a nonzero block component.** A rotation $R_\varphi$ in the $(j,k)$-plane commutes with every flip outside $\{j,k\}$ and with $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k=-I$ on that plane, so it preserves $\mathcal S_{jk}$, and it carries a diagonal tensor to one whose pair component is $\sin\varphi$ times the orbit direction $\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D$ at first order ([F37](../findings/fence-posts.md)). **So the statement to aim at is: every critical point of $P$ in $\mathcal S_{jk}$ with $\theta>\sqrt{3/2}$ is a $(j,k)$-rotation of a diagonal one.** *This is the stratum where the orbit degeneracy lives, which L220's four-set stratum avoided.*

A screen, not a proof.*

## The handles for a derivation

- **The eigenvalue condition** is [L218](../findings/second-order.md)'s form: at unit $x\perp\mathbf1$ representing $\nu$, $\langle L_D\nu,\nu\rangle=\tfrac12\langle K,G_x\rangle$ with $\lVert G_x\rVert^2=3$, so the critical equation $L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$ gives $\langle K,G_x\rangle=\theta$, a pinning of the component of $D$ along the projection of $G_x$ to $\mathcal D_n$.
- **The rotation freedom** gives one first-order condition, $\langle\nu,\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D\rangle=0$, at the angle minimising $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, which removes exactly the orbit direction.
- **$P(\nu)=0$** on a single block, as in L220.

*Next: derive $\nu\circ\nu$ for the pair block in closed form, which decides whether L220's chain (pin, pair with the pinned direction, bound the coupling) transfers.*

## $\nu\circ\nu$ for the pair block is L218's coefficient matrix, and the pinning of L220 transfers

**Closed form** (fitted exactly at $n=7$, residual $10^{-32}$, and to be derived). For $\nu\in V_{jk}$ with coordinates $x\in\mathbb R^{n-2}$, $\sum_ix_i=0$, in the basis $e_{ji}\odot e_{ki}$ of common norm $\sqrt2$ (so $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2\lVert x\rVert^2$), $\nu\circ\nu\in\mathcal D_n$ has sectional matrix
$$K_{ji}=K_{ki}=x_i^2,\qquad K_{jk}=-\lVert x\rVert^2,\qquad K_{il}=2x_ix_l\quad(i,l\notin\{j,k\}),$$
**which is $G_x$ of [L218](../findings/second-order.md)'s proof, scaled.** Its rows sum to zero by $\sum x=0$, $\lVert G_x\rVert^2=3\lVert x\rVert^4$, so **$\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, the same constant as on a four-set block**, and $P(\nu)=0$.

**The pinning.** $\langle L_D\nu,\nu\rangle=\langle D,\nu\circ\nu\rangle$, so the critical equation $L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$ gives, with $\hat g=\nu\circ\nu/\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert$,
$$\langle D,\hat g\rangle=\frac{\theta\lVert\nu\rVert^2/2}{\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2}=\frac\theta{\sqrt3},$$
**exactly L220's $\lVert q\rVert=\theta/\sqrt3$**, with the plane $\Pi_Q$ replaced by the line through $\hat g$.

**Why the chain cannot close as in L220, and what must replace it.** A $(j,k)$-rotation of the balanced product lies in $\mathcal S_{jk}$ with $\nu\ne0$ and $\theta=\Theta_n>\sqrt{3/2}$, so no argument using only the pinning and bounds on $P(\hat g)$ and the coupling can give $\theta\le\sqrt{3/2}$. **The minimising rotation must enter**: at the angle minimising $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, $\nu\perp\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D$, which in coordinates says $x\perp(K_{ji}-K_{ki})_i$. *And $\hat g$ lies in no subalgebra of $\mathcal D_n$, so L220's step 6 gains the term $2\langle D,\cdot\rangle$ through $\hat g\circ\hat g$, which a derivation must control.* **Next: $P(\hat g)$, $\hat g\circ\hat g$, and what the rotation condition does to $\langle K,G_x\rangle$.**

## $P$ on the pinned direction, derived

**For $G_x$ as above, $P(G_x)=3p_1^3-15p_1p_2+10s_3^2$** with $p_1=\lVert x\rVert^2$, $p_2=\sum x_i^4$, $s_3=\sum x_i^3$, and $\lVert G_x\rVert^2=3p_1^2$ (checked against the definition at $n=6,9,12$ to ten digits). *Derivation: the cubes give $2p_3-p_1^3+8e$ with $e=\sum_{i<l}x_i^3x_l^3=\tfrac12(s_3^2-p_3)$, the triangles through $\{j,k\}$ give $-p_1p_2$, those through one of $j,k$ give $4e$, and those outside give $8e_3(x^2)$ with $6e_3=p_1^3-3p_1p_2+2p_3$, and the $p_3$ terms cancel.* **So at unit $x$, $P(\hat g)=\frac{3-15p_2+10s_3^2}{3\sqrt3}\le\frac{3-5p_2}{3\sqrt3}<\frac1{\sqrt3}$**, by $s_3^2\le p_1p_2$. *Where L220 had $P(q)\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q\rVert^3$, the pinned direction here carries at most $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, smaller, which is what a transferred chain would use, and the rotation condition is what it would still need.*

## The chain without the rotation condition, screened: far from closing

*With the pinning $\langle D,\hat g\rangle=\theta/\sqrt3$, $D=\tfrac\theta{\sqrt3}\hat g+r$, and the pairing of the $\mathcal D_n$ equation with $\hat g$, $\tfrac{\theta^2}{\sqrt3}=\tfrac{\theta^2}3P(\hat g)+\tfrac{2\theta}{\sqrt3}\langle r,\hat g\circ\hat g\rangle+\langle L_{\hat g}r,r\rangle+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2N$. Over 300 unit $x$ (random and two-valued) the constants are $\sup P(\hat g)=0.096,0.289,0.371$ at $n=8,12,16$, $\sup\lVert\pi_\perp(\hat g\circ\hat g)\rVert=0.72$ to $0.78$, and the coupling on $\hat g^\perp$ reaches $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ at every $n$, [L218](../findings/second-order.md)'s constant.* **Bounding the three terms by these suprema leaves slack $+0.43$ to $+0.49$ at $\theta=\Theta_n$ and at $\sqrt{3/2}$**, so the decoupled chain admits both, as it must, since rotated maximisers live in this stratum. **The rotation condition has to cut roughly half a unit from the coupling and cross terms**, and the identity F37 supplies does not do it by itself: splitting $L_W(\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}W)=\tfrac\theta2\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}W$ by character and pairing its $V_{jk}$ part with $\nu$ gives $\langle\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}\nu,\hat g\rangle=0$ at EVERY critical point, since $\langle L_D\,\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D,\nu\rangle=\tfrac\theta2\langle\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D,\nu\rangle$ cancels the right side whatever the angle. *So it is F37's orbit identity, carrying no information about the minimising angle, and the rotation condition $\langle\nu,\mathrm{ad}_{E_{jk}}D\rangle=0$ has to enter through a second-order quantity, the minimum of $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ over the angle, as I102's second variation did.*

## The minimising rotation, at second order: the pair mass is bounded by the row asymmetry of $D$

**Derived.** With $\xi=E_{jk}$ and $W(\varphi)=e^{\varphi\xi}\cdot W$, the character of $\xi$ is $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$, so $\xi\cdot D\in V_{jk}$, $\xi\cdot\nu\in\mathcal D_n$, and $\xi^2$ preserves each summand. The pair part along the curve is $\nu(\varphi)=\nu+\varphi\,\xi\cdot D+\tfrac{\varphi^2}2\xi^2\cdot\nu+O(\varphi^3)$, so
$$\lVert\nu(\varphi)\rVert^2=\lVert\nu\rVert^2+2\varphi\langle\nu,\xi\cdot D\rangle+\varphi^2\big(\lVert\xi\cdot D\rVert^2-\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2\big)+O(\varphi^3),$$
using $\langle\nu,\xi^2\cdot\nu\rangle=-\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2$. **At an angle minimising $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$: $\langle\nu,\xi\cdot D\rangle=0$ and $\lVert\xi\cdot D\rVert^2\ge\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2$.**

**The constants** (screened at $n=8$ to eight digits, to be derived from the bracket): $\lVert\xi\cdot D\rVert^2=2\sum_i(K_{ki}-K_{ji})^2$ and $\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2=4\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. Hence
$$\lVert\nu\rVert^2\ \le\ \tfrac12\sum_{i\notin\{j,k\}}(K_{ki}-K_{ji})^2 .$$
**The pair component at the minimising angle is bounded by the antisymmetric part of rows $j$ and $k$ of $D$**, while the pinning $\langle D,\hat g\rangle=\theta/\sqrt3$ involves only the symmetric part $K_{ji}+K_{ki}$, $K_{jk}$ and the block off $\{j,k\}$, because $G_x$ is symmetric in $j$ and $k$. *So the two constraints act on orthogonal pieces of $D$, and together $\lVert D\rVert^2\ge\tfrac{\theta^2}3+\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, which with $\lVert D\rVert^2+\lVert\nu\rVert^2=1$ gives $\theta^2\le3(1-2\lVert\nu\rVert^2)$: a bound only once $\lVert\nu\rVert^2>\tfrac14$ at $\theta^2=\tfrac32$.* **This is the first inequality in the pair-block stratum that uses the angle and bounds $\nu$ by $D$; closing needs it inside the chain, where the antisymmetric piece of $r$ should couple weakly to $\hat g$.**

## The chain split by the $(j,k)$-swap, with the angle inequality inside: closer, not closed

*The swap of $j$ and $k$ acts on $\mathcal D_n$, $\hat g$ is swap-even, so $L_{\hat g}$ preserves the even and odd parts, $\langle r,\hat g\circ\hat g\rangle$ sees only the even part $r^+$, and the odd part of $r$ is the odd part $D^-$ of $D$, whose mass $\tfrac12\sum_i(K_{ki}-K_{ji})^2$ is at least $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ by the angle inequality. Screened over 200 unit $x$ at $n=8,12,16$: the coupling of $\hat g$ on the even part off $\hat g$ is at most $0.44$, $0.49$, $0.51$, and **on the odd part it is $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ at every $n$**. With the odd mass constrained to be at least $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, the chain's slack at $\nu\ne0$ is $+0.12$, $+0.29$, $+0.35$ at $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$ and $+0.25$, $+0.27$, $+0.26$ at $\Theta_n$.* **So splitting by parity halves the slack, and what remains is structural: [L218](../findings/second-order.md)'s top coupling lives on the swap-odd part, exactly where the angle inequality puts the mass the pair component needs.** *The next estimate has to tie the odd part's direction to $\nu$ itself, since the angle inequality uses only the odd part's size: the second variation's constant $2\sum(K_{ki}-K_{ji})^2$ weighs every odd direction equally, while the coupling singles out one.*

## The odd part of the critical equation fixes the odd coupling exactly

**Derived.** Split $D=D^++D^-$ by the $(j,k)$-swap. $\nu\circ\nu=G_x$ is swap-even, and $(D\circ D)^-=2D^+\circ D^-$, so the odd part of $D\circ D+\nu\circ\nu=\theta D$ reads
$$2L_{D^+}D^-=\theta D^- ,$$
**so $D^-$, when nonzero, is a $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvector of $L_{D^+}$ on the odd part.** With $D^+=\tfrac\theta{\sqrt3}\hat g+r^+$, pairing with $D^-$ gives $\tfrac\theta{\sqrt3}\langle L_{\hat g}D^-,D^-\rangle=\tfrac\theta2\lVert D^-\rVert^2-\langle L_{r^+}D^-,D^-\rangle$, **so the odd coupling in the chain equals $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert D^-\rVert^2-\tfrac{\sqrt3}\theta\langle L_{r^+}D^-,D^-\rangle$ exactly**, its worst-case value corrected by a term in $r^+$.

**Where $r^+=0$ the chain closes.** The chain becomes $\tfrac{\theta^2}{\sqrt3}=\tfrac{\theta^2}3P(\hat g)+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2(\lVert D^-\rVert^2+\lVert\nu\rVert^2)$, and the norm budget $\tfrac{\theta^2}3+\lVert D^-\rVert^2+\lVert\nu\rVert^2=1$ turns it into $\theta^2\big(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2-\tfrac{P(\hat g)}3\big)=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, so $\theta^2=\big(1-\tfrac{2P(\hat g)}{3\sqrt3}\big)^{-1}\le\big(1-\tfrac29\big)^{-1}=\tfrac97$ by $P(\hat g)<\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$. **So a critical point in $\mathcal S_{jk}$ whose even part is exactly $\tfrac\theta{\sqrt3}\hat g$ has $\theta^2\le\tfrac97<\tfrac32$.** *What remains is the three $r^+$ terms: $\tfrac{2\theta}{\sqrt3}\langle r^+,\hat g\circ\hat g\rangle+\langle L_{\hat g}r^+,r^+\rangle-\tfrac{\sqrt3}\theta\langle L_{r^+}D^-,D^-\rangle$, with the budget $\tfrac{\theta^2}3+\lVert r^+\rVert^2+\lVert D^-\rVert^2+\lVert\nu\rVert^2=1$.*

## With explicit constants the chain reopens: the $r^+$ terms are F67's shape inside the even part

## $L_{G_x}$ on the swap-odd part, derived: the rotation condition removes the $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ coupling

**Derived.** The swap-odd part of $\mathcal D_n$ is $\{Y:\ Y_{ja}=y_a,\ Y_{ka}=-y_a,\ \text{all else }0,\ \sum_ay_a=0\}$, of dimension $n-3$. With the diagonal product $(A\circ B)_{ab}=A_{ab}B_{ab}+\tfrac12\sum_c(A_{ac}B_{cb}+B_{ac}A_{cb})$ and $G=G_x$: $(G\circ Y)_{ja}=x_a^2y_a+\tfrac12\big(G_{jk}Y_{ka}+\sum_{c\ne a}Y_{jc}G_{ca}\big)=x_a^2y_a+\tfrac12\big(\lVert x\rVert^2y_a+2x_a(\langle x,y\rangle-x_ay_a)\big)$, so
$$L_{G_x}Y\ \longleftrightarrow\ y\mapsto\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2y+x\,\langle x,y\rangle ,$$
with the even part of $G\circ Y$ zero (checked against the definition at $n=7,10,13$). **So on the odd part $L_{\hat g}$ has eigenvalue $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ on the single direction $y=x$ and $\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$ on its $n-4$-dimensional complement**, which are exactly the two shape-independent eigenvalues of the spectrum table.

**The first-order rotation condition is $\langle x,y\rangle=0$.** $\xi\cdot D\in V_{jk}$ has coordinates proportional to $(K_{ki}-K_{ji})_i=-2y$, and $\nu$ has coordinates $x$, so $\langle\nu,\xi\cdot D\rangle=0$ says $D^-\perp$ the $x$-pattern. **At the minimising angle, therefore, $\langle L_{\hat g}D^-,D^-\rangle=\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}\lVert D^-\rVert^2$ exactly**, a third of the worst case the parity chain used.

**It does not close the decoupled chain, and the screen says where the failure lives.** *With the odd coupling $\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$ and the sampled even constants, the slack at $\sqrt{3/2}$ is $+0.35$, unchanged from $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, because the maximum sits at $\lVert\nu\rVert^2\to0$, $\lVert D^-\rVert^2\to0$ and $\lVert r^+\rVert\approx0.7$.* **That regime is a nearly diagonal critical point: as $\nu\to0$ the even equation tends to $D^+\circ D^+=\theta D^+$, and $\nu$ becomes a $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvector of $L_{D^+}$ on $V_{jk}$ orthogonal to the orbit direction.** *At a sphere product with $p,q\ge3$, $(p,q)\ne(3,3)$, [L27](../findings/nondegeneracy.md) makes the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace exactly the orbit tangent, so such a $\nu$ does not exist, and [L216](../findings/second-order.md) turns that gap into an isolation radius.* **So the pair-block stratum splits into small $\lVert\nu\rVert$, where the nondegeneracy of the diagonal critical points above $\sqrt{3/2}$ excludes it, and large $\lVert\nu\rVert$, where the chain with the reduced odd coupling should close; the boundary between them is set by L216's radius against the chain's slack as a function of $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$.**

**The split, measured.** *With the reduced odd coupling and the sampled even constants ($P\le0.371$, cross $0.782$, even coupling $0.508$), the chain's largest slack at $\sqrt{3/2}$ as a function of a floor on $\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ is $+0.353$ (floor $0$), $+0.278$ ($0.05$), $+0.192$ ($0.10$), $+0.089$ ($0.15$), $-0.051$ ($0.20$), $-0.392$ ($0.25$).* **So the decoupled chain closes the pair-block stratum wherever $\lVert\nu\rVert^2\ge0.2$, and the rest must come from the diagonal side.** *That remainder is not small: $\lVert\nu\rVert^2<0.2$ is a geodesic distance up to about $0.45$ from $\mathcal D_n$ after the minimising rotation, far beyond [L216](../findings/second-order.md)'s isolation radius ($0.0132$ at $n=12$ for the balanced product), so isolation alone does not meet the chain, and the joint (trust-region) treatment of the even terms, which the exact screen showed closes everything, is still what the small-$\nu$ regime needs.* **Owed for a lemma: the even constants derived rather than sampled, and a bridge for $0<\lVert\nu\rVert^2<0.2$.**

## $L_{G_x}$ on the swap-even part, derived: a Dirichlet form

**Derived.** A swap-even $E\in\mathcal D_n$ has $E_{ja}=E_{ka}=u_a$, $E_{jk}=\kappa$ and the block $S$ off $\{j,k\}$, with $\sum_au_a+\kappa=0$ and $\sum_bS_{ab}=-2u_a$, and $\lVert E\rVert^2=2\lVert u\rVert^2+\kappa^2+\sum_{a<b}S_{ab}^2$. The diagonal product gives $(G\circ E)_{ja}=-\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2u_a+\tfrac12(Sx^{2})_a+\tfrac12\kappa x_a^2+x_a\langle x,u\rangle$, $(G\circ E)_{jk}=-\lVert x\rVert^2\kappa+\langle x^2,u\rangle$ and $(G\circ E)_{ab}=2x_ax_bS_{ab}+x_a^2u_b+x_b^2u_a+x_a(Sx)_b+x_b(Sx)_a-(x_a^2+x_b^2)S_{ab}$, hence
$$\langle L_{G_x}E,E\rangle=-\lVert x\rVert^2\big(\lVert u\rVert^2+\kappa^2\big)+2\langle u,Sx^2\rangle+2\kappa\langle x^2,u\rangle+2\langle x,u\rangle^2+\lVert Sx\rVert^2-\sum_{a<b}(x_a-x_b)^2S_{ab}^2 ,$$
with $x^2$ the entrywise square (checked against the definition at $n=7,10,13$ to ten digits). **The last term is L57's Dirichlet energy with weights $(x_a-x_b)^2$, and it enters with a minus sign**, so the even coupling is small wherever $S$ lives on pairs where $x$ differs, and its large values need $S$ concentrated where $x$ is constant. *This is the explicit operator from which the even constants ($\lambda_{\max}\le0.51$ sampled) and the trust-region value are to be derived, and it is where L57's own Cauchy--Schwarz step should re-enter.*

## The even equation paired with $r^+$: a second relation, and with it the stratum closes by screen far below $\sqrt{3/2}$

**Derived.** Pairing the even equation $D^+\circ D^++D^-\circ D^-+G_x=\theta D^+$ with $r^+\perp\hat g$, and writing $a=\theta/\sqrt3$, $Q(r)=\langle L_{\hat g}r,r\rangle$, $C=\langle L_{r^+}D^-,D^-\rangle$:

## Constants for form B, and why the hand inequality still fails at $\lVert\nu\rVert\to0$

## The three fitted facts, derived by hand

*The user's standing instruction applies from here: proofs, not samples. The three facts above labelled fitted or screened are derived as follows, and every other identity in this note (the pinning, $P(G_x)$, the odd action, the even form, the second variation, the odd critical equation) was already derived by hand.*

**1. $\nu\circ\nu=G_x$.** With $Q(W)=W^2+W^\#$ and the sharp product's diagonal entries $(R^\#)_{ab,ab}=\sum_{c,c'}\big[R_{ac,ac'}R_{bc,bc'}-R_{ac,bc'}R_{bc,ac'}\big]$, which on a diagonal tensor reduce to $\sum_cK_{ac}K_{bc}=(S^2)_{ab}$ (fixing the convention), take $\nu=\sum_ax_a(e_{ja}\odot e_{ka})$, whose only entries are $\nu_{ja,ka}=\nu_{ka,ja}=x_a$.
- $(\nu^2)_{ja,ja}=(\nu^2)_{ka,ka}=x_a^2$, and $\nu^2$ vanishes on every other diagonal entry.
- At $(ab)$ with $a,b\notin\{j,k\}$: the first term needs $(ac),(ac')$ and $(bc),(bc')$ to be pairings $(jd),(kd)$, which forces $\{c,c'\}=\{j,k\}$, giving $2x_ax_b$; the second term needs $(ac)$ and $(bc')$ to share an index with $d$, impossible. So $(\nu^\#)_{ab,ab}=2x_ax_b$.
- At $(ja)$: every first-term factor $R_{jc,jc'}$ would pair $(jc)$ with $(jc')$, not a pattern pairing, and the second term forces a repeated index. So $(\nu^\#)_{ja,ja}=0$, likewise at $(ka)$.
- At $(jk)$: the first term vanishes as before, and the second term at $c=c'=a$ gives $-R_{ja,ka}R_{ka,ja}=-x_a^2$. So $(\nu^\#)_{jk,jk}=-\lVert x\rVert^2$.
- The off-diagonal entries of $\nu\circ\nu$ vanish by character, $(\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k)^2=1$.
**Hence $\nu\circ\nu$ has $K_{ja}=K_{ka}=x_a^2$, $K_{jk}=-\lVert x\rVert^2$, $K_{ab}=2x_ax_b$, which is $G_x$.** $\square$

**2. $\lVert\xi\cdot D\rVert^2=2\sum_a(K_{ka}-K_{ja})^2$.** $\xi=E_{jk}$ acts on $\Lambda^2$ by $e_{ja}\mapsto e_{ka}$, $e_{ka}\mapsto-e_{ja}$, $e_{jk}\mapsto0$, and $\xi\cdot W=[\Lambda^2\xi,W]$. For diagonal $D$, $[X,D]_{\alpha\beta}=X_{\alpha\beta}(D_{\beta\beta}-D_{\alpha\alpha})$, so $\xi\cdot D$ has entries $K_{ja}-K_{ka}$ at $(ka,ja)$ and $(ja,ka)$ and nothing else: it lies in $V_{jk}$ with coordinates $K_{ja}-K_{ka}$, and its norm is twice the sum of their squares. $\square$

**3. $\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2=4\lVert\nu\rVert^2$.** $[X,\nu]_{\alpha\alpha}=\sum_\beta(X_{\alpha\beta}\nu_{\beta\alpha}-\nu_{\alpha\beta}X_{\beta\alpha})$ gives $-2x_a$ at $(ja)$ and $2x_a$ at $(ka)$, and every off-diagonal entry vanishes, since $X$ and $\nu$ connect only $(ja)$ with $(ka)$. So $\xi\cdot\nu\in\mathcal D_n$ with norm$^2$ $\sum_a8x_a^2=4\cdot2\lVert x\rVert^2=4\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. $\square$

**What the pair-block stratum now has on proof, not screen:** the subalgebra, $\nu\circ\nu=G_x$ and $\lVert G_x\rVert^2=3\lVert x\rVert^4$, the pinning $\langle D,\hat g\rangle=\theta/\sqrt3$, $P(G_x)=3p_1^3-15p_1p_2+10s_3^2$, the odd action and its eigenvalues, the first-order rotation condition $\langle x,y\rangle=0$ and hence odd coupling $\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$ at the minimising angle, the second variation $\lVert\nu\rVert^2\le\tfrac12\sum_a(K_{ka}-K_{ja})^2$, the odd critical equation $2L_{D^+}D^-=\theta D^-$, the even form, and the second relation. **What is not on proof is the conclusion**: every screen that suggested closure is only a screen, and the proof still needs the pair-block gap at diagonal critical points above $\sqrt{3/2}$.

## The pair-block gap, reduced by hand to one inequality on the diagonal locus

**What the record holds.** [DGK 2023](../references/deninger-grundhoefer-kramer-2023-srg-quadratic/notes.md) solve the diagonal criticality equation in families (strongly regular graphs, multiplicative characters, Jäger's composite and prime constructions), and [R15](../routes.md) records that none of those families enters $(\sqrt{3/2},\Theta_n]$ except the balanced product, **but that last clause was established over $n\le200$ by evaluation, not proved, and no classification of all diagonal critical points above $\sqrt{3/2}$ exists on the record.** *So a gap cannot be read off a list; it has to be derived.*

**Derived: the pair-block Rayleigh quotient at a diagonal critical point is a form in $D$.** At a unit diagonal critical point $D$, $Q(D)=\theta D$, and for unit $x\perp\mathbf1$ the pair-block form is $\langle K,G_x\rangle=\langle D,G_x\rangle$. Pairing $Q(D)=\theta D$ with $G_x$ and using total symmetry, $\theta\langle D,G_x\rangle=\langle D\circ D,G_x\rangle=\langle L_{G_x}D,D\rangle$. With the even and odd forms derived above, and $D^-$ having odd coordinates $y$ with $\lVert D^-\rVert^2=2\lVert y\rVert^2$,
$$\langle K,G_x\rangle=\frac1\theta\Big[Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2+2\langle x,y\rangle^2\Big].$$
**For $x$ orthogonal to the orbit direction, $\langle x,y\rangle=0$, and the pair-block top eigenvalue off the orbit direction is $\tfrac1{2\theta}\max_x\big[Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2\big]$.** So the gap $\le\tfrac\theta2-\gamma$ is exactly
$$Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2\ \le\ \theta^2-2\gamma\theta\qquad\text{for every unit }x\perp\mathbf1,\ x\perp y ,$$
*an inequality about the diagonal critical point $D$ alone*, with $Q_{\mathrm{even}}$ the Dirichlet form above. **At the orbit direction itself the extra term $2\langle x,y\rangle^2$ is what lifts the quotient to exactly $\tfrac\theta2$** at the balanced product, which is F37 read in these coordinates. *This is the next derivation: bound $Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)$ at a diagonal critical point using its own criticality ($S^{\circ2}+S^2=\theta S+\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}$) and L57's chain, with no census of solutions.*
