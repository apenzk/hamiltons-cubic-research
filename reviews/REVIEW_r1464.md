## A. Fidelity

**The record's claim**, quoted from [route-a.md:205](../findings/route-a.md#L205): *"The same disjointness kills the trilinear coupling $T(u,H,H)$, so $\langle L_eH,H\rangle=0$ and the margin is $\tfrac32\theta^*=1.837$."* Statement (c) at [route-a.md:193](../findings/route-a.md#L193) takes *"$H$ a Weyl tensor of the complementary $\mathbb R^{n-4}$"*, and the Setting at [route-a.md:182](../findings/route-a.md#L182) fixes $e=u/\theta^*$.

**$T(u,H,H)$ is $\langle u,Q(H)\rangle$.** [route-a.md:608](../findings/route-a.md#L608) defines $T$ as the symmetric trilinear form with $T(W,W,W)=P(W)$ and $\langle F_uh,k\rangle=T(u,h,k)$, and (a)'s proof at [route-a.md:197](../findings/route-a.md#L197) identifies $F_u h=u\circ h$. So $T(u,H,H)=\langle u\circ H,H\rangle$, and full symmetry moves it to $\langle H\circ H,u\rangle=\langle Q(H),u\rangle$, since $X\circ X=Q(X)$ ([FoxProduct.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/FoxProduct.lean)'s `foxProd_self`). The Lean statement `innerOp (specOp lamU omU) (Qop (specOp mu eta)) = 0` is therefore the coupling, and `lamU`, `omU` are the unit padded competitor r1463 confirmed.

**The hypotheses on $H$ are the record's.** `hoff` makes every presenting bivector vanish on rows $a<4$, and skewness makes it vanish on columns $b<4$ too, so $H$ lives on $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^{n-4})$. `hB` and `hRic` are Bianchi and Ricci-flatness in $\mathbb R^n$. Ricci-flatness in $\mathbb R^n$ equals Ricci-flatness in $\mathbb R^{n-4}$ here, since the contraction terms with $c<4$ vanish. Every Weyl tensor of $\mathbb R^{n-4}$ has a spectral presentation of this shape, so the quantifier covers the record's $H$.

## B. Proofs

**`innerOp_eq_R4`.** `innerOp A B` is $\tfrac12\sum_{ij}\mathrm{inner2}(A\varphi_{ij},B\varphi_{ij})$ ([WeylReal.lean:135](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L135)), and `inner2` is $\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^\top Y$. For skew $X,Y$, $\mathrm{inner2}(X,Y)=\tfrac12\sum_{kl}\langle X,\varphi_{kl}\rangle\langle Y,\varphi_{kl}\rangle$, the $\tfrac12$ paying for ordered pairs, since $\mathrm{inner2}(\varphi_{kl},\varphi_{kl})=1$. So the product is $\tfrac14\sum R4(A)R4(B)$. The proof uses `inner2_eq_sum` and `inner2_phi` at the skew values, which is this computation. Note, not a gap: [L50Spectra.lean:51](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L50Spectra.lean#L51)'s `innerOp_R4` already states the same identity, with a skewness hypothesis on the second operator only.

**`R4_Qop_zero`.** The split `R4 (Qop H) = R4 (compOp H) + R4 (sharpOp H)` is additivity of `inner2` in its first slot. `R4_compOp_sum` ([L50Spectra.lean:73](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L50Spectra.lean#L73)) gives $(H^2)_{ijkl}=\tfrac12\sum_{mn}H_{ijmn}H_{klmn}$, which needs `hskew` and `hsym` only. `R4_sharpOp` and `sharpBil_phi_B` ([L209BianchiHalf.lean:107](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L209BianchiHalf.lean#L107)) give $(H^\#)_{ijkl}=B_{ijkl}-B_{ijlk}$ with $B_{ijkl}=\sum_{pq}H_{ipkq}H_{jplq}$. Its section declares `hB` but `include`s only `hR hskew hsym`, and the call passes three hypotheses, so Bianchi is unused. Each of $H_{ijmn}H_{klmn}$, $H_{ipkq}H_{jplq}$ and $H_{iplq}H_{jpkq}$ carries $H_{i\cdots}$, which vanishes, so `simp [B4, hH]` closes it. Correct.

**`coupling_zero`.** `Qop_isWeylOp` ([L209WeylClosure.lean:110](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L209WeylClosure.lean#L110)) supplies skew values of $Q(H)$ for `innerOp_eq_R4`. Each product $R4(U)_{ijkl}R4(Q(H))_{ijkl}$ has a vanishing factor, from `hUS` off $S$ and from `R4_Qop_zero` on $S$. Correct.

**`L134c_coupling`.** $S=\{x:x<4\}$. For $i\notin S$, `comp_specOp_apply` writes $u_{ijkl}$ as a sum of $\lambda_\gamma\,\omega_\gamma(i,j)\,\omega_\gamma(k,l)$ and `pad_zero` kills $\omega_\gamma(i,j)$ at $i\ge4$. For $i\in S$, `hoff` kills $\eta_\delta(i,j)$ at $i<4$. `comp` and `R4` agree definitionally here, and the build accepts `exact this`. Correct. Two linter notes from the build, not gaps: flexible `simp at hi` at [L134Coupling.lean:82](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L134Coupling.lean#L82) and [:89](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L134Coupling.lean#L89).

**Does $H$ need to be Ricci-flat? No.** The vanishing needs $H$ linear, bivector-preserving and self-adjoint on bivectors. `coupling_zero` asks for `IsWeylOp` because `Qop_isWeylOp` is the packaged route to skew values of $Q(H)$, which needs only skewness of $H$. So `hRic` and `hB` are over-assumptions relative to the proof. *Arguing the opposite:* the record's hypothesis is "H a Weyl tensor", and a Weyl tensor is Bianchi and Ricci-flat, so the theorem quantifies over exactly the record's objects and loses nothing the record uses. It is faithful, and a candidate gap is withdrawn.
