### Reviewer 304: the second-order operator on the diagonal locus

**Method.** Everything below runs on an implementation written from the definitions in [scripts/reviewers/r304/](../scripts/reviewers/r304/), importing nothing under review: the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ structure constants in the orthonormal basis $B_{ij}=(E_{ij}-E_{ji})/\sqrt2$, the bilinear $\sharp$ as $(R\sharp S)_{\alpha\beta}=\sum c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}c_{\beta\varepsilon\zeta}R_{\gamma\varepsilon}S_{\delta\zeta}$, $Q(W)=W^2+W\sharp W$, and $L_WH=\tfrac12\big(Q(W+H)-Q(W)-Q(H)\big)=\tfrac12(WH+HW)+W\sharp H$ by polarisation. The normalisation is fixed independently of the record and then checked against it: $\theta$ from the operator, from $\tfrac12(\sum M_{ab}^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3)$ and from the cube-plus-triangle graph formula agree to $10^{-12}$ at $S^3\times S^3$, $S^3\times S^5$, $S^6\times S^6$, $S^4\times S^8$, returning $\theta_0(6)=1.054092553389$; the criticality residual there is $3\times10^{-17}$. $\mathcal W_n$ is built as the joint kernel of first Bianchi and $\mathrm{Ric}$ on $\mathrm{Sym}(\Lambda^2)$ and comes out $35,84,168,300$ at $n=5,6,7,8$, matching $n(n+1)(n+2)(n-3)/12$.

#### J1, CONFIRMED, and it is a theorem rather than an observation, but the displayed identity carries a spurious diagonal

**Diagonality needs no criticality, no Ricci-flatness and no symmetry.** Two independent arguments, both checked. The coordinate sign-flip group $\mathbb Z_2^n$ acts on $\Lambda^2$ by $\varphi_{ij}\mapsto\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j\varphi_{ij}$, and an operator commutes with all of it exactly when it is pair-diagonal; $L_W$ is equivariant for any diagonal $W$, so it preserves the invariants. Directly from the structure constants: $c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}c_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ is supported on $\alpha=\beta$, because $\gamma,\delta$ determine the third edge of their triangle uniquely. Measured off-diagonal mass of $L_WH$ is **exactly $0.0$** (not $10^{-16}$) at $S^3\times S^5$, $S^6\times S^6$, at random *non-critical* diagonal $W$ ($n=6,7$, criticality residual $0.25$ and $0.33$), at a diagonal $W$ that is *not even Ricci-flat* (row sums $4.4$), and at a diagonal critical point with $|\mathrm{Aut}(M)|=2$ at $n=8$.

**The $n\times n$ formula is right off the diagonal and wrong on it.** For hollow $M,N$, $(W\sharp H)_{(ij),(ij)}=\tfrac12\sum_k(M_{ik}N_{jk}+M_{jk}N_{ik})=\tfrac12(MN+NM)_{ij}$ and $\tfrac12(WH+HW)_{(ij),(ij)}=M_{ij}N_{ij}$, giving the claim for $i\ne j$. But the pair-weight matrix of $L_WH$ is hollow by construction, while $M\circ N+\tfrac12(MN+NM)$ has diagonal $\langle M_i,N_i\rangle$, which is **not small**: measured $0.19$, $0.16$, $0.10$ on unit-norm admissible $N$ at $S^3\times S^5$, at non-critical points and at the $n=8$ asymmetric point. So the correct statement is $\mathcal L(N)=\mathrm{hollow}\big(M\circ N+\tfrac12(MN+NM)\big)=\tfrac12D\big[\Phi-\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(s)\big](M)[N]$, the derivative of the **whole** criticality map including its multiplier term, not of $\Phi$ alone. Against that form the residual is $3\times10^{-17}$ everywhere tested.

**The hollowing is load-bearing, not cosmetic.** Row sums of $M\circ N$ are $\langle M_i,N_i\rangle$ and row sums of $\tfrac12(MN+NM)$ are $0$; removing the diagonal moves exactly $\langle M_i,N_i\rangle$ out of the second term, and the two cancel. That cancellation **is** the statement that $L_W$ preserves Ricci-flatness on the locus. Written without the hollow projection the map has non-zero row sums and leaves the diagonal Weyl space, so the record's own display is the one form of the identity that does not close inside $n\times n$ matrices.

#### J2, CONFIRMED, with a proof

$\langle N,M\circ N\rangle=\sum_{ij}M_{ij}N_{ij}^2$ and $\langle N,\tfrac12(MN+NM)\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(NMN)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(MN^2)$; the hollow correction is annihilated because $N$ is hollow, so the Rayleigh form is insensitive to the J1 correction above. Residual $\le10^{-16}$ on random admissible $N$ at every point tested. Both terms of $\mathcal L$ are self-adjoint in the Frobenius product, so the compression to the admissible space is symmetric and its Rayleigh quotients are the operator's own.

#### J3, the dimension count is right; "replacing a $\binom n2\times\binom n2$ problem" is not

The admissible space $\{N$ hollow symmetric, $N\mathbf1=0\}$ has dimension $9,14,20$ at $n=6,7,8$, matching $n(n-3)/2$, and every one of its elements lies in $\mathcal W_n$ to $2\times10^{-15}$, first Bianchi is indeed automatic. $L_W$ maps $\mathcal W_n$ into itself with leakage $10^{-15}$, and $M$ is an eigenvector at eigenvalue $\theta$, so $e^\perp$ is preserved and the compression is legitimate.

**What fails is the completeness.** The $\mathbb Z_2^n$-decomposition splits $L_W|_{\mathcal W_n}$ into the diagonal block and its complement, so the diagonal computation sees one block of two. Independently of the other reviewer, the same witnesses appeared here: at $S^2\times S^4$, $S^2\times S^5$ and $S^2\times S^6$ the diagonal block gives $\lambda_{\max}/\theta=0.333,\ 0.375,\ 0.400$ (passing) while the full operator gives $0.667,\ 0.625,\ 0.600$, failing $\tfrac12$. And at the sphere products the full space attains **exactly** $\theta/2$ on orbit directions while the diagonal block reaches only $0.264$ of $0.527$ at $S^3\times S^3$, so the sharp case is invisible to the reduction at every maximiser. This corroborates the withdrawal in commit d0def55; the surviving reading (diagonal $\lambda_{\max}>\theta/2$ certifies non-maximality), is what all the work below uses.

**Not vacuous, though.** At the $C_5$ blow-up the diagonal block returns $\lambda_{\max}/\theta=1.206011$, reproducing the record's full-space value exactly, so on that point the $10\times10$ problem carries the whole violation that $\mathrm{Sym}_0$ only partly saw.

#### J4, REFUTED, and the family it declares vacuous proves something

**$\dim\ker M$ is the number of connected components of the weighted graph, not $1$.** Over $\sim$400 exact diagonal critical points at $n=6,7,8,10$ (residual $\le10^{-15}$), $\ker M$ equals the span of the connected-component indicators to $10^{-15}$ in every single case, with $\dim\ker M$ reaching $2,3,4$. The premise "$\dim\ker M=1$ at every point tested" is an artefact of sampling connected points; $M\mathbf1=0$ forces one kernel vector per component, not one in total.

**And the family is live wherever $M$ is disconnected.** Two copies of the four-vertex point $M_{01}=M_{23}=2c,\ M_{02}=M_{03}=M_{12}=M_{13}=-c$ at $n=8$, each scaled by $1/\sqrt2$, is an exact critical point (residual $2.8\times10^{-17}$, $\theta=0.612372$) with $\ker M=\langle\mathbf1_A,\mathbf1_B\rangle$. Taking $a=\mathbf1_A$ gives $Ma=0$, so $N_{ij}=2M_{ij}(a_i+a_j)$ has zero row sums and is admissible; after removing its $M$-component it still has norm $2.83$, so it is **not** the radial direction. The claimed identity for $\mathcal L N$ holds there at $10^{-16}$, and the withdrawn $\theta$-free criterion evaluates to $+2.449$, correctly, since the point is not a maximiser.

**The clean statement the "vacuous" reading hides.** For $M=M_A\oplus M_B$ block-diagonal, $N=M_A\oplus0$ gives $M\circ N=M_A^{\circ2}\oplus0$ and $\tfrac12(MN+NM)=M_A^2\oplus0$, so $\mathcal L N=\theta N$ **exactly**, by block-$A$ criticality alone. Hence:

> every disconnected diagonal critical point with $\theta>0$ fails second-order maximality, with an explicit eigenvector at eigenvalue exactly $\theta$, twice the permitted $\theta/2$.

Verified at the $n=8$ direct sum ($0.612372$ against $\theta/2=0.306186$) and at every padded point in the sweeps, where $\lambda_{\max}=\theta$ on the nose. What survives of J4 is the connected case: there $\ker M=\langle\mathbf1\rangle$, $N=4M$, and the family is radial, which is why no non-radial member was ever seen.

#### J5, the outcome holds, the statement does not

Three corrections, all measured at exact critical points.

- **Two of the three candidates are one.** Criticality gives $M^{\circ2}+M^2=\theta M+\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(s)$, and $\theta M$, $\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(s)$ both project to zero in the admissible space, so $\mathrm{proj}(M^{\circ2})=-\mathrm{proj}(M^2)$ **identically**, measured $6\times10^{-15}$, $2\times10^{-15}$, $5\times10^{-16}$, $1\times10^{-14}$ at four points. The Rayleigh quotient is quadratic, so the two can never differ. That is why the $n=9$ witness reads $0.059$ twice; it is forced, not evidence.
- **At the $C_5$ blow-up the projections do not vanish.** $\lVert\mathrm{proj}(M^{\circ2})\rVert=\lVert\mathrm{proj}(M^2)\rVert=0.2108$ and $\lVert\mathrm{proj}(M^{\circ3})\rVert=0.0471$. What vanishes is their **Rayleigh quotient**, exactly $0$, a different fact with a different cause.
- **The right class is all $\mathrm{Aut}(M)$-invariant matrices.** The projection commutes with the automorphism action, so any equivariantly built candidate lands in the invariant part of the admissible space. That part has dimension $0$ at $S^3\times S^3$ (of $8$) and at $S^3\times S^5$ (of $19$) (by the count $3$ block parameters minus $2$ row-sum constraints minus the $M$ direction) which is why "exactly zero" at sphere products is a theorem and not a coincidence. At the $C_5$ blow-up the invariant part is $1$-dimensional of $34$, and $\mathcal L$ acts on it with eigenvalue exactly $0$.

This is a different fact from the kernel result: $\ker M$ is about $M$ annihilating vectors, the invariant subspace is about the symmetry group's fixed set.

#### J6, the phenomenon is real at symmetric points; the closure of the constructive class is REFUTED

**Confirmed at the blow-up, and in the stronger form that matters.** V3 needs *some* admissible direction above $\theta/2$, not the top one, so the operative question is the maximum of the Rayleigh quotient over invariant directions. At the $C_5$ blow-up that maximum is $0.000000$ against $\theta/2=0.335410$, so no equivariant construction can work there. Two further $n=8$ points with $\Delta>0$ behave the same way, $|\mathrm{Aut}|=16$ gives $-0.293$ against $+0.278$, $|\mathrm{Aut}|=14$ gives $-0.463$ against $+0.231$, so the phenomenon is wider than the blow-up. The top eigenspace facts also reproduce: multiplicity $2$, invariant as a set (commutator $7.5\times10^{-16}$), minimum deviation of a top eigenvector from pointwise-fixed $0.331$.

**But it is a property of large symmetry groups, not of $\Delta>0$ points.** At an $n=8$ critical point with $\Delta=11.157$, $\theta=0.161868$ and $|\mathrm{Aut}(M)|=2$, the top eigenspace is **one-dimensional** and its eigenvector is fixed pointwise by the automorphism group, deviation $0.0000$. The invariant part of the admissible space has dimension $10$ of $19$ and its maximum Rayleigh quotient is $0.635744$, the full $\lambda_{\max}$, against $\theta/2=0.080934$. So at a low-symmetry point the violating direction *is* canonical and equivariant, and an equivariant recipe is entirely unobstructed. **The claimed consequence (that a proof of V3 cannot exhibit its test direction equivariantly), is false as stated.** What is true, and is what the evidence supports, is narrower: a *uniform* equivariant recipe, one that works the same way at every point, cannot succeed, because it must also fire at the highly symmetric points where the invariant subspace carries nothing above $\theta/2$.

**On the group.** For the diagonal block, $\mathrm{Aut}(M)\le S_n$ is the right group: the signed permutations are exactly the stabiliser elements preserving the diagonal locus, and the sign flips act trivially on a connected $M$. The full stabiliser in $O(n)$ is strictly bigger and its extra directions leave the locus, $\dim\mathfrak k(W)=5$ at the $C_5$ blow-up ($\mathfrak{so}(2)^5$, one per twin block), $6$ at $S^3\times S^3$ and $13$ at $S^3\times S^5$, matching $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(q)$. So $320$ is right for the $n\times n$ statement, and any full-space version of J6 must use $\mathfrak k(W)$ instead, where the invariants are fewer still.

#### The prize: not found, and the barrier is now a number

Searched for a diagonal critical point with $\theta>0$, $\Delta>0$ and $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L|_{e^\perp})\le\theta/2$ across: $\sim700$ converged random critical points at $n=6,7,8,9,10$ (over $200$ distinct spectra, residual $\le10^{-13}$); $946$ distinct block-constant "generalised blow-up" critical points with $3$, $4$ and $5$ blocks at $n=8,10,12,14,16$; sphere products balanced and unbalanced; padded and direct-sum points; the $C_5$ blow-up at $k=1,2$; and a penalty search directly minimising $\lambda_{\max}-\theta/2$ subject to criticality and $\Delta\ge\tfrac12$. **No hit.**

The margin is not thin. Over $66$ distinct $\Delta>0$ critical points with $\theta>0$ at $n\le10$, the minimum of $\lambda_{\max}/\theta$ is $1.0417$, every one of them clears not just $\tfrac12$ but $1$. The block-constant family agrees at $n$ up to $16$, minimum ratio $1.006$ at $\theta=0.865$, $\Delta=2.08$. Meanwhile $\Delta=0$ points split cleanly: sphere products sit at ratio $0.25$–$0.375$, and the decomposable ones sit at exactly $1$ by the J4 eigenvector. **So the empirical shape of the diagonal half of V3 is much stronger than V3 asks**: $\Delta>0$ appears to force $\lambda_{\max}\ge\theta$, not $\lambda_{\max}>\theta/2$. A counterexample would need $\theta>2\lambda_{\max}\gtrsim1.1$, i.e. a $\Delta>0$ point with $\theta$ at the level of $\theta_0(n)$ itself, which is the diagonal conjecture's own boundary.

#### One lead, in closed form

Admissible directions with a hand-computable Rayleigh quotient: for distinct $a,b,c,d$ take $u=e_a-e_b$, $v=e_c-e_d$ and $N=uv^{\mathsf T}+vu^{\mathsf T}$, which is hollow, symmetric and has zero row sums, with

$$\frac{\langle N,\mathcal LN\rangle}{\lVert N\rVert^2}=\frac{M_{ac}+M_{ad}+M_{bc}+M_{bd}-2M_{ab}-2M_{cd}}4 .$$

Checked against the operator to $10^{-12}$. Maximised over $4$-subsets and pairings (after removing the $M$-component) this **certifies second-order failure at $10$ of the $13$ $\Delta>0$ critical points with $\theta>0$ at $n=6,7,8$**, with no eigen-solve anywhere, and correctly stays below $\theta/2$ at every sphere product. It fails at the two largest-$\theta$ members ($\theta=0.649,\Delta=2.25$ and $\theta=0.651,\Delta=4.52$), so it is a partial certificate, but it is an explicit, four-index, criticality-free family, which is the shape a proof of the diagonal half would want.
