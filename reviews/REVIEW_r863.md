## Conventions, quoted from the brief

- The inner product on the $\Pi_\alpha$ is the plain $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)$ with no factor $\tfrac12$ (given 10), and I write $\lVert A\rVert_F^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{\mathsf T}A)$ throughout for matrices that are not being read as bivectors.

## 1. The commutator identity

Derived from scratch. Let $P_\alpha,P_\beta$ be two-planes with principal angles $\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2$ and principal vectors $u,v$ for $P_\alpha$ and $u',v'$ for $P_\beta$, so that $u\cdot u'=\cos\vartheta_1$, $v\cdot v'=\cos\vartheta_2$ and $u\cdot v'=v\cdot u'=0$.

**Step one, the collapse to one angle.** With $u\wedge v=vu^{\mathsf T}-uv^{\mathsf T}$ and $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$, expanding the four terms gives $\langle u\wedge v,u'\wedge v'\rangle=(u\cdot u')(v\cdot v')-(u\cdot v')(v\cdot u')$, the determinant of the $2\times2$ matrix of inner products. In principal position that is $\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2$. So $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality is exactly $\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2=0$, that is, at least one principal angle is right. This is the record's "orthogonality having killed the second angle", and it is correct. Note what it does **not** say: it kills one angle, not necessarily the second, and when both are right the pair is $S=0$.

**Step two, the product.** Take $\vartheta_2=\pi/2$ and write $c=\cos\vartheta_1$, $y=v'$. Then
$$\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=(uu^{\mathsf T}+vv^{\mathsf T})(u'u'^{\mathsf T}+yy^{\mathsf T})=(u\cdot u')uu'^{\mathsf T}+(u\cdot y)uy^{\mathsf T}+(v\cdot u')vu'^{\mathsf T}+(v\cdot y)vy^{\mathsf T},$$
and the last three coefficients are $u\cdot y=0$, $v\cdot u'=0$ and $v\cdot y=\cos\vartheta_2=0$. So $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=c\,uu'^{\mathsf T}$ exactly as written, all three other terms vanishing, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=c(u'\cdot u)=c^2=S_{\alpha\beta}$.

**Step three, the norm.** $[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]=c\,(uu'^{\mathsf T}-u'u^{\mathsf T})=:cM$ with $M$ skew. Then $\lVert M\rVert_F^2=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M^2)$ and
$$M^2=c\,uu'^{\mathsf T}-uu^{\mathsf T}-u'u'^{\mathsf T}+c\,u'u^{\mathsf T},\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M^2)=2c^2-2,$$
so $\lVert M\rVert_F^2=2(1-c^2)$ and $\lVert[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]\rVert_F^2=2c^2(1-c^2)=2S_{\alpha\beta}(1-S_{\alpha\beta})$. The identity is correct, and the record's $[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]=\sqrt{S_{\alpha\beta}}(uu'^{\mathsf T}-u'u^{\mathsf T})$ is correct with the sign of $u'$ chosen so that $c\ge0$, which principal position permits.

**Which normalisation the factor 2 needs.** The plain one, $\lVert A\rVert_F^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{\mathsf T}A)$. This is a real trap rather than a pedantic one: $[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]$ is a **skew** matrix, hence an element of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ in the record's own realisation, and the record's inner product there carries the $\tfrac12$, under which the same commutator has squared length $S_{\alpha\beta}(1-S_{\alpha\beta})$. The record's neighbouring given 15, $\lVert[\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta]\rVert^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)$, does use the $\tfrac12$ convention: at $P_\alpha=\{e_1,e_2\}$ and $P_\beta=\{e_1,e_3\}$ one computes $[\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta]=e_3e_2^{\mathsf T}-e_2e_3^{\mathsf T}=e_2\wedge e_3$, a unit bivector, against $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=1$. So the two displayed identities of the record, one line apart in the programme, are normalised differently, and only the subscript $F$ distinguishes them. The Lean file settles which is meant: `frobSq_commutator_inst` sums $\sum_i\sum_j M_{ij}^2$ with no halving, and returns $288/625=2\cdot\tfrac9{25}\cdot\tfrac{16}{25}$ at the instance $u=e_0$, $v=e_1$, $u'=\tfrac35e_0+\tfrac45e_2$, $y=e_3$. I checked that instance is genuinely $\Lambda^2$-orthogonal, its Plücker determinant being $\det\begin{pmatrix}3/5&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=0$, so it does test the hypothesis it is meant to test. **Recommendation: write the identity with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}$ explicit, not with a subscript $F$.**

**Is the identity true without $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality?** No. With both angles alive, $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=c_1uu'^{\mathsf T}+c_2vv'^{\mathsf T}$, the two skew parts are Frobenius-orthogonal in principal position (all four cross inner products vanish), and
$$\lVert[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]\rVert_F^2=2\big(c_1^2(1-c_1^2)+c_2^2(1-c_2^2)\big),\qquad S_{\alpha\beta}=c_1^2+c_2^2 .$$
At an isoclinic pair, $c_1=c_2=c$, the left side is $4c^2(1-c^2)$ and $2S(1-S)=4c^2(1-2c^2)$, which differ at every $c\ne0$. The failure is loud rather than subtle: at $c^2>1/2$ the record's right side is **negative** while the left side is a sum of squares. Screened at $c^2=\cos^2(0.6)$: left $0.868697$, right $2\cdot1.362358\cdot(-0.362358)=-0.987365$. So $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality is not decoration.

## 2. Integrality equals commutativity, and the hypothesis is load-bearing

**Forward.** Under (H1)–(H3), $S_{\alpha\beta}(1-S_{\alpha\beta})=0$ at every pair, so the identity gives $\lVert[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]\rVert_F^2=0$ and the projections commute.

**Backward.** If they commute then $2S_{\alpha\beta}(1-S_{\alpha\beta})=0$, so $S_{\alpha\beta}\in\{0,1\}$. The record's "the converse is immediate: diagonal matrices commute" proves a different converse, the one from the frame conclusion back to integrality, which is also correct and also immediate. Both directions hold.

**Where the equivalence fails.** Exactly where the second principal angle is not right. Take $\mathbb R^4$, $P=\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$ and $P'=\mathrm{span}\{\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(e_1+e_3),\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(e_2+e_4)\}$, an isoclinic pair at $c_1=c_2=1/\sqrt2$. Then $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi\Pi')=\tfrac12+\tfrac12=1$, perfectly integral, while $\lVert[\Pi,\Pi']\rVert_F^2=2(\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12)=1\ne0$. The two planes share no line and are not orthogonal, and the Plücker inner product of their bivectors is $c_1c_2=\tfrac12\ne0$, so the pair is outside (H1). This is a closed-form witness that integrality alone does **not** give commutativity, and it is the same object as the complex-line pair of item 12 at Hermitian angle $45^\circ$. The hypothesis is load-bearing, and in the record's chain it enters twice: once to collapse the angles and once to make $S\in[0,1]$ at all, since $S$ can reach $2$ off the locus.

## 3. The two-line proof of L201

**What the two lines give.** A commuting family of real symmetric matrices admits a common orthonormal eigenbasis $f_1,\dots,f_n$ of $\mathbb R^n$. Each $\Pi_\alpha$ is then diagonal in that frame with entries in $\{0,1\}$ and trace two, hence $\Pi_\alpha=f_{i}f_{i}^{\mathsf T}+f_{j}f_{j}^{\mathsf T}$ for a unique unordered pair $i\ne j$, so $P_\alpha$ is a coordinate two-plane of the frame $f$. That much is airtight and needs neither $n\ge3$ nor the family being a basis, only pairwise commutativity and each $\Pi_\alpha$ being a rank-two orthogonal projection.

**What the two lines do not give.** The stated conclusion is that the frame has the $P_\alpha$ as **exactly** its coordinate two-planes. That is a bijection between the index set and the $\binom n2$ coordinate planes, and it needs two further observations the record does not write.

- *Injectivity.* If $P_\alpha=P_\beta$ then $\Lambda^2P_\alpha$ is one-dimensional and contains both unit vectors $\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta$, so $\omega_\beta=\pm\omega_\alpha$, contradicting $\langle\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta\rangle=0$. This uses orthonormality, not commutativity.
- *Surjectivity.* The index set has $\binom n2$ elements because $\{\omega_\alpha\}$ is a basis, and there are $\binom n2$ coordinate two-planes, so an injection is a bijection.

So **something does need the family to be a basis**, and it is precisely the word *exactly*. Drop it and the conclusion survives as "each $P_\alpha$ is a coordinate plane of one common frame", which is what an arbitrary commuting subfamily gives. The Lean statement is the honest version: `integrality_forces_frame` in [L201Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L201Statement.lean) concludes $\exists f,i,j$ with $f$ orthonormal, $i\,a\ne j\,a$ and each plane equal to $\mathrm{planeProj}(f_{i(a)},f_{j(a)})$, with no injectivity and no surjectivity asserted. Two further remarks on that file: its `sorry` is still open, so the theorem is machine-stated and not machine-checked, and its docstring still says "the derivation in the findings block runs by induction on `n`", which the record has now deleted. **The Lean docstring is stale with respect to the claim under audit.**

**What the deleted induction had that this does not.** I cannot inspect it, since the brief forbids reading [REVIEW_r856.md](REVIEW_r856.md) and [REVIEW_r857.md](REVIEW_r857.md), so I answer only for what the replacement lacks. The commutator proof is non-constructive about the frame: it produces one from spectral theory, where an induction on $n$ builds it plane by plane and would carry the exhaustion count along as part of the construction. The elision above is the natural cost of that trade, and it is one line to repair. The record's claim that the new proof supersedes the old is fair on length and is not fair on completeness until the count is written.

## 4. The bound as positivity

Correct, including the diagonal accounting. Summing the identity over ordered pairs $\alpha\ne\beta$,
$$\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\lVert[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]\rVert_F^2=2\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}S_{\alpha\beta}-2\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}S_{\alpha\beta}^2 .$$
The first sum is $n(n-1)(n-2)$, which I re-derived rather than took: $\sum_{\alpha,\beta}S_{\alpha\beta}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha)^2\big)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((n-1)^2\mathrm{Id}\big)=n(n-1)^2$ by given 1, and the diagonal removes $\sum_\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha^2)=2\binom n2=n(n-1)$. The second sum is $\Xi-2n(n-1)$, since $\Xi$ includes the diagonal and $S_{\alpha\alpha}=2$ contributes $4\binom n2=2n(n-1)$. Hence
$$\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\lVert[\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta]\rVert_F^2=2\big(n(n-1)(n-2)+2n(n-1)-\Xi\big)=2\big(n^2(n-1)-\Xi\big),$$
exactly as claimed, and non-negativity is the bound. Equality holds iff every off-diagonal commutator vanishes iff every off-diagonal $S_{\alpha\beta}\in\{0,1\}$, which is L201's hypothesis, so the record's "the two were never independent" is right. Two independent confirmations. At the coordinate configuration, given 11 gives $S$ the eigenvalues $2n-2$, $n-2$ with multiplicity $n-1$ and $0$, so $\Xi=\lVert S\rVert_F^2=4(n-1)^2+(n-1)(n-2)^2=(n-1)n^2$, the bound met. At the rotated witness, worked out in item 11 below, $\Xi=44+4(\cos^4t+\sin^4t)$ and the summed commutators are $16\cos^2t\sin^2t$, and $2(48-\Xi)=8(1-\cos^4t-\sin^4t)=16\cos^2t\sin^2t$ identically. Screened at $t=0.37$: both sides $1.818657$.

## 5. The I75 row bound

Correct at every index. L199 gives $\theta\lambda_k\le\lambda_k^2+\tfrac12R_k$ with $R_k=\sum_\beta\lambda_\beta^2\lVert[v_k,v_\beta]\rVert^2$, under no decomposability hypothesis.

**Where hollowness enters.** At $\beta=k$ the commutator $[v_k,v_k]$ is zero, so that term drops. This matters, because the bridge's diagonal value is $S_{kk}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_k^2)=2$, not $1$, so a reader who applied the cap $S\le1$ across the whole sum would be applying a cap that is false at exactly one term while the true value there is $0$. The record's word "hollowness" is the right one and the step is sound.

**Where the cap enters.** For $\beta\ne k$ the bridge gives $\lVert[v_k,v_\beta]\rVert^2=S_{k\beta}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_k\Pi_\beta)\le1$, the upper end being a shared line. This is where decomposability is spent, and it is spent only on pairs of eigenvectors that are $\Lambda^2$-orthogonal, which eigenvectors of a symmetric operator at distinct eigenvalues are, and which can be arranged within an eigenspace.

**Where unit norm enters.** $R_k\le\sum_{\beta\ne k}\lambda_\beta^2=\lVert W\rVert^2-\lambda_k^2=1-\lambda_k^2$, using given 13 and $\lVert W\rVert=1$. Then $\theta\lambda_k\le\lambda_k^2+\tfrac12(1-\lambda_k^2)=(1+\lambda_k^2)/2$ at every index. Correct.

**Two small corrections.** The record's "strictly sharper than $\theta\lambda_k\le1$ at every $\lambda_k<1$" should read $\lvert\lambda_k\rvert<1$, since $(1+\lambda_k^2)/2<1$ is $\lambda_k^2<1$. At unit norm $\lvert\lambda_k\rvert\le1$ always, with equality only if every other eigenvalue vanishes, so the sharpening is in force at every index of every $W$ that is not that degenerate one. And at a negative index the inequality is vacuous for $\theta>0$, which the record itself says two paragraphs later and does not say here.

## 6. The corollary as a test

**The arithmetic, exact.** Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has blocks $c\,\mathrm{diag}(2,-1,-1)$ and $0$, so its spectrum on $\Lambda^2$ is $2c,-c,-c$ and zeros. Unit norm is $6c^2=1$, so $c=1/\sqrt6$ and $\lambda_{\max}=2/\sqrt6=0.816497$, $\lambda_{\max}^2=2/3$. Then $\theta\lambda_{\max}=\sqrt{3/2}\cdot2/\sqrt6=2\sqrt3/\sqrt{12}=1$ exactly, against $(1+\tfrac23)/2=\tfrac56$. Confirmed. "Fails it by a fifth" is $1\big/\tfrac56=\tfrac65$, a fair reading of the excess.

**Does the test exclude anything the record does not already exclude?** Not demonstrably. As a criterion it is not vacuous: its window is $(1+\lambda_{\max}^2)/2<\theta\lambda_{\max}\le1$, of width $(1-\lambda_{\max}^2)/2$, which tends to the whole of $(\tfrac12,1]$ as $\lambda_{\max}\to0$, so on the decomposable locus the bound is asymptotically **half** of L198 rather than a marginal gain. But the brief exhibits exactly one point inside that window, and that point the record says it had already excluded by a Plücker obstruction. So the honest statement is that the test recovers a known exclusion by a cheaper route and is structurally capable of new ones, and that no new one is in hand. Finding a second point in the window is a search, which this footing forbids, so I record the block rather than working around it.

**And one strengthening the record leaves on the table.** The derivation uses decomposability only at pairs $(k,\beta)$ with $\lambda_k\ne0$ and $\lambda_\beta\ne0$: at $\lambda_k=0$ the conclusion is the vacuous $0\le\tfrac12$, and at $\lambda_\beta=0$ the term carries weight $\lambda_\beta^2=0$ whatever the commutator does. So what is actually proved is
> a critical point with $\theta\lambda_{\max}>(1+\lambda_{\max}^2)/2$ carries no orthonormal decomposable eigenbasis **of $(\ker W)^{\perp}$**,

which is strictly stronger than the recorded statement, and strictly more useful, because the padded points are exactly the ones with large kernels and the recorded form lets a reader think the kernel's own basis has to be decomposable too.

**"L198 is sharp only off the decomposable locus."** It follows, with one step the record omits. On the locus $\theta\lambda_{\max}\le(1+\lambda_{\max}^2)/2\le1$ with equality throughout only at $\lambda_{\max}=1$, and $\lambda_{\max}=1$ at unit norm forces every other eigenvalue to vanish, whence $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=1\ne0$, which trace-freeness forbids. So $\theta\lambda_{\max}<1$ strictly on the locus. The missing step is the appeal to trace-freeness, and the sentence is true because padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ attains L198 and lies off the locus.

## 7. Non-composition with the global bound

**The figures, exact.** For $S^6\times S^6$ at unit radii, $n=12$ and the scalar curvature is $2\cdot6\cdot5=60$, so the Weyl shift is $S/(n(n-1))=60/132=5/11=0.454545$. Sectional curvature $1$ on the $2\binom62=30$ within-factor planes and $0$ on the $6\cdot6=36$ cross planes gives Weyl eigenvalues $6/11=0.545455$ and $-5/11=-0.454545$, and $30+36=66=\binom{12}2$. Trace-free: $30\cdot\tfrac6{11}-36\cdot\tfrac5{11}=0$. Norm: $\lVert W\rVert^2=30\cdot\tfrac{36}{121}+36\cdot\tfrac{25}{121}=\tfrac{1980}{121}=\tfrac{180}{11}=16.363636$, so the record's $\sqrt{16.36}$ is right. Normalised, $\lambda_{\max}=\tfrac6{11}\sqrt{\tfrac{11}{180}}=1/\sqrt{55}=0.134840$, matching $0.1349$. All confirmed.

**One figure is wrong.** The bound is $(1+\lambda_{\max}^2)/(2\lambda_{\max})=\tfrac{56}{55}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt{55}}2=28/\sqrt{55}=3.775519$, not $3.774$. The record's value is off in the fourth significant figure, and to four figures it should read $3.776$. Against $\theta_0(12)=\sqrt{220}/12=1.236033$, which is right, nothing in the conclusion changes.

**The reason for non-composition is correct, and weaker than the truth.** The record says the global $\theta\le(n-1)/\sqrt{n+1}$ runs through L107's $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$, and the new row is vacuous at a negative eigenvalue, so it cannot touch the composition. That is right. The stronger reason is that the new row can never yield anything below $\theta\le1$ at all: $(1+x^2)/(2x)\ge1$ for $x>0$ by AM-GM, with equality only at $x=1$, which trace-freeness forbids, so the row bound's value is always strictly above $1$. Composing it with a lower bound on $\lambda_{\max}$ cannot rescue it either, since $\lambda_{\max}\gtrsim N^{-1/2}$ at unit norm gives only $\theta\lesssim\sqrt N/2$, which grows. The balanced product's $3.776$ is that behaviour at one point rather than an accident of that point. So "the scoreboard is unmoved" understates it: the row bound cannot move the scoreboard at any $n$ where the target is below $1$.

## 8. The isotropy form

**Derivation.** At exactly two eigenvalues, $W=\nu\,\mathrm{Id}+(\mu-\nu)\Pi_E$ with $E=E_\mu$ and $a=\dim E$. Ricci-flatness is $\mu\Theta(E)+\nu\Theta(E^{\perp})=0$ by given 2, and $\Theta(E)+\Theta(E^{\perp})=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$ by given 1, so eliminating $\Theta(E^{\perp})$,
$$(\mu-\nu)\Theta(E)=-\nu(n-1)\mathrm{Id}.$$
Since $\mu\ne\nu$, $\Theta(E)$ is a multiple of the identity, and the multiple is forced by $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Theta(E)=2a$ against $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb R^n}=n$, giving $\Theta(E)=(2a/n)\mathrm{Id}$. The constant is right.

**Equivalence or implication?** The record writes that Ricci-flatness *is turned into* isotropy, which asserts an equivalence, and the equivalence is genuine, but not for the reason the sentence suggests. Isotropy alone is strictly weaker: it leaves Ricci-flatness as one linear condition on $(\mu,\nu)$, namely $\mu\cdot\tfrac{2a}n+\nu(n-1-\tfrac{2a}n)=0$. What closes the gap is trace-freeness, $a\mu+(N-a)\nu=0$. Substituting $\mu=-(N-a)\nu/a$ into the Ricci condition gives
$$\nu\Big(n-1-\frac{2N}{n}\Big)=\nu\Big(n-1-\frac{n(n-1)}{n}\Big)=0,$$
identically zero at $N=\binom n2$. So isotropy plus trace-freeness is exactly Ricci-flatness at two eigenvalues, and the count $N=n(n-1)/2$ is the whole content of the converse. This is precisely the hypothesis `hNn` in `two_eigenvalue_isotropic` in [L201Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L201Statement.lean), so the record knows the fact and states it only as a remark about the Lean version. The Lean theorem is gap-free and types the forward direction with `htf` and `hNn` as hypotheses. The converse is not typed anywhere I can see.

**And Ricci-flatness carries trace-freeness anyway.** Taking the trace of $\sum_\mu\lambda_\mu\Theta(E_\mu)=0$ gives $\sum_\mu\lambda_\mu\cdot2\dim E_\mu=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$, so trace-freeness is the trace of given 2 and not an extra assumption on $\mathcal W_n$.

## 9. Balancedness and the pencil exclusion

**Balancedness, closed form.** For $S^p\times S^q$ at unit radii the positive eigenspace is $E=\Lambda^2U_1\oplus\Lambda^2U_2$, and additivity with $\Theta(\Lambda^2U)=(\dim U-1)\Pi_U$ gives $\Theta(E)=(p-1)\Pi_{U_1}+(q-1)\Pi_{U_2}$. Since $U_1\oplus U_2=\mathbb R^n$ orthogonally, this operator has eigenvalues $p-1$ and $q-1$ on complementary nonzero subspaces, so it is isotropic exactly at $p=q$, and then equals $(p-1)\mathrm{Id}$. The check against the constant: $a=2\binom p2=p(p-1)$ and $n=2p$, so $2a/n=p-1$. Both verify. The hypothesis behind it is (H14): with unequal radii the operator has three eigenvalues, not two, so the two-eigenvalue frame already presumes equal radii, and "Ricci-flatness forces the product to be balanced" is a statement about the algebraic operator, not about which $S^p\times S^q$ can be Einstein at some pair of radii.

**Pencils, and what the claim assumes.** From given 16, $\Theta(f\wedge U')=r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}$ at $\lvert f\rvert=1$, $f\perp U'$, and $u_1,\dots,u_r$ orthonormal spanning $U'$. The orthogonality $f\perp U'$ is essential and unstated in the record's sentence: it is what makes each $f\wedge u_i$ a unit decomposable form with $(f\wedge u_i)(f\wedge u_i)^{\mathsf T}=ff^{\mathsf T}+u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$, and what makes the $r$ copies of $ff^{\mathsf T}$ add rather than interfere. Under it, the eigenvalues of $\Theta(f\wedge U')$ are $r$ on $\mathrm{span}\{f\}$, $1$ on $U'$, and $0$ on $(\mathrm{span}\{f\}\oplus U')^{\perp}$.

**So the record's parenthetical is wrong as an equivalence.** "Isotropic only at $r=1$" is true as an implication, isotropy forcing $r=1$, and false as a criterion: at $r=1$ the operator is $ff^{\mathsf T}+u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_P$, a single plane's projection, isotropic only when $n=2$. The correct statement is that **at $n\ge3$ no pencil eigenspace is isotropic at any $r$**, since isotropy needs both $r=1$ and $r+1=n$. The record's conclusion, that no pencil eigenspace of dimension above one can carry a two-eigenvalue Ricci-flat point, is therefore true and understated: dimension one is excluded as well, and the same rank argument generalises usefully. $\Theta(E)$ is a sum of $a$ rank-two projections, so $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\Theta(E)\le2a$, and isotropy with $a\ge1$ needs $2a\ge n$. Dually, $\Theta(E^{\perp})=(n-1-\tfrac{2a}n)\mathrm{Id}$ needs $2(N-a)\ge n$. So
$$\tfrac n2\ \le\ a\ \le\ N-\tfrac n2$$
is a necessary condition on any candidate, and it kills $a=1$ and $a=N-1$ at every $n\ge3$ without mentioning pencils at all.

## 10. The diagonal dictionary

**Isotropy is regularity, exactly.** On the coordinate configuration, $E$ is spanned by $\{\omega_\alpha:\alpha\in A\}$ with $A$ a set of coordinate two-planes, that is, an edge set of a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, and $\Theta(E)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\Pi_\alpha$ is a **diagonal** matrix whose $i$-th entry is $\#\{\alpha\in A:i\in\alpha\}=\deg_G(i)$. Because it is diagonal, equality of the diagonal entries is isotropy, so no separate argument over general unit $x$ is needed and the record's test at $x=e_i$ is not merely a necessary condition but the whole of it. The record does not say why $x=e_i$ suffices, and that is the one-line fill. The common value is $2a/n$ by $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Theta(E)=2a$, which is the handshake count, so the degree is $2\lvert A\rvert/n$ as claimed. The sphere product is $K_p\sqcup K_q$ with degrees $p-1$ and $q-1$, regular exactly at $p=q$, and then $2a/n=p(p-1)/p=p-1$ agrees.

**And a corollary the record misses.** A regular graph has integer degree, so **coordinate-spannedness forces $n\mid 2a$**. Any isotropic decomposable $E$ with $2a/n\notin\mathbb Z$ is automatically not coordinate-spanned, which turns the sharp question of item 11 into a search with a numerical entry condition. See item 14.

**Ricci-flatness gives regularity, and it gives it alone.** I checked this directly rather than through DGK. In the diagonal case Ricci-flatness is $\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\Pi_\alpha=0$, whose $i$-th diagonal entry is $\sum_{m\ne i}\lambda_{im}=0$: every vertex's incident weights sum to zero. At two values $\mu$ on $G$ and $\nu$ off it, this reads $d_i\mu+(n-1-d_i)\nu=0$, so $d_i=-(n-1)\nu/(\mu-\nu)$ is the same at every vertex. Regularity, from Ricci-flatness alone, with no reference to criticality. The record's split is therefore a correct attribution of sufficiency.

**But it is not a claim of exclusivity, and should not be read as one.** Relation (B) is also automatic here: on the coordinate configuration $c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\ne0$ exactly at triangles, so $T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=1$ on triangles and $0$ elsewhere, and (B) at $k=\{ij\}$ reads $\sum_{m\ne i,j}(\lambda_{im}+\lambda_{jm})=-2\lambda_{ij}$, which is the two vertex conditions at $i$ and at $j$ minus the shared edge twice. That is consistent with given 12's remark that (B) is an identity rather than criticality. Relation (C) at $k=\{ij\}$ reads $\sum_m\lambda_{im}\lambda_{jm}=\lambda_{ij}(\theta-\lambda_{ij})$, which is the common-neighbour count condition and is where strength comes from. Whether (C) alone would force the degree to be constant is a question about non-regular graphs with constant common-neighbour counts, and I cannot settle it from the brief. So: the split is correct in the direction it is used, and the record should not be read as asserting that criticality is silent about regularity.

## 11. The sharp question and the witness's pair table

**Coordinate-diagonality is equivalent to coordinate-spannedness.** With $W=\nu\,\mathrm{Id}+(\mu-\nu)\Pi_E$ and $\mu\ne\nu$, $W$ is diagonal in the basis $\{f_i\wedge f_j\}$ of a frame $f$ iff $\Pi_E$ is, iff every $f_i\wedge f_j$ is an eigenvector of $\Pi_E$, iff every $f_i\wedge f_j$ lies in $E$ or in $E^{\perp}$, iff $E$ is spanned by a subset of the $f_i\wedge f_j$. The equivalence is exact, and it uses $\mu\ne\nu$, which the two-eigenvalue hypothesis supplies.

**The witness's pair table.** With $\omega_1=e_1\wedge g_2$, $\omega_2=e_1\wedge g_3$, $\omega_3=e_1\wedge e_4$, $\omega_4=e_2\wedge e_3$, $\omega_5=e_2\wedge e_4$, $\omega_6=e_3\wedge e_4$ and weights $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1,-1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, the positive eigenspace carries indices $\{1,2,5,6\}$ and the negative $\{3,4\}$. ## 12. The isoclinic exclusion

**Two complex lines in $\mathbb C^2$ are isoclinic.** Take unit $z,w\in\mathbb C^2$ with $\lvert\langle z,w\rangle_{\mathbb C}\rvert=\cos\vartheta$, and realise $L_1=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb R}\{z,Jz\}$, $L_2=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb R}\{w,Jw\}$. Because $\Pi_{L_1}$ commutes with $J$, $\lvert\Pi_{L_1}w\rvert=\lvert\Pi_{L_1}Jw\rvert=\cos\vartheta$, and the operator $\Pi_{L_2}\Pi_{L_1}\Pi_{L_2}$ restricted to $L_2$ is $\cos^2\vartheta\cdot\mathrm{Id}_2$. So both real principal angles equal the Hermitian angle: isoclinic, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_1\Pi_2)=2\cos^2\vartheta$. Screened at $\vartheta=0.6$: $1.362358=2\cos^2(0.6)$.

**And $\langle\omega_1,\omega_2\rangle=\cos^2\vartheta$.** By the Plücker determinant of item 1 in principal position, $\langle\omega_1,\omega_2\rangle=\pm\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2=\pm\cos^2\vartheta$, and with the complex orientations on both lines the sign is positive. Screened: $0.681179=\cos^2(0.6)$. So two complex lines have $\Lambda^2$-orthogonal bivectors exactly when they are Hermitian-orthogonal.

**Does that exclude the family?** Yes, for the family as posed, and here is a second proof of it that also explains why. All complex-line unit bivectors in $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$ satisfy $\langle\omega_L,\hat\omega_J\rangle=1/\sqrt2$ at the normalised Kähler form $\hat\omega_J=\omega_J/\sqrt2$, so they lie on a two-sphere of radius $1/\sqrt2$ centred at $\hat\omega_J/\sqrt2$ inside the self-dual space. For two of them, $\langle\omega_1,\omega_2\rangle=\tfrac12+\langle p_1,p_2\rangle$ with $\lVert p_i\rVert^2=\tfrac12$, which vanishes only at $p_2=-p_1$, that is, at antipodal points of $\mathbb{CP}^1$, and a point has one antipode. So a $\Lambda^2$-orthonormal set of complex-line bivectors has at most **two** elements, and $\mathbb C^2$ having complex dimension two says the same thing. Hence no $E$ of dimension three or more has an orthonormal basis of complex-line forms. At dimension two the pair is Hermitian-orthogonal, giving $L_1\perp L_2$, $\Theta(E)=\mathrm{Id}=(2\cdot2/4)\mathrm{Id}$, and $E=\mathrm{span}\{f_{12},f_{34}\}$ in the obvious frame, so it is coordinate-spanned and no counterexample. The record's conclusion, that $\Lambda^2$-orthonormality is the binding constraint rather than isotropy, is right.

**One caveat on scope.** The argument excludes bases **all** of whose members are complex lines. A mixed configuration, some members complex lines and some not, is untouched by it, and the record's "one family is already excluded" should be read as that family and no wider.

## 13. The kernel, and the ranges

**Claim 1.** No kernel enters. The dimensions it needs are that each $P_\alpha$ is two-dimensional, which decomposability plus unit norm gives, and the cardinality $\binom n2$, which it needs and does not state, as item 3 records. $n\ge3$ is stated and unused.

**Claim 2.** This is where a kernel is most likely to be present, since padded points have one, and the record's statement is the weaker of the two available. As item 6 shows, the kernel's indices are inert on both sides: at $\lambda_k=0$ the row is vacuous, and at $\lambda_\beta=0$ the term is weightless. So the claim needs decomposability only on $(\ker W)^{\perp}$ and should say so. It does not state any dimension, and none is needed.

**Claim 3.** Here the kernel is not merely absent, it is **forbidden**, and the record does not say so. If the two eigenvalues were $\mu\ne0$ and $\nu=0$, Ricci-flatness would give $\mu\Theta(E)=0$, hence $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Theta(E)=2a=0$ and $a=0$, a contradiction. So a genuinely two-eigenvalue Ricci-flat $W$ is invertible on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$. The consequence for the reader is that "at two eigenvalues" must mean exactly two distinct eigenvalues on all of $\Lambda^2$, and the padded critical points, kernel and all, are outside every claim in this block. The dimensions the block needs are $1\le a\le N-1$ and $N=\binom n2$, and the last is stated only as a Lean hypothesis. One consequence worth recording: the record lists $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ as "the trivial family" of the classification, and it does satisfy isotropy, $\Theta(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}=(2N/n)\mathrm{Id}$, but at $a=N$ trace-freeness forces $\mu=0$ and so $W=0$. It solves the isotropy question and carries no two-eigenvalue point.

## 14. What would break each claim, and what I did

**Claim 1.** The breaking object is an orthonormal decomposable basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ with every $S_{\alpha\beta}\in\{0,1\}$ whose planes are not the coordinate planes of one frame. It cannot exist: the identity forces every pair to commute and spectral theory does the rest. I attacked instead the two places the chain could be wrong: the collapse of the second angle, which I re-derived from the Plücker determinant, and the normalisation of the factor 2, where I found the real hazard reported in item 1. I also built the object that breaks the **hypothesis-free** version, the isoclinic pair at $45^\circ$ with $S=1$ and non-vanishing commutator, in closed form, and confirmed the general two-angle formula $2\sum_ic_i^2s_i^2$ that explains why.

**Claim 2.** The breaking object is a critical Weyl tensor of unit norm with an orthonormal decomposable eigenbasis of $(\ker W)^{\perp}$ and $\theta\lambda_{\max}>(1+\lambda_{\max}^2)/2$. Finding one is a search of the critical locus, which this footing forbids, so I recorded the block instead of working around it. What I did do is check that every ingredient is exact, that the window is not empty as a region, and that the only point the record puts in it is one it had already excluded. I also checked the two places the inequality could leak, the diagonal term of the bridge and the kernel indices, and both are sound.

**Claim 3.** The breaking object is a subspace $E\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ with $\Theta(E)$ isotropic, an orthonormal decomposable basis, a complement with one too, and a span meeting no frame. Two things I can hand back without searching.

- **The entry condition.** By item 10 a coordinate-spanned isotropic $E$ has integer degree $2a/n$, so the hunt should start where $n\nmid2a$, and by item 9 it must respect $n/2\le a\le N-n/2$. At $n=5$ that leaves $a\in\{3,4,6,7\}$ as the non-integral-degree candidates, against the coordinate case $a=5$, whose graph is the pentagon $C_5$.
- **And the case $n=4$ is closed, affirmatively.** By given 3 a Weyl tensor at $n=4$ is block diagonal with both chirality blocks trace-free. If the spectrum is $\{\mu,\nu\}$ with $\mu\ne\nu$ and the blocks carry $k_1$ and $k_2$ copies of $\mu$, then $k_i\mu+(3-k_i)\nu=0$ for both, so subtracting, $(k_1-k_2)(\mu-\nu)=0$ and $k_1=k_2=k$, whence $a=2k$ is **even**: only $a\in\{2,4\}$ occurs, and $a=3$ is empty. At $a=2$, $\Theta(E)=\mathrm{Id}_4$ with $E$ spanned by two decomposable orthonormal forms, so $\Pi_1+\Pi_2=\mathrm{Id}$ forces $P_2=P_1^{\perp}$ and $E=\mathrm{span}\{f_{12},f_{34}\}$ for any frame adapted to the splitting. At $a=4$, $\Theta(E^{\perp})=3\,\mathrm{Id}-2\,\mathrm{Id}=\mathrm{Id}$ and the same argument applies to the two-dimensional $E^{\perp}$, giving $E^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}\{f_{12},f_{34}\}$ and $E=\mathrm{span}\{f_{13},f_{14},f_{23},f_{24}\}$. Both are coordinate-spanned. **So the sharp question has answer yes at $n=4$, and I76 begins at $n\ge5$.** The rotated witness is the $a=4$ instance of this, which is why it passes, and its weights $(\tfrac12,-1)$ are the $k=2$ solution of $2\mu+\nu=0$.

For the record, the smallest live case, $n=5$ with $a=3$, has its Gram matrix forced: $S\lambda=0$ with $\mu/\nu=-7/3$ gives $\sum_{\beta\in A}S_{\alpha\beta}=4a/n=12/5$ at every index, so the three positive planes are pairwise at $S=1/5$ and each of the seven complementary planes meets them with cross-row sum $12/5$. Deciding whether that configuration is realisable is the next step and it is a construction, not a sweep, so it belongs in the analysis lane.

**Claim 1: HOLDS, ONE ELISION.** The commutator identity, the collapse of the second angle, both directions of the integrality-commutativity equivalence, the simultaneous-diagonalisation step and the summed bound with its diagonal accounting are all correct, and I re-derived each rather than checking it. The owed citation for the classical two-projection half remains owed, and the record says so.

**Claim 2: HOLDS, ONE FIGURE WRONG, CONCLUSION UNDERSTATED.** The row bound is correct at every index, hollowness and unit norm enter exactly where the record puts them, and the padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ figures are exact. Three corrections: the balanced product's bound is $28/\sqrt{55}=3.775519$, not $3.774$; "strictly sharper at every $\lambda_k<1$" should read $\lvert\lambda_k\rvert<1$; and "L198 is sharp only off the decomposable locus" needs trace-freeness to rule out $\lambda_{\max}=1$. One deflation: no point other than padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is exhibited in the test's window, so the test recovers a known exclusion and demonstrates no new one, and the reason the bound cannot compose is stronger than the record gives, since $(1+x^2)/(2x)\ge1$ makes it incapable of ever yielding $\theta<1$.

**Claim 3: HOLDS, TWO IMPRECISIONS.** The isotropy form, its constant, balancedness, the pencil exclusion's conclusion, the diagonal dictionary, the equivalence of coordinate-diagonality with coordinate-spannedness, the cross-pair half of the witness table and the isoclinic exclusion all verify in closed form. **Nothing in the three chains is refuted.** The four defects are a missing count, an arithmetic slip in the fourth digit, a quantifier written backwards in a parenthetical, and a miscount in a checked-by-hand table. Every one is a one-line repair, and none of them touches a conclusion.
