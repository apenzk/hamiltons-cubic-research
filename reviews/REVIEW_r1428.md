## 1. The $n=8$ argument

Let $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{spin}(7)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$, $\dim\mathfrak h=21$, and suppose $V\subset\mathbb R^8$ is $\mathfrak h$-invariant with $\dim V=k$, $1\le k\le7$. Skewness makes $V^\perp$ invariant too, so $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(V)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(V^\perp)$, of dimension $\binom k2+\binom{8-k}2$. This is symmetric under $k\mapsto8-k$ by exchanging $V$ and $V^\perp$, is below $21$ on $2\le k\le6$, and equals $21$ at $k\in\{1,7\}$. There a subspace of equal finite dimension inside the containment is the whole, so $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{so}(W)$ for the $7$-dimensional one of $V,V^\perp$, and $\mathfrak h$ annihilates the line $W^\perp$. So irreducibility reduces to: $\mathfrak{spin}(7)$ annihilates no nonzero vector of $\mathbb R^8$. The record's reason, transitivity on $S^7$, is true ($\mathrm{Spin}(7)/G_2=S^7$) and implies it, since at a fixed $x$ the orbit map would have rank $0$ and not $7$.

The step has a proof lighter than transitivity. In [REVIEW_I40b](REVIEW_I40b.md)'s octonion model, for orthonormal imaginary $u,v$ the element $X=L_uL_v-L_vL_u=2L_uL_v$ lies in $\mathfrak h$, and $L_uL_v=-L_vL_u$, $L_u^2=L_v^2=-1$ give $(L_uL_v)^2=-L_u^2L_v^2=-1$. So $X^2=-4$, $X$ is invertible, and $Xx=0$ forces $x=0$. The same holds in [REVIEW_I40a](REVIEW_I40a.md)'s Clifford model with $X=e_1e_2$. This is also the natural Lean target, a skew $8\times8$ matrix squaring to $-4$ built from octonion multiplication.

The $O(8)$ conclusion: an $O(8)$-conjugate of an involution has an $O(8)$-conjugate fixed algebra, and $v\mapsto gv$ intertwines the two actions on $\mathbb R^8$, so irreducibility and dimension are preserved. The list's fixed algebras are $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(8-p)$, $0\le p\le4$, of dimensions $28,21,16,13,12$, and $\mathfrak u(4)$ of dimension $16$. Only $p=1$ has dimension $21$, and it is reducible. The record's "all of which are reducible" should read "all of which, at $p\ge1$, are reducible, and $p=0$ has dimension $28$", as REVIEW_I40a does by dimension first.
