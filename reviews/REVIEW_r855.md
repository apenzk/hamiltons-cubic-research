## Conventions, quoted from the brief

- The inner product on $\Lambda^2$ is $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$, making $\{\varphi_{ab}\}_{a<b}$ orthonormal, and "diagonal" means diagonal in the coordinate $2$-planes of an orthonormal frame.
- **[L180](../findings/diagonal-half.md)(1)**: with $W=\sum_i\lambda_i\,\omega_i\otimes\omega_i$ nonzero in $\mathcal W_n$, the $\omega_i$ orthonormal and every $\lambda_i\ne0$, and $A_i=-\omega_i^2=\omega_i\omega_i^{\mathsf T}$, Ricci-flatness is exactly $\sum_i\lambda_i\,\omega_i^2=0$, equivalently $\sum_i\lambda_iA_i=0$ with every $A_i$ positive semidefinite of trace $2$.
- **The specialisation**: at a decomposable $\omega=u\wedge v$ with $u,v$ orthonormal, $\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}=uu^{\mathsf T}+vv^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_P$. True for the normalisation $u\wedge v=vu^{\mathsf T}-uv^{\mathsf T}$, the record's $\varphi_{ab}=E_{ba}-E_{ab}$, and the $1/\sqrt2$ wedge gives $\tfrac12\Pi_P$.
- **[Ghorpade–Patil–Pillai](../references/decomposable-subspaces-grassmann-codes/notes.md) Theorem 6** at $\ell=2$: a subspace of $\Lambda^2V$ every element of which is decomposable is a pencil $f\wedge U$ or $\Lambda^2U$ with $\dim U=3$. At subspace dimension two the two types coincide, and Corollary 7 caps a pencil at $r\le n-1$.
- **$\mathfrak b(P_E)$ is the trace of $\mathrm{Pf}$ restricted to $E$**, so the $\Lambda^4$ test is necessary for $E$ to carry an orthonormal decomposable basis.
- **[W1](../findings/INDEX-traps.md)**: $\ker W\ne0$ is this record's recurring obstruction, and a check run only on objects with trivial kernel never reaches it. Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is an exact critical point at every $n\ge4$ with a kernel.

## 1. (A) The setup, and the locus it actually names

Theorem 6 is invoked correctly. Its hypothesis is that every element of the subspace is decomposable, the block supposes exactly that of each eigenspace, and the conclusion at $\ell=2$ is the stated dichotomy. Nothing has been upgraded silently: the sentence says *suppose*, and the dichotomy is a theorem about the supposed class.

What is wrong is the label. The block one screen above, at [diagonal-half.md:1134](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1134), records the chain of three strictly nested conditions in its own words: all-decomposable, then an orthonormal decomposable basis, then a decomposable basis, and it names both separators. The first inclusion is strict at $\mathrm{span}\{e_1\wedge e_2,\,e_3\wedge e_4\}$, which has the orthonormal decomposable basis $\{e_1\wedge e_2,\,e_3\wedge e_4\}$ while $\cos t\,e_1\wedge e_2+\sin t\,e_3\wedge e_4$ has $\mathrm{Pf}=\tfrac12\sin 2t\ne0$ off the axes. The second is strict at $\mathrm{span}\{e_1\wedge e_2,\,e_3\wedge e_4,\,(e_1+e_3)\wedge(e_2+e_4)\}$ at $\mathfrak b(P_E)=-\tfrac12$.

So an eigenspace of a $W$ passing the $\Lambda^4$ test is **not** all-decomposable. The test $\mathfrak b(P_E)=0$ is the trace of $\mathrm{Pf}$ on $E$ and is only necessary for the MIDDLE condition, which is itself strictly weaker than all-decomposability. And what coordinate-diagonality needs is the third condition sharpened to a frame-compatible basis. The block's locus is therefore the smallest of the three, and the strictness witness for the first inclusion is [r849](REVIEW_r849.md)'s standing obstruction, which is the object the item has been stuck on. Calling the all-decomposable case "what sub-step (a) leaves" reverses that: what sub-step (a) leaves is the case the block excludes.

There is a second, quantitative consequence of the supposition that goes unremarked and lands on [W1](../findings/INDEX-traps.md). If "every eigenspace" includes the kernel, then Corollary 7 caps the kernel at $n-1$, so $\dim\ker W\le n-1$ and the $\Lambda^2$-rank of $W$ is at least $\binom n2-(n-1)=\binom{n-1}2$. At $n=5$ that is a rank of at least six. Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has $\Lambda^2$-rank three and kernel of dimension $\binom n2-3$, which exceeds $n-1$ at every $n\ge5$, so the locus excludes the record's own exact critical point by the count alone, and excludes it at $n=4$ as well because its $\lambda=2$ eigenform $(e_{12}+e_{34})/\sqrt2$ is not decomposable. The locus is a high-rank locus, and the kernel obstruction is not reached on it. That is worth one clause in the block.

## 2. (B) The pencil sum, and where orthonormality enters

**Derivation.** Let $E=f\wedge U$ be a pencil. Replacing $U$ by $U'=\pi_{f^\perp}U$ changes nothing, since $f\wedge u=f\wedge\pi_{f^\perp}u$, so take $U'\perp f$ of dimension $r$. For $u,v\in f^\perp$, with $f\wedge u=uf^{\mathsf T}-fu^{\mathsf T}$,
$$(f\wedge u)^{\mathsf T}(f\wedge v)=(fu^{\mathsf T}-uf^{\mathsf T})(vf^{\mathsf T}-fv^{\mathsf T}),$$
whose trace is $2\lVert f\rVert^2(u\cdot v)-2(u\cdot f)(v\cdot f)$, so
$$\langle f\wedge u,\;f\wedge v\rangle=\lVert f\rVert^2(u\cdot v)-(u\cdot f)(v\cdot f)=\lVert f\rVert^2\,\langle \pi_{f^\perp}u,\;\pi_{f^\perp}v\rangle .$$
At $\lVert f\rVert=1$ and $u_i\in f^\perp$ this says $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ is orthonormal exactly when $\{u_i\}$ is. Then $(\hat f,u_1,\dots,u_r)$ is an orthonormal set, each support plane is the coordinate plane $\langle \hat f,u_i\rangle$ of a frame extending it, and the specialisation gives $\Pi_{\langle f,u_i\rangle}=ff^{\mathsf T}+u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$. Summing, $f$ appearing once per basis element and the $u_i$ exhausting an orthonormal basis of $U'$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^r\Pi_{\langle f,u_i\rangle}=r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\sum_{i=1}^ru_iu_i^{\mathsf T}=r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'} .$$
The identity is true. Both substantive steps are orthonormality steps: $\Pi_P=ff^{\mathsf T}+u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$ needs $f\perp u_i$ with both unit, and $\sum_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_{U'}$ needs $\{u_i\}$ orthonormal.

**Does orthonormality of $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ force it?** No, and the failure is asymmetric in a way the block's own notation hides. The displayed formula above shows the wedge sees only $\lVert f\rVert^2$ times the Gram matrix of the projections $\pi_{f^\perp}u_i$. So orthonormality of the wedge basis forces $\{\lVert f\rVert\,\pi_{f^\perp}u_i\}$ orthonormal and nothing more. The plane $\langle f,u_i\rangle$ and the wedge are both invariant under $u_i\mapsto u_i+c_if$, so the $\perp f$ part is genuinely free. But **$\lVert f\rVert=1$ is not forced**, because $(f,u_i)\mapsto(tf,\;u_i/t)$ leaves every wedge fixed. And the right-hand side is not invariant under that rescaling: $\Pi_{U'}$ is written as a projection and self-normalises, while $r\,ff^{\mathsf T}$ does not.

**The trap, in closed form.** At $n=5$ take $f=2e_1$ and $u_i=e_{i+1}/2$ for $i=1,2,3$. Then $f\wedge u_i=\varphi_{1,i+1}$, so $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ is an orthonormal basis of the pencil, and the true sum of plane projections is $\operatorname{diag}(3,1,1,1,0)$ while $r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}=\operatorname{diag}(12,1,1,1,0)$. Screened, and closed form. Normalising $f$ repairs it exactly.

So the identity needs a hypothesis the block does not state, namely $\lVert f\rVert=1$, with $\{u_i\}$ then an orthonormal basis of $U'=\pi_{f^\perp}U$. The fix is four words. **[r852](REVIEW_r852.md)'s D1 already carries the inner-product computation and the frame $(\hat f,u_1,\dots,u_r)$, with $\lVert f\rVert$ explicit**, so the block's clause is one line from a recorded result and is not cited to it.

## 3. (C) The $\Lambda^2U$ sum, and the docstring's reason

**Derivation.** Let $\{h_1,h_2,h_3\}$ be an orthonormal basis of $U$. Then $\{h_2\wedge h_3,\,h_3\wedge h_1,\,h_1\wedge h_2\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2U$, and by [r852](REVIEW_r852.md)'s D2 every orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2U$ is of that shape, via the Hodge star inside $U$. The three support planes are $\langle h_2,h_3\rangle$, $\langle h_3,h_1\rangle$, $\langle h_1,h_2\rangle$, so
$$\sum\Pi=(h_2h_2^{\mathsf T}+h_3h_3^{\mathsf T})+(h_3h_3^{\mathsf T}+h_1h_1^{\mathsf T})+(h_1h_1^{\mathsf T}+h_2h_2^{\mathsf T})=2\sum_{i=1}^3h_ih_i^{\mathsf T}=2\,\Pi_U .$$
Read the middle step as it is written: each $h_ih_i^{\mathsf T}$ occurs exactly twice because $h_i$ lies in two of the three coordinate planes of a three-space, and $\sum_ih_ih_i^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_U$ because $\{h_i\}$ is orthonormal. **So the Lean docstring's reason, "each direction appears in two of the three planes", is the correct reason and the whole of the combinatorics.** Trace check: $3\times2=6=2\times3$.

## 4. (D) The assembled equation

It follows, and the grouping is sound. Three steps are used silently, all of them free, and two of them were flagged twenty lines earlier in the same section.

1. **The grouping.** Every element of an eigenspace carries the same $\lambda$, and a union of orthonormal bases of the eigenspaces is an orthonormal eigenbasis of $\Lambda^2$, so $\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\Pi_{P_\alpha}=\sum_\mu\lambda_\mu\big(\sum_{\alpha\in E_\mu}\Pi_{P_\alpha}\big)$. Legitimate, and the choice of the structured basis inside each eigenspace is legitimate because any orthonormal basis of an eigenspace is an eigenbasis of it.
2. **Basis-independence, which is the reason the closed forms are facts and not choices.** The map $S^2(\Lambda^2)\to S^2(\mathbb R^n)$ sending $\omega\otimes\omega\mapsto\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}$ is linear and $\sum_{\alpha\in E}\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha=P_E$ for every orthonormal basis of $E$, so $\sum_{\alpha\in E}\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ depends only on $E$. The two closed forms are therefore the value of one linear functional of $P_{E_\mu}$, computed in a convenient basis, and the equation is well posed as written. This is a strengthening the block does not have and should, because it is what licenses picking the structured basis.
3. **The type assignment is unambiguous.** At $r_\mu=1$ the two types coincide and $ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}=\Pi_P$, independent of the choice of line in the plane. At $r_\mu=2$ the two planes meet in a line by Lemma 3, so $f$ is canonical up to sign and $ff^{\mathsf T}$ is canonical. From $r_\mu=3$ the types are genuinely distinct, a pencil having a common line and $\Lambda^2U$ having none, and both contribute trace $2r_\mu$, so no eigenspace can be double-counted.
4. **The specialisation** is applied correctly, and only under the normalisation $u\wedge v=vu^{\mathsf T}-uv^{\mathsf T}$. The $1/\sqrt2$ wedge returns $\tfrac12\Pi_P$ and every coefficient in the equation halves. Unstated here, and already recorded as an inherited hypothesis at [diagonal-half.md:1116](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1116).
5. **The zero eigenspace is handled correctly, and by accident.** L180 assumes every $\lambda_i\ne0$ while the equation ranges over a full eigenbasis. Dropping the kernel's terms is free, because they enter as $0\cdot\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ and vanish whether or not $\omega_\alpha$ is decomposable, so the extension of L180(1) to a spectrum carrying zeros costs nothing, its proof spending no nonvanishing anywhere. **That extension is exactly the second of the two hypotheses [diagonal-half.md:1116](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1116) records as inherited without notice, and it is inherited without notice again here.** The equation is right. What the omission does damage to is the two counts, which is §5.

## 5. (E) The two counts

**The dimension bound and the eigenspace count.** All-decomposable subspaces of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ have dimension at most $n-1$ for $n\ge4$: Corollary 7 caps type I at $n-1$ and type II at $3$, and $3\le n-1$ from $n\ge4$. Eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal and exhaust $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, so their number is at least $\binom n2/(n-1)=n/2$, hence at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$. **The arithmetic is right.** The bound itself fails at $n=3$, where $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^3=\Lambda^2U$ is all-decomposable of dimension $3>n-1$, which is outside the $n\ge5$ context and worth a domain clause only.

**The trace identity.** Each eigenspace contributes $\operatorname{tr}(r_\mu\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'_\mu})=2r_\mu$ or $\operatorname{tr}(2\Pi_{U_\nu})=6$, while the unweighted total over all of $\Lambda^2$ has trace $\sum_\alpha\operatorname{tr}\Pi_{P_\alpha}=2\binom n2$, each plane projection having trace $2$. Equating, $\sum_\mu r_\mu+3\cdot\#\{\Lambda^2\text{ types}\}=\binom n2$. **The identity is right, and it is a statement about ALL eigenspaces.** It is also the trace of (F): $\operatorname{tr}\big((n-1)\mathrm{Id}\big)=n(n-1)=2\binom n2$.

**Where both counts break.** Both are derived from the unweighted total over the whole of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, so both range over every eigenspace, kernel included. The equation ranges over the eigenspaces with $\lambda\ne0$, necessarily, because the kernel's terms are zero and are absent from the display. The block writes both counts with the same indices $\mu$ and $\nu$ as the equation, immediately after it, under "two counts come with it for free". Under that reading both are false whenever $\ker W\ne0$:
$$\sum_\mu r_\mu+3\cdot\#\{\Lambda^2\text{ types}\}=\binom n2-\dim\ker W,\qquad \#\{\text{eigenspaces}\}\ \ge\ \frac{\binom n2-\dim\ker W}{n-1}.$$

**Exhibit, closed form, $n=4$.** Let $E_1=e_1\wedge\langle e_2,e_3,e_4\rangle=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_{12},\varphi_{13},\varphi_{14}\}$, a pencil of dimension $3=n-1$, and $E_2=\Lambda^2\langle e_2,e_3,e_4\rangle=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_{23},\varphi_{24},\varphi_{34}\}$, a $\Lambda^2U$ at $\dim U=3$. They are orthogonal and exhaust $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$, and both are all-decomposable, so $W=\lambda P_{E_1}$ with $\lambda\ne0$ has **every** eigenspace all-decomposable and $\dim\ker W=3\le n-1$. Over all eigenspaces the counts read $3+3=6=\binom42$ and two eigenspaces $=n/2$, both correct. Over the equation's index set they read $\sum_\mu r_\mu=3\ne6$ and one eigenspace against $n/2=2$, both wrong. One exhibit, both counts, no search.

This $W$ is not Ricci-flat: $\lambda(3e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{\langle e_2,e_3,e_4\rangle})=\lambda\operatorname{diag}(3,1,1,1)\ne0$, and adding a second weight $\lambda_2$ on $E_2$ gives $\operatorname{diag}(3\lambda_1,\lambda_1+2\lambda_2,\dots)=0$ only at $\lambda_1=0$. **So the exhibit establishes that the counts are sensitive to the index set and not that they fail on the Ricci-flat locus.** Whether the Ricci-flat all-decomposable locus admits a kernel at all is a question this review does not settle and did not search: it is exactly [W1](../findings/INDEX-traps.md), and the block does not settle it either. The defect is one clause of scope, which is why the verdict is REFUTED AS STATED and not REFUTED.

## 6. (F) The unweighted total, proved and strengthened

**Claim.** For every orthonormal basis $\{\omega_\alpha\}$ of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, $\sum_\alpha\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$.

**Proof, two lines.** The map $T:S^2(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)\to S^2(\mathbb R^n)$ with $T(\omega\otimes\omega)=\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}$ is linear, and $\sum_\alpha\omega_\alpha\otimes\omega_\alpha=\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^2}$ for every orthonormal basis, so $\sum_\alpha\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}=T(\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^2})$ is basis-independent. Evaluate on the coordinate basis: $\varphi_{ab}\varphi_{ab}^{\mathsf T}=e_ae_a^{\mathsf T}+e_be_b^{\mathsf T}$, and each index $a$ lies in exactly $n-1$ pairs, so the sum is $(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$. $\square$

**So decomposability is not needed and the identity is not about planes.** At a decomposable $\omega_\alpha$ the summand is $\Pi_{P_\alpha}$, which is how the block reads it, but the identity holds verbatim with no decomposable element in the basis. Screen, on objects built for the purpose: a random rotation of the coordinate basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ at $n=4,5,6$ is orthonormal, contains zero decomposable elements, and returns $(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$ in each case. Reported as a screen. The closed-form instance that makes the point without any arithmetic is $n=4$: each of the six unit self-dual and anti-self-dual forms has $\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}=\tfrac12\mathrm{Id}_4$, since $\varphi_{12}\varphi_{34}^{\mathsf T}=0$ on disjoint index pairs, and $6\times\tfrac12=3=n-1$.

**This is a GAP.** [diagonal-half.md:1116](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1116) already records "$\sum_\alpha\Pi_{P_\alpha}=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$ for any orthonormal decomposable basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, checked at $n=4,5,6,7$" and credits it to [r849](REVIEW_r849.md) and [r850](REVIEW_r850.md). The block under review reports it again, twenty lines below, as "exact on the screen ... at $n=4$ to $7$", with no citation and still with no derivation. The repair is to cite the earlier line and replace both screens with the two-line argument above, which also drops the inert hypothesis.

## 7. (G) The Lean file against its row

**Build and axioms.** The file compiles inside the project. A scratch file, since removed, reported

```

```

No `sorryAx`, so the row's "Kernel-checked, no compiler-trusting tactic" and "the build carrying no gap" are both confirmed as far as axioms go.

**What the two theorems are about.** `planeProj u v` is *defined* as `outer u + outer v`, and neither theorem assumes anything of `u`, `v` or `f`. So:

- `sum_planeProj_pencil` unfolds to $\sum_{i<r}(A+B_i)=r\cdot A+\sum_{i<r}B_i$ for arbitrary symmetric rank-one-shaped matrices, which is `Finset.sum_add_distrib` composed with `Finset.sum_const`. It is a statement about finite sums in an additive commutative monoid. It applies, and I instantiated it, at $f=u_i=(1,1)$ in $\mathbb R^2$ with $r=3$, where `planeProj u u = 2 • outer u` has rank one, trace $2\lVert u\rVert^2=4$, and is not a projection onto anything.
- `sum_planeProj_lambda2` unfolds to $(A+B)+(B+C)+(A+C)=2(A+B+C)$, discharged by `ring` entrywise. This one does carry (C)'s combinatorial content, the multiplicity two, even without orthonormality.

**So `sum_planeProj_pencil` does not state (B).** (B) has exactly two substantive steps, §2 above, and the file contains neither. The first, $\Pi_{\langle f,u_i\rangle}=ff^{\mathsf T}+u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$, is absorbed into the definition of `planeProj`. The second, $\sum_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_{U'}$, **never appears at all**: the theorem's right-hand side is `∑ i, outer (u i)`, left as a sum, so the projection $\Pi_{U'}$ that (B) is about is not in the statement. Nor is $\lVert f\rVert=1$, whose absence §2 shows to be a real gap in the prose claim. The theorems are true and non-vacuous as monoid and ring identities, and trivial as mathematics: they are the bookkeeping of (B) and (C) with the geometry removed.

**Is the row honest?** Partially, and it is better than most rows of its kind. It discloses the decisive point in its own words: "it assumes rather than proves that the vectors are orthonormal, `planeProj` being the outer-product sum by definition rather than a projection derived from a plane", and it names what the file does not state, the equation itself. The file header is honest too: "Neither theorem needs the plane, the wedge or any spectral data: they are identities between outer products, which is why they are provable here."

Two things the row does overstate.

1. Its prose says the plane projections "add to $r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}$", which is (B), while the theorem it paraphrases has $\sum_i\texttt{outer}(u_i)$ on the right. The identification of that sum with a projection is the half of (B) that is not definitional, and its absence is disclosed nowhere. The disclosure covers orthonormality of each pair, not the completeness of $\{u_i\}$ as a basis of $U'$.
2. "**BOTH EIGENSPACE SUMS PROVED OUTRIGHT AT EVERY DIMENSION**" in bold capitals is what a reader takes away, and what is proved outright at every dimension is $\sum_i(A+B_i)=rA+\sum B_i$. The honest headline is that the two closed forms are typed with their orthonormality content assumed by definition, which is what the row's own small print says.

**Recommendation.** Either strengthen the file, defining `planeProj` from a genuine orthogonality hypothesis and stating $\sum_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$ as the projection onto the span, or demote the row's headline to match its small print. The second costs nothing and is enough.

## 8. (H) Is "a reduction" accurate?

No, on two independent counts.

**First, the direction is right but the locus is wrong.** The regrouping in §4 is lossless, so on the all-decomposable locus the equation is genuinely *equivalent* to Ricci-flatness, not merely implied by it. That is more than the brief's minimum. But the object being restated is a HYPOTHESIS, not the obligation. The obligation, per [diagonal-half.md:1110](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1110), is existential: that *some* decomposable orthonormal eigenbasis of $W$ lies in a common frame. The equation does not transform that goal, name a sufficient condition for it, or reduce its quantifier. It rewrites Ricci-flatness in coordinates adapted to the locus. **"A restatement of Ricci-flatness on the all-decomposable sub-locus" is what it is.** And that locus is the smallest of the record's three nested conditions (§1), so "the remaining half" is wrong twice over: wrong class, and not the half that remains.

**Second, and sharper: the record already knows an identity of this shape cannot close it.** [diagonal-half.md:1110](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1110) and the block below it record that the universal reading of $\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\Pi_{P_\alpha}=0$ is FALSE, with the record's own witness at $n=4$ and weights $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1,-1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$: every weight nonzero, the identity satisfied at every $\sin2t\ne0$, and the six planes in no common frame, only two lines lying in three of the six supports where a frame needs four. The cross-eigenspace equation is that identity regrouped. So a solution of the equation does not give a frame, and no argument that consumes only the equation can prove I36b. The block's escape is that reselection inside each eigenspace is free, so the equation constrains $W$ rather than a chosen basis. That is correct and is the reason the route is not dead, but it means the equation must be spent **together with** the reselection freedom, and it is the second ingredient that carries the existential. Saying the item is reduced "to one linear identity in the pencil data" drops that ingredient from the price.

**What is accurate and should be kept.** The block's disclaimer, "What is NOT claimed: that this closes anything", is the right instinct and is honest. The equation is a useful normal form, the two closed forms are correct, and starting from an equation rather than a search is the right move under the standing rule. One sentence of relabelling and it is unobjectionable.

## 10. Arguing the opposite, and what is withdrawn

- **Against A.** The block says *suppose*, so it is a hypothesis honestly declared, and the round it belongs to was about the all-decomposable case. Read narrowly, the invocation of Theorem 6 is impeccable. **Kept**, because the defect is not in the invocation but in the sentence "which is what sub-step (a) leaves" and in (H)'s "the remaining half", and both are claims about scope that the block's own neighbour at [diagonal-half.md:1134](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1134) contradicts.
- **Against B.** One could say $ff^{\mathsf T}$ obviously means the unit $f$, since $f$ names a line and a line has a unit vector up to sign. That is the charitable reading and it is probably what was meant. **Kept as CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA rather than escalated**, because the record's convention is to state normalisations, the neighbouring block was corrected for exactly this class of omission at the wedge, and the screen in §2 shows the error is a factor of four rather than a subtlety.
- **Against C.** Nothing to argue: the identity, its hypotheses and the docstring's reason are all correct. **No defect found**, and this is recorded as a clean pass.
- **Against D.** The zero eigenspace could be said to be handled trivially and therefore not worth a clause. **Conceded in part**: the equation is correct and the extension is free, so the verdict is CONFIRMED. The clause is owed anyway, because L180 carries $\lambda_i\ne0$ as a hypothesis and the equation quietly drops it for the second time in this section.
- **Against E.** One could read "eigenspaces" throughout as ranging over all of them, kernel included, in which case both counts are simply true and there is no defect. That reading is available, and it is the one under which the counts were derived. **Kept as REFUTED AS STATED**, because the counts are attached to the equation by "come with it", carry its indices, and the equation cannot include the kernel. The exhibit in §5 shows the two readings give different numbers on an object satisfying the block's own hypothesis, which is what makes the ambiguity a defect rather than a quibble.
- **Against F.** The block's screen is not wrong, only weaker than the truth. **Kept as CONFIRMED with a strengthening and a GAP**, because a two-line basis-free proof exists, and reporting a provable identity as a screen at four dimensions is what the standing analysis-only rule exists to prevent.
- **Against G.** The row discloses the definitional `planeProj` in its own small print, so a careful reader is warned. **Kept as TRIVIAL with the row PARTIALLY HONEST**, because the missing $\Pi_{U'}$ is a second gap that the disclosure does not reach and the bold headline points the other way.

What survives is the observation in §1 that the locus excludes it, which is a scope point rather than a refutation.

## 11. What would break the route, and what was done to look for it

**The route is: classify each eigenspace by Theorem 6, write Ricci-flatness in the resulting pencil data, and squeeze a common frame out of that equation.** Three things would break it.

1. **An all-decomposable, Ricci-flat, non-coordinate-diagonal $W$ at some $n\ge5$.** This is the direct falsifier. I did not hunt for it: hunting is a sweep, and the standing rule forbids it. What I did instead was read the record's nearest object, the $n=4$ witness at weights $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1,-1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, and check what it does and does not show. It satisfies the identity with all weights nonzero and its planes lie in no common frame, but reselection collapses it to a coordinate-diagonal point, so it breaks the universal reading and not the route. **That is the shape a real falsifier would have to beat: it must survive reselection inside every eigenspace, and by [r852](REVIEW_r852.md) reselection is free in both types, so the falsifier must fail on cross-eigenspace compatibility alone.**
2. **The equation being too weak to carry the existential.** Argued in §8, and this is the live risk. The equation is one symmetric-matrix identity, $\binom{n+1}2$ scalar equations, against pencil data of dimension growing like $n^2$ and an existential quantifier over frames. Nothing in the block bounds the solution set of the equation, and the record's witness shows the solution set is strictly larger than the frame-compatible locus in the naive reading. **A proof of I36b will have to spend the reselection freedom as a second ingredient, and the block prices only the equation.**
3. **The locus being the wrong one, which §1 establishes.** Even a complete solution of the equation on the all-decomposable locus leaves both strict inclusions of the record's three-condition chain untouched, and the second inclusion's separator is [r849](REVIEW_r849.md)'s obstruction, which is what the item has been stuck on. **The route as scoped cannot reach $\mathrm{span}\{e_1\wedge e_2,\,e_3\wedge e_4\}$ as an eigenspace, and that is the object I36b needs.**

**How I looked.** Derived (B), (C), (D) and (F) from the inner product and the wedge convention with no numerics; built the two closed-form exhibits by hand, the $\lVert f\rVert=2$ pencil and the $n=4$ pencil-plus-$\Lambda^2U$ split; screened those two and (F)'s basis-independence in seconds of arithmetic on objects constructed for the purpose, reported as screens; built the Lean file, ran `#print axioms` on both theorems in a scratch file that has been removed, and instantiated `sum_planeProj_pencil` at non-orthonormal data to establish that it constrains nothing; grepped `findings/` for the three objects and read [L180](../findings/diagonal-half.md), [L185](../findings/diagonal-half.md) and [r852](REVIEW_r852.md) in full. No search, sweep, census or optimisation was run.

## Overall verdict

**The mathematics is right and the framing is not.** Both closed forms are true, (C) exactly as written and (B) once $\lVert f\rVert=1$ is added; the assembled equation follows from [L180](../findings/diagonal-half.md)(1) and is in fact equivalent to Ricci-flatness on the stated locus; (F) is true, stronger than claimed, and provable in two lines; and the Lean file is clean but trivial, its row honest in the small print and overstated in the headline. Three claims fail as stated: the locus is the smallest of the record's three nested conditions rather than what sub-step (a) leaves, the two counts range over an index set the equation cannot have, and "a reduction of the remaining half" describes a restatement of a hypothesis on a proper sub-case. Two facts are re-derivations of recorded ones, at [diagonal-half.md:1116](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1116) and in [r852](REVIEW_r852.md)'s D1.

**Keep the block. Five clauses fix it, none of them touching the equation.**
