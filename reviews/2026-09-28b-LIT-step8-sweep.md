## The four-set route's first link: criticality removes the pair part's linear error (derived here, unreviewed)

Split $W=A+B_p+B_f$ in the maximal frame into diagonal, pair and four-set parts, $\lVert A\rVert=t$. By [L120](../findings/second-order.md) every cross term between different sign characters vanishes, so
$$P(W)=P(A)+P(B_p)+P(B_f)+3\langle L_AB_p,B_p\rangle+3\langle L_AB_f,B_f\rangle+3T(B_p,B_p,B_f)+3T(B_f,B_f,B_p),$$
and the one term linear in $B_p$ is $3T(B_f,B_f,B_p)$, the source of the crude $\sqrt n$. **Criticality removes it.** The pair-character part of $Q(W)=\rho W$ is $\rho B_p=\pi_pQ(B_f)+\pi_pQ(B_p)+2L_AB_p+2\pi_pQ(B_p,B_f)$, and pairing with $B_p$ gives $T(B_f,B_f,B_p)=\rho\lVert B_p\rVert^2-P(B_p)-2\langle L_AB_p,B_p\rangle-2T(B_f,B_p,B_p)$. So
$$\rho=P(B_f)+3\langle L_AB_f,B_f\rangle+P(A)+3\rho\lVert B_p\rVert^2-3\langle L_AB_p,B_p\rangle-2P(B_p)-3T(B_p,B_p,B_f),$$
**in which every term but $P(B_f)$ is $O(t)$ or $O(\lVert B_p\rVert^2)=O((n-1)t^2)$.** **Typed**: `pair_identity` and `small_mass_bound` in [PairIdentity.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/PairIdentity.lean), standard three axioms. *The Lean proof needs only $T(A,A,B_f)=0$ among the cross terms: $T(A,A,B_p)$ and $T(A,B_p,B_f)$ cancel against three times the paired criticality, so the identity holds in any frame where the diagonal part's square has no four-set component.* *Gate 1: the identity holds to $1\times10^{-15}$ at $S^4\times S^4$ rotated into a random frame ($t=0.236$, pair and four-set masses $0.47$ each), a critical point in a generic frame ([check_I121_pair_identity.py](../scripts/misc/check_I121_pair_identity.py)).* With $S_F=\sup P$ on the unit four-set sector, $\bar\mu=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, and $K$ an $O(1)$ constant from the norm of $Q$ as a bilinear map,
$$\rho\ \le\ S_F+3\bar\mu\,t+K(n-1)\,t^2+\theta_0t^3 .$$
**So at even $n$ (L57 enters through $P(A)\le\theta_0t^3$), if $S_F<\theta_0(n)$, a global maximiser in the maximal frame has $t\gtrsim\sqrt{(\theta_0-S_F)/(Kn)}$**, of order $n^{-1/2}$ against Schur's $n^{-1}$. **$\bar\mu=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ is on the record as [L218](../findings/second-order.md)**, $\Lambda_n=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ exactly, which caps both $\langle L_AB_f,B_f\rangle$ and $\langle L_AB_p,B_p\rangle$ by $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2t$ times the squared norm. *Agreement: the four-set case also follows in two lines from L121, $\mu_Q^2=\tfrac18[3(A^2+B^2+C^2)-(A+B+C)^2]\le\tfrac38(A^2+B^2+C^2)\le\tfrac34\lVert\hat A\rVert^2$, equality at the padded $S^2\times S^2$.* *The hope, owed item (iii), is that this meets the regime where a joint bound works; the first sweep's attempt 4 still fails at small $t$ and its $t_*\sim n^{-1/2}$ is stated only for concentrated diagonal parts* ([r1828](REVIEW_r1828.md)). *What the route owes is (i) $S_F<\theta_0(n)$ at every even $n\ge12$, screened at $n\le12$ with room $0.2$ to $0.3$ and an I122-type supremum, (ii) $\bar\mu$ and $K$ explicit, (iii) the joint bound's constant at $t\sim n^{-1/2}$.*

## The route's constants, explicit (derived here, unreviewed)

- **$K$ from Banach's theorem.** $T(X,Y,Z)=\langle Q(X,Y),Z\rangle$ is a symmetric trilinear form on the Hilbert space $\mathcal W_n$, and on a real Hilbert space the norm of a symmetric multilinear form equals the supremum of its polynomial on the unit sphere (Banach 1938, Kellogg 1928 in finite dimension). So $\lvert T(X,Y,Z)\rvert\le\Theta_n\lVert X\rVert\lVert Y\rVert\lVert Z\rVert$, which is $\rho$ at a global maximiser.
- **The bound.** With [L218](../findings/second-order.md) for both $L_A$ terms and $P(A)\le\theta_0t^3$,
$$\rho\ \le\ S_F\lVert B_f\rVert^3+\tfrac{3\sqrt3}2\,t\,\lVert B_f\rVert^2+\theta_0t^3+\lVert B_p\rVert^2\Big(6\rho+\tfrac{3\sqrt3}2\,t+2\rho\lVert B_p\rVert\Big),\qquad\lVert B_p\rVert^2\le(n-1)t^2 .$$
- **What it gives, honestly.** Solving the bound, linear term kept, against Schur's exact floor $\sqrt{6/((n+1)(n+2))}$ with $\rho=\theta_0(n)$: room $\delta=0.17$ gives $c\approx0.131$ and a crossover at $n=346$, room $0.20$ gives $n=294$, room $0.10$ gives $n=586$ ([r1827](REVIEW_r1827.md), recomputed here). **The route is asymptotic, and even that needs the room bounded below uniformly in $n$**, which is unstated and unknown: the screened room is an upper bound measured at $n\le14$ and shrinks with $n$.

## Batch 4, outward, and the exact witness behind the screen

- **Searched**: maximising the projection of a group orbit onto a fixed subspace (Kostant's and Kirwan's convexity theorems, Atiyah–Guillemin–Sternberg), and Schur–Horn for diagonals of self-adjoint operators. **Nothing transferable**: the convexity theorems need a polar action, and $O(n)$ on $\mathcal W_n$ is not polar (the orbit space has dimension $\dim\mathcal W_n-\dim O(n)$, far above $\dim\mathcal D_n$). Schur–Horn over all bases of $\Lambda^2$ gives only $t\le1$, and nothing restricts it to the frame-induced bases $\{g\cdot e_i\wedge e_j\}$.
- **The exact witness** ([check_I121_rotated_product.py](../scripts/misc/check_I121_rotated_product.py)), **filed as [post F81](../findings/fence-posts.md)** after Gate 2 ([r1833](REVIEW_r1833.md), [r1834](REVIEW_r1834.md), both CONFIRMED on the witness and the proof, $e$ being the displayed operator divided by its norm $\tfrac{m^2(m-1)}{4(2m-1)}$, which leaves $t^2$ unchanged). Rotate the balanced product $e$ of $S^m\times S^m$ by $\tfrac\pi4$ in the $m$ planes $(p_k,q_k)$ pairing the two factors. The result is critical with value $\theta_0(n)$, has **pair part exactly $0$** (to $10^{-31}$ at $n=8,12$) and **diagonal mass $t^2=\tfrac1n$** ($\tfrac18$ and $\tfrac1{12}$). *At angle $\theta$ the pair entry is proportional to $\sin4\theta$, zero exactly at multiples of $\tfrac\pi4$, the product frame included.*
- **Proof of the witness (derived here, unreviewed).** In the product frame $e=\alpha(\Pi_{\Lambda^2P}+\Pi_{\Lambda^2Q'})+\beta\Pi_{P\otimes Q'}$ with $\alpha=\tfrac{m(m-1)}N$, $\beta=-\tfrac{(m-1)^2}N$, $N=(n-1)(n-2)$. The rotation sends the factors to $P'=\operatorname{span}\{u_k\}$, $Q''=\operatorname{span}\{v_k\}$, $u_k=\tfrac{e_{p_k}+e_{q_k}}{\sqrt2}$, $v_k=\tfrac{-e_{p_k}+e_{q_k}}{\sqrt2}$, so $g\cdot e=\beta\,I+(\alpha-\beta)(\Pi_{\Lambda^2P'}+\Pi_{\Lambda^2Q''})$. Each $e_a$ has $u$-coordinate $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\delta_{k(a)}$ and $v$-coordinate $\tfrac{\sigma_a}{\sqrt2}\delta_{k(a)}$, $\sigma=-1$ on $P$ and $+1$ on $Q'$, so $\langle(\Pi_{\Lambda^2P'}+\Pi_{\Lambda^2Q''})\,e_a\wedge e_c,\,e_b\wedge e_d\rangle$ is a sum of two $2\times2$ Gram determinants. **Diagonal**: $e_{p_k}\wedge e_{q_k}=u_k\wedge v_k$ has entry $\beta$, every other pair has half its mass in $\Lambda^2P'\oplus\Lambda^2Q''$ and entry $\tfrac{\alpha+\beta}2=\tfrac{m-1}{2N}$, so $t^2=\dfrac{m\beta^2+(\binom n2-m)\big(\tfrac{\alpha+\beta}2\big)^2}{m(m-1)\alpha^2+m^2\beta^2}=\dfrac{m-\tfrac12}{m(2m-1)}=\dfrac1n$. **Pair part**: at $(a,c;b,c)$, $a\ne b$, the entry is $(\alpha-\beta)\cdot\tfrac14(1+\sigma_a\sigma_b)\big(\delta_{k(a)k(b)}-\delta_{k(a)k(c)}\delta_{k(c)k(b)}\big)$, which vanishes: if $k(a)=k(b)$ then $\{a,b\}=\{p_k,q_k\}$ and $\sigma_a\sigma_b=-1$, and otherwise both deltas vanish. $\square$
- **What it settles, as scoped by Gate 2.** A critical point at the maximal value sits in the pair-free subspace at $t=n^{-1/2}$, the four-set route's own scale. **So at $n\ge12$ no strict bound (small $t$ forcing value below $\theta_0$, or at most $\sqrt{3/2}$) built from criticality and the sign-character sectors alone can separate small diagonal mass from the maximiser, down to $t^2=\tfrac1n$**; a non-strict bound is Conjecture A itself, and $t^2\ll\tfrac1n$ is untouched, and the screen's near-rotated product at $t=0.3$ was this object's neighbour. What excludes it is the maximal frame: the inverse matching rotation restores $t=1$. **Its frame is a critical point of the diagonal mass with negative semidefinite Hessian, and no single-plane rotation raises $t^2$** ([r1833](REVIEW_r1833.md), [r1834](REVIEW_r1834.md), screened), while rotating all matching planes together gives $t^2=\tfrac1n+(1-\tfrac1n)\sin^4(2\varphi)$ (fitted). *So the frame inequality the node needs must reach fourth order and move several planes at once, of which the matching rotation is the example.*

## Post F81's successor, formulated: visibility to a matching rotation (derived here, unreviewed)

- **The fourth-order expansion.** For $\xi\in\mathfrak{so}(n)$ put $D_k=\pi_{\mathcal D}(\xi^k\cdot W)$, so $f(\varphi)=\lVert\pi_{\mathcal D}(e^{\varphi\xi}W)\rVert^2=t^2+c_2\varphi^2+c_3\varphi^3+c_4\varphi^4+\dots$ with $c_2=\lVert D_1\rVert^2+\langle D_0,D_2\rangle$, $c_3=\tfrac13\langle D_0,D_3\rangle+\langle D_1,D_2\rangle$ and $c_4=\tfrac1{12}\langle D_0,D_4\rangle+\tfrac13\langle D_1,D_3\rangle+\tfrac14\lVert D_2\rVert^2$. **At a pair-free $W$, $D_1=0$**, since a one-plane generator moves the diagonal only through that plane's pair profile, **so $c_2=\langle A,D_2\rangle$ is $O(t)$ while $c_4$ carries $\tfrac14\lVert D_2\rVert^2$, whose four-set share is $O(1)$.** At small $t$ a multi-plane rotation that sees the four-set part therefore raises the diagonal mass at a small angle, and the witness is the extreme case, where $c_2=c_3=0$.
- **In finite form this is attempt 1's exact identity, unaveraged** ([first sweep](2026-09-28-LIT-step8-sweep.md)). In a maximal frame every perfect matching $M$ satisfies $\sum_{e<f\in M}\Delta_{ef}\le0$, so its **visible four-set energy** $E(M)=\sum_{e<f\in M}(\text{the pairing's combination})^2$ is at most the $A$-side, $\sum\tfrac14\square_{ef}^2$ plus the $\delta$ terms, which is $O(t^2)$ on average over $M$ by the forced sums. *Averaged over $M$ the visible energy is only $\tfrac32\lVert B_f\rVert^2/((n-1)(n-3))$, which is why attempt 1 washed out.*
- **The statement the node now needs, "visibility"**, in its corrected form below: in the maximal frame, a matching sees a fixed share of the whole off-diagonal part. *Random four-set tensors hide from every matching, at $E(M)\sim\lVert B_f\rVert^2/n^2$, but they have low $P$. The witness is fully visible to its own matching. So visibility is exactly the claim that high value forces structure a rotation can see, the conjecture's core in a combinatorial form.* **Next, a bounded screen of the visible fraction $\max_ME(M)/\lVert B_f\rVert^2$** at the high-value pair-free tensors the $n=12$ ascent found, against random ones.

## The matching inequality, constant $1$, and the visibility route as it stands

*Gate 2 ([r1836](REVIEW_r1836.md), [r1837](REVIEW_r1837.md), independently): the expansion and $D_1=0$ at pair-free $W$ CONFIRMED (also for multi-plane generators), the matching inequality CONFIRMED and sharpened, the route's $n$-free floor REFUTED as written, the screen REFUTED as support. Folded here.*

- **The matching inequality.** *In a globally maximal frame at even $n$, every perfect matching $M$ satisfies*
$$E(M)+\sum_{e\ne f\in M}(x^e_k+x^e_l)^2+\tfrac14\sum_{e<f}s_{ef}^2\ \le\ t^2-\sum_{e\in M}a_e^2\ \le\ t^2,$$
*with $E(M)=\sum_{e<f\in M}(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})^2$ and $s_{ef}=a_{ik}+a_{il}+a_{jk}+a_{jl}$.* **Proof.** The $\tfrac\pi4$ rotation along $M$ changes the diagonal mass by $\sum_{e<f}\Delta_{ef}\le0$. On the block diagonal $m=(a_{ik},a_{il},a_{jk},a_{jl})$ of an edge pair, the three subtracted terms $\tfrac14\square^2$, $(\delta^f_i+\delta^f_j)^2$, $(\delta^e_k+\delta^e_l)^2$ are the three non-constant Hadamard contrasts of $m$ over $4$, so by Parseval they sum to $\lVert m\rVert^2-\tfrac14s_{ef}^2$, and the blocks partition the cross entries. $\square$ *Validated here by the Hadamard algebra. An earlier bound of $3t^2$ counted each block three times.* The combination $W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk}$ is independent of the planes' orientation, and $E(M)\le\lVert B_f\rVert^2$ for every $M$.
- **The route, corrected.** Visibility must be stated **in the globally maximal frame and for the whole off-diagonal part**: *a unit $W$ with $P(W)>\sqrt{3/2}$ has, in its maximal frame, a perfect matching with $E(M)+\sum(x^e_k+x^e_l)^2\ge c\,(1-t^2)$.* With the inequality this gives $t^2\ge\tfrac c{1+c}$, **with no factor of $n$**. *Stated for the four-set part alone, as first written, the pair mass enters through the one-plane bound $(n-1)t^2$ and the floor is only $t^2\ge\tfrac c{1+cn}$, of order $\tfrac1n$ ([r1836](REVIEW_r1836.md), [r1837](REVIEW_r1837.md)).* **Visibility is a sufficient condition, not shown equivalent to the node.**
- **The screen does not bear on the route.** Its three kinds of tensor were measured in frames that are not maximal (the witness in its rotated frame, the others at $t=0$), where the inequality fails, so its shares ($1$, $0.54$, $0.04$) cannot be fed in. In a maximal frame every known high-value tensor has $t=1$ and no off-diagonal part, so **visibility cannot be tested on known examples**, and a screen that bears on it would first move each tensor to its maximal frame, an optimisation over $O(n)$ of unknown cost, which waits under the analysis-only rule.

## Visibility, attempt 1: organising $P$ by matchings fails

*Derived here, the premise checked and refuted by a screen.* $P$'s cubic couplings among four-set blocks live on rectangle triples $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3=Q_1\triangle Q_2$, and $Q_1=\{a,b,c,d\}$, $Q_2=\{a,b,e,f\}$, $Q_3=\{c,d,e,f\}$ are seen together by every matching containing $ab$, $cd$, $ef$. **If the coupling depended only on those three visible combinations**, $P(B_f)$ would be a sum over matchings of triangle sums in their visible combinations, and the spectral bound $\sum_{\text{triangles}}w_{ab}w_{bc}w_{ca}\le\tfrac{\sqrt2}3\big(\sum w^2\big)^{3/2}$ would tie high $P$ to a large $\max_ME(M)$. **The premise is false**: at $n=6$ the coupling restricted to the kernel of one block's visible combination has norm $0.289$ against $0.577$ overall ([check_I121_rectangle_coupling.py](../scripts/misc/check_I121_rectangle_coupling.py)), so the invisible directions of one matching couple too. *And even granted, averaging over matchings costs a factor $n-5$, giving visibility only of order $P^2/n^2$.* **Obstacle**: a four-set block's two directions are seen by different pairings, so the cubic mixes matchings, and a proof of visibility has to use more than one matching per rectangle.

## Visibility, attempt 2: the frame Hessian as one matrix (derived here, unreviewed)

- **The Hessian at a pair-free frame.** With $D_1=0$ the second-order coefficient of $f(\varphi)=\lVert\pi_{\mathcal D}(e^{\varphi\xi}W)\rVert^2$ is $H(\xi)=\langle A,\pi_{\mathcal D}(\xi^2\cdot W)\rangle$. Its diagonal-part share is $\langle A,\xi^2\cdot A\rangle=-\lVert\xi\cdot A\rVert^2$, since $\pi_{\mathcal D}A=A$ and the action is skew. The four-set part enters only through products $\xi_e\xi_f$ of two disjoint planes, each weighted by that pairing's rectangle difference $\square_{ef}$ times its combination, **agreement with [r1797](REVIEW_r1797.md) O7 and [r1798](REVIEW_r1798.md)**. So $H(\xi)=-\lVert\xi\cdot A\rVert^2+2\sum_{e<f}\xi_e\xi_f\,\kappa\,\square_{ef}\,(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})$, $\kappa$ a fixed constant.
- **Negative semidefiniteness along matching directions.** For a perfect matching $M$ and signs $\varepsilon_e$, $\xi=\sum_{e\in M}\varepsilon_eE_e$ gives
$$2\kappa\sum_{e<f\in M}\varepsilon_e\varepsilon_f\,\square_{ef}\,(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})\ \le\ \sum_{e\in M}\lVert E_e\cdot A\rVert^2\ =\ O(t^2),$$
**a $\square$-weighted visible energy capped at second order**, for every matching and every sign pattern. *So second order already enforces visibility wherever the rectangle differences are not small, and cannot where they vanish.* **The witness is exactly that case**: its diagonal is $\beta$ on the matching edges and $\tfrac{\alpha+\beta}2$ elsewhere, so every $\square_{ef}$ between matching edges is $0$, which is why [post F81](../findings/fence-posts.md) needed fourth order.
- **What attempt 3 must do.** Split the off-diagonal part by the size of the rectangle differences it pairs with: where $\lvert\square\rvert$ is large, second order caps it; where $\square\approx0$ the diagonal is block-constant along the matching, the witness's regime, and the fourth-order term $\tfrac14\lVert\pi_{\mathcal D}(\xi^2\cdot B)\rVert^2$ against $O(t)$ must cap it on the Hessian's null directions. *Unreviewed.* **$\kappa=2$ for unit plane generators**: the block's diagonal mass is $4(\alpha_{II}^2+\alpha_{IZ}^2+\alpha_{ZI}^2+\alpha_{ZZ}^2)$ with $\alpha_{ZZ}=\tfrac14\square$, and rotating the two planes by $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ carries $\alpha_{XX}=\tfrac12(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})$ into $\alpha_{ZZ}$ at order $4\varphi_1\varphi_2$, so the mass gains $8\alpha_{ZZ}\cdot4\varphi_1\varphi_2\alpha_{XX}=4\varphi_1\varphi_2\,\square\,(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})$ at second order.

## Visibility, the first direct test: frame-free, at $n=8$ (a screen)

([screen_I121_invisible.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_invisible.py), penalised ascent over unit pure four-set tensors under $E_M(B)\le\varepsilon$ for all $105$ matchings.) Every unit four-set tensor has $\max_ME_M(B)\ge\tfrac{3/2}{(n-1)(n-3)}=0.043$ at $n=8$, the average, so small $\varepsilon$ is infeasible.

| $\varepsilon$ | largest $P$ found | its largest visible share |
|---|---|---|
| $0.02$, $0.05$ | none feasible found | |
| $0.1$ | $0.708$ | $0.102$ |
| $0.2$ | $0.8333=S_F(8)$ | $0.191$ |
| $0.4$ | $0.8333$ | $0.326$ |

- **Visibility holds only in a weak form.** The four-set sector's maximiser is seen at most $19\%$ by any matching, so the constant is small, $c\approx0.2$ rather than $0.5$, and tensors seen at most $10\%$ reach $0.708$. *At $n=12$ an unconstrained four-set ascent at $P=0.95$ was seen $54\%$, so $c$ need not fall with $n$.*
- **What even this would give.** If a share $c\approx0.19$ held **in the maximal frame for the whole off-diagonal part**, the matching inequality would give $t^2\ge\tfrac c{1+c}\approx0.16$, $t\ge0.4$. That form is untested: a frame-free screen measures pure four-set tensors in the frame they were built in, and moving a tensor to its maximal frame is an optimisation of unknown cost.

## Visibility in arbitrary frames: the product stays visible (a screen)

([screen_I121_product_random_frame.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_product_random_frame.py).) The balanced product at $n=8$ in four random frames ($t^2=0.05$ to $0.10$): the best matching sees **$0.16$ to $0.21$ of the whole off-diagonal part**, four-set combinations and pair terms together, above the $0.05$ to $0.11$ a maximal frame would allow, which confirms those frames are not maximal. **So the high-value tensor stays visible at about $0.2$ in arbitrary frames**, the same share as the four-set maximiser.

**The route in its frame-free form.** *Conjecture $\mathrm V_n(c)$: every unit $W$ with $P(W)>\sqrt{3/2}$ has, in every orthonormal frame, a perfect matching seeing at least $c\,(1-t^2)$ of its off-diagonal part.* With the matching inequality in the maximal frame this gives $t^2\ge\tfrac c{1+c}$ at a global maximiser, a floor with no factor of $n$ (about $0.14$ at $c=0.16$). **What decides it is the adversarial frame**, the one minimising the best matching's share, and whether $c$ holds as $n$ grows.

- **At $n=12$ it does not hold.** Three random frames of the product ($t^2=0.02$ to $0.07$) give a best share of $0.117$ to $0.129$, against $0.16$ to $0.21$ at $n=8$: **the share falls like $1.5/n$** ($1.5/8=0.19$, $1.5/12=0.125$). So $\mathrm V_n(c)$ has no $n$-free constant, since random frames of the maximiser itself give $c\approx1.5/n$, and the frame-free route yields at best $t^2\gtrsim\tfrac{1.5}n$. *That still beats Schur's $\tfrac6{n^2}$ by a factor of about $\tfrac n4$.*
- **Adversarial frames at $n=8$** ([screen_I121_product_adversarial.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_product_adversarial.py), local descent over $O(8)$ from four starts): the best share falls to **$0.084$ to $0.092$, about $0.7/n$**, the frames still non-maximal (share above $t^2/(1-t^2)$). The frame-free floor is then $t^2\gtrsim0.7/n$: at $n=8$ it equals Schur's $\tfrac6{90}$, and it beats Schur only by about $\tfrac n9$.
- **The $n^{-1/2}$ barrier.** The witness of [post F81](../findings/fence-posts.md) sits at $t^2=\tfrac1n$ with value $\theta_0$. The pair identity's route stops at $t\sim n^{-1/2}$, and so does frame-free visibility. **Every argument so far that uses the maximal frame only through second-order conditions and matching inequalities stops at $t\sim n^{-1/2}$**, and an $n$-free floor needs a property of the maximal frame that no frame-free statement has.
