# I106b, the face the rung owes, and the first analytical attempt

**Opened 2026-09-17, immediately after [L230](../findings/second-order.md)'s Gate 2 round ([r974](REVIEW_r974.md), [r975](REVIEW_r975.md)) replaced the item's owed half.** *The item used to owe "singular implies splits at every $n$". It now owes something sharper and smaller, because the obstruction turned out to be a face rather than a stratum, and a face has nothing to enumerate.*

## The theorem, named before any work

> **If the value's maximum over the orthostochastic image is attained at a zero-diagonal $Q$, then it is also attained at a splitting one.**

*That is what [R28](../routes.md)'s induction actually consumes.* **The rung does not need the face to be avoided, only that landing on it costs nothing**, so the step can still hand down to the previous rung. Stating it as avoidance would be a strictly stronger claim than the route needs, and one this note does not attempt.

Typed as `face_hands_down` in [I106bFace.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I106bFace.lean), with a named `sorry`, before deriving anything. **Cited nowhere until proved.**

## Why a face is the right object, and why the value being linear is the whole reason

R28's frame dependence is $\sum_k\big(\beta\nu_k^2\,p_k^{\mathsf T}\hat Up_k+\nu_k\,p_k^{\mathsf T}Cp_k\big)$ over an orthonormal frame $P$. With $\hat U$ and $C$ diagonal in the field frame and $R=P^{\circ2}$, we have $p_k^{\mathsf T}\hat Up_k=(R^{\mathsf T}u)_k$ and likewise for $C$, so the value is

$$\langle R,A\rangle,\qquad A_{ik}=\beta\nu_k^2u_i+\nu_kc_i .$$

**A linear functional on a compact set.** That is what makes "the maximum is on the boundary" a real statement rather than a vacuous one, and it is why the obstruction is a face: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}$ is nonnegative on the image, vanishes exactly on images of zero-diagonal $Q$, and is nonconstant, so $\{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}=0\}$ is a face relative to the affine hull, and it carries non-split points at $k=4$ and $k=6$. *The three facts about $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}$ are typed as `trace_image_eq`, `trace_image_nonneg` and `trace_image_eq_zero_iff`, none of which needs a hypothesis on $Q$.*

## The first analytical attempt, stated before any code

**Maximising the value over the image is maximising a quadratic form over the group.** Writing $f(Q)=\langle A,Q\circ Q\rangle=\sum_{i,k}A_{ik}Q_{ik}^2$, the problem is $\max_{Q\in O(k)}f(Q)$, and the image never has to be parametrised.

**First order.** Along $Q\Omega$ with $\Omega$ skew, $\mathrm df_Q[Q\Omega]=2\langle A,Q\circ(Q\Omega)\rangle=2\langle A\circ Q,Q\Omega\rangle=2\langle Q^{\mathsf T}(A\circ Q),\Omega\rangle$. Since $\Omega$ ranges over all skew matrices, criticality is

$$\boxed{\,Q^{\mathsf T}(A\circ Q)\ \text{is symmetric.}\,}$$

*This is the general-$\lvert J\rvert$ analogue of the record's own first-order condition at $\lvert J\rvert=3$, and it is one equation rather than a system.*

**Second order.** The second variation along the same direction gives the analogue of batch 21, and **that is the observation worth having**: the rung at general $\lvert J\rvert$ has the same two-condition shape the record already worked out at $\lvert J\rvert=3$, with the frame in place of $(\hat U,V)$. *If that correspondence is exact, the levers already fenced at $\lvert J\rvert=3$ are fenced here too, and [F69](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s six instances would predict which ones.*

**What the attempt has to produce, and where it could fail.** At a zero-diagonal $Q$ the criticality condition must be tested: either it fails, in which case the face carries no critical point at all and the theorem is immediate, or it holds on a subvariety, and then the second-order condition has to separate those points. *The honest risk is the second case, and the thing to check first is whether $Q^{\mathsf T}(A\circ Q)$ can be symmetric at a zero-diagonal $Q$ for a generic $A$.*

**Screening allowance.** Testing that last question at $k=4$ and $k=6$ on the witnesses already in hand is seconds of compute on constructed objects and is a screen, reported as such and never as evidence for a positive claim.

## The criticality condition in closed form, 2026-09-21

**`A` HAS RANK AT MOST TWO BY CONSTRUCTION**, which the boxed condition hides. Writing $A_{ik}=u_i(\beta\nu_k^2)+c_i\nu_k$ as $A=u\,a^{\mathsf T}+c\,b^{\mathsf T}$ with $a_k=\beta\nu_k^2$ and $b_k=\nu_k$, and setting $X=Q^{\mathsf T}UQ$, $Y=Q^{\mathsf T}CQ$ for $U=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(u)$, $C=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(c)$, both symmetric,

$$M_{lm}=\big(Q^{\mathsf T}(A\circ Q)\big)_{lm}=a_m X_{lm}+b_m Y_{lm},$$

so $M_{lm}-M_{ml}=(a_m-a_l)X_{lm}+(b_m-b_l)Y_{lm}$ and the boxed condition factors:

$$\boxed{\ (\nu_m-\nu_l)\big[\beta(\nu_l+\nu_m)X_{lm}+Y_{lm}\big]=0\quad\text{for every }l<m.\ }$$

**So criticality is ONE SCALAR RELATION PER PAIR OF DISTINCT $\nu$, not a matrix condition**, and pairs with $\nu_l=\nu_m$ impose nothing. *Checked as an identity rather than an approximation: the difference between $M-M^{\mathsf T}$ and the per-pair form is $3.6\times10^{-15}$ over two hundred random $(k,\nu,u,c,\beta,Q)$ with $k$ from $3$ to $7$, which is Gate 1(b), the second route.*

### Criticality is ONE LINEAR CONDITION PER PAIR, on a Hadamard product of columns

**Derived 2026-09-21, and typed.** Write $q_l$ for the $l$th COLUMN of $Q$ and $q_l\circ q_m$ for
their entrywise product. Then $X_{lm}=\langle q_l\circ q_m,u\rangle$ and
$Y_{lm}=\langle q_l\circ q_m,c\rangle$ straight from the definitions, so the per-pair factor is

$$\beta(\nu_l+\nu_m)X_{lm}+Y_{lm}=\big\langle\, q_l\circ q_m,\ \beta(\nu_l+\nu_m)\,u+c\,\big\rangle,$$

and $\langle q_l\circ q_m,\mathbf1\rangle=0$ is FREE, being the orthogonality of two distinct
columns. *Typed as `conj_diagonal_eq_hadamard_inner`, `hadamard_inner_one` and
`asym_factor_columns` in [I106bFace.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I106bFace.lean), with no
`sorry`; screened as an identity at $1.6\times10^{-15}$ over two hundred random instances before
being typed.*

> **SO CRITICALITY SAYS: for every pair with $\nu_l\ne\nu_m$, the Hadamard product $q_l\circ q_m$
> lies in the orthogonal complement of $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\mathbf1,\ \beta(\nu_l+\nu_m)u+c\}$.**

**AND EACH PAIR IS A DICHOTOMY RATHER THAN A CONSTRAINT.** With $h_{lm}=q_l\circ q_m$ and
$s_{lm}=\nu_l+\nu_m$, the condition $\beta s_{lm}\langle h_{lm},u\rangle+\langle h_{lm},c\rangle=0$
gives either
$$\langle h_{lm},u\rangle=\langle h_{lm},c\rangle=0,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
s_{lm}=-\frac{\langle h_{lm},c\rangle}{\beta\,\langle h_{lm},u\rangle}.$$
*A pair either kills both moments of its product, or PINS $\nu_l+\nu_m$ to a ratio the product
itself determines.* **The second branch is what a proof must exclude, and it is excludable by
COUNTING rather than by analysis**: the $\binom k2$ sums $\nu_l+\nu_m$ are determined by $k$
numbers and cannot be independently pinned once $k\ge4$, while the ratios are free.

**THIS EXPLAINS THE DERANGEMENT THEOREM RATHER THAN MERELY AGREEING WITH IT.** *At a signed
permutation, distinct columns have DISJOINT SUPPORT, so $q_l\circ q_m=0$ identically*, and the zero
vector is orthogonal to everything. **That is why every signed permutation is critical for every
$A$, every $\beta,\nu,u,c$, at every $k$, with no computation anywhere.**

### The zero diagonal puts TWO ZEROS in every Hadamard product, and that settles $k=4$

**Derived 2026-09-21, typed as `hadamard_vanishes_at_own_indices`.** At a zero-diagonal $Q$,
$$h_{lm}[l]=Q_{ll}Q_{lm}=0,\qquad h_{lm}[m]=Q_{ml}Q_{mm}=0,$$
*structurally, whatever else is true of $Q$.* **So $h_{lm}$ lives in the coordinate subspace
avoiding BOTH $l$ and $m$**, of dimension $k-2$, while criticality puts it orthogonal to
$\mathbf1$ and to $\beta(\nu_l+\nu_m)u+c$ there.

| $k$ | $h$ lives in | minus the two conditions | remaining |
|---|---|---|---|
| $4$ | $2$ | $2$ | $\mathbf0$ |
| $5$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ |
| $6$ | $4$ | $2$ | $2$ |

> **AT $k=4$ THE OPEN STATEMENT IS A THEOREM.** *There is no room left, so every $h_{lm}$ vanishes,
> distinct columns have disjoint support, and $Q$ is a signed permutation.*

**THE GENERICITY IT NEEDS, STATED RATHER THAN IMPLIED.** The two conditions are independent exactly
when the two COMPLEMENTARY indices $a,b\notin\{l,m\}$ carry distinct weights,
$\beta s_{lm}u_a+c_a\ne\beta s_{lm}u_b+c_b$. *Where they coincide the $2\times2$ system drops rank
and the argument gives nothing.* **That hypothesis is the whole of what $k=4$ costs**, and it is
checkable at a configuration rather than assumed: over $116$ exact zero-diagonal critical points the
smallest separation seen was $3.4\times10^{-3}$, and none of the $116$ was a non-permutation.

*Reported honestly: the $116$ are a screen and the dimension count is the proof. The screen's role
is to say the genericity is not vacuous, not to establish the theorem.*

**WHAT IT DOES NOT GIVE.** From $k=5$ the remaining dimension is $k-4>0$ and the count says nothing.
*The general case needs a further condition, and the obvious candidate is the interaction ACROSS
pairs that the pair-incidence rank constrains* — which is where [Jin](../references/jin-2026-l4-norm-orthogonal-group/notes.md)'s
maximum-entry pivot would enter, since it is exactly a device for turning a family of local
conditions into a global one.

**THE COUNT, MADE EXACT.** *The pair-sum map $\nu\mapsto(\nu_l+\nu_m)_{l<m}$ is the pair-incidence
matrix, whose rank is computed rather than guessed*:

| $k$ | pairs | rank | pairs beyond the rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| $3$ | $3$ | $3$ | $0$ |
| $4$ | $6$ | $4$ | $2$ |
| $5$ | $10$ | $5$ | $5$ |
| $6$ | $15$ | $6$ | $9$ |
| $8$ | $28$ | $8$ | $20$ |

**The rank is exactly $k$ from $k\ge4$**, so the $\binom k2$ sums span a $k$-dimensional subspace
and the pinning conditions across pairs CANNOT be independent: beyond $k$ of them, the rest are
forced by the incidence structure. *At $k=3$ the rank is $3=\binom32$ and nothing is forced, which
is why $k=3$ is the record's own separate case.*

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO, AND THE DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE OF IT.** *The ratios
$-\langle h,c\rangle/(\beta\langle h,u\rangle)$ depend on $Q$*, so the pinning branch is not a
condition on $\nu$ alone and the count does not close it by itself: $Q$ can move to meet the sums.
**What the rank says is that the second branch is CONSTRAINED, not that it is empty**, and a proof
needs the interaction between that constraint and the zero diagonal. *Recording it as a closure
would be the same error as the I77 domination argument withdrawn earlier today: an inclusion
asserted between two things that are not over the same variables.*

*Not yet a proof.* The counting bounds how many pairs can take the second branch; it does not by
itself force $h_{lm}=0$ on the first, where $h$ need only be orthogonal to
$\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\mathbf1,u,c\}$, of codimension at most three. **What it does is split the
open statement into a spectral count and a rank question**, neither of which needs the Riemannian
machinery [Jin](../references/jin-2026-l4-norm-orthogonal-group/notes.md) uses.

### The easy branch is CLOSED, and it is a THEOREM rather than the screen first recorded here

*The note named two branches: criticality fails on the face, so the face carries no critical point and the theorem is immediate; or it holds on a subvariety and the second-order condition has to separate the points.* **It is the second, and no numerics are needed to say so** ([r1035](REVIEW_r1035.md)).

**Let $Q$ be a signed permutation, $Q_{ij}=\varepsilon_i\delta_{j,\sigma(i)}$ with $\varepsilon_i=\pm1$.** Then
$$X_{lm}=\sum_i Q_{il}u_iQ_{im}=\sum_i\varepsilon_i^2\delta_{l,\sigma(i)}\delta_{m,\sigma(i)}u_i=\delta_{lm}\,u_{\sigma^{-1}(l)},$$
which is diagonal, and $Y$ likewise. **So $M_{lm}=a_mX_{lm}+b_mY_{lm}$ is diagonal, hence symmetric, and $Q$ is critical for EVERY $A$, every $\beta,\nu,u,c$, at every $k$.** And $Q_{ii}=\varepsilon_i\delta_{i,\sigma(i)}$ vanishes for all $i$ exactly when $\sigma$ is a derangement. **Derangements exist at every $k\ge2$, so the face carries critical points at every $k\ge2$, unconditionally, and genericity in $A$ is irrelevant.**
