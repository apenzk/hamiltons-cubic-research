## A. Fidelity of L178Pad.lean

The source is [diagonal-half.md:603](../findings/diagonal-half.md) to line 613, Lemma L178's statement and steps 1 to 5, read verbatim.

- **A-1. The model.** `om a` ([CP2Real.lean:98](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/CP2Real.lean)) is `J a / √2` embedded in the first four coordinates by `Emb`. The three `J a` ([CP2Real.lean:36](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/CP2Real.lean)) read as 2-forms −e₀₁ − e₂₃, −e₀₂ + e₁₃ and −e₀₃ − e₁₂, and with ⋆e₀₁ = e₂₃, ⋆e₀₂ = −e₁₃, ⋆e₀₃ = e₁₂ each is fixed by ⋆, so the three are self-dual for one orientation. `inner2 = ½ tr(XᵀY)` and tr(JₐᵀJ_b) = 4δ give an orthonormal triple, so they are a standard orthonormal basis of Λ⁺ℝ⁴ padded into Λ²ℝⁿ. `Wm n μ X = Σ μₐ ⟨X, ωₐ⟩ ωₐ` ([L178Pad.lean:29](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L178Pad.lean)) sends ω_b to μ_b ω_b (`Wm_om`, line 70) and kills everything orthogonal to the ωₐ (`Wm_perp`, line 75), so it is supported on Λ²ℝ⁴, self-dual, and diagonal in that basis. The Weyl conditions (`Wm_weyl`, line 39) use Σμ = 0 exactly where the record's step 4 says Bianchi and Ricci are spent: both the Bianchi sum and the Ricci contraction factor as (Σμ) times a quantity common to the three forms, which is ωₐ ∧ ωₐ = vol and ωₐ² = −½Π for self-dual forms. `IsWeyl` is [WeylReal.lean:126](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean)'s, and θ, `Pcubic`, `normSq`, `trCube` and `sharpPair` are the layer's operator-model definitions at general n (lines 142 to 180), so the theorems are about the layer's θ and not a coordinate proxy. Correct.
- **A-2. The invariants.** `Wm_values` (line 96) gives ‖W‖² = Σμ² = p₂, tr W³ = Σμ³ = p₃ and ⟨W, W#⟩ = 6μ₀μ₁μ₂ = 6e₃, through `Wm_frame`'s eigenframe ω ∪ g with the g-weights zero and L41Statement's frame identities. The last is step 2's ⟨W, W#⟩ = 6e₃ with γ² = 2, derived here from the layer's own # through `cst_om` and `eps_sum` rather than assumed. `Wm_P` (line 115) gives P = p₃ + 6e₃ = 3e₃ + 6e₃ = 9e₃ at e₁ = 0, which is step 4 (Newton's p₃ = 3e₃ is the `ring` after substituting μ₂ = −μ₀ − μ₁). Unnormalised, so it is step 4 at every scale rather than at p₂ = 1 only. Correct.
- **A-3. The discriminant.** At e₁ = 0 the μₐ are the roots of x³ + e₂x − e₃ with e₂ = −p₂/2, whose discriminant ∏(μᵢ − μⱼ)² is −4e₂³ − 27e₃² = p₂³/2 − 27e₃². Doubling gives p₂³ − 54e₃² = 2∏(μᵢ − μⱼ)², which is `disc_identity` (line 125), proved by `ring` after the substitution. At p₂ = 1 it is step 5's ½ − 27e₃² ≥ 0. Correct.
- **A-4. Step 5 and equality.** `Wm_bound` (line 133) is P² = 81e₃² ≤ (3/2)p₂³, equivalent to 54e₃² ≤ p₂³, the nonnegativity of the discriminant. `Wm_theta_le` (line 160) turns it into θ ≤ √(3/2) through |P| ≤ √(3/2)‖W‖³. `Wm_bound_eq_iff` (line 140) says P² = (3/2)‖W‖⁶ iff two weights coincide, which is step 5's "equality forces the discriminant to vanish, hence a repeated root" in its sign-closed form |θ| = √(3/2), the form L178's statement also records. The docstring's "at unit norm μ = ±(2,−1,−1)/√6 up to order" is right: a repeated trace-free weight is (a, a, −2a) up to order with 6a² = 1. The one-sided equality θ = +√(3/2) (the repeated pair negative) is not stated separately, and the frame rigidity is milestone 2 and beyond, as the plan says. The hypothesis `h0 : μ ≠ 0` of `Wm_theta_le` is not needed for the truth of the statement (at μ = 0, θ = 0/0 = 0 in Lean) and is harmless.
- **A-5. Witness.** n = 4, μ = (3, −1, −2): Σμ = 0, p₂ = 14, e₃ = 6, so P = 54 and P² = 2916 < (3/2)·14³ = 4116, strict since the weights are distinct, and p₂³ − 54e₃² = 2744 − 1944 = 800 = 2·(4·1·(−5))². The equality case: μ = (1, 1, −2) gives P = −18, P² = 324 = (3/2)·6³, θ = −√(3/2), the anti-aligned CP² value.
