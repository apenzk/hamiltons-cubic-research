## 0. The instrument I built first, before reading the derivation

Every row multiplier is one polynomial, and writing it that way makes the whole of D1 visible. Fix a class $i$, put $\mu_j=m_j-\delta_{ij}$ and $w^{(i)}_{jk}=2\mu_j\mu_k$ for $j<k$, $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$ for $j=k$. For a multiplier $\varphi(x)=\alpha+\beta x+\gamma x^2$ with polarisation $\psi(x,y)=\alpha+\beta\tfrac{x+y}2+\gamma xy$, the combination $\alpha(\mathrm A_i)+\beta(\mathrm B_i)+\gamma(\mathrm C_i)$ of [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s reduced system is exactly

$$\sum_{j\le k}w^{(i)}_{jk}\,\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)\,t_{ijk}\;=\;2(n-2)\alpha\;-\;2\nu_i\beta\;+\;2\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)\gamma .$$

*This reproduces both of [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s coefficient identities on the nose, weights included.* With $\varphi=(x-u)(x+w)$ in a class-$1$ row, $\psi(u,-w)=-\tfrac12(u+w)^2$ and $w^{(1)}_{12}=2(r-1)(N-r)$ give $-(r-1)(N-r)(u+w)^2t_{112}=2u\theta-2(n-1)uw$; with $\varphi=(x-u)^2$ in a class-$2$ row, $\psi(-w,-w)=(u+w)^2$ and $w^{(2)}_{22}=(N-r-1)(N-r-2)$ give $(N-r-1)(N-r-2)(u+w)^2t_{222}=2(n-2)u^2-4uw-2w^2-2w\theta$. Both are the recorded lines character for character, which is the check that the instrument is the right one.

**The point of writing it this way.** Whether a multiplier annihilates the coefficient of $t_{ijk}$ is a question about the *pair-point* $(X_{jk},Y_{jk})=\big(\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2,\nu_j\nu_k\big)$, because $\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)=\alpha+\beta X_{jk}+\gamma Y_{jk}$ is an affine function of it. So a multiplier is a *line* in the $(X,Y)$-plane, and "how many coefficients can one multiplier kill" is "how many pair-points can be collinear".

## 1. D1, the row half is unconditional, and "generically" is the wrong word for it

**The geometry.** $P_{jj}=(\nu_j,\nu_j^2)$ lies on the parabola $Y=X^2$; $P_{jk}$ is the intersection of the tangents at $\nu_j$ and at $\nu_k$, since substituting $X=\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2$ into $Y=2\nu_jX-\nu_j^2$ returns $\nu_j\nu_k$. Now count collinearity. A line $Y=pX+q$ carries $P_{jk}$ iff the polarisation of $f(x)=x^2-px-q$ vanishes at $(\nu_j,\nu_k)$. If $f$ has distinct roots the vanishing locus is the graph of a Möbius **involution** of $\mathbb P^1$, so among $d$ distinct values it carries at most two pair-points, two fixed values, or one fixed value and one transposed couple, and no third configuration exists. If $f$ has a double root $a$ the polarisation factors as $(x-a)(y-a)$, so the line is the tangent at $a$ and carries the $d$ pair-points $P_{pk}$ when $a=\nu_p$, and none otherwise. A vertical line $X=c$ carries at most two, since $2\nu_j=\nu_k+\nu_l$ can hold once but $2\nu_j=2\nu_k$ never.

> **So one quadratic multiplier annihilates at most $\max(d,2)$ of a row's $\binom{d+1}2$ coefficients, at every spectrum. Isolating one variable needs $\binom{d+1}2-1$ annihilations, and $\binom{d+1}2-1\le\max(d,2)$ holds only at $d=2$.** The $d=2$ isolation is the arithmetic accident $3-1=2$; at $d=3$ it is $6-1=5$ against a ceiling of $3$, and the gap widens with $d$.

**What this corrects.** The derivation's "$3$ free parameters cannot meet $5$ annihilations" is a dimension count over a *fixed* set of conditions, and the conditions here move with $\nu$, three parameters can meet five conditions on a non-generic configuration, which is precisely the attack the brief asks for. The answer is that no such spectrum exists, but the reason is the involution/tangent dichotomy above, not the count. **So the conclusion stands with the word "generically" deleted from the row half, which is a strengthening.**

**The cross-row half, I reached the revision's correction independently, and one clause in it overstates.** The original text offered $3d=9$ parameters against $10$ types; [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) proves the reduced system has rank $3d-2=7$, so two multiplier directions annihilate every type and the right-hand side together, and the nine homogeneous conditions are satisfied identically by that kernel. The revised file now carries this, from [r343](REVIEW_r343.md), with the criterion "every type is isolable exactly when $3d-2=\binom{d+2}3$". *Two riders.* **First**, that criterion decides when *all* types are isolable; the failure of the equality leaves a $7$-dimensional image inside a $10$-dimensional coefficient space, and such a subspace can still contain a coordinate vector at a non-generic spectrum, so the negative direction of the cross-row claim is still a genericity statement, and only the row half is unconditional. **Second**, "annihilating the six-type support of a row is six conditions on a seven-dimensional effective space, so it is always solvable" needs the rider that the surviving multiplier must have $\sum_i\gamma_i\nu_i\ne0$; a nontrivial solution with $\sum_i\gamma_i\nu_i=0$ is a relation among the $t$ carrying no $\theta$ and certifies nothing.

**A non-quadratic device does not exist inside this calculus, and the record says why.** A row carries exactly three equations, so quadratics exhaust its multipliers; anything further is a fourth relation, which is [I15](../plan.md), and the record already holds that such a relation is quadratic in the *signed* $c_{abc}$ and invisible to every polynomial in $T$, with [DERIVE-t7.md](DERIVE-t7.md) proving it cannot come from the off-diagonal block. A cross-row device does exist, by [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s dual completeness the optimal certificate *is* one, but it annihilates the six *supported* types and is nonpositive on the four vanishing ones, which is a statement about a $4$-subset, not about isolating a facet. **That is the honest form of "the method does not generalise": at $d=2$ the optimum binds one constraint, at $d=3$ it binds four, so the successor object is a vanishing set and not a facet.** The derivation reaches that conclusion in §2; §1 should be read as motivating it rather than as an obstruction theorem.

## 2. D1, continued, the record already holds the per-row answer, and already knows it is not enough

**This is the review's most load-bearing finding and it is a record hit.** The per-row condition I reconstruct above is [Theorem B of DERIVE-t11.md](DERIVE-t11.md), the triangle $\Delta(L_a,U_a)$ with vertices $(L,L^2)$, $(U,U^2)$, $\big(\tfrac{L+U}2,LU\big)$, cut out by the chord and the two tangents, and equal to [r337](REVIEW_r337.md)'s complete per-row hull whenever the reduced extremes carry multiplicity $\ge2$, which at $d=3$ with every $m_i\ge3$ holds in every row. The three edges are exactly my three admissible multiplier types: the chord is $\varphi=(x-\nu_{\max})(x-\nu_{\min})$, the tangents are $\varphi=(x-\nu_{\max})^2$ and $\varphi=(x-\nu_{\min})^2$, and their contact sets are the collinear families the dichotomy above enumerates.

Two consequences bear directly on the item:

- **The claim in [plan.md](../plan.md) that "the mechanism has to be built, not reused" is half wrong.** The per-row mechanism is built, filed and Gate-2'd, in closed form, at every $d$. Both of [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s branches survive verbatim to $d=3$ as *valid inequalities*, the $\Pi$-bound $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_{\min}\rvert$ ([Lemma L107](../findings/bounds.md), already on record at all $d$) from the chord at the $\arg\max$ row, and $\theta\le\nu_{\min}-2\nu_{\max}-(n-2)\nu_{\max}^2/\nu_{\min}$ from the tangent at $\nu_{\max}$ in the $\arg\min$ row. What they lose is not validity but *facethood*.
- **And the per-row family is already known to be insufficient at $d=3$.** [DERIVE-t11.md](DERIVE-t11.md) §5 records its $n=12$ witness $(30,20,16)$ passing the full hull at every row at $\theta=1.29>\theta_0(12)=1.236$. So no combination of per-row conditions can close I13, and the whole of the remaining content is the cross-row coupling, which is the LP over $\Pi(\lambda)$, which is where the screened $0.9243$ at that same witness comes from. **That is the sharpest available statement of why I13 needs the vertex enumeration and not a better inequality**, and the derivation would be stronger for citing it than for the parameter count it uses.

## 3. D2, the linear programme, step by step

I re-derived each step rather than checking the prose.

- **Dimension.** $V+1-(3d-2)$ with $V=\binom{d+2}3=10$ admissible types at $m_i\ge3$ and $3d-2=7$: the affine hull of $\Pi(\lambda)$ in $(t,\theta)\in\mathbb R^{11}$ has dimension $4$. ✓
- **Compactness.** $t\ge0$ against $(\mathrm A_i)$, whose coefficients are $2\mu_j\mu_k$ and $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$, all strictly positive at $m_i\ge3$, bounds every type by $2(n-2)$ over its coefficient; every type sits in some row; and $(\mathrm C_i)$ at any $\nu_i\ne0$ then bounds $\theta$ two-sidedly. Kernel-freeness is in the hypothesis, and tracelessness supplies a nonzero $\nu_i$ in any case. So $\Pi(\lambda)$ is a polytope: **it is neither unbounded nor does it contain a line, and the attack fails.** ✓
- **Attainment and the count of zeros.** A point of a polytope is extreme iff its active constraint functionals span the $4$-dimensional direction space, and the only constraints are the ten $t_\tau\ge0$; spanning needs at least four of them, so every vertex has **at least four** vanishing types. If the feasible set is lower-dimensional the active set only grows, so the conclusion is insensitive to whether the polytope is full-dimensional in its affine hull, the brief's first attack is answered, and "exactly four" would be *false*, since [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s equality case is already a vertex with more. ✓
- **The 210.** Naming four zeros adds four equations to the seven independent ones, giving $11$ equations in $11$ unknowns. Every vertex has *some* four-subset of its zero set whose functionals are independent, so the nonsingular solves among the $\binom{10}4=210$ contain every vertex, and $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is the maximum of $\theta$ over those that come back feasible. ✓

**The bracket clause is where D2 breaks.** For any $G$-invariant $T\ge0$ the equivalence $t_{ijk}=0\iff\langle[E_i,E_j],E_k\rangle=0$ is exact, and the orbit-average worry in the brief is unfounded: $t$ is a nonnegative average, so $t_{ijk}=0$ forces every $T_{abc}$ in the orbit to zero, hence $c_{abc}=0$ there, hence the bilinear statement by expanding in the eigenbasis, and the hollow diagonal costs nothing, since the terms it omits ($a=b$ within a class) vanish by antisymmetry of $c$ anyway. **What fails is that the vertex need not be anybody's $T$.** The theorem's "equivalently" therefore asserts of an abstract $t$ a property that only a realised $t$ has, and it contradicts the derivation's own final paragraph. **The revision repairs exactly this, from [r343](REVIEW_r343.md), and I record the agreement, two reviewers on cold briefs landed on the same clause.** *The correction, as I would word it:* "attained at a $t$ with at least four of the ten $t_{ijk}$ zero, and if that $t$ is realised by an orthonormal frame of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, at a configuration in which at least four bracket types vanish."

**Two further caveats the derivation should carry.**

- **The four-zeros conclusion is fragile under strengthening.** $\Pi(\lambda)$ drops the pair cap $\sum_k\mu^{(ij)}_kt_{ijk}\le2$, which [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) records as valid and available at no cost. Adding it keeps the same affine hull but adds non-coordinate inequalities, so a vertex of the strengthened polytope can have fewer than four vanishing types and the enumeration is no longer over $\binom{10}4$ sets. Whoever sharpens I13 with the cap must redo §2.
- **A $\theta$-saturating $W$ need not itself sit at a vertex.** If $W$ has $\theta=\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ then its averaged $t$ lies in the optimal *face*, which is a vertex only when the optimum is unique. So the enumeration bounds $\theta^\ast$, and reads back as a grading statement about $W$ only through uniqueness. At $d=2$ that uniqueness is what [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) supplies; at $d=3$ nothing supplies it yet.

## 4. D3, the $d=2$ specialisation, and the one sentence that is wrong (and still is)

Dimension $4+1-4=1$; a vertex of a $1$-dimensional polytope has at least one active constraint; the two endpoints are the two vertices. **Both vanishings at the balanced product are correct**, and both readings agree: analytically, at $r=n(n-2)/4$ the two branches $(n-1)w$ and $[(n-2)u^2-2uw-w^2]/w$ are *equal*, so the two constraints $t_{112}\ge0$ and $t_{222}\ge0$ bind at one and the same value of $\theta$; structurally, $E_{\max}=\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{so}(n/2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n/2)$ is a subalgebra of dimension $n(n-2)/4=r$, which is $t_{112}=0$, and $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\perp\mathfrak m$ for the symmetric pair, which is $t_{222}=0$. The signs check: $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ is Einstein, so $W=R-\tfrac{\mathrm{scal}}{n(n-1)}\mathrm{Id}$ on $\Lambda^2$ has eigenvalue $1-c>0$ on $\Lambda^2$ of each factor and $-c<0$ on the mixed block of dimension $n^2/4=N-r$.

> **The error, and the revision leaves it standing.** "*a degenerate vertex where the segment's two endpoints meet*" is false. The two constraints that bind together are both *upper* constraints and they bind at the *same, upper* endpoint; the lower endpoint is a different point, cut by $t_{111}\ge0$ or $t_{122}\ge0$. The record measures the segment and it is not short: [bounds-relaxations.md](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) gives the $S^3\times S^3$ segment as $\theta\in[\sqrt{10}/18,\sqrt{10}/3]$, a factor of six, and says in terms that the exactness "is a property of which facet binds, not of the segment being short". **Correction:** *a degenerate vertex, in that two of the four nonnegativity constraints are simultaneously active at a $1$-dimensional polytope's upper endpoint.*

A second, milder overstatement: "at least one of four types vanishing at an endpoint, which is exactly Lemma L105's two upper facets". The vertex theorem alone gives four candidate endpoints and does not say which are upper; that $t_{112}$ and $t_{222}$ are the upper pair and $t_{111},t_{122}$ the lower is [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s own content, not a consequence of the vertex count. The consistency claim is true; it just borrows more from L105 than "exactly" suggests.

## 5. D4, true, empty, and now correctly labelled

At $m_i\ge3$ every $(\mathrm A_i)$ coefficient is strictly positive and the right-hand side is $2(n-2)>0$, so no row can be emptied. ✓ **But a row has six types and a four-subset kills at most four of them, so the cut removes none of the 210.** I reached that before reading the revision, which now states it in those terms; the heading it replaces, "Three types can never vanish, by (A) and positivity", was false, (A) forces *one* nonzero type, not three, since $t_{123}$ lies in all three rows and loads every one of them by itself.

**I checked the replacement (B) cuts and they hold.** With the labelling $\nu_1>\nu_2>\nu_3$ and row $1$ of (B) reading $\sum_{j\le k}w^{(1)}_{jk}X_{jk}t_{1jk}=-2\nu_1<0$, the pair-means available to row $1$ are $\nu_1$, $\tfrac{\nu_1+\nu_2}2$, $\tfrac{\nu_1+\nu_3}2$, $\nu_2$, $\tfrac{\nu_2+\nu_3}2$, $\nu_3$, and a negative total needs a negative one among the supported types. Under $\nu_2>0$ and $\nu_2+\nu_3>0$ the first five reduce to a single candidate: $\nu_2>-\nu_3$ with $\nu_1>\nu_2$ gives $\nu_1+\nu_3>0$, so every mean except $\nu_3$ itself is positive and $t_{133}>0$ is forced. *That step looks like a missing hypothesis and is not, the ordering supplies it, and I nearly filed it as a discrepancy before checking.* The mirror cut is the same computation in row $3$, where $-2\nu_3>0$ needs a positive mean and $\nu_2<0$ with $\nu_1+\nu_2<0$ leaves only $\nu_1$, forcing $t_{113}>0$.

**Where the residual cut lives.** For a named four-subset the $11\times11$ solve returns a unique $(t,\theta)$ and the remaining test is whether the six surviving $t$ come back nonnegative. That is spectrum-dependent, and running all $210$ at a named $\lambda$ is a computation this brief does not authorise. **What it would decide:** feasible-with-$\theta>\theta_0(n)$ at some kernel-free $n=12$ spectrum refutes the pinned class beyond $d=2$ and is a fence post; infeasible-or-below across them, with $d\ge4$ handled, is the theorem I13 is for. Logged, not started.

## 6. D5, correct in substance, over-tight in its analogy

The scope paragraph is right that a vertex is a candidate grading and that $\Pi(\lambda)$ holds tensors no frame realises. Two corrections.

- **Jacobi is not the whole of what decides.** [LIT-spectra.md](LIT-spectra.md) states the dropped condition as "$T$ is the squared structure-constant tensor of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ in *some* orthonormal frame, a codimension-heavy condition on a totally symmetric nonnegative $3$-tensor with $\binom I3$ entries, and the record currently imposes exactly one of its consequences, the Killing row mass (A)". Jacobi is one shadow of that; the pair cap is a second, and [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) records it as valid and dropped. Saying "Jacobi is what decides" names one obstruction where the record names a class of them.
- **"The same gap" as the nilsoliton analogue is the overstatement [LIT-spectra.md](LIT-spectra.md) exists to prevent.** The citation of Payne §6.4 is accurate, a one-parameter family of nonnegative solutions, Jacobi selecting one point. But that file's own reading is that the analogy breaks exactly here: "in the nilsoliton problem the bracket varies and the metric is fixed; here the bracket is fixed and the frame varies", and Nikolayevsky's equivalence works there because a diagonal group moves the magnitudes inside a support while preserving Jacobi, whereas "this record has no such group ... there is no action moving $T$ at fixed spectrum". So the nilsoliton case is a *precedent that the relaxation exceeds the realisable set*, and not the same gap. **Correction:** "that gap is the analogue of the one [LIT-spectra.md](LIT-spectra.md) records", with the disanalogy named.

**On what the record settles about realisability.** At $d=2$ it settles both sets: $t_{112}=0$ is realised across the whole subalgebra family, the $n=8$ torus at $0.540062$, $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{su}(3))$ at $0.836660$, per [post F40](../findings/fence-posts.md), and $t_{112}=t_{222}=0$ is realised exactly by the symmetric pairs, cut to the balanced product by Lemma L23's cap. At $d=3$ nothing was settled when this derivation was written; [DERIVE-sp3b.md](DERIVE-sp3b.md) has since named two four-subsets that *are* realised, $\{112,113,222,333\}$ for a $\mathbb Z_3$-grading and $\{113,123,223,333\}$ for a symmetric pair with split even part, with Gate 2 owed on it. **D5 should be read as still open rather than as unanswerable.**

## 7. The owed step, as far as analysis reaches

**(i) The ten coefficient functions.** They are one function, not ten: the coefficient of $t_{ijk}$ in the multiplier combination $\alpha(\mathrm A_i)+\beta(\mathrm B_i)+\gamma(\mathrm C_i)$ of row $i$ is $F^{(i)}_{jk}=w^{(i)}_{jk}\,\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)$, with $w^{(i)}_{jk}$ and $\psi$ as in §0. Written out at $d=3$, row $1$ carries $w^{(1)}=\big((m_1-1)(m_1-2),\,2(m_1-1)m_2,\,2(m_1-1)m_3,\,m_2(m_2-1),\,2m_2m_3,\,m_3(m_3-1)\big)$ against the types $(t_{111},t_{112},t_{113},t_{122},t_{123},t_{133})$ and the pair-points $\big((\nu_1,\nu_1^2),(X_{12},\nu_1\nu_2),(X_{13},\nu_1\nu_3),(\nu_2,\nu_2^2),(X_{23},\nu_2\nu_3),(\nu_3,\nu_3^2)\big)$; rows $2$ and $3$ are the two cyclic images, with $\mu_j=m_j-\delta_{ij}$ throughout. The three right-hand sides are $2(n-2)$, $-2\nu_i$, $2\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)$. **The row measure is a probability measure with $\mathbb E_i[X]=-\nu_i/(n-2)$ and $\mathbb E_i[Y]=\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)/(n-2)$, which is the form the enumeration should be run in.**

**(ii) A closed form for 45 of the 210, by hand.** A four-subset contained in one row leaves that row exactly two types, and there are $\binom64=15$ such subsets per row and no overlap between rows, since two rows share only three types. For those $45$ the row alone determines everything: writing $p,q$ for the two weighted survivors and $X_1<X_2$, $Y_1,Y_2$ for their pair-points,

$$p=\frac{2\big[\nu_i+(n-2)X_2\big]}{X_2-X_1},\qquad q=\frac{-2\big[\nu_i+(n-2)X_1\big]}{X_2-X_1},\qquad \theta=\nu_i+\frac{n-2}{\nu_i}\cdot\frac{Y_1(X_2-\mu)+Y_2(\mu-X_1)}{X_2-X_1},\quad \mu=\frac{-\nu_i}{n-2},$$

**and nonnegativity of the two survivors is exactly $X_1\le-\nu_i/(n-2)\le X_2$**, the row's two-point measure must straddle its own pinned mean. The four types outside the row are then fixed by the four remaining independent equations, and their signs are the residual test. *So a third of the enumeration is closed-form and needs no solver; the remaining $165$ spread their zeros over two or three rows and do not decouple.*

**(iii) A structural cut worth more than the arithmetic one.** Saturating the $\Pi$-bound at $d=3$ is not a one-type condition as it is at $d=2$: the chord at the $\arg\max$ row and the tangent at the $\arg\min$ row both deliver $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_{\min}\rvert$, so equality forces both contact conditions at once, which with $1=\max$, $2=\text{mid}$, $3=\min$ is the five-element set $\{t_{112},t_{113},t_{122},t_{123},t_{223}\}$. Realised, that says $[E_1,E_1]\perp E_2,E_3$ and $[E_2,E_2]\perp E_1,E_3$ and $[E_1,E_2]\perp E_1,E_2,E_3$: **$E_{\max}$ and $E_{\mathrm{mid}}$ are commuting subalgebras and their sum is a subalgebra**, the $d=3$ successor to "$t_{112}=0$ says $E_{\max}$ is a subalgebra". *Cross-check, and it lands:* [DERIVE-sp3b.md](DERIVE-sp3b.md)'s symmetric-pair vertex $\{113,123,223,333\}$ contains the tangent contact set $\{113,123,223\}$, so that vertex sits exactly at $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_{\min}\rvert$. **This is the currency [I15](../plan.md) asked for and it is reachable without the enumeration.**
