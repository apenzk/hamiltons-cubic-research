# R48, the torus families of the other simple algebras: working note

*[R48](../routes.md): U$_{\mathrm s}'$ at the torus-invariant idempotents of every compact simple $\mathfrak h$, by the machinery of [L274](../findings/second-order.md) to [L276](../findings/second-order.md). Chosen on the [2026-09-27b sweep](2026-09-27b-LIT-step8-sweep.md).*

## Batch 1 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): $\mathfrak{su}(k)$

*The slice.* With $\langle X,Y\rangle=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}XY$ on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$, root vectors $(E_{ij}-E_{ji})/\sqrt2$, $i(E_{ij}+E_{ji})/\sqrt2$ and an orthonormal Cartan basis, $c=k$ and $N=k^2-1$. A torus-invariant $X$ is a symmetric $A$ on the Cartan subalgebra plus a scalar $\mu_{ij}$ on each root plane. With $M=(\mu_{ij})$ (symmetric, zero diagonal), $W=M\circ M$, $d_i=\sum_jW_{ij}$ and $L_W=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(d)-W$ the weighted Laplacian,
$$F(X)=3\langle A,L_W\rangle+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3,\qquad\lVert X\rVert^2=\lVert A\rVert^2+\sum_{i\ne j}\mu_{ij}^2,$$
checked at $X=I$ ($F=k(k-1)(k+1)=cN$). This is [L271](../findings/second-order.md)'s form on the face $P=Q$ of $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$, the normalisations agreeing through $\mathfrak u(k)\subset\mathfrak{so}(2k)$, and the idempotent system is the same, $A=L_W$ and $\mu_{ij}(1-d_i-d_j-2\mu_{ij}^2)=(M^2)_{ij}$. Only the target differs: U$_{\mathrm s}'$ on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$ asks $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge(k^2-1)/k^2$, the value at the uniform solution $\mu\equiv\tfrac1k$, which is heavier than $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$'s $I/c$.

*H fails on this slice, so R48 targets the norm form.* The ratio $F/\lVert X\rVert^3$ on the slice reaches $2/\sqrt3$ at every $k=3$ to $10$ (screen), the value of a single root $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, above $\kappa=k/\sqrt{k^2-1}$. That point is only semidefinite on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$ and belongs to the subalgebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, where [L260](../findings/second-order.md) places it, so it does not bear on U$_{\mathrm s}'$ for $\mathfrak{su}(k)$. What $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ allowed, proving U$_{\mathrm s}'$ through H on the slice, is unavailable here.

*The norm form screens true with a thin margin.* Solving the system from 400 random positive starts at $k=3$ to $7$: the uniform solution is the only positive one at $k=3,4$. From $k=5$ there are non-uniform positive-definite solutions (so U fails on this family too, as [F80](../findings/fence-posts.md) leads one to expect), all heavier than the target: $0.960069$ against $0.96$ at $k=5$, $0.972654$ against $0.972222$ at $k=6$, $0.979601$ against $0.979592$ at $k=7$.

*A J1-type inequality, and why it is the right one.* On the slice put $S=\sum_id_i=\lVert M\rVert^2$ and $g=\lVert L_W\rVert$, so that $S^2-g^2=\sum_{i\ne j}(d_id_j-W_{ij}^2)$. Then
$$\textbf{(J1}_A\textbf{)}\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3\,\sqrt S\le\sqrt{\tfrac{k-1}k}\;\sum_{i\ne j}\big(d_id_j-W_{ij}^2\big)$$
screens exact at $k=3$ to $8$ (200 BFGS starts each, maximum $0$), with equality at the uniform point and at a single edge. At an idempotent $F(C)=\lVert C\rVert^2$ gives $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=b^2-2g^2$ with $b^2=S$, and J1$_A$ then gives $g^2(2b-\lambda)\ge b^3-\lambda b^4$, $\lambda=\sqrt{(k-1)/k}$, so for $b>\lambda/2$
$$\lVert C\rVert^2=g^2+b^2\ge b^2+\frac{b^3-\lambda b^4}{2b-\lambda},$$
which equals $(k^2-1)/k^2$ at $b=\lambda$, the uniform solution. **It does not stay above the target**: on $(\tfrac\lambda2,\sqrt{(k^2-1)}/k)$ it falls about $0.27$ below at every $k=3$ to $20$ (near $b\approx0.6$), and the traceless cubic bound excludes only $b<b_{\min}\approx0.55$. So the two scalar relations, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=b^2-2g^2$ and J1$_A$, do not carry U$_{\mathrm s}'$ here. What they leave out is positive definiteness, which is exactly what separates $\mathfrak{su}(k)$'s idempotents from the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ points that break H, so a proof has to use it.

*J1$_A$ is sharper than L275.* L275's constant at size $k$, $\sqrt{2k}(k-2)/(k-1)^{3/2}$, exceeds $\sqrt{(k-1)/k}$, so L275 does not give J1$_A$. Its harmonic-mean step, $f(p,q)=\tfrac{(1-p)(1-q)}{1-pq}\le\tfrac12(2-p-q)$, loses exactly at the uniform point, where $f=\tfrac{1-p}{1+p}$. A proof of J1$_A$ has to keep $f$, and the walk inequality it needs is
$$\sum_i\pi_i\sum_jq_{ij}\,\frac{(1-q_{ij})(1-q_{ji})}{1-q_{ij}q_{ji}}\le\frac{k-1}k\Big(1-\sum_i\pi_i^2-\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}^2\Big),$$
tight at the uniform walk.

## Batch 2 (research level): what H actually asks of a proper subalgebra

*[L270](../findings/second-order.md) (3) uses U$_{\mathrm s}'$ only through $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge d_{\mathfrak h}/c_{\mathfrak h}^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$, the second step being [L256](../findings/second-order.md).* So on a simple $\mathfrak h\subsetneq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ H needs only $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$, and L256's ratio table puts $d_{\mathfrak h}/c_{\mathfrak h}^2$ far above that for every type but $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ with $k$ near $n$: $c/\sqrt d\le4/\sqrt3$ for $A_r$ and $C_r$, $8/\sqrt{14}$ for $\mathfrak g_2$ and below $1$ for the other exceptional types, against $2(n-2)/\sqrt N$, which is above $2.3$ from $n=12$. **The sharp U$_{\mathrm s}'$ is therefore needed only on $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ for $k$ close to $n$, and a crude norm bound may settle every other simple subalgebra.** What such a bound would be is open. A PSD idempotent has $\lVert C\rVert^2=F(C)\le c\,\lambda_{\max}^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}C$ in the eigenbasis, which is an upper bound and gives nothing. A lower bound has to use $C\#C=C$ as an equation, not only $F(C)=\lVert C\rVert^2$.

*Screen (R49 evidence).* Solving $C\#C=C$ from 60 random positive-definite starts on $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and $\mathfrak{so}(5)=\mathfrak{sp}(2)$ finds only $I/c$. It is marginally stable on $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ (top eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ on $C^\perp$) and unstable on $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ ($\tfrac23$), as [L265](../findings/second-order.md) has it.

## Batch 3 (research level): two more scalar routes on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$, both closed

- *The traceless cubic bound.* With $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3\le c_kb^3$, $c_k=(k-2)/\sqrt{k(k-1)}$, the identity $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=b^2-2g^2$ gives $q=b^2+g^2\ge\tfrac12(3b^2-c_kb^3)$, which is exact at the uniform solution and increasing in $b$ there. It reaches the target only if $\sum_id_i=b^2\ge1-\tfrac1k$ at every positive solution, and the non-uniform solutions violate that ($\sum d=0.7966<0.8$ at $k=5$, $0.8240<0.8333$ at $k=6$). Those solutions are heavier because their concentration $\sum d^2+\sum W^2$ grows faster than $\sum d$ falls.
- *The Perron root.* The system is $N^2-N=D^2-2M^{\circ3}$ with $N=M+D$, and at the uniform solution $\rho(N)$ equals the target. At the non-uniform solutions $\rho(N)$ and $\rho(M)$ both fall below it while $q$ rises, so no chain through $\rho(N)$ gives the norm bound.

Both are closures of the same kind as batch 1's: a scalar identity tight at the uniform point, which the non-uniform solutions evade in the direction the bound needs.

## Batch 4 (screen): H on the torus slice of $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$

Problem P covers odd $n\ge13$, where the relevant family is $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$'s torus slice: a Cartan block $A$, a scalar on each long root plane ($U_{ij}$, $V_{ij}$ for $\varepsilon_i\mp\varepsilon_j$) and one on each short root plane ($w_i$ for $\varepsilon_i$). On that slice $\sup F/\lVert X\rVert^3$ (8 BFGS starts) is $2/\sqrt3>\kappa$ at $n=7$, the value of a root $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as on $\mathfrak{so}(8)$, and **equals $\kappa$ at $n=9$ and $11$, attained at $I$**. So the odd family behaves like $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$'s: H on the slice is the right target, and a J1 with the short roots added is the natural statement. Deriving $F$ on this slice in the coordinates $(A,U,V,w)$ is the next step.

## Batch 5: $F$ on $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$'s torus slice

In [L271](../findings/second-order.md)'s coordinates plus a weight $w_i$ on each short root plane ($P=U+V$, $Q=U-V$, $d_i=\sum_l(U_{il}^2+V_{il}^2)$),
$$F=3\sum_iA_{ii}(d_i+w_i^2)-3\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}P_{ij}Q_{ij}+\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)+3\,w^{\mathsf T}Pw,\qquad\lVert X\rVert^2=\lVert A\rVert^2+\lVert U\rVert^2+\lVert V\rVert^2+2\lVert w\rVert^2,$$
fitted exactly (misfit $10^{-14}$ at $m=4$ on random points, the coefficients $3$ and $3$ being the only free ones). So the odd slice is J1's with the Cartan weight shifted to $d_i+w_i^2$ and one new cubic, $3w^{\mathsf T}Pw$, from the triangles $\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j=\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j$ through two short roots. What changes in [L273](../findings/second-order.md)'s reduction is that $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_\mu=b^2-\lVert w\rVert^2$ is no longer $b^2$. The next step is the odd J1 with $\lambda$ fixed by the uniform point, and its screen.

## Batch 6: the odd J1, screened exact, and its reduction to H

*The statement.* On $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$'s torus slice with $b^2=\lVert U\rVert^2+\lVert V\rVert^2+2\lVert w\rVert^2=1$ and $g^2=\sum_i(d_i+w_i^2)^2+\sum_{i\ne j}P_{ij}^2Q_{ij}^2$,
$$\textbf{(J1}_{\rm odd}\textbf{)}\qquad\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)+3\,w^{\mathsf T}Pw+\lambda g^2\le c_0,\qquad\lambda=\frac{3\sqrt2\,m}{2(2m-1)},\quad c_0=\frac{\sqrt2\,(2m-1)(4m-1)}{8m^2}.$$
The constants are fixed by the uniform point ($U=V=w=\tfrac1{m\sqrt2}$, $g_u^2=(2m-1)^2/(4m^3)$) and by tangency there to $\Psi_\kappa(g)=\min_a(\kappa(1+a^2)^{3/2}-3ag)$, $\kappa=(2m-1)/\sqrt{m(2m+1)}$, at $a_u=1/\sqrt{2m}$, which is [L273](../findings/second-order.md) (2)'s mechanism.

*Screens.* J1$_{\rm odd}$ at $m=5$ to $8$ ($n=11$ to $17$, 40 BFGS starts, half near the uniform point): maximum of the left side minus $c_0$ at most $6\times10^{-12}$, tight at the uniform point. The reduction step $c_0-\lambda g^2\le\Psi_\kappa(g)$ on $g\in[0.3/\sqrt m,1]$: maximum $-3\times10^{-9}$ to $-9\times10^{-8}$, touching at $g_u$ only. So, as on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$, H on the odd slice follows from one quartic inequality in the root weights, and J1$_{\rm odd}$ is the next derivation: [L277](../findings/second-order.md)'s chain with the short-root term $3w^{\mathsf T}Pw$ in the rows and the shifted Cartan weight in the pairing.

*The embedding.* Put $U'_{ij}=U_{ij}$, $V'_{ij}=V_{ij}$ for $i,j\le m$ and $U'_{i,m+1}=V'_{i,m+1}=w_i/\sqrt2$. Then $\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P'^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q'^3)=\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)+3w^{\mathsf T}Pw$, $b'^2=b^2$, $d'_i=d_i+w_i^2$ for $i\le m$, $d'_{m+1}=\lVert w\rVert^2$, $Q'=0$ on the new edges, and $g'^2=g^2+\lVert w\rVert^4$ (all checked by hand). So the odd slice is the even slice at $m+1$ with one vertex whose edges lie on the face $Q=0$ ($\gamma=1$). J1 at $m+1$ gives $T_{\rm odd}\le\lambda_{m+1}(1-g^2-\lVert w\rVert^4)$, weaker than J1$_{\rm odd}$ because the odd uniform point maps to a non-uniform even configuration. A proof of J1$_{\rm odd}$ is L277's chain with one distinguished vertex and a $\pi$-only inequality adapted to it.

*The first two steps of L277's chain miss.* Through the embedding, the cocktail-party rows and the pairing with $R'_{ij}=d'_id'_j-P'^2_{ij}Q'^2_{ij}$ give $T_{\rm odd}\le\sqrt{E'\sum R'}$ with $\sum R'=1-g^2-\lVert w\rVert^4$. Screened against J1$_{\rm odd}$'s $c_0-\lambda g^2$ this overshoots by at most $7\times10^{-5}$, $2.7\times10^{-5}$ and $1.2\times10^{-5}$ at $m=5,6,7$ (Nelder–Mead, 30 starts). The mismatch is in the form: the pairing produces a product $\sqrt{E}\sqrt{\sum R}$, while J1$_{\rm odd}$'s right side is $c_0-\lambda g^2$ with $c_0\ne\lambda$. So the odd chain needs pairing weights adapted to the distinguished vertex, for instance $R$ carrying a constant part that makes $\sum R$ proportional to $c_0-\lambda g^2$.

*Where the chain loses.* At the odd uniform point the cocktail-party rows are exact ($T=c_0-\lambda g^2$). The pairing is not. With $\tilde R=R'+\varepsilon x'+\tfrac12d'_{m+1}x'\,[\text{new edges}]$, $\varepsilon=c_0/\lambda-1$ (the unique $x$-proportional correction with $\sum\tilde R=(c_0-\lambda g^2)/\lambda$ identically), the Cauchy–Schwarz ratio $\sqrt{s^2ee'}/\tilde R$ at the uniform point is $1.184$ on the old pairs against $1.159$ on the new edges ($m=5$), so the pairing loses $4\times10^{-5}$ there and the screen overshoots by $9.8\times10^{-5}$. **A pairing tight at the odd uniform point needs a correction term that is not proportional to $x$ and sums to zero**, so that the two ratios can be equalised. That is the obstruction to transferring L277's chain.

*A zero-sum correction fails, and splitting the pairing works.* The correction $\nu\,x'_{ij}([\text{new}]-2\lVert w\rVert^2)$ equalises the two ratios only at $\nu\approx4.2$, where some weights go negative. **Splitting the Cauchy–Schwarz by edge group** instead (the old pairs among $1,\dots,m$, and the edges at the distinguished vertex $m+1$, each paired against its own $R'$) keeps each group tight at the odd uniform point by symmetry. Then
$$\textbf{(S2)}\qquad\sqrt{F_oR_o}+\sqrt{F_nR_n}\le c_0-\lambda g^2,\qquad F_\bullet=\sum_{\bullet}\frac{s^2ee'}{R'},\ R_\bullet=\sum_\bullet R',$$
screens exact at $m=5,6,7$ (30 Nelder–Mead starts; maximum $\le0$, tight at the uniform point). **So J1$_{\rm odd}$ ⟸ the cocktail-party rows (exact at the uniform point) + the split pairing + S2**, and S2 is a walk inequality with one distinguished vertex, the odd analogue of MW'.

*S2 linearises.* With $\sqrt{ab}\le\tfrac12(ta+b/t)$ at the two constants $t_o=\sqrt{R_o/F_o}$, $t_n=\sqrt{R_n/F_n}$ taken at the uniform point ($t_o=0.81822$, $t_n=0.79550$ at $m=5$),
$$\tfrac12\big(t_oF_o+R_o/t_o\big)+\tfrac12\big(t_nF_n+R_n/t_n\big)\le c_0-\lambda g^2$$
screens exact at $m=5,6,7$ (maximum $\le0$, tight at the uniform point). This is the odd analogue of J1*b, linear in the walk sums and the pair weights with one coefficient per edge class. The route to a proof is L276's: the per-pair interpolation on the old edges (the new edges have $\gamma=1$, where it is AM-GM), decoupling into an odd MW', a row chord, and a $\pi$-only inequality with one distinguished vertex.

*The odd MW', screened exact.* Bounding $F_o$ by the per-pair interpolation (L275_interp) and decoupling $\gamma$ per ordered old pair, as in L276, but **keeping the new edges exact** ($\gamma=1$ there, so their ratio is exactly $2w(1-q_{i,m+1})(1-q_{m+1,i})$, and the AM-GM symmetrisation would lose at the uniform point because $\pi_i\ne\pi_{m+1}$), the inequality
$$\sum_{\text{old}}\max_{\gamma}\Big[\tfrac{t_o}2w(1-q)(1+\gamma(1-2q))+\big(\lambda-\tfrac1{2t_o}\big)w^2(1-\gamma^2)\Big]+t_n\!\!\sum_{\text{new}}\!w(1-q)(1-q^{\mathsf T})+\tfrac1{2t_o}\!\sum_{\text{old}}\!\pi_i\pi_j+\tfrac1{2t_n}\!\sum_{\text{new}}\!\pi_i\pi_j+\lambda\sum_{i\le m}\pi_i^2\le c_0$$
on the embedded walk ($\pi_i=d'_i$, $q=w'/\pi$, $\sum\pi=1$) screens exact at $m=5,6,7$ (maximum $\le10^{-15}$, at the uniform point). With the symmetrised new edges it fails by $6\times10^{-4}$ at the uniform point itself. **So J1$_{\rm odd}$ ⟸ rows ⟸ split pairing ⟸ linearisation ⟸ this odd MW'**, and the odd MW' is L276's problem with one distinguished vertex. Its proof (a row chord for the old rows with one new entry, the row of vertex $m+1$, and a $\pi$-only inequality) is the next derivation.

## Batch 7: the odd MW' with its constants

The linearisation constants are $t_o=\dfrac{\sqrt2(2m-1)^2}{4m(2m-3)}$ and $t_n=\dfrac{\sqrt2(2m-1)}{4(m-1)}$ (from the uniform point, sympy). On the embedded walk, with the distinguished vertex written $0$, $\pi_0=\lVert w\rVert^2$ and old vertices $1,\dots,m$, the odd MW' is
$$\sum_{i\ge1}\pi_i\!\!\sum_{j\ge1,\,j\ne i}\!\!\tfrac{t_o}2F_{\kappa_o}(q_{ij},\pi_i)\;+\;2t_n\sum_{i\ge1}w_{i0}(1-q_{i0})(1-q_{0i})\;+\;\tfrac1{2t_o}\!\!\sum_{i\ne j\ge1}\!\!\pi_i\pi_j+\tfrac1{t_n}\pi_0\sum_{i\ge1}\pi_i+\lambda\sum_{i\ge1}\pi_i^2\;\le\;c_0,$$
with $F_\kappa(q,\pi)=\max_{\gamma\in[0,1]}[q(1-q)(1+\gamma(1-2q))+\kappa\pi q^2(1-\gamma^2)]$ (L276's) at $\kappa_o=(2\lambda-1/t_o)/t_o$. The old rows' entries sum to $1-q_{i0}$, so each old row is an MW' row on $m-1$ slots scaled by its new entry, and vertex $0$'s row enters only through the pair products $(1-q_{i0})(1-q_{0i})$.

## Batch 8 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): the odd MW' reduced to a $(\pi,r)$ inequality

*Notation.* Old vertices $1,\dots,m$, the distinguished vertex $0$, $u_i=w_{i0}$, $r_i=q_{i0}=u_i/\pi_i$, $a_i=\pi_i-u_i$ (vertex $i$'s mass on old edges), $\pi_0=\sum_iu_i$, $n=m-1$ old slots per old row, $\sigma=1-r$. At the odd uniform point $\pi_i=\tfrac{2m-1}{2m^2}$, $r_i=\tfrac1{2m-1}$, $q_{ij}=\tfrac2{2m-1}$ on old slots and $q_{0i}=\tfrac1m$.

*A weighted AM-GM split of the new edges is exact, and the row-0 chord it enables fails.* With $\alpha^2=\tfrac{2m-1}{2m}$, $(1-r)(1-q_{0i})\le\tfrac12\big(\alpha^2(1-r)^2+(1-q_{0i})^2/\alpha^2\big)$ is tight at the uniform point, and the odd MW' with the new edges split this way screens exact at $m=5$ to $8$ (24 Nelder–Mead starts, maximum $\le0$). Row $0$ then carries $\tfrac{t_n}{\alpha^2}\pi_0\sum_iq_{0i}(1-q_{0i})^2$, whose Lemma A chord has slope $\beta_0=\tfrac{t_n}{\alpha^2}\tfrac{m-1}m=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$. But a column Cauchy–Schwarz over old rows and row $0$ together is tight at the uniform point only when the weights are in the ratio $\beta^o:\beta_0=\tfrac{2m-1}{2m}$ (the entries being $\tfrac2{2m-1}$ and $\tfrac1m$), which pins the old-row slope at $\beta^o=t_n\tfrac{m-1}m$, and the old-row chord with that slope fails at every $m$: by $0.106$ at $m=5$ down to $0.043$ at $m=10$, at an old point mass. Closed.

*Keeping row 0 explicit works.* Take each old row at fixed $r$: its old entries sum to $\sigma$, and L276's row chord in scaled form is
$$\sum_{j\ \mathrm{old}}F_{\kappa_o}(q_{ij},\pi)\le G_u+\beta\,(\rho_u-\rho),\qquad G_u=2\sigma\big(1-\tfrac\sigma n\big)^2,\ \rho_u=\tfrac{\sigma^2}n,\ \beta=\frac{G_u-F_{\kappa_o}(\sigma,\pi)}{\sigma^2(1-1/n)},$$
fixed by the uniform row and a point mass. It screens valid at $m=7,8$ over $\pi\in[0,\tfrac12]$, $r\in[0,0.98]$ (maximum excess $6\times10^{-17}$), and fails at $m=5,6$ ($6.6\times10^{-2}$, $1.25\times10^{-2}$), as L276's own chord fails at $n=5$. The column Cauchy–Schwarz over old rows, $\sum_{k\ne i}\beta_k\pi_kq_{ki}^2\ge a_i^2/T_i$ with $T_i=\sum_{k\ne i}\pi_k/\beta_k$, is tight at the uniform point by symmetry. What remains is
$$\tfrac{t_o}2\Big[\sum_i\pi_i\,\frac{nG_u(r_i)-F_{\kappa_o}(\sigma_i,\pi_i)}{n-1}-\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{T_i}\Big]+2t_n\sum_iu_i(1-r_i)\Big(1-\frac{u_i}{\pi_0}\Big)+\frac{S^2-s_o}{2t_o}+\frac{\pi_0S}{t_n}+\lambda s_o\le c_0,$$
with $S=\sum_i\pi_i=1-\pi_0$ and $s_o=\sum_i\pi_i^2$, in the $2m$ variables $(\pi_i,r_i)$. It screens exact at $m=7,8,10$ (40 Nelder–Mead starts each, maximum at the uniform point).

*Titu's form: the plain one fails, the weighted one keeps it exact.* $\sum_ia_i^2/T_i\ge(\sum a_i)^2/\sum T_i$ fails by $0.163$ at $m=7$, at an old edge ($\pi_1=\pi_2=\tfrac12$), where L276's column bound is what carries the slack. $\sum_ia_i^2/T_i\ge(\sum_ia_i^2)^2/\sum_ia_i^2T_i$, L276's $H_c$ form, keeps the reduced inequality exact at $m=7,8,10$, and $\sum_ia_i^2T_i=\Lambda\sum_ia_i^2-\sum_ia_i^2\pi_i/\beta_i$ with $\Lambda=\sum_k\pi_k/\beta_k$, so every term is now a function of separable sums.

*What a proof of the odd MW' at $m\ge7$ now needs.* (a) The scaled row chord, a one-parameter extension of L276's R0 and R½ (for $r\le\tfrac12$ the point-mass value $F(\sigma,\pi)=\sigma(1-\sigma)+\kappa_o\pi\sigma^2$ is affine in $\pi$, so the endpoint reduction applies unchanged; for $r>\tfrac12$ it is convex in $\pi$ and the chord of it in $\pi$ gives a slope affine in $\pi$ that agrees at the endpoints). (b) The weighted-Titu inequality in separable sums, which is the new part.

## Batch 9 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): the odd MW' at $m\ge7$ reduced to one variable

Three simplifications of batch 8's chain, each screened exact, leave a chain whose every step but one is a classical inequality.

*(1) L276's own slope serves every scaled row.* The scaled row chord holds with the $r$-independent slope $\beta(\pi)=b_0(1-c'\pi)$, $b_0=\tfrac{2(n-1)}n$, $c'=\tfrac{n^2\kappa_o}{2(n-1)^2}$ (L276's at $\kappa_o$): $\sum_jF_{\kappa_o}(q_j,\pi)\le G_u(\sigma)+\beta(\pi)\big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}n-\sum_jq_j^2\big)$ for $\sum_jq_j=\sigma\in(0,1]$, $\pi\in[0,\tfrac12]$ (screen at $m=7,8,10$, $\pi$ on 11 points, $r$ on 20, maximum excess $7\times10^{-16}$). Equivalently, with $\psi=F_{\kappa_o}(\cdot,\pi)+\beta(\pi)q^2$, the uniform point maximises $\sum_j\psi(q_j)$ on every scaled simplex $\sum_jq_j=\sigma\le1$; at $\sigma=1$ this is L276's chord, since $F_\kappa(q,\pi)$ depends on $\kappa\pi$ alone and $\kappa_o\le\kappa(n)$. A slope below the maximal one is always valid, because $\rho\ge\sigma^2/n$.

*(2) The new edges become separable.* The weighted AM-GM split of batch 8 is kept, and row $0$'s share $\tfrac{t_n}{\alpha^2}\pi_0\sum_iq_{0i}(1-q_{0i})^2$ is bounded by Lemma A's chord on $m$ slots, taken at the reduced slope $c_0=\tfrac{t_o}2\tilde\beta_0$, $\tilde\beta_0=\tfrac{2m}{2m-1}\beta(\pi_u)$, which is below the maximal $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$ ($0.611$, $0.630$, $0.652$ at $m=7,8,10$).

*(3) Row 0 joins the column Cauchy–Schwarz.* With weights $\beta(\pi_k)$ on old rows and $\tilde\beta_0$ on row $0$, the ratio of the entries at the uniform point ($\tfrac2{2m-1}$ against $\tfrac1m$) is matched exactly, so the bound is tight there, and column $i$'s mass is the full $\pi_i$:

**Statement (the odd L271).** *On $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$ with $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$, Cartan $\mathfrak t=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(h_k)$, $h_k=e_{2k-1}\wedge e_{2k}$, long root planes $R_{\varepsilon_i\pm\varepsilon_j}$ inside the block $B_{ij}=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_a\wedge e_b:a\in\{2i-1,2i\},\,b\in\{2j-1,2j\}\}$ and short root planes $R_{\varepsilon_i}=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{2i-1}\wedge e_{2m+1},\,e_{2i}\wedge e_{2m+1}\}$, every torus-invariant symmetric $C$ is $A\oplus\bigoplus_\alpha\mu_\alpha I_{R_\alpha}$. With $U_{ij}=\mu_{\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j}$, $V_{ij}=\mu_{\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j}$, $w_i=\mu_{\varepsilon_i}$, $P=U+V$, $Q=U-V$ and $d_i=\sum_l(U_{il}^2+V_{il}^2)$,*
$$\lVert C\rVert^2=\lVert A\rVert^2+\lVert U\rVert^2+\lVert V\rVert^2+2\lVert w\rVert^2,\qquad F(C)=3\sum_iA_{ii}(d_i+w_i^2)-3\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}P_{ij}Q_{ij}+\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)+3\,w^{\mathsf T}Pw .$$
*This is batch 5's fitted formula.*

**Proof.** Distinct torus weights forbid mixing ($B_m$'s positive roots are pairwise distinct and nonzero), and a symmetric operator on a plane commuting with its rotation is scalar, which gives the form of $C$. Write $C=E+S$ with $E=A\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\ \rm long}\mu_\alpha I_{R_\alpha}$, supported on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_a\wedge e_b:a,b\le2m\}$ and zero on its complement $\mathfrak p=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_a\wedge e_{2m+1}\}$, and $S=\bigoplus_iw_iI_{R_{\varepsilon_i}}$, supported on $\mathfrak p$ and zero on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$. The two supports are orthogonal, so $\lVert C\rVert^2=\lVert E\rVert^2+\lVert S\rVert^2$, with $\lVert E\rVert^2=\lVert A\rVert^2+\lVert U\rVert^2+\lVert V\rVert^2$ by [L271](../findings/second-order.md) (1) and $\lVert S\rVert^2=2\lVert w\rVert^2$.

$F(X,Y,Z)=\langle X\#Y,Z\rangle$ is symmetric and trilinear, and in any orthonormal basis $(e_\beta)$ of $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$, $F(X,Y,Z)=\tfrac12\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\langle Y[Xe_\beta,Ze_\gamma],[e_\beta,e_\gamma]\rangle$. Hence $F(X,Y,Z)=0$ whenever $Y$ kills every bracket $[Xu,Zv]$. Expand $F(E+S)=F(E)+3F(E,E,S)+3F(S,E,S)+F(S,S,S)$, using the symmetry to put $E$ in the middle of the mixed terms.

1. $F(S,S,S)=0$: $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(2m)$, which $S$ kills.
2. $F(E,E,S)=F(E,S,E)=0$: $[\mathfrak{so}(2m),\mathfrak{so}(2m)]\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(2m)$, which $S$ kills.
3. $F(E)$ is $L271$ (1)'s value: in the coordinate basis only $e_\beta,e_\gamma\in\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ contribute (E kills $\mathfrak p$), and the brackets and inner products of elements of $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ are those of $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ itself. So $F(E)=3\sum_iA_{ii}d_i-3\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}P_{ij}Q_{ij}+\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)$.
4. $F(S,E,S)=\tfrac12\sum_{\beta,\gamma}w_\beta w_\gamma\langle E[e_\beta,e_\gamma],[e_\beta,e_\gamma]\rangle$ over the coordinate vectors $e_\beta=e_a\wedge e_{2m+1}$ of $\mathfrak p$, $w_\beta=w_i$ for $a\in\{2i-1,2i\}$, with $[e_a\wedge e_{2m+1},e_b\wedge e_{2m+1}]=\pm e_a\wedge e_b$. For $a,b$ the two indices of one $i$ the bracket is $\pm h_i$ and $\langle Eh_i,h_i\rangle=A_{ii}$, two ordered pairs per $i$. For $a\in\{2i-1,2i\}$, $b\in\{2j-1,2j\}$, $i\ne j$, the bracket is $\pm$ a coordinate vector of $B_{ij}$, on which $E$'s diagonal entry is $\tfrac12(U_{ij}+V_{ij})=\tfrac12P_{ij}$ ([L271](../findings/second-order.md) (3)'s proof), eight ordered pairs per unordered $\{i,j\}$. So $F(S,E,S)=\sum_iw_i^2A_{ii}+\sum_{i\ne j}w_iw_jP_{ij}=\sum_iw_i^2A_{ii}+w^{\mathsf T}Pw$.

Summing, $F(C)=F(E)+3\sum_iw_i^2A_{ii}+3w^{\mathsf T}Pw$, which is the statement. $\square$

*Check.* At $C=I$ ($A=I$, $U=V=J-I$, $w=\mathbf 1$, so $P=2(J-I)$, $Q=0$, $d_i+w_i^2=2m-1$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(J-I)^3=m(m-1)(m-2)$) the formula gives $3m(2m-1)+4m(m-1)(m-2)+6m(m-1)=m(4m^2-1)$, and $I\#I=(n-2)I$ gives $F(I)=(2m-1)\dim\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)=m(2m-1)(2m+1)$, the same.

**H on the odd torus slice.** With the odd L271, batch 11's reduction is a proof: $F=3\langle A,A_\mu\rangle+\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)+3w^{\mathsf T}Pw$ with $\lVert A_\mu\rVert=g$, and J1$_{\rm odd}$ ([L279](../findings/second-order.md)) plus the tangency of $h$ at $t_u$ give $F(C)\le\frac{2m-1}{\sqrt{m(2m+1)}}\lVert C\rVert^3$ for every torus-invariant $C\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^2\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$, $m\ge7$, which is statement H on the torus slice of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ at odd $n\ge15$.

## Batch 13 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): what H at even $n\ge12$ still asks of $\mathfrak{so}(k)$'s torus family

By batch 2, a standardly embedded $\mathfrak{so}(k)\subsetneq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ ($c=k-2$, $d=\binom k2$) needs only $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$ at its stable positive-definite idempotents, and on the torus family that follows from $F(C)\le\kappa_n\lVert C\rVert^3$ with the ambient constant $\kappa_n=\tfrac{n-2}{\sqrt N}$, since $F(C)=\lVert C\rVert^2$ at an idempotent. $\kappa_k$ increases in $k$, so $\kappa_n>\kappa_k$ and the requirement has slack $\kappa_n/\kappa_k-1$. [L277](../findings/second-order.md) and [L281](../findings/second-order.md) give the sharp $\kappa_k$ for $k\ge14$, so every $k\ge14$ is settled for every $n>k$. For $k\le13$ the worst ambient case is the smallest admissible $n$: $n=12$ for $k\le11$, $n=14$ for $k=12,13$. The slack there:

| $k$ | 13 | 12 | 11 | 10 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| worst $n$ | 14 | 14 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 |
| slack | 1.0% | 2.2% | 1.4% | 3.2% | 5.5% | 8.6% | 12.8% | 19.2% | 29.8% |

$k=12$ at $n=12$ is the sharp case itself, where MW' at $m=6$ rests on one certificate ([the J1 working note](2026-09-27-I117-J1.md), batch 13), [L276](../findings/second-order.md) covering $m\ge7$. **The decoupled bound fails at every $k$**: bounding $T$ by its maximum and $g$ by its maximum separately needs $\min_a\big(\kappa'(1+a^2)^{3/2}-3ag_{\max}\big)\ge T_{\max}$, and with $g_{\max}\approx1$ the left side is near $0.08$ at $\kappa'\approx1.2$ against $T_{\max}$ near $\kappa_k$. So J1's coupling of $T$ to $g$ is what carries the bound at small $k$ as at large, and the small cases need J1 and J1$_{\rm odd}$ with relaxed constants: $T+\lambda'g^2\le c_0'$ with $c_0'+\tfrac{9a^2}{4\lambda'}\le\kappa_n(1+a^2)^{3/2}$ for all $a$, which leaves one free parameter to spend where L276's chord fails ($n=m-1<6$ slots).

## Batch 14 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): every $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ torus slice with $k\le2M$ obeys $\mathfrak{so}(2M)$'s bound

**Statement.** *Let $M\ge7$ and $\kappa_{2M}=\tfrac{2(M-1)}{\sqrt{M(2M-1)}}$. (1) For $m\le M$, every torus-invariant $C\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^2\mathfrak{so}(2m)$ has $F(C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert C\rVert^3$. (2) For $m+1\le M$, every torus-invariant $C\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^2\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$ has $F(C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert C\rVert^3$.*

**Proof.** Write [L271](../findings/second-order.md)'s form and norm on $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$'s slice as $F_E(A,U,V)=3\sum_iA_{ii}d_i-3\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}P_{ij}Q_{ij}+\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)$ and $N_E=\lVert A\rVert^2+\tfrac12\lVert P\rVert^2+\tfrac12\lVert Q\rVert^2$. Border $A$ by a zero row and column, and $U$, $V$ by one vector $z$ on a new index $m+1$ with a zero corner (batch 11's embedding). Then $P'=\mathop{\mathrm{bord}}(P,2z)$ and $Q'=\mathop{\mathrm{bord}}(Q,0)$, $d'_i=d_i+2z_i^2$ for $i\le m$, $A'$ vanishes on the new index, and $Q'$ on the new pairs. So $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathop{\mathrm{bord}}X)^3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^3+3\sum_{b,c}y_bX_{bc}y_c$ gives
$$F_E(A',U',V')=3\sum_iA_{ii}(d_i+2z_i^2)-3\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}P_{ij}Q_{ij}+\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)+6z^{\mathsf T}Pz,\qquad N_E(A',U',V')=N_E(A,U,V)+4\lVert z\rVert^2 .$$
(1) At $z=0$ both are unchanged, so $\mathfrak{so}(2m)$'s slice sits in $\mathfrak{so}(2m+2)$'s with the same $F$ and $\lVert C\rVert$. Iterating up to size $M$ and applying [L277](../findings/second-order.md) there gives $F(C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert C\rVert^3$. (2) At $z=w/\sqrt2$, $2z_i^2=w_i^2$ and $6z^{\mathsf T}Pz=3w^{\mathsf T}Pw$, so $F_E$ and $N_E$ at the bordered triple are [L280](../findings/second-order.md)'s $F$ and $\lVert C\rVert^2$ on $\mathfrak{so}(2m+1)$, and (1) at size $m+1\le M$ finishes. $\square$

**What it gives.** $\kappa$ increases in the dimension, so take $M=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ for $n\ge14$. Every standardly embedded $\mathfrak{so}(k)\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with $k<n$ has $k\le2M$, and $k\le2M-1$ when $k$ is odd, so (1) or (2) applies and its torus family has $F(C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert C\rVert^3\le\kappa_n\lVert C\rVert^3$. At an idempotent $F(C)=\lVert C\rVert^2$, so $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge1/\kappa_n^2=N/(n-2)^2$, which is all H at $n$ asks of a proper subalgebra (batch 2). With L277 ($k=n$ even) and L281 ($k=n$ odd), **the torus families of the standardly embedded $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ ask nothing more of H at any $n\ge14$.** At $n=12$ the same embedding reduces every $k\le11$ to $\mathfrak{so}(12)$'s own slice, where MW' at $m=6$ holds with one step resting on a machine certificate ([the J1 working note](2026-09-27-I117-J1.md), batch 13) and no numbered result covers it, so the $n=12$ consequence is conditional on that certificate. $n=13$ reduces the same way to $\mathfrak{so}(12)$'s slice for $k\le12$, and $k=13$ lies below L281's $m\ge7$. This supersedes batch 13's slack table for $n\ge14$.

## Batch 15 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): $\mathfrak{su}(k)$'s torus family sits in $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$'s slice

**Statement.** *Let $\mathfrak{su}(k)\subset\mathfrak u(k)\subset\mathfrak{so}(2k)$ be the standard embedding ($\mathbb C^k=\mathbb R^{2k}$, $\mathfrak u(k)$ the skew matrices commuting with the complex structure $J$), with the inner product restricted from $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$. For $M\ge7$ and $k\le M$, every symmetric operator $C$ on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$ invariant under the maximal torus $T'$ of $SU(k)$ has $F_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}(C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert C\rVert^3$, where $F_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}$ is $\mathfrak{su}(k)$'s own cubic form.*

**Proof.** Extend $C$ by zero to $\hat C$ on $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$. (a) *The cubic form restricts.* In an orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$ adapted to $\mathfrak{su}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(k)^\perp$, $F(\hat C)=\tfrac12\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\langle\hat C[\hat Ce_\beta,\hat Ce_\gamma],[e_\beta,e_\gamma]\rangle$ has non-zero terms only for $e_\beta,e_\gamma\in\mathfrak{su}(k)$, where the brackets and inner products are $\mathfrak{su}(k)$'s, so $F(\hat C)=F_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}(C)$, and $\lVert\hat C\rVert=\lVert C\rVert$. (b) *$\hat C$ is invariant under the whole maximal torus $T$ of $U(k)$, which is a maximal torus of $SO(2k)$.* $T=T'\cdot Z$ with $Z$ the centre of $U(k)$: a diagonal unitary splits as its mean phase times a determinant-one diagonal. $\mathrm{Ad}(Z)$ is the identity on $\mathfrak u(k)\supset\mathfrak{su}(k)$ and preserves $\mathfrak{su}(k)^\perp$, on which $\hat C$ vanishes, so $\hat C$ commutes with $\mathrm{Ad}(Z)$, and with $\mathrm{Ad}(T')$ by hypothesis. (c) So $\hat C$ is a point of $\mathfrak{so}(2k)$'s torus slice, and [L282](../findings/second-order.md) (1) gives $F(\hat C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert\hat C\rVert^3$. $\square$

*In [L271](../findings/second-order.md)'s coordinates* the roots $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ are the $J$-commuting planes and $\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j$ the anticommuting ones, so $\hat C$ is `torusOp A U 0` with $A\mathbf 1=0$ (its Cartan block orthogonal to $J=\sum_ph_p$). Conversely every $T'$-invariant symmetric $C$ on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$ is of this form: it preserves the zero-weight space $\mathfrak t'$ and is a scalar on each root plane, the roots $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ staying pairwise distinct on $T'$.

*Screen.* A numpy build of $\mathfrak{su}(3)\subset\mathfrak{so}(6)$ and $\mathfrak{su}(4)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$ with an exactly $T'$-invariant random $C$: $\hat C$ is invariant under the full $U(k)$ torus to $10^{-15}$, and $F(\hat C)=F_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}(C)$ to $10^{-14}$.

**What it gives.** For $n\ge14$ and $\mathfrak{su}(k)$ standardly embedded in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ ($2k\le n$), take $M=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\ge k$: every torus-invariant $C$ on $\mathfrak{su}(k)$ has $F(C)\le\kappa_{2M}\lVert C\rVert^3\le\kappa_n\lVert C\rVert^3$, $\kappa$ increasing in the dimension, so $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$ at its torus-invariant idempotents, which is what H at $n$ asks of $\mathfrak{su}(k)$'s torus family (batch 2). This does not contradict batch 1: H *with $\mathfrak{su}(k)$'s own constant* fails on its slice, and H at $n$ asks only for the ambient one. The argument needs $\mathfrak h$'s maximal torus together with its centraliser's to fill a maximal torus of an $\mathfrak{so}(n')\supseteq\mathfrak h$, $n'\le n$: rank of $\mathfrak h$ plus rank of the centraliser equal to $\lfloor n'/2\rfloor$. For $\mathfrak{sp}(k)\subset\mathfrak{so}(4k)$ ($k\ge2$) it is $k+1<2k$, and for $\mathfrak g_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)$ it is $2<3$, so those stay open, as do the embeddings failing the count. Some non-standard embeddings pass it, $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb C^4=\mathbb R^6$ for one, and those are standard $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ under an isomorphism, which L282 already covers.
