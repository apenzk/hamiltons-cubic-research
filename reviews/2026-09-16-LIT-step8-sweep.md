## Batch 1, the rung mechanism stripped of names: maximisers of trace polynomials over tuples, and when they commute

**Three searches.** (i) "maximum of trace polynomial over tuples of Hermitian matrices attained at commuting matrices"; (ii) the $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(ABAB)\le\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^2B^2)$ family with equality iff commuting; (iii) Schur–Horn for several matrices.

**What the field answers, and why most of it is screened out.** *The trace-polynomial optimisation literature* (Klep, Volčič, Burgdorf, Povh; "Optimization over trace polynomials", Ann. Henri Poincaré 2022; the tracial moment problem) answers the general question with **a hierarchy of semidefinite relaxations** converging to the optimum, and extracts optimisers by a GNS construction under a flatness condition. **That is a certificate route with no analytical endgame**, exactly the shape the skill refuses, and it carries no theorem that the optimum of a trace polynomial sits at a commuting tuple, because in general it does not. *Screened out on the rule rather than on content.* (ii) is the Böttcher–Wenzel and Chan–Kwong family, already in the record from the 14c sweep, and it gives equality cases of specific inequalities rather than a mechanism for climbing. Not new.

**What is live: multivariable Schur–Horn theorems** (Massey and Ravichandran; and Argerami–Massey, "Non-commutative Schur–Horn theorems and extended majorization", arXiv 0712.2246). *The classical theorem is the record's own rung at $\lvert J\rvert=1$ and $2$: the diagonal of an orbit is the permutohedron, a linear functional on it peaks at a vertex, which is Birkhoff.* **The multivariable version characterises the joint diagonals of a commuting tuple, and the abstract's own words are that the naive statement, the convex hull of the joint permutations, "does not hold in full generality".** That is precisely where the record's $\lvert J\rvert=3$ attempt died: three doubly stochastic matrices that conflict, the relaxed maximum exceeding the true one by $3.82$. **If the paper gives the exact joint-diagonal set, its extreme points are the rung**, and the mechanism would then be one argument for every $\lvert J\rvert$ rather than a bespoke one per rung. *To be screened against the literature index and stored before it is read.*

**Screened against the literature index: ABSENT.** No row of [INDEX-literature.md](../findings/INDEX-literature.md) carries Schur–Horn in any form; the record's one use is the classical theorem on the bispectral side ([bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md), "what it actually consumes"), and the nearest filed source is Chayes for Fan's theorem. **So multivariable Schur–Horn is new to the record.** arXiv [1411.4457](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4457), Massey and Ravichandran, Proc. LMS 112 (2016) 206–234, stored under `references/massey-ravichandran-2014-multivariable-schur-horn/`. *One caveat read from the abstract before the paper: the complete description is for tuples whose joint spectrum is finite, which every matrix tuple has, so the finite-dimensional case is fully covered and the infinite-dimensional obstructions it names do not bite here.*

**And stripping the names further exposes a second field, batch 2.** *The record's $\lvert J\rvert=3$ attempt relaxed three frame-dependent matrices $(F^{\mathsf T}Q)^{\circ2}$, $(F^{\mathsf T}P)^{\circ2}$, $(Q^{\mathsf T}P)^{\circ2}$ to independent DOUBLY STOCHASTIC matrices and overshot by $3.82$.* **Two classical facts bear on that directly.** The image of $O(k)$ under $Q\mapsto Q^{\circ2}$ is the set of ORTHOSTOCHASTIC matrices, a proper subset of Birkhoff's polytope for $k\ge3$ (Au-Yeung and Poon, 1979), so the relaxation was to the wrong set. And a value that is a linear functional of such data is a point of a **joint $c$-numerical range**, whose convexity (Westwick's theorem for one operator; Au-Yeung and Poon for the joint range of three Hermitian matrices, which fails for more) is exactly the statement that its maximum over frames is attained at a permutation, which is a commuting configuration. **That is a rung-shaped mechanism with a classical name, and the number three in "three Hermitian matrices" is worth attention, since the record's value at $\lvert J\rvert=3$ pairs against exactly three operators.**

## Batch 2, the classical name for the node: joint $c$-numerical ranges over frames, and orthostochastic matrices

**The derivation that produces the name, from the record's own reduction.** At $\lvert J\rvert=3$ the value is $\Phi=\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat U^3+\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\langle\hat U,A\rangle+\langle V,C\rangle$ with $A,C$ diagonal in $F$. Fix $\hat U$ and write $V=\sum_k\nu_k\,p_kp_k^{\mathsf T}$ over an orthonormal frame $(p_k)$. Then $V^2=\sum_k\nu_k^2p_kp_k^{\mathsf T}$ in the SAME frame, so the frame-dependent part is
$$\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\langle V,C\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^3\Big(\beta\nu_k^2\;p_k^{\mathsf T}\hat Up_k+\nu_k\;p_k^{\mathsf T}Cp_k\Big),$$
**a linear functional, with weights $w_k=(\beta\nu_k^2,\nu_k)$, on the set of triples $\big(p_k^{\mathsf T}\hat Up_k,\;p_k^{\mathsf T}Cp_k\big)_{k=1,2,3}$ over frames.** That set is the joint $c$-numerical range of the pair $(\hat U,C)$ taken over full frames, and the same rewriting applies to the $\hat U$ frame against $(A,V^2)$. *Screened symbolically below the fold when the source is read.*

**The two classical facts, by name.** (i) Convexity: Westwick (1975) for the $c$-numerical range of one operator with real $c$; Au-Yeung and Poon (1979) for the joint range of THREE Hermitian matrices at $n\ge3$, which fails for more, and Poon's later bound $2k(n-k)+1$ on how many operators keep the $k$-th joint range convex. *Convexity puts the maximum of a linear functional at an extreme point.* (ii) The frame data $(F^{\mathsf T}Q)^{\circ2}$ ranges over ORTHOSTOCHASTIC matrices, a proper subset of Birkhoff's polytope at $k\ge3$, with the $3\times3$ case characterised exactly by $(1-x-y-z-w+xw+yz)^2=4xyzw$ (Au-Yeung and Poon 1979) and the unistochastic problem completely solved at $N=3$ (Bengtsson, Ericsson, Kuś, Tadej, Życzkowski, CMP 2005). **The record's three-alignment relaxation used Birkhoff's polytope and overshot by $3.82$; the exact set is smaller and has a name.**

**How the two batches fit, and where the record's own witness sits.** Convexity of the range gives an extreme point; whether that extreme point is a PERMUTATION frame, hence a commuting configuration, is exactly the multivariable Schur–Horn question of batch 1, which "does not hold in full generality". *[Post F68](../findings/fence-posts.md) is a case where it does not*: at fixed separate norms the maximiser is off the diagonal, so the extreme point is not a permutation frame there. **At fixed total norm the record's screens find the diagonal maximiser every time, so the norm trade between $\hat U$ and $V$ is what restores it, and that trade lives outside the classical range theory, which fixes the spectra.** *So the rung, if this is it, is a joint numerical range convexity statement PLUS a norm-trade argument, and neither half is on the record under this name.*

**Screened against the record.** *Numerical range, Au-Yeung, Westwick, orthostochastic, unistochastic: absent from the literature index, the findings, the sweeps and the register.* **Von Neumann's inequality and Fan's majorisation are on the record, and they are the single-operator, single-frame corners of this field.** Sources stored below.

## Batch 3, the sources read at the theorems: one screened OUT, one is the field, and the mechanism restated correctly

**The derivation of batch 2 is screened**: over $2000$ random $(\hat U,C,P,\nu,\beta)$ the frame-linear-functional identity holds to $5.7\cdot10^{-14}$.

**Massey and Ravichandran, screened OUT, and batch 1 is corrected.** *Every theorem of the paper is set in a type II$_1$ factor (Theorems 1, 2, 7, 10, 23, 24, 26, 27) or in $B(H)$ (Theorem 11), and matrices are type I.* **The sharper reason is that it answers the wrong question**: it characterises the joint diagonals of a COMMUTING tuple, and the record's functional does not ask that. *Writing the frame sum as traces, the $V$-frame dependence is $\beta\langle\hat U,V^2\rangle+\langle C,V\rangle$, a linear functional on the joint orbit of the commuting pair $(V^2,V)$ paired against the targets $(\hat U,C)$, and those targets do NOT commute*, $\hat U$ being aligned with $A+\beta V^2$ and $C$ with the field frame. **That is a joint numerical range question, not a Schur–Horn one**, and batch 1's "if the paper gives the exact joint-diagonal set, its extreme points are the rung" was the wrong reading of the right field.

**Poon, "Convexity of the joint numerical range", read at the theorems** ([stored](../references/poon-joint-numerical-range-convexity/)). *Theorem 1.2*: for an $m$-tuple of Hermitian matrices, $W_k(A)$ is convex if and only if for every $X$ in the convex hull of the orbit of a rank-$k$ projection there is a projection $P$ in the orbit with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_jP=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_jX$ for all $j$. **That is exactly the question "is the relaxation to the convex hull exact"**, which the record's three-alignment relaxation asked and answered no by $3.82$. *Theorem 4.2*: for any three Hermitian $A_1,A_2,A_3$ with $\{I,A_1,A_2,A_3\}$ independent there is an $A_4$ making $W_1$ non-convex, so **three operators is where convexity is generic and four is where it breaks**. *Theorem 5.1* is the real symmetric case, which is the record's.

**Au-Yeung and Poon 1979 refused by the publisher, the fallback stored.** *ScienceDirect answered the request with an HTML challenge page carrying a `.pdf` name, the trap the skill names; the placeholder is deleted and the refusal recorded.* The open copy of its content is Bengtsson, Ericsson, Kuś, Tadej and Życzkowski's survey ([arXiv 0708.4051](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.4051), stored), which carries the $3\times3$ orthostochastic characterisation.

**The mechanism, stated correctly now.** At $\lvert J\rvert=3$ and fixed spectra, the value's dependence on the $V$-frame is a linear functional on $\{(P\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu^2)P^{\mathsf T},P\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu)P^{\mathsf T}):P\in O(3)\}$ paired against a non-commuting pair $(\hat U,C)$. *The frame data is one orthostochastic matrix, and the functional is a generalised numerical range of the PAIR $(\hat U,C)$ with two different weight vectors $\beta\nu^2$ and $\nu$.* **Whether such a range is convex, and whether its extreme points are permutation frames, is the literature of "pairs of matrices with convex generalised numerical ranges"**, which is where the next source is taken from. *And the record's own witness [F68](../findings/fence-posts.md) says the extreme point is NOT always a permutation frame at fixed spectra, so the rung must add the norm trade, which this theory does not contain.*

**Batch 3, continued: the third source out, and the survey delivers a geometric fact the record lacks.** *Cheung, "New pairs of matrices with convex generalized numerical ranges" ([arXiv 1612.05058](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05058), stored), treats the single range $W_A(B)=\{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AU^*BU)\}$ for $2\times2$ blocks padded by zeros.* **Screened OUT**: the record's functional is the SUM of two such ranges evaluated at the same frame, $\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat U\,PD_2P^{\mathsf T})+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C\,PD_1P^{\mathsf T})$ with $D_2=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu^2)$ and $D_1=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu)$, and the paper's dimension is wrong besides.

**Bengtsson, Ericsson, Kuś, Tadej and Życzkowski, read at the theorems.** *Theorem 2.4: at $n=3$ the singular points of the map $\varphi_r:O(3)\to$ orthostochastic, $Q\mapsto Q^{\circ2}$, are exactly the orthogonal matrices that SPLIT.* **A split orthogonal matrix preserves a coordinate vector, and that is precisely the stratum [L229](../findings/second-order.md) closed, where the two blocks share an eigenvector of the field frame.** *So on the record's OPEN stratum, $\varphi_r$ is a local diffeomorphism onto its image, the orthostochastic matrix is a smooth coordinate for the frame, and the value is a linear functional on a $3$-manifold inside the $4$-dimensional Birkhoff polytope, with the manifold's defining equation given by Au-Yeung and Poon.* **The record's three-alignment relaxation replaced that manifold by its convex hull and overshot by $3.82$; the exact problem is the linear functional on the manifold itself, whose maximum sits either on the boundary, which is the split stratum and is done, or at an interior tangency of a hyperplane to the manifold.** Theorem 2.5 adds that for the complex and quaternionic versions every real orthogonal matrix is critical, which is why the real case is the one with structure.

**What this gives the brief.** *A rung mechanism of the following shape*: at every $\lvert J\rvert$, the frame dependence of the value is a linear functional on the image of $O(\lvert J\rvert)$ under $Q\mapsto Q^{\circ2}$; the boundary of that image is the split locus, which is the previous rung; and the interior is where a hyperplane is tangent to the orthostochastic variety. **Whether that tangency can occur at a maximum is the whole question, and at $\lvert J\rvert=3$ it is a question about one explicit equation.** *This is not yet a proof of anything. It is the node restated in a classical vocabulary in which its boundary is the previous rung, which no earlier formulation had.*
