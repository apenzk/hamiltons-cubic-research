## A. `L24_sharp_vec`

**The nonzero step.** If `extend a` vanished on every skew $X$, then `extend_apply` gives $a=0$ on every element of `skewSub`, so `normSq2 a = 0`, contradicting `ha`. So `hne` holds and `weyl_moves` at $n\ge2$ supplies $p,q$ and a skew $Y$ with $D_{\varphi_{pq}}(\mathrm{extend}\,a)Y\ne0$.

**The witness.** `Dop_weylOp` makes $D:=D_{\varphi_{pq}}(\mathrm{extend}\,a)$ an `IsWeylOp`, so `restrict hw.2` is a `CurvOp2` that is `IsWeyl2` (`isWeyl2_restrict`), and `extend_restrict_on_skew` gives `hvw`: `extend (restrict hw.2)` agrees with $D$ on skew inputs. Every later use of `hvw` is inside `innerOp` or `foxTri`, both of which evaluate their arguments at the $\varphi_{ij}$ only (`innerOp_congr_skew`, `foxTri_congr_on_skew`), so the substitution is sound.

**$v\perp a$.** `innerOp2 a v` $=$ `innerOp (extend a) (extend v)` by `innerOp_extend` $=$ `innerOp (extend a) D` by `hvw`. `innerOp_Dop_skew` at $F=G=u$ gives $\langle Du,u\rangle=-\langle u,Du\rangle$, and `innerOp_comm'` makes these equal, so both are $0$.

**$v\ne0$.** If `restrict hw.2 = 0`, then $DY=\mathrm{extend}(\mathrm{restrict})Y=\mathrm{extend}\,0\,Y=0$, contradicting `hYne`.

**Criticality on the wider class.** The class $C$ is linear and bivector-valued maps. For $r\in C$, `extend (restrictLin hl hs)` agrees with $r$ on skew inputs, and `extend a` is skew-valued everywhere (`extend_skew`), so `foxTri_congr_on_skew` replaces $r$. Then `foxTri_extend` gives `foxTri2 a a (restrictLin …)` $=\langle a\circ a,\cdot\rangle=\langle Q(a),\cdot\rangle$ by `foxProd2_self`, $=\lambda\langle a,\cdot\rangle$ by `hcrit`, and `innerOp_extend` with `innerOp_congr_skew` carries it back to $\lambda\,$`innerOp (extend a) r`. Every step is an identity. The derivation is legitimate because `hcrit` is an equation between operators on all of $\Lambda^2$, so it holds against every test map, Weyl or not. It is also the record's hypothesis: [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) line 151 defines "critical point" as $Q(W)=\theta W$, and for Weyl $W$ this equals the Weyl-sphere Lagrange condition $\pi_{\mathcal W}Q(W)=\theta W$ because $Q(W)=W\circ W\in\mathcal W_n$ (L209's closure, `isWeyl2_foxProd2`). `L24_nonvacuous` produces a maximiser satisfying exactly this `Qop2 a = Pcubic2 a • a`.

**Closure.** `orbit_half` needs `hC` (every member linear and bivector-valued, which is $C$'s definition) and `hCD` (`Dop_lin`, `Dop_skew` at skew $\varphi_{pq}$). Both are supplied. The test map is `extend Z`, a member of $C$ by `isLinOp_extend` and `extend_skew`.

**The read-back.** The goal $\langle Z,a\circ v\rangle=\langle Z,\tfrac\lambda2v\rangle$ for all $Z$ becomes `foxTri2 v a Z` $=\tfrac\lambda2\,$`innerOp2 v Z` after `innerOp2_comm`, `innerOp2_smul_right` and `foxTri2_comm`. `foxTri_extend` and `innerOp_extend` lift both sides to bare operators, `hvw` replaces `extend v` by $D$ in the first slot of each, and `horb` at `extend Z` closes it. `eq_of_innerOp2` then gives the operator identity, by positive definiteness of `innerOp2`. Each bridge is applied to what it claims.

## C. `L24_lambda_max_eq` and line 69

**The record's claim.** Line 69: the orbit directions are exact $\tfrac12$-eigenvectors in $W^\perp$ at every critical point with $\theta\ne0$, and with L24's bound at a local maximiser this makes $\tfrac12$ the value of $\lambda_{\max}(L_e|_{W^\perp})$ there. `L24_lambda_max_eq` states exactly that, under L24's hypotheses (unit Weyl $a$, criticality, `IsLocalMaxOn` on the unit Weyl sphere, $P(a)>0$ per the Scope paragraph at line 73), plus $n\ge2$ and nontrivial $W^\perp$.

**`BddAbove`.** For nonzero $y$: $\langle fy,y\rangle\le\lVert fy\rVert\lVert y\rVert\le\lVert f\rVert\lVert y\rVert^2$ (`real_inner_le_norm`, `le_opNorm` of `toContinuousLinearMap f`), so the quotient is at most $\lVert f\rVert$. Correct. This matters because `⨆` over `ℝ` is `sSup`, which Mathlib sets to $0$ on unbounded sets, and `le_ciSup` requires the bound.

**`le_ciSup`.** At the eigenvector $x$ from `L24_sharp`, $\langle fx,x\rangle=\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2$ and the quotient is $\tfrac12$. So $\tfrac12\le\sup$, and `L24_lambda_max` gives $\sup\le\tfrac12$. `le_antisymm` closes. The index type is nonempty (it contains $x$), so the supremum is the genuine one.

**Screen 2.** `Nontrivial (weylPerpR a)` follows from `L24_sharp_vec ha hWa hcrit hn`, which exhibits a nonzero element. The instance is therefore removable from this theorem. It is inherited from `L24_lambda_max`, which has no `hn` to derive it from.

## D. `rayleighGE_half`

**The definition.** `RayleighGE u θ c` asks for a Weyl $X$ with `innerOp X X ≠ 0`, `innerOp X u = 0` and $c\langle X,X\rangle\le T(\mathrm{idemOf}\,u\,\theta,X,X)$, where `idemOf u θ` $=\theta^{-1}u$. At unit $u$ and the record's $\theta$ this is $\langle L_eX,X\rangle\ge c\lVert X\rVert^2$ at one admissible $X$, the quadratic-form reading of $\lambda_{\max}(L_e|_{W^\perp})\ge c$.

**Criticality.** `hcrit : ∀ r, IsWeylOp r → foxTri u u r = θ * innerOp u r` is `orbit_half_weyl`'s hypothesis. r1447 item C showed the record's $Q(u)=\theta u$ implies it through `foxProd_self`, with the same $\theta$ and no stray factor. It is tested against Weyl $r$ only, so it is the Weyl-sphere first-order condition, at arbitrary norm. At $r=u$ it gives $\theta=P(u)/\lVert u\rVert^2$, and `hne` makes $\lVert u\rVert\ne0$ (reconstruction by `skew_expand`), so $\theta$ is determined by $u$. At unit $u$ it is the record's $\theta$. At other norms `RayleighGE` is scale-invariant along $(u,\theta)\mapsto(cu,c\theta)$, since `idemOf` is unchanged, so no reading is lost.

**The proof.** Witness $X=Du$ with $D=D_{\varphi_{pq}}$ from `weyl_moves`. Nonzero: `innerOp X X = 0` forces $X\varphi_{ij}=0$ for all $i,j$ (sum of nonnegative `inner2` squares), hence $XY=0$ at the skew $Y$ by `skew_expand` and linearity, contradicting `hYne`. Orthogonal: `innerOp_Dop_skew` and `innerOp_comm'`. Inequality: $T(\theta^{-1}u,X,X)=\theta^{-1}T(X,u,X)$ by `foxTri_comm` and `foxProd_smul_right`, and `orbit_half_weyl` gives $T(X,u,X)=\tfrac\theta2\langle X,X\rangle$, so the right side is $\tfrac12\langle X,X\rangle$ exactly, using $\theta\ne0$ in `field_simp`.

**$\theta\ne0$.** Screen 1: `idemOf u 0 Z = 0`, since Lean's $0^{-1}=0$. So at $\theta=0$ the conclusion requires $\tfrac12\langle X,X\rangle\le0$ at a nonzero $X$, which is false. The hypothesis is the Lean image of "$e=W/\theta$ is defined", and it is consumed.
