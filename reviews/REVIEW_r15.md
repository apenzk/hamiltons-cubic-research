### Reviewer 15: the graded-enumeration ansatz, the reducible-isotropy families, and the Petersen–Stanfield cross-check

**Every number confirmed, three framings refuted.** The sphere-product values match Nienhaus Proposition 2.25 and directly built sphere products to $\le7\times10^{-16}$, block by block, at $n=8,12,14$; the reducible-isotropy values are exact; and nothing above $\sqrt{3/2}$ surfaced in any search. New exact rationals for the $n=12$ splits $3{+}9$ through $6{+}6$: $\theta^2=176/135,\ 231/160,\ 264/175,\ 55/36$.

**The families are one-dimensional.** Ricci-flatness is two linear conditions, and the first Bianchi identity (checked by neither P2b script), cuts each family to a single Weyl operator. So "solving criticality within the family" is vacuous and the content is the verified $Q(W)=\theta W$ at $5\times10^{-16}$; "exactly one critical point per pair" becomes forced rather than empirical. A circle scan finds further stationary points, all Bianchi-violating. Separately, the space of $SO(4)\times SO(8)$-invariant symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^{12})$ has dimension $4$, not $3$ ($\Lambda^\pm$ splits, and the correct invariance group is $O(k)\times O(l)$) but enlarging the family leaves the same single Weyl operator, since Bianchi kills the extra direction.

**Corrections forced.** The sphere labels were off by one: split $\mathbb R^k\oplus\mathbb R^l$ is $S^k\times S^l$, so the $1.228239$ point at $n=12$ is $S^5\times S^7$. The Hermitian family is $\mathbb{CP}^m$, not $SO(2m)/U(m)$, verified by decoding $W=c(R-I/2(n-1))$ against Petersen–Stanfield §3.2 and by an independent Fubini–Study construction, and it is prior art, already returned by [review_r11_unitary_pairs.py](scripts/reviewers/r11/review_r11_unitary_pairs.py). Its closed form is $\theta^2=(m+1)(2m-1)/(3m(m-1))$, confirmed at $m=8$, decreasing to $2/3$ and equal to exactly $3/2$ at $m=2$, so $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is the first member of the family. Three of the four P2b rows have slack $0.57$–$0.96$ and are therefore **ungraded**, so presenting them as graded points the enumeration missed is wrong. The $n=8$ rows are triality copies of $S^3\times S^5$ and $S^2\times S^6$.

**The false alarm.** The claim that the graded enumeration never handled multi-factor isotropy is wrong: [review_r11_graded_ideals.py](scripts/reviewers/r11/review_r11_graded_ideals.py), the (I1) gate in [review_r11_cartan_sweep.py](scripts/reviewers/r11/review_r11_cartan_sweep.py), [review_r11_unitary_pairs.py](scripts/reviewers/r11/review_r11_unitary_pairs.py) and [review_r11_grassmann_pairs.py](scripts/reviewers/r11/review_r11_grassmann_pairs.py) all do. Only one convenience formula is partial, so the "exhaustiveness refuted" verdict is downgraded accordingly.

**On the literature.** Petersen–Stanfield Theorem A reads "only the complex Grassmannians **can** have three such eigenvalues", an inclusion, not an equivalence, and $\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(2,2)$ is the counterexample to the stronger reading. The suspicion that the Ricci-flat $W$ breaks the correspondence with their $R$ does **not** hold; the affine relation preserves the eigenvalue counts, verified on all five rows. The real over-reach is elsewhere: graded critical points here are indexed by symmetric *pairs* $(\mathfrak h,\mathfrak k)$ with $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$, while they classify symmetric *spaces*, so their tables cannot close the enumeration by citation.
