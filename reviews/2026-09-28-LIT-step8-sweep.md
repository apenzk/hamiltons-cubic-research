## Phase 3a, the screen: (MG) is an upper bound on the frame-split coupling

**Agreement, not new.** With $W=A+B$ in the maximal frame, $t=\lVert A\rVert$, $b=\lVert B\rVert$ and $a=\langle L_AB,B\rangle$, [L214](../findings/second-order.md)'s relations are $P(A)=\rho t^2-a$ and $P(B)=\rho b^2-2a$, and its test vector gives $\langle L_WX,X\rangle=\rho t^2b^2-a$ (L214 writes $t$ for the mass $t^2$ here). Hence, for $\rho>\sqrt{3/2}$, **(MG) $\iff P(A)\ge\rho t^3\iff a\le\rho\,t^2(1-t)$**, while L214's cap gives $a\ge\tfrac\rho2t^2(1-t^2)$. *So (MG) is the upper bound on the coupling that the record lacks.* In L214's unit form this is its identity $\rho=t\,P(\hat A)+\tfrac{1-t^2}t\langle L_{\hat A}\nu,\nu\rangle$ with $\hat A=A/t$, $\nu=B/b$, and [F67](../findings/fence-posts.md) is the statement that bounding $\langle L_{\hat A}\nu,\nu\rangle$ by its supremum over all $\nu\perp\mathcal D_n$ never beats $\Theta_n$, **the orbit direction alone carrying $\tfrac12\theta$**. F67's witnesses are an orbit direction, $\tfrac12\theta$ at the balanced product, and a four-set direction, $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ at a padded $S^2\times S^2$ through [L121](../findings/second-order.md), and four-set blocks carry no orbit line, **so removing the orbit direction does not clear F67 in general** ([r1816](REVIEW_r1816.md), [r1817](REVIEW_r1817.md)). At a product diagonal part two frame conditions clear it together: the first-order condition removes the orbit line from each mixed pair block, and the one-plane bound removes the within-factor pair blocks, whose top $\tau_P=d\tfrac{p-2}{2(p-1)}$ tends to $\tfrac d2$, because $\delta=0$ there. At a general diagonal part the one-plane bound only caps the within-factor mass, and that cap is the knapsack's binding constraint.

**The general sufficient condition (derived here, unreviewed).** $L_{\hat A}$ preserves each sign character ([L120](../findings/second-order.md)), and its spectrum on each is explicit for every diagonal $\hat A$: the four-set block by [L121](../findings/second-order.md), the pair block by [L122](../findings/second-order.md). Write $y_i$ for $B$'s mass on the $i$-th eigenmode, eigenvalue $\lambda_i$, so $\sum y_i=b^2$ and $a=t\sum\lambda_iy_i$. Then, for $\rho>\sqrt{3/2}$ and $0<t<1$,
$$(\mathrm{MG})\iff\sum_i\Big(\lambda_i-\frac{\rho\,t}{1+t}\Big)\,y_i\le0,$$
so (MG) is a knapsack: the modes with $\lambda_i>\rho t/(1+t)$ must carry little mass, and the maximal frame's inequalities are what bound it (the one-plane bound per pair block, the double rotation per four-set, the orbit line removed exactly).

**The foothold: a diagonal part proportional to a product.** *Gate 2 PASSED ([r1816](REVIEW_r1816.md), [r1817](REVIEW_r1817.md), independently): the coupling bound row by row, the value bound, the dichotomy, $c_*<d$, the threshold and the averaging corollary all CONFIRMED, with the corrections folded here. The scalar core is typed in [ProductFoothold.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/ProductFoothold.lean); unnumbered while the coupling bound is untyped at the operator level.* Suppose $\hat A=e$, the unit Weyl tensor of $S^p\times S^q$, $n=p+q$, $p,q\ge2$ and $(p,q)\ne(2,2)$, in its own frame, $Q(e)=de$, $d>0$. *Then $c_*<d$, since $c_*/d<1$ is $(2p-3)(2q-3)>1$, and $S^2\times S^2$ is the one product with $c_*=d$.* The character table of the [closed-branch analysis](2026-09-28-I121-MG-audit.md) (Gate 2, [r1801](REVIEW_r1801.md), [r1802](REVIEW_r1802.md)) gives, **using none of its $C=0$ rows' eigenvector argument**: a pair block inside a factor has $\delta=0$, so the one-plane bound makes $B$ vanish there; a mixed pair block carries $0$ and $\tfrac d2$ on the orbit line, which the first-order condition removes; four-sets inside a factor and split $3+1$ carry $0$; a $2+2$ four-set carries $\pm c_*$, $c_*=d\tfrac{n-2}{2(p-1)(q-1)}$, and its double rotation in the two within-factor planes sees only within-factor pair blocks, which vanish, so it forces $v+w=0$ and $B$ keeps the $+c_*$ mode only. So $a\le t\,c_*\,b^2$. With $P(A)=dt^3$, (MG) reads $\rho\le\max\{\sqrt{3/2},d\}$, which $\rho\le d$ implies, and the relation $P(A)=\rho t^2-a$ gives
$$\rho\ \le\ d\,t+c_*\,\frac{1-t^2}t .$$
At a global maximiser $\rho=\Theta_n\ge d$, so **either $b=0$ ($W$ is diagonal) or $t\le\dfrac{c_*}{d-c_*}$**, and (MG) holds whenever $t\ge c_*/(d-c_*)$. At the balanced product $p=q=m$, $c_*=\tfrac d{m-1}$ and the threshold is $t\ge\tfrac1{m-2}$. *No closedness is used, so this covers the nonclosed branch at such a diagonal.* **The threshold is below $1$, so says something, exactly when $(p-2)(q-2)\ge2$**: every $S^2\times S^q$ and $S^3\times S^3$ get nothing, and the balanced case bites from $m=4$ ([r1816](REVIEW_r1816.md) O5, [r1817](REVIEW_r1817.md) O3).

- **The double rotation is not load-bearing here**: the $-c_*$ mode lies below $c_*$ anyway, so $a\le t\,c_*\,b^2$ rests on the first-order condition and the one-plane bound alone, both of which a **local** maximiser of the diagonal mass satisfies. Global maximality enters only through $\rho=\Theta_n\ge d$ ([r1816](REVIEW_r1816.md) O1, O2, [r1817](REVIEW_r1817.md) O1).
- **The negated product is empty at $\rho>0$.** For $\hat A=-e$ the double rotation leaves only the $2+2$ mode of eigenvalue $-c_*$, so $a\le0$ while $P(A)=-dt^3$, and $\rho t^2=P(A)+a<0$. Here the double rotation is essential ([r1817](REVIEW_r1817.md) N1), and the $n\ge30$ corollary covers a diagonal part proportional to the balanced product of either sign.

**A corollary from averaging.** The maximal frame carries at least the $O(n)$-average diagonal mass, and by Schur's lemma on the irreducible $\mathcal W_n$ that average is $\dim\mathcal D_n/\dim\mathcal W_n$, so $t^2\ge\tfrac6{(n+1)(n+2)}$, in agreement with the frame average $\tfrac{12}{(n+1)(n+2)}$ of [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) over ordered pairs. Against the balanced threshold $\tfrac1{(m-2)^2}=\tfrac4{(n-4)^2}$ this wins exactly when $n^2-30n+44\ge0$, **so at even $n\ge30$ a global maximiser whose maximal-frame diagonal part is proportional to the balanced product is diagonal.** Below $n=30$ the case needs a frame lower bound on $t$ stronger than the average.

**What it leaves.** Diagonal parts not proportional to a product, where the pair blocks' $\delta$ is small rather than zero and the one-plane and double-rotation bounds bound masses instead of killing modes. This is the knapsack above with those bounds as its constraints.

**The knapsack with a cutoff, the form a general diagonal part needs (derived here, unreviewed).** Fix $\mu<\rho t/(1+t)$, call a mode *high* when $\lambda_i>\mu$ (eigenvalues of $L_{\hat A}$), and let $Y_h$ be $B$'s mass on high modes and $\lambda_{\max}$ the top eigenvalue there. Since $\sum_iy_i=b^2=1-t^2$ is fixed, $\sum_i\lambda_iy_i\le\lambda_{\max}Y_h+\mu(b^2-Y_h)$, so
$$(\mathrm{MG})\ \Longleftarrow\ (\lambda_{\max}-\mu)\,Y_h\ \le\ \Big(\frac{\rho t}{1+t}-\mu\Big)(1-t^2).$$
**The low modes pay for the high ones**, so the frame need only make $Y_h$ small, not zero. The frame supplies $Y_h\le\sum$ of the budgets of the blocks carrying a high mode, $2t^2\lVert\hat\delta^{jk}\rVert^2$ for a pair block by the one-plane bound, with the first-order condition $\langle\delta,x\rangle=0$ a linear constraint per pair block, which deletes an eigenmode only when $\hat A$ is critical, since $L_{\hat A}(\xi\cdot\hat A)=\tfrac12\,\xi\cdot Q(\hat A)$ ([r1817](REVIEW_r1817.md) O4). At a product diagonal part every high block has budget zero, which is the foothold. *Near a product, at a fixed $t$ strictly above the threshold, the only high modes are the within-factor pair tops $\tau_P$, whose budgets are $O(\varepsilon^2t^2)$ in the distance $\varepsilon$ from block-constancy, so the condition holds for $\varepsilon$ small at that $t$, and the allowed $\varepsilon$ shrinks as $t\to1$.*

## R53 at a general diagonal part, attempt 1: a global budget from rotating along a perfect matching

**Derived here, unreviewed. The two sums and the rotation identity are screened exact.**

- **Two sums are forced.** For a diagonal Weyl operator $A$, with $\mu_Q$ the four-set eigenvalue of [L121](../findings/second-order.md) and $\delta^{jk}_i=\tfrac12(a_{ji}-a_{ki})$ the pair profile,
$$\sum_Q\mu_Q^2=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}8\lVert A\rVert^2,\qquad\sum_{j<k}\lVert\delta^{jk}\rVert^2=\frac{n-1}2\lVert A\rVert^2 .$$
*Both are $S_n$-invariant quadratic forms on $\mathcal D_n$, which is the irreducible $S_n$-module of shape $(n-2,2)$, so each is a multiple of the norm, and the constants come from summing over disjoint and adjacent edge pairs with zero row sums. Screened at $n=6,9,12$ on random diagonal Weyl operators to $10^{-12}$.* **So a unit diagonal part carries on average $\mu_Q\sim\sqrt3/n$ per four-set, and a fixed cutoff can have order $n^2$ four-sets above it.**
- **The matching rotation.** Rotating by $\tfrac\pi4$ in every plane of a perfect matching $M$ moves each bivector $e_{ik}$, $i\in e$, $k\in f$, $e\ne f\in M$, inside the $4\times4$ block of the edge pair $(e,f)$ and fixes every $e_{ij}$ with $\{i,j\}\in M$, so the change of diagonal mass is $\sum_{e<f\in M}\Delta_{ef}$, each $\Delta_{ef}$ being `block_exchange` ([DoubleRotation.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/DoubleRotation.lean)) on that block: the four-set combination squared minus $\tfrac14\square^2$, plus the two pair terms $(x^e_k+x^e_l)^2-(\delta^e_k+\delta^e_l)^2$ and the same for $f$. *Screened on a random symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^8$ to $9\times10^{-14}$.* In a globally maximal frame every such change is $\le0$.
- **Averaged over a uniform perfect matching** ($P(e\in M)=\tfrac1{n-1}$, $P(e,f\in M)=\tfrac1{(n-1)(n-3)}$, and $\sum_kx^e_k=\sum_k\delta^e_k=0$), with r1798's identity $(v+w)^2+(u-w)^2+(u+v)^2=3(u^2+v^2+w^2)$ and the two sums above:
$$\tfrac32\lVert B_{\mathrm{four}}\rVert^2+\tfrac{n-4}2\lVert B_{\mathrm{pair}}\rVert^2\ \le\ t^2\,\frac{(n-1)(3n-10)}4 .$$
- **The obstacle.** At $n\ge7$ this gives $t^2\ge\frac6{6+(n-1)(3n-10)}$, weaker than the $O(n)$ average $\frac6{(n+1)(n+2)}$, and the pair mass it allows exceeds the one-plane bound's $(n-1)t^2$. **Averaged, the finite-angle information washes out**, and the four-set mass it permits is of order $n^2t^2$, so for $t\gtrsim1/n$ it caps nothing. The knapsack then rests on the four-set eigenvalues lying below $\rho t/(1+t)$, which the first sum does not force. *What survives is the unaveraged statement: the inequality holds for EVERY matching, so a matching fitted to where $B$ sits is the next attempt, and it is strongest when $B$ is concentrated, which is where [F67](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s four-set witness lives.*

## Attempts 2 and 3 at a general diagonal part, and the next one

- **Attempt 2, a matching fitted to $B$.** `block_exchange` has no cross terms between characters, so sign twists add nothing to a matching rotation, and a perfect matching sees exactly one of each four-set's three combinations ($v+w$, $u-w$, $u+v$ for its three pairings). **Obstacle**: $B$ spread evenly over the three pairings of its four-sets is controlled only at the averaged rate of attempt 1, and a single four-set needs all three pairings, which no one matching holds.
- **Attempt 3, identity (86) with the Peirce budget.** Since $z=b\hat A-t\nu$, $\eta=-\tfrac12+a/(\rho t^2b^2)$ is the normalised coupling itself, and (MG) is $\eta\le\tfrac{1-t}{2(1+t)}$. **Obstacle**: the Peirce budget $\lVert C\rVert^2\le\eta(\tfrac32-\eta)$ caps the three defects from above and grows with $\eta$, and none of the defects has a lower bound that grows with $\eta$ (all $\lambda_U=\bar\lambda$ is allowed), so (86) constrains a configuration where $\eta$ is small and says nothing where (MG) fails.
- **Next, attempt 4: the joint region on the diagonal locus.** A mode above $\rho t/(1+t)$ needs a large four-set difference $\mu_Q$ or a large pair top, and both are features of concentrated diagonal parts, while $P(\hat A)$ is largest at the balanced product, which is spread. So bound $\rho\le\max_t\big[t\,P(\hat A)+\tfrac{1-t^2}t\,R(\hat A,t)\big]$ with $R$ the knapsack's top value under the frame budgets, over the finite-dimensional $\mathcal D_n$, where $P(\hat A)$ has the closed form of `DiagCubicFormula` and the spectra are L121's and L122's. *This is F67's own successor criterion (keep $P(d)$ and $\langle L_d\nu,\nu\rangle$ coupled), made concrete by the budgets, and its first test is the padded $S^2\times S^2$ witness, where the four-set mode is $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$.*

## Attempt 4 at F67's witness, and where it fails (sketch, by hand)

- **At the witness it is sharp.** The zero-padded unit $S^2\times S^2$ is $\hat A$ with $\hat a_{12}=\hat a_{34}=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, $\hat a_{13}=\hat a_{14}=\hat a_{23}=\hat a_{24}=-\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$, and it is critical with $d=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, being $W^++W^-$ with each half a padded $\mathbb{CP}^2$ of value $\sqrt{3/2}$. Its four-set $\{1,2,3,4\}$ carries $\pm d$ (L121), the pairing $(12\,|\,34)$ has $\square=0$ and zero within-factor profiles, and the cross pairings cap that four-set's mass at about $t^2$. With $B$ there, $t^2\ge\tfrac12$ and the joint bound gives $\rho\le d/t\le\sqrt2\,d=\sqrt{3/2}$, **attained at $t^2=\tfrac12$ by the padded $\mathbb{CP}^2$ itself**, whose frame split this is ($a=\rho t^2-dt^3$ forces $B$ onto the $+d$ mode). *So (MG)'s constant $\sqrt{3/2}$ is exactly what the joint bound returns at F67's witness.*
- **At small $t$ it fails.** Every mode with positive eigenvalue lies above $\rho t/(1+t)$ once $t$ is small, and at a concentrated $\hat A$ the modes touching its support (four-sets with padded indices at $\tfrac{3\alpha}4\approx0.43$, pair blocks with one padded index) have budgets summing to order $n\,t^2$. Against $b^2=1-t^2$ this only gives $t\gtrsim n^{-1/2}$, and the bound $t\,P(\hat A)+\tfrac{1-t^2}tR$ then reaches about $2$ at $n=12$. **The crude budgets cannot exclude a global maximiser whose maximal-frame diagonal mass is small.**
- **What (MG) says at a global maximiser.** There $P(\hat A)\le\Theta_n=\rho$, which is $a\ge\rho t^2(1-t)$, so above $\sqrt{3/2}$ **(MG) holds exactly when $a=\rho t^2(1-t)$, i.e. when the normalised maximal-frame diagonal part is itself a global maximiser**. The node is therefore a statement about how far the best frame can see: a global maximiser's best diagonal view must be optimal.

## Phase 3a for the sharpened node: (MG) is the endpoint criterion on the great circle through $\hat A$, in both branches

*Gate 2 PASSED ([r1821](REVIEW_r1821.md), [r1822](REVIEW_r1822.md), independently, every claim CONFIRMED with the corrections folded here). **Both readers found the same thing: after L214 the criterion is exactly $p=\rho$, so the circle form is (MG) in new coordinates, and any content must come from the frame's constraints on which $\nu$ occur.** L214's relations alone give $g(\varphi_0)=\rho$ (`g_at_W`), and at $b>0$ the criterion can hold only with equality, since a strict one would put $g$ below $p$ on $(0,\tfrac\pi2]$. The equivalence uses no property of the frame. Typed in the draft CircleEndpoint.lean (`circle_cubic`, `mg_iff_value`, `tie_of_mg`, `g_at_W`), whose placement in the tree waits on the lean session's root import.*

- **The circle.** With $\hat A=A/t$ and $\nu=B/b$, $W=\cos\varphi_0\,\hat A+\sin\varphi_0\,\nu$, $\cos\varphi_0=t$, and on the circle $u(\varphi)=\cos\varphi\,\hat A+\sin\varphi\,\nu$
$$g(\varphi)=P(u(\varphi))=p\cos^3\varphi+3r\cos\varphi\sin^2\varphi+q\sin^3\varphi,\qquad p=P(\hat A),\ r=\langle L_{\hat A}\nu,\nu\rangle,\ q=P(\nu),$$
the $\cos^2\varphi\sin\varphi$ term vanishing because $Q(\hat A)$ is diagonal ([L120](../findings/second-order.md)). L214's relations are $\rho=tp+\tfrac{b^2}tr$ and $q=(\rho-2tr)/b$.
- **The equivalence.** At a global maximiser $g\le\rho=g(\varphi_0)$ and $p=g(0)\le\rho$, so above $\sqrt{3/2}$, **(MG) $\iff p=\rho\iff$ the arc $[0,\tfrac\pi2]$ of $g$ peaks at its diagonal end**, which is the outside author's binary endpoint criterion ([r1801](REVIEW_r1801.md) rebuilt it): it holds iff $p\ge2r$ and $p\,q^2\le(p+r)^2(p-2r)$. **No sign hypothesis is needed at a global maximiser with $0<t<1$**: $r\ge\tfrac{\rho t}2>0$ by L214's inequality, $p\le0$ makes both sides fail, and $q<0$ with $p\ge2r$ would put $g$ below $p$ on the open arc, so $q<0$ forces $p<2r$ and both sides fail ([r1821](REVIEW_r1821.md), [r1822](REVIEW_r1822.md)). *Closedness is not used: it makes the circle $P$-invariant, and the criterion needs only $g$.* **So the nonclosed branch meets the same criterion as the closed one, with $(p,r,q)=(d,\tau,q)$ at $\nu=f$.** *The closed branch reduces to a bound on $q$ alone ([I122](../plan.md)) only because the balanced product fixes $\tau=\tfrac d{m-1}$ by structure. In the nonclosed branch $r$ is a coupling tied to $\rho$ ([r1821](REVIEW_r1821.md)).* Necessity of $p\ge2r$ is second order at $\varphi=0$, and under (MG) the relations force $r=\tfrac{pt}{1+t}$.
- **What it names.** A proof of (MG) is a bound on the off-diagonal direction's own cubic $q=P(\nu)$ and its coupling $r$, jointly with the diagonal value $p$, for $\nu$ allowed by the maximal frame. *The crude $q\le\Theta_n$ is circular, and F67 forbids bounding $r$ and $p$ separately.*
