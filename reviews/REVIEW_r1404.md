## 1. The objects

The layer's pair basis is orthonormal for `inner2`, which is the record's $\lVert Z\rVert^2=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(Z^{\mathsf T}Z)$. Every operator-level quantity in the file (`innerOp`, `trCube`, `normSq`, `sharpPair`) is therefore the matrix of $M$ in an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2$, and reads as a trace or a Frobenius pairing without any constant. The four-index `R4 R i j k l` $=\langle R\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{kl}\rangle$ is that matrix indexed by ordered pairs, which is where the factors $8$ in $\alpha$, $4$ in $\lvert W\rvert^2$ and $\tfrac14$ in `innerOp_R4` come from.

The block's phrase "Hamilton's sharp with respect to the unnormalised $\phi_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{ji}$" names the basis whose structure constants define $\#$. That basis has Frobenius norm $\sqrt2$ and `inner2` norm $1$, so it is the layer's `phi` up to sign. `sharpQF` is the quadratic form $\tfrac12c^{\gamma\delta}{}_\alpha c^{\varepsilon\zeta}{}_\beta R_{\gamma\varepsilon}R_{\delta\zeta}\eta^\alpha\eta^\beta$ written as $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{ad}_\eta R\,\mathrm{ad}_\eta R)$, and at $R=\mathrm{id}$ it is $-\tfrac12$ of the Killing form, $(n-2)\lVert\eta\rVert^2$, matching the record's pinned $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$.

## 2. The identity and the transfer

`L50_bispectral` combines $P=\alpha/8+\beta/2$ (links), $\operatorname{tr}M^3=\alpha/8$ and $\operatorname{tr}_{S^2}\mathring W^3=\alpha/8-\beta$ (r1403). Then $\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac\alpha8-\tfrac12(\tfrac\alpha8-\beta)=\tfrac\alpha8+\tfrac\beta2$, which is the ring step. This is the block's own proof, with its sentence "expanding each in the coordinate pair basis" now carried out.

The transfer argument in the module docstring is correct and complete as prose: invariance of $S^2$, the kernel line $\mathbb Rg$ at Ricci-flat $R$, and the image in $S^2_0$ are the three facts needed, and all three are proved lemmas. What is untyped is the linear-algebra step from those facts to equality of traces over two subspaces, plus the eigen-decomposition that turns traces into power sums. The INDEX row lists both.

The theorem holds for every algebraic curvature operator in this sense, Ricci-flat or not, which is the sense in which the block calls Dai–Fu's Lemma 3.3 "the pointwise algebraic identity it is".

## 5. Screen at $R=\mathrm{Id}$

$R=\mathrm{Id}$ on $\Lambda^2$ satisfies all four hypotheses: `R4` $=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}$, and the three Bianchi terms cancel pairwise. Let $N=\binom n2$.

- Left side. $\operatorname{tr}\mathrm{Id}^3=N$ and $\mathrm{Id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{Id}$, so $P=N+(n-2)N=(n-1)N$, Hamilton's $Q(\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$.
- Second-kind side. $(\mathring Wh)_{ij}=\sum_{k,l}(\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}-\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl})h_{kl}=h_{ji}-\delta_{ij}\operatorname{tr}h$, so on $S^2$ it is $+1$ on $S^2_0$ (dimension $\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}2$) and $1-n$ on $g$. Hence $\operatorname{tr}_{S^2}\mathring W^3=\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}2-(n-1)^3$.
- Right side. $\tfrac32N-\tfrac12\big[\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}2-(n-1)^3\big]=(n-1)\big[\tfrac{3n}4-\tfrac{n+2}4+\tfrac{(n-1)^2}2\big]=(n-1)\cdot\tfrac{n(n-1)}2=(n-1)N$. Agrees at every $n$. At $n=4$ both sides are $18$.
- Norm. $\operatorname{tr}_{S^2}\mathring W^2=\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}2+(n-1)^2=\tfrac{3n(n-1)}2=3N=3\,$`normSq`. Agrees at every $n$.

The object is not Ricci-flat, so it tests the trace-form statement where a hidden trace-free assumption would show, and it shows why the transfer of item 2b needs Ricci-flatness: the $g$ line contributes $-(n-1)^3$ here.
