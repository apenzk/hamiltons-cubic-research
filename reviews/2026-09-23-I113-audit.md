## (b) The finite-reflection identity: TRUE, with its scope corrected, and typed

**Statement.** Let $T$ be a symmetric trilinear form, $W=D+N$ with $\langle D,N\rangle=0$, $\lvert W\rvert=1$, $T(W,W,X)=\lambda\langle W,X\rangle$ for all $X$, and $T(D,D,N)=0$. Then $2P(D)=P(N)+\lambda(3\lvert D\rvert^2-1)$ and $P(D+N)-P(D-N)=2P(N)$. So at a global maximiser, where $D-N$ is again a unit vector, $P(N)\ge0$.

**Proof.** Trilinear expansion of $P(D+N)$ and $\langle Q(W),D\rangle$ gives $\lambda=f+3t+p$ and $\lambda q=f+t$, with $T(D,D,N)=0$ killing both cross terms. The difference $P(D+N)-P(D-N)$ keeps only the odd terms in $N$. $\square$ **Lean**: `reflection_restriction` and `reflection_difference` in [I113Reflection.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I113Reflection.lean).

**The hypothesis that carries it is $T(D,D,N)=0$, and it holds when $D$ is the orthogonal projection onto a subspace $S$ with $Q(S)\subseteq S$.** The zero-extended $\mathcal W_{n-1}$ of a hyperplane qualifies ($\#$ stays inside $\mathfrak{so}(n-1)$), and so does the coordinate-diagonal locus of ANY orthonormal frame, which is the archive's own $D$ (its Theorem 3.1). **So [post F39](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s "in any frame" is the right quantifier**; its footer only failed to say that $D$ is the frame-diagonal part, now repaired. *A split along a subspace not closed under $Q$ does not give the identity.*

## (a) The second-kind/Hessian defect identity (1.5): TRUE, and weaker in its hypotheses than the archive says

The archive states $(\beta\mu-\lambda)\lvert Z(h)\rvert^2=\Delta_M-\Delta_H$ in three places and proves it in none. Its conventions are pinned by its own $\langle W,Z\rangle=\mu/2$: $\mu$ is an eigenvalue of $S:h\mapsto\sum_{jl}W_{ijkl}h_{jl}$ on traceless symmetric $h$, **which is MINUS Li's $\mathring W$, the record's standard** ([bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md)), so the archive's bottom eigenvector of $\mathring W$ is the TOP one of $S$ and **$M=\mu I-S$**, the other sign failing by $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$ ([r1095](REVIEW_r1095.md)), $Z=\tfrac12\pi_{\mathcal W}(h\owedge h)$, and the $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ basis is orthonormal for $\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{\mathsf T}B)$.

**Step 1, derived: the Hessian sum is a Casimir computation.** $T$ is $O(n)$-invariant, so $T(A\cdot X,Y,U)+T(X,A\cdot Y,U)+T(X,Y,A\cdot U)=0$. Put $\sum_\alpha A_\alpha\cdot A_\alpha=-c$ on $\mathcal W_n$. Applying the Casimir of $\mathcal W_n\otimes\mathcal W_n$ to $W\otimes Z$ and contracting with $T(\cdot,\cdot,Z)$, invariance applied twice moving the Casimir onto the third slot, gives $\sum_\alpha T(A_\alpha\cdot W,A_\alpha\cdot Z,Z)=\tfrac c2T(W,Z,Z)$. Invariance at $(W,Z,A\cdot Z)$ then gives $\sum_\alpha T(W,A_\alpha\cdot Z,A_\alpha\cdot Z)=\tfrac c2T(W,Z,Z)$, and with $\sum_\alpha\lvert A_\alpha\cdot Z\rvert^2=c\lvert Z\rvert^2$,
$$\sum_\alpha\langle K(A_\alpha\cdot Z),A_\alpha\cdot Z\rangle=\tfrac c2\big(\lambda\lvert Z\rvert^2-T(W,Z,Z)\big),\qquad c=4(n-1),$$
at ANY $\lambda$. So $\Delta_H=\lambda\lvert Z\rvert^2-T(W,Z,Z)$, and **$\lambda$ cancels from (1.5)**, which becomes
$$T(W,Z,Z)=\beta\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2-\Delta_M .$$

**Step 2, derived: the quartic identity, in $h$'s eigenframe.** Diagonalise $h=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(h_i)$, $\sum h_i=0$, $\sum h_i^2=1$, $q_i=h_i^2$, $t_4=\sum h_i^4$. Then $h\owedge h$ is a diagonal curvature operator with entries $2h_ih_j$, and removing its Ricci and scalar parts keeps it diagonal:
$$Z_{ij}=h_ih_j+a(q_i+q_j)+b,\qquad a=\tfrac1{n-2},\quad b=-\tfrac1{(n-1)(n-2)},$$
which is the archive's own formula. $T(W,Z,Z)=\langle W,Q(Z)\rangle$, $Q(Z)$ is diagonal by the diagonal $R^\#$ formula $Q(Z)_{ij}=z_{ij}^2+\sum_{m\ne i,j}z_{im}z_{jm}$, and $\langle W,D\rangle=\sum_{i<j}W_{ijij}D_{ij}$ for diagonal $D$. **$\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(W)=0$ kills every term of the form $f(i)+f(j)$ and every constant**, since $\sum_jW_{ijij}=0$. Keeping only the rest,
$$Q(Z)_{ij}\equiv\big(1-2a+(n-2)a^2\big)q_iq_j-(h_i^3h_j+h_ih_j^3)+h_ih_j,\qquad 1-2a+(n-2)a^2=\tfrac{n-3}{n-2}.$$
With $(\mathring Wh)_{ii}=\sum_jW_{ijij}h_j=\mu h_i$ and $\mathring W\,\mathrm{Id}=0$, the three sums are $\tfrac12\langle r_0,\mathring Wr_0\rangle$, $\mu t_4$ and $\tfrac12\mu$, so
$$T(W,Z,Z)=\tfrac{n-3}{2(n-2)}\langle r_0,\mathring Wr_0\rangle-\mu t_4+\tfrac12\mu .$$
And summing $z_{ij}^2$ directly, $\lvert Z\rvert^2=\tfrac n{n-1}\big[\tfrac12-t_4+\tfrac{n-3}{2(n-2)}(t_4-\tfrac1n)\big]$: the $t_4$ coefficient is $-\tfrac n{2(n-2)}$ on both sides, and with $d=n-2$ the constant is $\tfrac{d^2+d+1}{2d(d+1)}$ on both sides. So $\beta\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2-\Delta_M=\mu(\tfrac12-t_4)+\tfrac{n-3}{2(n-2)}\langle r_0,\mathring Wr_0\rangle=T(W,Z,Z)$. $\square$ *The eigen-condition enters only as $(\mathring Wh)_{ii}=\mu h_i$ in this frame.*

**Consequence.** **(1.5) uses neither criticality, nor maximality, nor that $h$ is the BOTTOM eigenvector**, where the archive says it "uses only criticality and the two positivity conditions". The positivities $M\succeq0$ and $K\succeq0$ enter only in reading $\Delta_M,\Delta_H\ge0$, **and that reading needs two things the identity does not**: $\mu$ the TOP eigenvalue of $S$ for $M\succeq0$, and $A_\alpha\cdot Z\perp W$ for $K$, which is positive only on $W^\perp$ and has $W$ as a $-\tfrac\lambda2$-eigenvector at a critical point. *The second holds at every eigenvector $h$ of $S$, since $\langle A\cdot Z,W\rangle\propto\mu\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(h[A,h])=0$* ([r1095](REVIEW_r1095.md), [r1096](REVIEW_r1096.md)). So the extremal eigenvector returns in the positivity reading, which is what makes [R35](../routes.md)'s high-energy branch the statement $\Delta_H\le\Delta_M$.
