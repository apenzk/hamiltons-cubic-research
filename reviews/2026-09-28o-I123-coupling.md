# I123: a coupling of P's two halves, first attempt

*Working note for [I123](../plan.md), route [R56](../routes.md), the [28n sweep](2026-09-28n-LIT-step8-sweep.md)'s pick. Nothing here is numbered. Gate 2 [r1901](REVIEW_r1901.md), [r1902](REVIEW_r1902.md): the three extensions CONFIRMED, the conclusion's Ricci candidate REFUTED as vacuous, corrections folded.*

**The object.** With $Q(X)=X^2+X^\#X$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak{so}(n)$ (the record's $Q$, $Q(I)=(n-1)I$, [conventions](../findings/conventions.md)) and $G(X)=\langle Q(X),X\rangle=\operatorname{tr}X^3+\langle X^\#X,X\rangle$, Problem P is $G\le\theta_0(n)\lVert W\rVert^3$ on the Weyl subspace. $G$ is the cubic of a fully symmetric trilinear form $T$, $G(X)=T(X,X,X)$, and $Q(X)$ is an algebraic curvature operator whenever $X$ is (Böhm–Wilking, the halved product the record uses).

## Three extensions of P beyond the Weyl subspace, and what each does

1. **Through $I$: neutral** ([28n](2026-09-28n-LIT-step8-sweep.md), batch 3). $G(W+cI)=P(W)+c^3(n-1)N$, the $\lVert W\rVert^2$ terms of the two halves cancelling.
2. **Through $\Lambda^4$: first order vanishes.** For $Y$ in the $\Lambda^4$ component, the orthogonal complement of the algebraic curvature operators, $T(W,W,Y)=\langle Q(W),Y\rangle=0$ because $Q(W)$ satisfies Bianchi, so $G(W+Y)=P(W)+3\langle Q(Y),W\rangle+G(Y)$. *A Weyl tensor is critical for $G$ in the $\Lambda^4$ directions, whatever its value.* **Extensions 1 and 2 are one fact**: $Q$ maps Weyl tensors to Weyl tensors ([BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) eq. (6), recorded in [problem.md](../findings/problem.md)), so $\langle Q(W),Z\rangle=0$ for every $Z$ orthogonal to the Weyl subspace, and the first-order term vanishes in every direction off it. Whether $G\le\theta_0\lVert\cdot\rVert^3$ holds on $\mathrm{Weyl}\oplus\Lambda^4$ turns on the second-order term $3\langle Q(Y),W\rangle+G(Y)$ and is undecided.
3. **Through the Ricci part: false as an extension.** On the scalar-flat algebraic curvature operators (traceless on $\Lambda^2$, Ricci allowed), $G$ exceeds $\theta_0$. The $O(p)\times O(q)$-invariant operators $aP_{\mathfrak{so}(p)}+bP_{\mathfrak{so}(q)}+cP_{\mathrm{mix}}$ are all algebraic curvature operators (spanned by $P_{\mathfrak{so}(p)}$, $P_{\mathfrak{so}(q)}$ and $I$), and with the record's products ($P_k^\#P_k=(k-2)P_k$, $P_k^\#P_{\mathrm{mix}}=\tfrac{k-1}2P_{\mathrm{mix}}$, $P_{\mathrm{mix}}^\#P_{\mathrm{mix}}=qP_{\mathfrak{so}(p)}+pP_{\mathfrak{so}(q)}$, the D6 note, batch 33)
$$G=a^3d_p(p-1)+b^3d_q(q-1)+c^3pq+\tfrac32pq\,c^2\big(a(p-1)+b(q-1)\big),\quad d_k=\tbinom k2 .$$
At $a=b=c=1$ it returns $(n-1)N$, and on the Ricci-free line $a=-\tfrac{q}{p-1}c$, $b=-\tfrac{p}{q-1}c$ it returns the sphere products' values, $\theta_0(12)=1.236033$ at $p=q=6$. **On the traceless plane at $n=12$, $p=q=6$, its maximum is exactly $\tfrac{2\sqrt{2310}}{77}=1.248376$**, at $a=\tfrac{\sqrt{2310}}{385}-\tfrac{\sqrt{210}}{210}$, $b=\tfrac{\sqrt{2310}}{385}+\tfrac{\sqrt{210}}{210}$, $c=-\tfrac{\sqrt{2310}}{462}$, a unit-norm point with trace $0$ whose Ricci tensor is $\mp\tfrac{\sqrt{210}}{84}$ on the two factors in the record's normalisation ($\mathrm{ric}(I)=\tfrac{n-1}2$, and $\mp\tfrac{\sqrt{210}}{42}$ as a sum of sectional curvatures) (solved by Lagrange multipliers, $\tfrac{120}{77}>\tfrac{55}{36}$ as `scalar_flat_beats` in [I123Lifts.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I123Lifts.lean), and the neighbouring splits give $1.24436$ at $p=5,7$ against their Weyl value $1.22824$). **The value is $\Theta(S^6\times S^7)=\Theta_{13}$, and at every even $n=2m$ the traceless maximum on the invariant plane at $p=q=m$ is $\Theta(S^m\times S^{m+1})$**, $\max^2=\tfrac{4m(m-1)}{(2m-1)(m+1)}$ ([r1901](REVIEW_r1901.md), symbolically, $\tfrac{120}{77}$ at $m=6$), the Weyl value of the sphere product one dimension up, which exceeds $\theta_0(2m)$. *It is not the hyperplane restriction of that Weyl tensor (the ratios $a/c$, $b/c$ differ), and why the two coincide is open.* *This sharpens [post F14](../findings/second-order.md) (the full curvature subspace peaks at the round sphere, $\sqrt{2(n-1)/n}$): removing the $I$ direction is not enough, the Ricci-free condition itself is load-bearing.*

## What the coupling must therefore use

**Ricci-flatness of the argument, not only tracelessness.** Extension 3 shows a scalar-flat operator with Ricci of both signs beating the balanced product, the conjectured maximiser over all Weyl tensors, inside a two-parameter family, so an inequality valid on all traceless curvature operators cannot be P, at every even $n$. Extension 1 shows the identity shift is invisible. Extension 2 is undecided at second order. *Reading the Ricci constraint through $Q$ is vacuous, since $\operatorname{Ric}Q(W)=0$ for every Weyl $W$. The record's inequality that does read Ricci off the Weyl subspace is the Böhm–Wilking and Beitz cone criterion $P(R)\le F(R)\langle\operatorname{ric}(R),\operatorname{ric}(\Phi(R))\rangle$ ([problem.md](../findings/problem.md)), whose $a=0$ slice produces $\theta_0$.* **Named obstacle for this attempt: extensions 1 and 3 fail, extension 2 is undecided, and the one open lift is $\mathrm{Weyl}\oplus\Lambda^4$ at second order.**

## Second attempt: the Ricci-free extension is false at every even $n\ge8$, although at $n=12$ it survives at the balanced product (derived, Gate 2 [r1904](REVIEW_r1904.md), [r1905](REVIEW_r1905.md))

**The space.** $\mathrm{Weyl}\oplus\Lambda^4$ is exactly the kernel of the Ricci contraction on $\operatorname{Sym}^2\Lambda^2$: the contraction vanishes on $\Lambda^4$ by antisymmetry and is injective on the Ricci and scalar parts of the curvature operators. In [L183](../findings/diagonal-half.md)'s terms, for $X=\sum\lambda_i\omega_i\omega_i^{\mathsf T}$ the Ricci contraction is $\sum\lambda_i\omega_i^2$ and the $\Lambda^4$ component is $\sum\lambda_i\,\omega_i\wedge\omega_i$, so this space keeps the Ricci condition and drops Bianchi ([L181](../findings/INDEX-results.md) is the block-diagonal instance, where the two are the diagonal and the off-diagonal of one matrix $M^{\mathsf T}\Lambda M$). **The candidate P$^+$** was $G(X)\le\theta_0(n)\lVert X\rVert^3$ for every Ricci-free symmetric $X$ on $\Lambda^2$, which restricts to P.

**At the balanced product at $n=12$ it survives.** At a unit $W_0$ with $Q(W_0)=\theta_0W_0$, the second variation of $G/\lVert\cdot\rVert^3$ along a unit $V\perp W_0$ is $6T(W_0,V,V)-3\theta_0$. For $V=V_W+Y$ with $V_W$ Weyl and $Y\in\Lambda^4$ the cross term $T(W_0,V_W,Y)=\langle B(W_0,V_W),Y\rangle$ vanishes, because $Q$ preserves Weyl and so does its polarisation, so the Hessian is P's on the Weyl part, nonpositive at the balanced product ([L3](../findings/second-order.md)), plus $Y\mapsto6T(W_0,Y,Y)-3\theta_0$ on $\Lambda^4$. *The $\Lambda^4$ gradient $3\langle Q(X),Y\rangle$ vanishes at every Weyl $X$, so a local maximum on the Weyl sphere with a negative definite $\Lambda^4$ block is a genuine local maximum on the Ricci-free sphere* ([r1904](REVIEW_r1904.md)). *Screened on all of $\Lambda^4(\mathbb R^{12})$ ($495$ dimensions, the halved $\#$ in [L265](../findings/second-order.md)'s form $C^\#X=\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_i\mathrm{ad}_{v_i}X\mathrm{ad}_{v_i}^{\mathsf T}$, checked on $I^\#I=10I$ and on every product's $\theta$):* the top of $T(W,Y,Y)$ is $0.40452$ at $S^6\times S^6$ against $\tfrac{\theta_0}2=0.61802$, $0.58621$ at $S^5\times S^7$ against $0.61412$, and $0.40825$ at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ against $0.61237$. **At $S^4\times S^8$ it is $0.87386$ against $0.60078$** (exactly $8\sqrt{21/1760}$, [r1904](REVIEW_r1904.md)), a $\Lambda^4$ ascent direction, the volume form of the $\mathbb R^4$ factor. *At $n=8$ even the balanced product fails the test: $S^4\times S^4$'s $\Lambda^4$ block tops at $0.654654$ against $\tfrac{\theta_0(8)}2=0.572822$* ([r1905](REVIEW_r1905.md), screen).

**And along that direction it fails, exactly.** On $\mathbb R^4\oplus\mathbb R^8$ take $X=\alpha P_++\beta P_-+bP_{\mathfrak{so}(8)}+cP_{\mathrm{mix}}$, with $P_\pm$ the projections on $\Lambda^2_\pm\mathbb R^4$. With $P_\pm^\#P_\pm=2P_\pm$ (their sum is $\mathfrak{so}(4)$'s $I^\#I=2I$, and they commute), $P_\pm^\#P_{\mathrm{mix}}=\tfrac34P_{\mathrm{mix}}$ (half of $P_{\mathfrak{so}(4)}^\#P_{\mathrm{mix}}=\tfrac32P_{\mathrm{mix}}$, by orientation reversal), $P_{\mathfrak{so}(8)}^\#P_{\mathrm{mix}}=\tfrac72P_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{mix}}^\#P_{\mathrm{mix}}=8P_{\mathfrak{so}(4)}+4P_{\mathfrak{so}(8)}$,
$$G=9\alpha^3+9\beta^3+196b^3+32c^3+72(\alpha+\beta)c^2+336bc^2,\qquad\lVert X\rVert^2=3\alpha^2+3\beta^2+28b^2+32c^2$$
(checked against a least-squares fit of the explicit $\#$). The Ricci-free plane is $\alpha=-\tfrac83c+t$, $\beta=-\tfrac83c-t$, $b=-\tfrac47c$: the $S^4\times S^8$ Weyl line plus $t$ times the $\mathbb R^4$ volume form $\star=P_+-P_-$, a $\Lambda^4$ element. There $G=-\tfrac{19360}{21}c^3-144ct^2$ and $\lVert X\rVert^2=\tfrac{1760}{21}c^2+6t^2$, and at $c=-1$, $t^2=\tfrac{3850}{441}$ the ratio is $\tfrac{8\sqrt{15015}}{715}=1.371029$, **against $\theta_0(12)=\tfrac{\sqrt{55}}6=1.236033$.** At $t=0$ it is $\tfrac{\sqrt{2310}}{40}=1.201561$, the product's own value. **So P$^+$ is false at $n=12$**: a Ricci-free symmetric operator, $S^4\times S^8$'s Weyl tensor tilted toward one chirality of its $4$-dimensional factor, beats every Weyl tensor's conjectured maximum by $11\%$.

**At every even $n=q+4\ge8$ the same tilt fails P$^+$, exactly.** On $\mathbb R^4\oplus\mathbb R^q$ the same products give $G=9\alpha^3+9\beta^3+\tbinom q2(q-1)b^3+4qc^3+9qc^2(\alpha+\beta)+4\big(\tbinom q2+q(q-1)\big)bc^2$ and $\lVert X\rVert^2=3\alpha^2+3\beta^2+\tbinom q2b^2+4qc^2$, and on the Ricci-free plane ($\alpha,\beta=-\tfrac q3c\pm t$, $b=-\tfrac4{q-1}c$, $c=-1$) with $s=9(q-1)t^2$, $G=\tfrac{2q}{3(q-1)}\big((q+2)(q+3)^2+3s\big)$ and $\lVert X\rVert^2=\tfrac2{3(q-1)}\big(q(q+2)(q+3)+s\big)$. $G>0$ on the whole plane at $c=-1$, and the ratio is stationary at $s=(q+2)(q+3)(q-3)\ge0$ (its maximum, the ratio decreasing for large $s$), where $$\rho^2=\frac{6q^2(q-1)}{(q+2)(q+3)(2q-3)},$$ against $\theta_0(2m)^2=\tfrac{(2m-1)(m-1)}{m^2}$ read off the same family's Weyl line. With $q=2m-4$, $\rho^2>\theta_0^2$ is $D(m)>0$ for $D=8m^5-64m^4+152m^3-73m^2-70m+11$, and $D(k+4)=8k^5+96k^4+408k^3+727k^2+498k+99$ has positive coefficients. *[r1905](REVIEW_r1905.md) screened the same values at $n=8$ to $16$ ($1.171080$ against $1.145644$ at $n=8$).*

## Third attempt: no inequality of the $I$-lift family between H and P is sharp at the balanced product (derived, in Lean, Gate 2 [r1915](REVIEW_r1915.md), [r1913](REVIEW_r1913.md))

**Statement.** *For $s\in[0,1]$ let $G_s(X)=s\operatorname{tr}X^3+\langle X^\#X,X\rangle$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak{so}(n)$, $g_s$ any valid cap $G_s\le g_s\lVert X\rVert^3$, and $b(s)$ the bound on $sA+B$ it gives at unit Weyl $W$ through $X=W+cI$, where $A=\operatorname{tr}W^3$, $B=\langle W^\#W,W\rangle$. At $n=2m$ the balanced product has $A=t_0=\frac1{m\sqrt{(m-1)(2m-1)}}$ and $B=\beta=\frac{2m-3}{\sqrt{(m-1)(2m-1)}}$, $t_0+\beta=\theta_0$. Then $b(s)-(st_0+\beta)\ge s\big(\sqrt{2(n-1)/n}-\theta_0\big)>0$ for every $s\in(0,1]$, an equality at $s=1$ when $g_1=(n-1)/\sqrt N$: every $s\in(0,1]$ misses the balanced product, and $s=0$ reaches it exactly when H holds.*

**Proof.** *$I^\#W=-W$ and $I^\#I=(n-2)I$ give $G_s(W+cI)=sA+B+3c(s-1)+c^3N(s+n-2)$ ([28n](2026-09-28n-LIT-step8-sweep.md), batch 3, at $s=1$), so $sA+B\le b(s)=\inf_c\big[g_s(1+c^2N)^{3/2}-3c(s-1)-c^3N(s+n-2)\big]$. With $u=c\sqrt N$, $\varphi(u)=(1+u^2)^{3/2}-u^3$ and $g_s\ge G_s(I)/\lVert I\rVert^3=(s+n-2)/\sqrt N$, the bracket is at least $f(u,s)=\big((s+n-2)\varphi(u)+3u(1-s)\big)/\sqrt N=(1-s)f(u,0)+sf(u,1)$. At $s=0$, $(n-2)\varphi(u)+3u$ is strictly convex with its minimum $(n-3)\sqrt{n/(n-2)}$ at $u=-1/\sqrt{n(n-2)}$, so $\min_uf(u,0)=(n-3)\sqrt{2/((n-1)(n-2))}$, which is $\beta$ at $n=2m$ and is H's cap on the Weyl subspace ([28m](2026-09-28m-LIT-step8-sweep.md), question 2). At $s=1$, $f(u,1)=(n-1)\varphi(u)/\sqrt N\ge\sqrt{2(n-1)/n}$ since $\varphi\ge1$ ($(1+u^2)^3-(1+u^3)^2=u^2(3u^2-2u+3)$), and $\theta_0^2=\frac{(2m-1)(m-1)}{m^2}<\frac{2m-1}m$. So $f(u,s)\ge(1-s)\beta+s\sqrt{2(n-1)/n}$ at every $u$.* $\square$ **Lean**: `phi_ge_one`, `lift_affine`, `theta0_lt_sphere`, `theta0_R_lt`, `lift_not_sharp`, and the quantitative gap `lift_gap` in [I123LiftFamily.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I123LiftFamily.lean). The expansion of $G_s(W+cI)$ and $\beta$ as the $s=0$ minimum enter as hypotheses.

- **What it closes.** In the plane of $(A,B)$ over unit Weyl tensors, P and H assert supporting lines of normals $(1,1)$ and $(0,1)$ through $(t_0,\beta)$, and together they give every normal $(s,1)$, $0\le s\le1$, since $sA+B=s(A+B)+(1-s)B$. The $I$-lift reaches the $s=0$ line exactly when H holds and none with $0<s\le1$, so it fails on inequalities that are conjecturally true, and batch 3's neutrality at $s=1$ ends a whole segment of such failures.
- **What it leaves.** Under H, P holds at every unit Weyl $W$ with $\operatorname{tr}W^3\le t_0$, since there $A+B\le t_0+\beta$. This is the general-$W$ form of the diagonal locus's trade-off $E-E_0\le T_{\max}-T$, whose split $\theta_0=\sqrt2c_n+\tfrac{2\sqrt2}{n\sqrt{(n-1)(n-2)}}$ is on file ([diagonal-half.md](../findings/diagonal-half.md), "And that decomposes $\theta_0$ itself"): H's cap $\beta$ on all unit Weyl tensors is that file's spectral cap $\sqrt2c_n$, at every $n$. A coupling has to certify the rest, where the cubic half exceeds $t_0$ (at $n=12$, $t_0=\frac1{6\sqrt{55}}=0.02247$), by an argument sharp at the balanced product in both halves.

## Where the coupling stands

**No $O(n)$-invariant linear enlargement of the Weyl subspace carries P's bound with P's functional and constant.** $\operatorname{Sym}^2\Lambda^2=\mathrm{Weyl}\oplus\operatorname{Ric}_0\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\Lambda^4$ has four pairwise non-isomorphic $O(n)$-irreducible summands, so each of the seven proper invariant enlargements contains $\mathbb RI$, $\operatorname{Ric}_0$ or $\Lambda^4$ ([r1904](REVIEW_r1904.md)), and each of those already breaks the bound: $I$ at every $n$ (the round sphere, [post F14](../findings/second-order.md)), the Ricci directions at every even $n=2m$ ($\Theta(S^m\times S^{m+1})$, extension 3), and $\Lambda^4$ at every even $n\ge8$ (the chirality tilt above). **So a coupling of the two halves that works on an invariant space must use both Weyl conditions, Ricci-flatness and first Bianchi, at once.** *Open: lifts that change the functional or the constant (a penalty on the $\Lambda^4$ or Ricci part, a weighted norm, the Böhm–Wilking and Beitz cone form), and non-invariant or nonlinear enlargements. The item stays open with no mechanism.*
