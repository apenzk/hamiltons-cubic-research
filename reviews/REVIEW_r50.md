### Reviewers 50 and 51: the basis-free projector, and a gap that was never certified

**They agree on all four verdicts, and between them they refuted the premise the whole cycle rested on.** Own implementations in [review_r50_*.py](scripts/) and [review_r51_*.py](scripts/); both ran the repository's code alongside their own and said which they kept. Reports in [review_r50_report.md](agent_logs/review_r50_report.md) and [review_r51_report.md](agent_logs/review_r51_report.md).

**J1 CONFIRMED, and stronger than it was recorded.** The basis-free projector $P(X)=Y-\frac2{n-2}\mathrm{KN}(\rho_0)-\frac1{n(n-1)}s\,\mathrm{KN}(g)$ is correct at $n=4,5,7,9,11,12,13,14$ (all outside the range originally checked), to $1.1\times10^{-15}$, with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P=\dim\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ exactly and **self-adjointness $\le8\times10^{-17}$, which had never been tested**; a projection must be self-adjoint, so this was a real gap in the evidence. Both reviewers pinned the two coefficients **independently rather than as a pair**, which was the specific attack the brief asked for: one by choosing inputs that annihilate each term in turn ($X=\mathrm{KN}(h_0)$ with $h_0$ traceless forces $s=0$ and isolates $\alpha$; $X=\mathrm{KN}(g)$ forces $\rho_0=0$ and isolates $\beta$), the other by fitting them as independent regressors at $n=5\ldots12$. Both match to eight or eleven digits, so no compensating pair of errors is possible. It is also derivable rather than fitted: with $\mathrm{KN}(h)=\tfrac12(h\owedge g)$ the classical curvature decomposition gives exactly these coefficients. **The trap it was written to avoid is real**, halving either coefficient alone leaves a map that still fixes $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ to $3\times10^{-16}$ while being $13.1\%$ or $2.6\%$ wrong off it.

**J2's premise REFUTED: the full operator at $n=14$ was never infeasible.** Vectorised, it costs about ten seconds end to end, basis $1.6$s, the full $3080\times3080$ $L_e$ $5.7$s, eigensolve $2.0$s, against the nine-minute timeout on record. **The timeout measured a Python-loop implementation, not the problem**, and the underlying cost was [weyl_basis](scripts/review_r30_core.py) materialising a $277$ MB dense array; building the nullspace in symmetric coordinates removes it. Confirmed here independently: the $n=14$ basis comes out at $3080$ in $1.5$ seconds. The projector is moreover **not needed for $L_e$ at all**, since $L_e$ already preserves $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, unprojected $e\circ X$ matches the full matrix to $1.8\times10^{-15}$.

**J3: nondegeneracy CONFIRMED and now certified rather than bounded, but the number reported for it was wrong by a factor of nine.** The true minimum of $(L_e-\tfrac12)^2$ off the orbit is $2.066116\times10^{-3}=(1/22)^2$ at $n=14$ and $3.086\times10^{-3}=(1/18)^2$ at $n=12$, agreed to seven digits by three independent routes (full eigensolve, deflated eigensolve, and matrix-free Lanczos), against the $1.9\times10^{-2}$ and $2.9\times10^{-2}$ recorded. **The reason is structural, not a tuning failure: a power iteration on a Rayleigh quotient produces an upper bound on the minimum, so it can never certify a gap**, and it stalled a decade high. What the eigensolve does establish is stronger than what was claimed: $E(\tfrac12)$ is **equal** to the orbit tangent rather than merely equidimensional ($60=60$ at $n=14$, $45=45$ at $n=12$), principal cosines $1.000000000000$, deflation leak $8.5\times10^{-16}$, and $|\lambda-\tfrac12|$ jumping from $10^{-15}$ straight to $4.5\times10^{-2}$. So the deflation worry the brief raised is settled outright.

**"The highest $\theta/\theta_0$ available among padded points" is false**, because odd-dimensional seeds were skipped: $S^6\times S^7$ padded $13\to14$ sits at $0.9895$, and padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $n=12$ sits at $0.9909$ against the $0.9708$ recorded for $S^5\times S^5$. Both are nondegenerate, so the conclusion is unaffected and only the superlative was wrong.

**A rational law for the gap, which is the cycle's most useful byproduct.** Any padding of a seed from $n_0$ gives $\min|\lambda-\tfrac12|=\frac1{2(n_0-1)}$, **independent of the target dimension $n$**. So the gap shrinks like $n_0^{-2}$ rather than sitting at a constant $10^{-2}$, and the $1/22$ and $1/18$ above are the $n_0=12$ and $n_0=10$ cases.

**J4's conclusion survives; its stated mechanism is false and contradicts the record's own next paragraph.** The claim that degenerate padded points are those already degenerate at their own dimension is wrong: the whole $S^2\times S^k$ family for $k=2,\ldots,8$ is **nondegenerate natively and degenerate at every padding**, eighteen counterexamples with excess growing linearly, closed form $q\,(n-n_0)$ for $S^2\times S^q$, doubled when both factors are $2$-spheres, generalising the $4(n-4)$ on record. This is Reviewer 44's finding, which [findings/INDEX.md](../findings/INDEX.md) already carried two paragraphs later, so the file asserted a mechanism and refuted it in the same section. The conclusion nonetheless holds and is now close to exhaustive: across all thirty sphere-product splittings, $\mathbb{CP}^3$ through $\mathbb{CP}^7$ and the Fox point padded into $n=14$, every degenerate point sits at $\theta/\theta_0\le0.8355$ and every point above that is nondegenerate, with roughly thirty-five padded points above $0.9269$ all clean. The $S^2$ family can never supply the counterexample, since $\theta(S^2\times S^q)\uparrow1$ while $\theta_0\uparrow\sqrt2$, capping its ratio at $1/\theta_0(n)\to0.707$.

**Scope limit stated by one reviewer and worth carrying:** the sweep covers two-block products plus $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ only. Its three-block construction was invalid ($|\mathrm{Ric}|\approx0.4$), so three-block products and the subalgebra family remain untested.

**A process failure worth recording.** The commit claiming the $n=14$ experiment added only the projector and a projector test; **no committed script reproduced the $1.9\times10^{-2}$ figure**, which had been produced inline and never saved. A number in the record that no script in the tree can regenerate is not a result.
