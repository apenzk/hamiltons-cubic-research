## I1, CONFIRMED, and it is L24 restated rather than weakened

I derived it from the definitions before opening the note. Let $W$ be a unit maximiser, $\theta=P(W)>0$, $T(x,y,z)=\langle x\circ y,z\rangle$ the symmetric trilinear form with $P(x)=T(x,x,x)$, and $X$ any unit vector of $\mathcal W_n$ with $c=\langle W,X\rangle$. Then $P(W+tX)=\theta+3t\theta c+3t^2\theta\langle L_eX,X\rangle+t^3P(X)$, using $T(W,W,X)=\langle Q(W),X\rangle=\theta c$ and $T(W,X,X)=\theta\langle L_eX,X\rangle$, while $\lVert W+tX\rVert^{-3}=1-3tc+t^2\big(\tfrac{15}2c^2-\tfrac32\big)+O(t^3)$.

Multiplying, the linear term of $f=P/\lVert\cdot\rVert^3$ cancels identically and the quadratic term is $\theta\big[3\langle L_eX,X\rangle-\tfrac32c^2-\tfrac32\big]$. Maximality forces it to be $\le0$, and $\theta>0$ divides out: $\langle L_eX,X\rangle\le\tfrac12(1+c^2)$. **The form is exactly as I1 states.**

The second derivation shows it is not new information. Write $X=cW+\sqrt{1-c^2}\,Y$ with $Y\perp W$ of unit norm. Since $L_eW=W$ and $W\perp Y$, the cross term $\langle L_eW,Y\rangle=\langle W,Y\rangle$ vanishes, so $\langle L_eX,X\rangle=c^2+(1-c^2)\langle L_eY,Y\rangle$; applying [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) to $Y$ gives $\le c^2+\tfrac{1-c^2}2=\tfrac{1+c^2}2$. **So I1 is an algebraic consequence of L24 in one line, and conversely L24 is I1 at $c=0$.** That is worth recording, because it means the corrected criterion introduces no new second-order input, the whole content of I12's repair is bookkeeping on where $W$'s own mass sits.

**On the brief's question of which identity is needed.** Neither $L_eW=W$ nor $L_eW=\theta W$ is a hypothesis; both are readings of criticality at $\lVert W\rVert=1$. The true one is $L_eW=e\circ W=Q(W)/\theta=W$, $W$ is the $1$-eigenvector of $L_e$, which is Krasnov–Tkachev's "$1$ simple". The statement $L_eW=\theta W$ is **false**; what is true with a $\theta$ in it is $L_WW=Q(W)=\theta W$. The first derivation above uses criticality exactly once, inside $T(W,W,X)=\theta\langle W,X\rangle$, and additionally needs $\theta=P(W)$, i.e. $\lVert W\rVert=1$. The hypothesis $\theta>0$ is load-bearing at the division, matching L24's recorded scope.

**On "sharp at $X=W$", true, and misplaced.** At $X=W$ one has $c=1$ and $\langle L_eW,W\rangle=P(W)/\theta=1=\tfrac{1+1}2$, so the general inequality is attained. But $X=W$ is the radial direction, which the sphere constraint excludes, so this is degenerate sharpness; and in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) the phrase sits attached to the $\Sigma\le\theta^2(1+\rho^2)^2$ conclusion, where $X$ ranges over a $4$-set Bianchi plane and can never equal $W$ unless $\operatorname{diag}(M)=0$ and $W$ is supported on a single $4$-subset. **So the $\Sigma$-form is nowhere known to be attained, and the note should not claim it is.** The honest and stronger sharpness statement is available: equality holds for *every* $c\in[-1,1]$, at $X=cW+\sqrt{1-c^2}\,Y$ with $Y$ an orbit tangent, since the orbit attains L24's $\tfrac12$ ([second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) records that the bound holds on all of $W^\perp$ with equality along the orbit). That also makes L24's "off the orbit" qualifier redundant here.

## I2, CONFIRMED, and the case the brief flagged is a non-issue

**Two independent proofs of the orthogonality, and the second uses no representation theory at all.**

*By characters.* The map $S\mapsto\chi_S$, $\chi_S(\varepsilon)=\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i$, is a group isomorphism from the subsets of $[n]$ under symmetric difference onto the dual of $\Gamma=\{\pm1\}^n$. Hence $\chi_S=\chi_{S'}$ iff $S\triangle S'=\emptyset$ iff $S=S'$. **Two distinct $4$-subsets sharing three indices differ by a $2$-set, which is nonempty, so their characters are distinct**, sharing three indices is no more dangerous than sharing none. The pairing on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ is $\Gamma$-invariant, so distinct character spaces are orthogonal.

*By monomials.* $V_S$ is spanned by the three monomials $\hat\varphi_\alpha\odot\hat\varphi_\beta$ with $\alpha,\beta$ disjoint $2$-sets and $\alpha\cup\beta=S$. Such a monomial determines $S$, so distinct $4$-subsets have disjoint spanning monomials; and under the identification of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ with symmetric operators, $\hat\varphi_\alpha\odot\hat\varphi_\beta\mapsto u v^{\mathsf T}+vu^{\mathsf T}$ with $u\perp v$ orthonormal, whose Frobenius pairings vanish unless the unordered pair coincides. **So the planes are orthogonal at every $n\ge4$, elementarily.** This second route is what makes the conclusion robust: it survives any dispute about which sign group is in play.

**The Bianchi cut preserves it, trivially.** The cut is taken inside each $V_S$, the single relation $W_{ijkl}+W_{iklj}+W_{iljk}=0$ on four distinct indices, Ricci-flatness being empty there, and a subspace of one member of an orthogonal family is still orthogonal to the others. Note also that $\mathcal W_n$ is $\Gamma$-invariant, so $\Pi_{\chi_S}W$ automatically lands in the $2$-dimensional Bianchi plane; nothing has to be checked.

**The $n\ge9$ restriction is unnecessary, and I recommend striking it.** [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) attaches it twice (to Corollary 2 and to Corollary 1$'$), sourced to [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md)'s fusion caveat. That caveat is a failure of $\Gamma\cap SO(n)$, and the note's *own* character lemma is stated for the **full** sign group, where even-subset characters are pairwise distinct at every $n$; [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md) records the same requirement. So the fusion never arises in this argument. Even granting the determinant-one group, fusion merges $\chi_S$ with $\chi_{S^{\mathsf c}}$ into one isotypic, it does not make the two constituent monomial spans non-orthogonal, which is all the budget needs. **Recording a scope limit that does not exist is a live hazard**: it invites a later cycle to spend effort "repairing" $n=4,6,8$, and it would block any future transfer of the budget to the low-$n$ witnesses the record cares about, such as the $n=4$ champion in [target.md](../findings/target.md).

**A free sharpening the note misses.** Every $X_S$ lies in a nontrivial character space, hence is orthogonal to the whole trivial space (the diagonal operators) so $\rho_S=\langle X_S,W\rangle=\langle X_S,R\rangle$. Bessel against $R$ rather than $W$ gives $\sum_S\rho_S^2\le\lVert R\rVert^2=1-\tfrac{t_F}2$. The note derives $\rho^2\le1-t_F/2$ for a *single* subset in Corollary 1$'$ and never carries it back into the budget. Corollary 2 then improves to: at most $\lfloor(1-t_F/2)/t\rfloor$ subsets have $\rho^2>t$. A second free factor of two is available by using both eigendirections $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma$ of each block.

**Corollary 2's arithmetic checks.** From $\sum\rho^2\le1$, $k$ subsets with $\rho^2>t$ give $kt<1$, so $k\le\lfloor1/t\rfloor$ (loose by one at integer $1/t$, in the safe direction). The mean bound $1/\binom n4$ is right, and $1/\binom{12}4=2.02\times10^{-3}$ matches the quoted $(1+2\times10^{-3})^2$. The word "typical" is doing unearned work (a bound on the mean constrains no individual subset) but the statement it stands for is Corollary 2 itself, so nothing is wrong beneath the phrasing.

## I3, CONFIRMED as an inequality, with the chain overstated and the corollary weak

The chain is sound. For $X$ the top eigenvector of the $4$-set block: $\langle X,L_WX\rangle=\langle X,L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}X\rangle+\langle X,L_RX\rangle$ by bilinearity of $\circ$; [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md) kills the second term since $X$ sits in the single character $\chi_S$ and $R$ has zero diagonal; [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) gives the first as $\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma$. Dividing by $\theta$ and applying I1, $\sqrt\Sigma\le\theta(1+\rho^2)$, and $\rho^2\le1$ gives $\Sigma\le4\theta^2$. Each hypothesis is met: $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ is coordinate-diagonal in $F$ and lies in $\mathcal W_n$ because Ricci-flatness $\sum_bM_{ab}=0$ is a condition on frame-diagonal entries alone; L121's scope correction licenses it at a non-critical diagonal operator; the Weyl projection inside $Q$ is orthogonal and $\Gamma$-equivariant, so it disturbs neither step.

**I verified L121's spectrum independently rather than taking it.** With $G$ as stated, $\operatorname{tr}G=A+B+C$ and $\operatorname{tr}(G^2)=3(A^2+B^2+C^2)$; since $\mathbf 1$ is an eigenvector at $A+B+C$, the remaining two eigenvalues $\pm\nu$ satisfy $2\nu^2=3\sum A^2-(A+B+C)^2=2\Sigma$, so they are $\pm\sqrt\Sigma$ and $L_M|_{\mathbf 1^\perp}=\tfrac12G|_{\mathbf 1^\perp}$ has $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma$. L121 is right as written.

**Where I3 overstates.** The corollary's own text says the budget is what makes it work, "the projection defect cannot be large … because it is paid for out of $\lVert W\rVert^2=1$". For Corollary 1 that is decoration: $\rho^2=\langle X,W\rangle^2\le\lVert X\rVert^2\lVert W\rVert^2=1$ is Cauchy–Schwarz on a single unit vector, and no orthogonality of anything is used. I2 earns its place only in Corollary 2 and in the sharpened budget above. Stating the dependency correctly matters because it tells a later cycle which lemma is load-bearing for which corollary.

**And Corollary 1 is far from biting.** I derived the sphere-product value in closed form: with cross entry $d$ and row sums zero, $\sqrt\Sigma=|d|(n-1)(n-2)/((p-1)(q-1))$ at a $(2,2)$-split $4$-subset and $\theta=(n-1)|d|$, so $\Sigma/\theta^2=\big((n-2)/((p-1)(q-1))\big)^2$, which is $16/(n-2)^2$ at the balanced product. *Screened in exact rationals on vertex matrices I constructed:* $1$ at $n=6$, $4/9$ at $n=8$, $0.16$ at $n=12$. So $\Sigma=\theta^2$ is attained **only** at $S^3\times S^3$, and at the conjectured maximiser at $n=12$ the certificate permits $4\theta^2$ against an actual $0.16\theta^2$, a factor of $25$. Corollary 1 can therefore exclude only points whose frame-diagonal part is wildly spread compared with any sphere product, and no amount of tightening the constant $4$ toward $1$ would reach the conjectured maximiser's own margin.

## I4, CONFIRMED, with the note's calibration replaced by a derivation, and its owed Gate 2 item settled

**The relation is right and can be derived rather than calibrated.** [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) records $\sum d_\varphi^2=t/2$ with $d_\varphi$ the entries $K_{ab}$ and $t=\lVert K\rVert_F^2$; since $K$ is the full symmetric hollow $n\times n$ pair matrix, $\lVert K\rVert_F^2=2\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^2$, and the same file records that $\operatorname{diag}M$ in the $\varphi$-basis of $\Lambda^2$ is exactly $K_{ab}$. Hence $\lVert\operatorname{diag}(M)\rVert^2=\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^2=t_F/2$, which is **$t_F=2\lVert\operatorname{diag}(M)\rVert^2$ on the nose**. The note instead argues from "the sphere products returning $t=2$", which is a consistency check on one family; the record already contains the identity.

**The decomposition is orthogonal for free.** $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ and $R$ are the diagonal and off-diagonal parts of one symmetric matrix in an orthonormal bivector basis, so $\lVert W\rVert^2=\lVert\operatorname{diag}(M)\rVert^2+\lVert R\rVert^2$ is the trivial Frobenius split; the five rotations of $S^3\times S^3$ the note cites confirm the implementation, not the mathematics.

**The Gate 2 item the note leaves owed ("whether a factor of two there moves the whole constant"), is settled here, and the answer is that the normalisation is forced.** Under the record's $\Lambda^2$ pairing $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$ from [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md), $\varphi_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}$ is a **unit** vector, so $M_{ab}=\langle W\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{ab}\rangle=W_{abab}=K_{ab}$; under the rival reading of the same file's $\lVert\varphi_{ab}\rVert^2=2$ line as the operative one, $M_{ab}=2K_{ab}$. The two are separated by a hand computation at $S^3\times S^3$: row sums zero give $2c+3d=0$, normalisation $6c^2+9d^2=1$ gives $c=1/\sqrt{10}$ and $d=-2/(3\sqrt{10})$, and a $(2,2)$-split $4$-subset has $A=2c$, $B=C=2d$, so $\Sigma=4(c-d)^2=10/9$. That is **exactly** $\theta_0(6)^2=10/9$, saturating L121. The rival reading returns $40/9>\theta^2$, which would violate L121 at a genuine critical point. **So the $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}$ reading is the only consistent one, and the constant in I4 stands.**

**The $t_F=2$ end is substantive, not formal.** At $t_F=2$ the orthogonal split forces $\lVert R\rVert=0$, hence $R=0$, hence $W$ *is* coordinate-diagonal in $F$, which is precisely L121's hypothesis. Simultaneously $\rho=\langle X,R\rangle=0$, so I1 collapses to L24 and the factor $(2-t_F/2)^2$ collapses to $1$. The endpoint is recovered by the mathematics rather than matched by the algebra, exactly as the note claims.

## I5, CONFIRMED, with one word to change

The monotonicity is immediate: $\tfrac{d}{dt_F}(2-t_F/2)^2=-(2-t_F/2)<0$ on $[0,2]$. The record's $t$ is the extremal value over $O(n)$, [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) fixes diagonality as off-diagonal mass minimised over $O(n)$, hence diagonal mass maximised, so $t\ge t_F$ for every frame, and substituting $t$ for $t_F$ replaces the bound by a smaller right-hand side. **That is a strengthening, so it would be asserted without justification.** I5's reasoning is correct as given, and its identification of this as soundness rather than sharpness is the right classification: the error would be an unproved claim, not a lost constant.

At the extreme $t=2$ the substitution asserts $\Sigma\le\theta^2$ in an arbitrary frame, which is exactly what [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md)'s "what it does not give" paragraph says is unlicensed. **The one word to change is "refutes".** I1 removes the *derivation* of $\Sigma\le\theta^2$ off the eigenframe; it exhibits no frame and no critical point where the inequality fails. Nothing in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) does either, the $0.243$ measurement shows $c\ne0$, not $\Sigma>\theta^2$. So the honest statement is that $\Sigma\le\theta^2$ off the eigenframe is **unproved**, and it may well be true; treating it as refuted would misrecord an open question as a closed one.

## An extra defect, outside the five claims

**The boxed "THEOREM (the reduction, unconditional)" does not follow, for a reason independent of the projection defect the note itself later records.** Its hypothesis is that $X$ is a unit vector in the $4$-set Bianchi plane *orthogonal to $W$*. That plane is $2$-dimensional, so the condition $X\perp W$ pins $X$ to a line in it, and that line is generically **not** the top eigenvector of the block. Its left-hand side is nevertheless the block's top eigenvalue, which depends on no $X$ at all. For a non-eigenvector $X$ in the plane, $\langle X,L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}X\rangle$ lies strictly inside $[-\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma,\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma]$, so bounding it bounds nothing about $\sqrt\Sigma$. The theorem is not vacuous (such an $X$ exists), it is a non-sequitur. **The corrected theorem repairs this by the right move**, dropping $X\perp W$ so that $X$ can be the eigenvector and paying $c^2$ for it; but the refuted box is left standing in the file labelled "unconditional", above a proof whose middle step ("Lemma L24 bounds the total") is the very hypothesis the file later shows fails.

**Two further errors in that display.** First, it is off by $\sqrt2$ as typeset: it reads $\tfrac12\big[(A-B)^2+(B-C)^2+(C-A)^2\big]^{1/2}$, which is $\tfrac12\sqrt{2\Sigma}$, where L121's eigenvalue is $\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma$, the $\tfrac12$ inside L121's radical has been dropped. Second, it carries a factor written $\big\lvert\langle X,\cdot\rangle\text{-coefficient}\big\rvert$, which is not defined anywhere in the file and makes the display unusable as stated.

**And one self-contradiction.** The corrected theorem says the bound "reduces to Lemma L121's inequality exactly when $c=0$, in particular in the eigenframe and at every sphere product, where the plan records $c=0$". The second clause is wrong as phrased: the same file measures $\lvert\langle X,W\rangle\rvert$ up to $0.243$ over $45$ directions **at $S^3\times S^3$** in a rotated frame. $c=0$ is a property of the eigenframe, not of the point, and the whole subject of this note is what happens away from the eigenframe.

## What I would change in the record

- Strike the $n\ge9$ restriction from the budget and from Corollary 1$'$, replacing it with the monomial-orthogonality argument, which holds at every $n\ge4$ and needs no sign group.
- Upgrade the budget to $\sum\rho^2\le1-t_F/2$, and Corollary 2 to $\lfloor(1-t_F/2)/t\rfloor$.
- Withdraw or repair the boxed "reduction, unconditional" theorem; at minimum remove the word "unconditional" and the undefined coefficient, and restore the $\tfrac12$ inside the radical.
- Replace "sharp at $X=W$" on the $\Sigma$-form with the true sharpness statement (equality at $X=cW+\sqrt{1-c^2}\,Y$ for $Y$ an orbit tangent, at every $c$) and note that the $\Sigma$-form is nowhere known to be attained.
- Replace "refuted" by "unjustified" in I5, and record $\Sigma\le\theta^2$ off the eigenframe as an open question rather than a closed one.
- Record that $t_F=2\lVert\operatorname{diag}(M)\rVert^2$ follows from [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md)'s $\sum d_\varphi^2=t/2$, and that the $M_{ab}=K_{ab}$ normalisation is forced by the exact identity $\Sigma=\theta_0(6)^2$ at $S^3\times S^3$.
- Record the certificate's own margin, $\Sigma/\theta^2=16/(n-2)^2$ at the balanced product, so that no later cycle mistakes Corollary 1 for something that bites near the conjectured maximiser.
