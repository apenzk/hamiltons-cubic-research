### Reviewer 19: the coupled $\gamma$ bound and the repair of Theorem 4.17 at $n=12,14$

**The requirement and the subdivision are exact, and the second is a theorem.** $f'(\varphi)=3\sin\varphi\cdot\tilde f(\varphi)$ exactly and $\tilde f(\pi/6)=(5\alpha+\sqrt3\gamma-3)/4$, so $\gamma_{\max}(\alpha)<(3-5\alpha)/\sqrt3$ is the requirement with no slack; Nienhaus's minimiser quadratic $(1-\alpha)x^2-cx+\alpha$ checks out. The decomposition $(\mathbb Ru\oplus T_u\mathcal O)^\perp=V_{1/3}\oplus V_{\le0}$ holds exactly at $n=10,12,14,16$ ($\mathrm{mult}(+1)=1$ and $\mathrm{mult}(+\tfrac12)=\dim\mathcal O$ ($26/34/42/50$)) and it follows from Theorem 4.14(a),(b) with Remark 4.16 rather than being a numerical accident. The $\alpha$ identity $\alpha=(1-s^2)/3+s^2c_-^2\mu_N$ is an identity: the cross term dies because $F_uW_1=\tfrac13\theta^*W_1$ and $W_1\perp W_2$, and $W_2$ spanning several eigenspaces is harmless.

**The two vanishing couplings are real, and established more strongly than claimed.** Building the full $770\times770$ matrix of $F_{W_1}$ for **all 78** basis vectors of $V_{1/3}$ at $n=10$ and reading the eigenspace blocks gives $T(W_1,W_1',N)\le8\times10^{-16}$ for every negative eigenspace, $T(W_1,K,N)\le6\times10^{-16}$, and $T(W_1,W_1',W_1'')\le8\times10^{-16}$, exhaustive rather than sampled, and re-confirming $P\equiv0$ on $V_{1/3}$. $A=\sqrt{5/6}$ exact to $6.7\times10^{-16}$. **No structural certificate exists**: no single circle in the isotropy explains the pattern, since weight-additivity would force all weights to zero. These are exhaustively verified numerics, **not proofs**.

**Three corrections.** Both $B$ constants were set *below* the suprema they cap, $0.666$ and $0.815$ against the exact $2/3$ and $\sqrt{2/3}=0.8164966$, so the "bound" was not one; the impact is nil ($n=16$ moves $-0.0079\to-0.0081$) but it is fixed. $B(c_0)=B_{\ker}c_0^2+B_{\mathrm{neg}}c_-^2$ is an **upper bound**, not the equality it was written as, since the two constants are attained at different $W_1$. And $s$ was pinned to one endpoint: with $\mu_N\in[-1,-\tfrac16]$, $s^2$ ranges over $[3\varepsilon/(1+3t),\,6\varepsilon/(2+t)]$, though in the decisive region $s\le0.34$ the objective increases in $s$ so the endpoint used is the right one.

**Two findings that reshape the result.** The coupled bound has a **closed form**: its maximiser is always $c_0=1$, so $W_2$ lies entirely in $\ker F_u$, $s^2=3\varepsilon$ exactly, and

$$\gamma_{\text{coupled}}=\sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big[3(1-3\varepsilon)\sqrt{3\varepsilon}\sqrt{\tfrac56}+3\sqrt{1-3\varepsilon}\,(3\varepsilon)\tfrac23+(3\varepsilon)^{3/2}\theta_0(n)\Big].$$

Only $\sqrt{5/6}$, $2/3$ and $\theta_0(n)$ are load-bearing; $B_{\mathrm{neg}}$ never enters a verdict. And the repair is an **interval-covering** statement: Nienhaus's branch covers $\alpha\le0.32228$ ($n=12$), $0.30247$ ($n=14$), $0.29018$ ($n=16$), while the coupled branch covers $\alpha\ge0.296$ for **every** $n$. So $n=16$ fails through a gap of width $0.0058$, widening to $0.061$ at $n=100$.

**The statement survives where the bound does not.** Direct maximisation on the exact constraint set, exact block-rescaling retraction, independently reproducing the $0.608$ and $0.534$ already on file, gives true $\sup\gamma=0.636203$ at $n=16$, $\alpha=0.295$, against a requirement of $0.880459$: a margin of $+0.244$ where the bound fails by $0.0081$. The bound overshoots the truth by a factor $1.40$. **Theorem 4.17's estimate is not in trouble at $n\ge16$; this route to it is.** These are ascent values and so lower bounds on the sup, but a missed optimum would have to beat them by $34\%$. A rerun at $n=12$, $\alpha=0.29$ with 40 restarts reproduced $0.659733$ exactly, so the $1.3\%$ spread against $n=16$'s $0.668270$ is real rather than under-convergence: $\gamma_{\max}$ is $n$-independent to about $1\%$ over $12\le n\le16$, not exactly.

**Where it is actually tightest is not the disputed window.** The ratio $\gamma_{\max}/\text{requirement}$ peaks near $\alpha=0.25$ at $0.825$ ($n=12$) and $0.834$ ($n=14$), scaling roughly with $c_n$ and extrapolating to $\approx0.93$ as $n\to\infty$. Anyone claiming the estimate for large $n$ should compute $\gamma_{\max}(0.25)$ at $n=18$–$20$ rather than only near $0.295$.

**Unflagged in the original claim:** the repair is conditional on Conjecture A past $n=12$, since $c_n=\sqrt{2/3}\,\theta_0(n)$ enters both branches.
