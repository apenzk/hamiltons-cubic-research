# I13, step 1, the $d=3$ optimum is a partial grading, and the enumeration is finite

**Record search, by mechanism, before deriving.** The multiplier/facet mechanism appears in [bounds.md](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) only in [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) and [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md), written this cycle. The three-eigenvalue regime is named in bounds.md as the one the per-row family has never been read in, and nothing on the record carries a facet description of it. No outward search: the object is this programme's own polytope.

## 1. The obstruction that rules out reusing the $d=2$ method verbatim

At $d=2$ each facet was isolated inside a single row: the multiplier polynomial $\varphi(x)=(x-u)(x+w)$ annihilated every coefficient in the class-$1$ rows except $t_{112}$'s, and $\varphi(x)=(x-u)^2$ did the same in the class-$2$ rows for $t_{222}$. **That is an accident of $d=2$ and it does not survive.**

A row carries a quadratic multiplier, so $3$ free parameters. The coefficients it acts on are indexed by the unordered pairs with repetition from $d$ values, of which there are $\binom{d+1}2$, three at $d=2$, **six** at $d=3$. Isolating one variable inside a row means annihilating $\binom{d+1}2-1$ coefficients: two at $d=2$, which $3$ parameters do with one to spare, and **five at $d=3$, which $3$ parameters cannot**. Combining across all $d$ rows gives $3d=9$ multiplier parameters against $10$ types, **but the count as first written here was wrong, and [r343](REVIEW_r343.md) supplies the right one.** [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s two dependencies are a two-dimensional kernel of the multiplier action, so the image on type-coefficients has dimension $3d-2$, not $3d$; the honest criterion is that **every type is isolable exactly when $3d-2=\binom{d+2}3$**, which holds at $d=1$ ($1=1$) and $d=2$ ($4=4$) and at no larger $d$, at $d=3$ it is $7<10$. *The earlier "9 homogeneous conditions on 9 parameters generically forces zero" is false as an argument, since the two dependencies satisfy those conditions identically.*

> **So at $d=3$ the ten facets are not all reachable by multiplier isolation, and [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s proof does not generalise in that form.** *What does generalise, per [r343](REVIEW_r343.md): annihilating the six-type support of a row rather than isolating one type is six conditions on a seven-dimensional effective space, so it is always solvable, and strong duality guarantees the resulting certificate, that gives the closed-form $\theta$ at a named support without solving for $t$.* The route is therefore built rather than reused, and the rest of this note builds it.

## 2. What replaces it: the optimum is a vertex, and a vertex is a partial grading

**Vertex count.** By [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) the feasible set at $d=3$ is $\{t\ge0\}$ intersected with an affine space of dimension $\binom{d+2}3-3d+3=4$, and $\theta$ is a linear functional on it, bounded (Lemma L104's compactness). So $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is attained at a vertex, and a vertex of a $4$-dimensional polyhedron cut from the nonnegative orthant has **at least four of the ten type-variables equal to zero.**

*Consistency at $d=2$*: feasible dimension $1$, so at least one of the four types vanishes at an endpoint, which is exactly what Lemma L105 found, with the two upper facets $t_{112}=0$ and $t_{222}=0$. **And the balanced product has both vanishing at once**, a degenerate vertex where the segment's two endpoints meet; that degeneracy *is* the equality case.

**What a vanishing variable means.** $t_{ijk}$ is the common value of $T_{abc}=\langle[v_a,v_b],v_c\rangle^2$ over a $G$-orbit, and $T\ge0$, so
$$t_{ijk}=0\iff\langle[E_i,E_j],E_k\rangle=0,$$
a bracket-orthogonality between eigenspaces. **A vertex of the pinned polytope is therefore a partial grading of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ relative to the eigenspace decomposition**, a list of forbidden bracket types.

> **THEOREM (I13, step 1).** *Let $W$ be a $\theta$-critical point of $\mathcal W_n$ whose spectrum takes three distinct values with every multiplicity at least $3$. Then $\theta\le\theta^\ast(\lambda)$, and $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is attained at a $t$ with at least four of the ten $t_{ijk}$ zero; equivalently, at a configuration in which at least four of the ten bracket types $\langle[E_i,E_j],E_k\rangle$ vanish. Hence $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is the maximum of at most $\binom{10}4=210$ linear solves, each determined by naming which four types vanish.*

**Proof.** Lemma L104 gives the polytope, its dimension and its compactness; a linear functional on a compact polyhedron attains its maximum at a vertex; a vertex of a $4$-dimensional polyhedron in the nonnegative orthant has at least four active nonnegativity constraints, which are four vanishing coordinates. The equivalence $t_{ijk}=0\iff\langle[E_i,E_j],E_k\rangle=0$ is nonnegativity of $T$ together with the orbit-constancy of $t$, **and it holds only at a $t$ some $W$ actually realises**, since $\Pi(\lambda)$ contains tensors that are no frame's bracket tensor. $\square$

**Two corrections to the theorem as first stated** ([r343](REVIEW_r343.md)). *(i)* The bracket reading was written unconditionally and presupposes realisability; it describes $\Pi(\lambda)$'s vertices, and only a realisable vertex is a partial grading. *(ii)* "the maximum of at most $\binom{10}4$ linear solves" omits the **feasibility filter**: a named support gives a value only if its linear system is feasible with the remaining $t\ge0$, and the unfiltered maximum over supports strictly exceeds $\theta^\ast$, so a fence post read off it would mean nothing.

**Why this is the route and not a restatement.** It converts a four-dimensional continuous optimisation into a finite list of named algebraic configurations, each of which is a *statement about brackets* rather than about numbers, which is the currency [I15](../plan.md) needs and the currency Jacobi is written in. The $d=2$ case is the same theorem with $\binom41=4$ cases, two of which are upper bounds; the enumeration there was invisible because it was so short.

## 3. What cuts the 210 down, and what is owed

**The (A) cut is empty, and [r343](REVIEW_r343.md) measured it.** Row $i$ of (A) forces at least one nonzero variable per row, which is true and **inert at four zeros: all 210 four-subsets survive it.** *What does bite is (B), and then (C)*: $t_{133}>0$ whenever $\nu_2>0$ and $\nu_2+\nu_3>0$, and $t_{113}>0$ whenever $\nu_2<0$ and $\nu_1+\nu_2<0$, each removing $84$ of the $210$; a (C) cut removes a further $28$ once $\theta>0$. **So the sign pattern of the spectrum, not (A), is what prunes the enumeration.**

**Jacobi is the next cut and it is exactly I15.** A set of forbidden bracket types must be closed under the Jacobi identity: if $\langle[E_i,E_j],E_k\rangle=0$ for several pairs, the identity relates the surviving brackets and can force a further vanishing or a contradiction. *At $d=2$ this is visible in closed form:* $t_{112}=0$ says $E_{\max}$ is a subalgebra, $t_{222}=0$ says $E_{\min}$ is bracket-orthogonal to itself, and both together are a symmetric pair, a $\mathbb Z_2$-grading. **At $d=3$ the analogous question is which of the 210 four-subsets are the forbidden-bracket sets of an actual $\mathbb Z_3$- or $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$-graded decomposition**, and that is a question about $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with a finite answer.

**What is owed, in order.** *(i)* The ten coefficient functions $F$ written out at $d=3$, so a named support gives its $\theta$ in closed form. *(ii)* The (A)-positivity and Jacobi cuts applied to the 210. *(iii)* The surviving supports evaluated at the $n=12$ witness spectrum, which is where the screened $0.9243$ has to come from. **None of these needs a solver**; each is a linear solve in at most six unknowns once the support is named.

**Scope, stated so it is not overread.** This is a statement about $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$, the pinned bound, not about critical points: $\Pi(\lambda)$ contains tensors no frame realises, so a vertex need not be a real grading of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, it is a *candidate* grading, and Jacobi is what decides. That gap is the same one [LIT-spectra](LIT-spectra.md) records in the nilsoliton analogue, where Payne's linear system has a one-parameter family of nonnegative solutions and Jacobi selects a single point.
