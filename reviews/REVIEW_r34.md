**M4 CONFIRMED, and two of the four zeros are now proved rather than measured.** Both reviewers proved $L_e|_{\mathrm{Weyl}_k}=0$ for all $p,q$, by different routes: one via $\Phi(V)=2V$ on $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ where $\Phi(V)_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\{A_\alpha,A_\beta\}V)$, established by Schur plus an $n$-independence argument through zero-padding; the other by writing $e=\eta I_n+(A-\eta)I_p+(B-\eta)I_q$ and using $L_{I_n}\equiv0$ on $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ (Böhm–Wilking, $\mathrm{Ric}=0$) with a support argument for the rest. A separate simplification: the orbit's $\tfrac12$ needs no computation at all, since differentiating $Q(W_t)=\theta W_t$ along the orbit gives $e\circ\dot W=\tfrac12\dot W$ at **every** critical point.

**M6 REFUTED as a method, and it was redundant as well as unsound.** "Equal joint Casimir $\Rightarrow$ isomorphic" does not follow, and it fails inside the splits the brief cited as verification: at $(4,4)$ the $10$-dimensional $\mathrm{Weyl}_4$ Casimir eigenspace has $\dim\mathrm{Comm}_{SO(4)^2}=2$, being $\mathrm{Weyl}_4^+\oplus\mathrm{Weyl}_4^-$ (non-isomorphic with identical Casimir) and the $36$-dimensional $\Lambda^2\otimes\Lambda^2$ block has $\dim\mathrm{Comm}_{SO}=4$. So $(4,4)$ and $(4,6)$ verify nothing about $SO$-irreducibility. A clean counterexample outside the problem: $\Lambda^3(\mathbb R^9)$ and $S^2_0(\mathbb R^9)$ are non-isomorphic $\mathfrak{so}(9)$-modules sharing Casimir $18$, with further pairs at $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ and $\mathfrak{so}(7)$.

**M7's conclusion is now a theorem and its stated mechanism is REFUTED.** The claim that $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^3)\cong\mathbb R^3$ makes the orbit summand and $\Lambda^2\otimes\Lambda^2$ isomorphic, so that Schur permits mixing, is false: under $O(3)$, $\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^3)\cong\mathbb R^3\otimes\det\not\cong\mathbb R^3$. Measured on $E(\tfrac12)$ at $(3,3)$: $\dim\mathrm{Comm}_{SO(3)^2}=4$ but $\dim\mathrm{Comm}_{O(3)^2}=2$, with reflection character $0=3+(-3)$. $L_e$ is $O(p)\times O(q)$-equivariant to $2\times10^{-15}$, so Schur permits **no** mixing and each summand carries $\tfrac12$ separately. The $18$ is an **eigenvalue coincidence**, not representation mixing. The conclusion survives and is now proved twice: $\tfrac12-\mu=\frac{(p-2)(q-2)-1}{2(p-1)(q-1)}$, so $\mu=\tfrac12$ iff $p=q=3$; and a symbolic solve over all $45$ summand pairs finds that coincidence to be the only one with $p,q\ge3$, over all $p,q$ rather than a tested range.

**M1: the decomposition is quoted correctly but attributed wrongly, and the misattribution hides where the argument is soft.** The sentence carrying "$O(k)\times O(l)$ for any $k,l\ge3$" is the unnumbered summary display *after* Proposition 4.6; Proposition 4.6 itself decomposes $S^2(\mathfrak{so}(n))$ and carries "$k,l\ge5$ **and $k,l\ne6$**". What matters is that **every split with $p\in\{3,4\}$, including $(3,3)$, rests on Remark 4.7(ii)**, one asserted sentence: Appendix I proves only $X_k$ under $SO(k)$ for $k\ge5$.

**M8, reconciled.** The two verdicts differ in wording and agree in substance, because they answer different questions. The logic *is* upper-bound-shaped, Schur makes $L_e$ a scalar on each summand, so each summand's value is the eigenvalue rather than a lower bound, and no lower-bound step does hidden work. But one input is asserted rather than proved in the source, precisely at $k,l\le4$. So: **the upper bound is genuine, and the result is conditional on Nienhaus's $O(k)\times O(l)$ irreducibility at $k,l\ge3$, which has not been re-derived here.**
