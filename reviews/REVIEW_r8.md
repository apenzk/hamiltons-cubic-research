### Reviewer 8: the Bianchi-defect family and its ceiling

**Method.** Own implementation from the definitions, 4-tensor representation, $\#$ via $(R^\#)_{ijkl}=\sum_{pq}(R_{ipkq}R_{jplq}-R_{iplq}R_{jpkq})$, own Bianchi/Ricci/Weyl projections, own Lie-algebra and symmetric-space constructions; nothing imported from [scripts/](scripts/) or the reviewed agent's scratchpad. Validated before use: $\mathrm{Id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{Id}$ exactly, trilinear symmetry to $10^{-10}$, $\mathrm{Alt}$ idempotent and self-adjoint, $\theta_0(n)$ exact at $n=6,8,10,12$, and $\dim\mathcal W_n$ matching theory. Critically, **Reviewer 6's identity $\operatorname{scal}(R^2+R^\#)=\|\mathrm{Ric}\|^2+6\|\mathrm{Alt}\|^2$ was reproduced at zero residual for $n=4$–$7$**, which pins this reviewer's $\mathrm{Alt}$ normalization to Reviewer 6's and makes the two audits directly comparable.

**1. The counterexample machine, CONFIRMED, with two corrections to its statement.** Both halves are sound, and both were checked rather than assumed: $g\in\mathrm{Stab}(W)$ commutes with $W$, hence preserves eigenspaces, hence commutes with $P_\pm$, so $W_-$ is $\mathrm{Stab}$-invariant and $\mathrm{Alt}$ is equivariant; and $Q$ genuinely maps $\mathcal W_n\to\mathcal W_n$ (verified: $\|\mathrm{Ric}(Q(W))\|,\|\mathrm{Alt}(Q(W))\|\le10^{-14}$ on random Weyl tensors), so $\mathcal W_n^K$ is $Q$-stable and $\dim=1$ forces $Q(W)=\lambda W$. **Corrections:** (a) *irreducibility of $K$ is not needed*, the working hypotheses are exactly $(\Lambda^4)^K=0$ and $\dim\mathcal W_n^K=1$; (b) *$K$ must be the full, possibly disconnected, isotropy group*, for $SU(4)/SO(4)$, $\dim\mathcal W_9^{\mathfrak{so}(4)}=2$ at the Lie-algebra level, and only adjoining $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1)\in O(4)\setminus SO(4)$ cuts it to $1$. As a control, the machine correctly predicts its own failure at $\mathbb{CP}^2$, where $\dim(\Lambda^4)^K=1$.

**2. The family, CONFIRMED, and reduced to a closed form.** All six reported values reproduce exactly; every member is Bianchi and Ricci-free to $10^{-15}$, critical to $\le1.2\times10^{-15}$, defect $\le4.8\times10^{-16}$. The audit then explained them: each has **exactly two eigenvalues**, $W\propto\Pi_{\mathfrak h}-\tfrac rN\mathrm{Id}$ with $r=\dim\mathfrak h$, $N=\binom n2$, giving $Q(W)=\tfrac{2r}nW$ and $P/\|W\|^3=\tfrac2n\sqrt{rN/(N-r)}$. Matched to $10^{-15}$ on eight cases, then confirmed on two **held-out** spaces never used in the fit: $SU(5)/SO(5)$ ($n=14$, $0.478828671$) and $SU(2)^3$ ($n=9$, $0.769800359$, which is exactly the 3-block sphere product, tying the new family back to the existing catalogue).

**3. Non-diagonalizability, CONFIRMED, stronger than claimed, with the stated hypothesis corrected.** Testing $\min_{a,b}\mathrm{dist}(a\wedge b,E_+)^2/\|a\wedge b\|^2$ over 400 BFGS restarts shows $E_+$ contains **no decomposable bivector at all** (min $0.2$–$0.5$), stronger than "not spanned by decomposables"; the predicted exception $SU(2)^2$ passes at $5.5\times10^{-18}$. The root-system step is correct and was re-derived. **But the stated hypothesis "$\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak g\ge2$" is wrong**, the argument needs $\mathfrak g$ *simple* (connected Dynkin diagram), and relatedly "the lone exception is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$" misdescribes $S^3\times S^3$, whose algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (rank 2, min $\mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{ad}_Z=2$). Further, **the proof covers only the Lie-group rows**: for symmetric-space rows $E_+$ is $\mathfrak h|_{\mathfrak m}$ rather than an adjoint image, so roots are the wrong tool (Wu has $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{so}(3)$, rank 1); the conclusion holds numerically there but is not proven by the stated route.

**4. Zero-padding, CONFIRMED, with a mechanism and a wording ambiguity surfaced.** Padding preserves criticality because $\#$ is block-local ($[\Lambda^2\mathbb R^k,\Lambda^2\mathbb R^k]\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^k$ and $W$ annihilates the rest, so cross-blocks vanish). Padded $SU(3)$ at $n=12$: residual $5.2\times10^{-16}$, $P=0.8366600265$ unchanged, defect $3.6\times10^{-16}$, still non-diagonal. **Ambiguity surfaced:** building $\mathbb{CP}^2$'s *Weyl operator* first and then padding gives a critical point at $n=5,6,8,12$ with $P=\sqrt{3/2}$ and spectrum $2:{-}1:{-}1$, apparently contradicting item 4 of "What the proof attempts established", which says the padded $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is idempotent only at $n=4,5$. There is no mathematical contradiction: the two differ in order of operations. Pad-then-project (the original computation) and project-then-pad (this audit) are different objects, and the latter is the Ricci-flat one, i.e. exactly $\mathcal S^4(\alpha)$. Item 4's phrasing should be disambiguated.

**5. Codimension, CONFIRMED.** Rank of $dF$, $F(W)=\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)$, by central differences over a full basis: $15,35,70$ at $n=6,7,8$, exactly $\binom n4$. At balanced sphere products the rank drops to $9$ and $36$, exactly $\binom p2\binom q2$, confirming the singularity claim.

**The question that mattered, is $0.837$ a ceiling or a sample maximum? A ceiling, and structurally so.** The compact symmetric spaces with $b_4=0$ are exactly the group manifolds, $SU(k)/SO(k)$, $SU(2k)/Sp(k)$, $E_6/F_4$ and spheres, so the closed form gives a **complete enumeration** over two-eigenvalue members; the maximum is $1.0541$ ($S^3\times S^3$, diagonal) and $0.8367$ ($SU(3)$, non-diagonal). Inverting the formula, $P\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ requires $\dim\mathfrak h\ge\tfrac{3n}{7n-4}\binom n2\approx0.44\binom n2$; the only subalgebras of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ that large are $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ itself (Weyl $=0$) and $\mathfrak u(n/2)$, i.e. $\mathbb{CP}^{n/2}$, precisely where the invariant $4$-form $\omega\wedge\omega$ appears. **[CORRECTED, see [findings/graded-half.md](findings/graded-half.md).] The inequality is right and was later re-derived in general as the Dimension Lemma; the enumeration is incomplete. $\mathfrak{sp}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(2)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$, the isotropy of $\mathbb{HP}^2$, also exceeds the bound at $\dim 13>12.92$, and is neither $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ nor $\mathfrak u(4)$.** **So $b_4=0$ and $P\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ are in direct competition: large isotropy is what makes $P$ large, and large isotropy inside $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ forces a unitary factor, hence nonzero defect.** A search outside the $\dim\mathcal W_n^K=1$ hypothesis (2-dimensional invariant families, $n=11$–$16$, every critical ray enumerated) found exactly three rays per family, sphere product on top, a low diagonal one, and the padded Lie-group tensor pinned at $0.836660$, with no new non-diagonal branch.

**Overall verdict.** **No error found in the substance**; all five claims CONFIRMED, three with precision corrections to their *statements*. The audit's own contribution is the closed form, which converts the ceiling from an observation into a computation with a structural explanation. **Confidence:** high for claims 1, 2, 4, 5, for the closed form (held-out predictions to 9 digits), and for non-diagonalizability. Moderate-to-high on the ceiling itself, the enumeration is complete for two-eigenvalue members, but non-symmetric isotropy-irreducible spaces (Wolf's list) can carry more eigenvalues and were not swept; that is the one gap the reviewer flagged as worth closing next.

**Addendum, a third held-out confirmation, at the threshold-relevant dimension.** The final search family, $SU(3)\times SU(3)$ at $n=16$ with $\dim\mathcal W_{16}^K=3$, yields **seven** critical rays, all with defect $\le3.6\times10^{-16}$: $1.280869=\theta_0(16)$ (diagonalizable, the balanced sphere product), $0.836660$ twice (**not** diagonalizable, padded $SU(3)$ into either factor), $0.700467$ twice (diagonalizable), $0.591608$ (**not**), and $0.537086$ (**not**, the equal-scaling $SU(3)^2$ bi-invariant). That last value matches the closed form $\tfrac2{16}\sqrt{16\cdot120/104}=0.5370861555$ to ten digits, a third held-out confirmation on a space never used in the fit. Every non-diagonal branch again sits at or below $0.836660$, with the sphere product alone above it, so the ceiling survives a family with a three-dimensional invariant space, not merely the $\dim=1$ case the generator was stated for.
