## Batch 2, the problem as Hamilton's ODE, and a dimension coincidence run to ground

**The lead.** *Brendle's Ricci flow with surgery on PIC manifolds holds for $n\ge12$ ([references/brendle-2019-pic-surgery](../references/brendle-2019-pic-surgery/)), Chen lowered it to $n\ge9$ in four pages ([references/pic-nine-2024](../references/pic-nine-2024/)), and Cho reached $n=7,8$ ([references/cho-2026-pic-cones-7-8](../references/cho-2026-pic-cones-7-8/)).* **Those are this programme's thresholds, $12$ and the crossover $6+2\sqrt7$, to the integer.** *And $P(W)=\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$ is half the rate of change of $\lvert R\rvert^2$ along $R'=Q(R)$, so its maximum on the Weyl sphere is exactly the kind of constant a pinching family has to beat.* All four sources fetched, extracted and read at the statements.

**The outcome, and it is a fence post.** *The $n\ge12$ is a RICCI-side constraint and the Weyl tensor never enters it.* **Brendle states the transformation $\ell_{a,b}$ rescales scalar curvature by $1+2(n-1)a$ and trace-free Ricci by $1+(n-2)b$ "and the Weyl tensor is unchanged"**, so the whole pinching machinery is blind to the Weyl part by construction. *His Lemma 3.3, the one place $n\ge12$ is used, is the elementary polynomial inequality $2+(n-8)b-2(n+2)(n-2)b^2-n(n-2)^2b^3>0$ together with a bound on the auxiliary $\omega$, both in the cone parameters alone.* **Chen says where the threshold lives in one sentence: "the restriction $n\ge12$ appears in Step 2", joining two families of cones toward PIC1, and his whole improvement is sharper estimates of $\tfrac{d}{dt}(\mathrm{Ric}_{11}+\mathrm{Ric}_{22})$ and $\tfrac{d}{dt}(\mathrm{Ric}_{11}-\mathrm{Ric}_{22})$ using $\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathop{\mathrm{scal}}(S)=P\lvert\mathrm{Ric}\rvert^2+Q\mathop{\mathrm{scal}}^2$.** *Cho confirms at $n=7,8$: "the Weyl component is not estimated separately", and the proof uses only the positivity of one coefficient in the cone parameters.* **So the PIC dimension thresholds are Ricci bookkeeping and say nothing about the cubic Weyl constant. Recorded so nobody chases the coincidence again.**

**What the ODE framing does leave standing.** *Wilking's Lie-algebraic form $\langle R^\#x,y\rangle=-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{ad}_xR\,\mathrm{ad}_yR)$ is already filed at [references/wilking-lie-algebraic-cones](../references/wilking-lie-algebraic-cones/) and already opened the target this record calls fence post 33, a bound on $\lvert\langle M^\#,M\rangle\rvert$ through the $\mathrm{ad}$-form; that node was swept on 2026-08-31 and is not reopened here.* **The one structural fact worth carrying is negative and sharp: every pinching-cone argument in the literature is built to be Weyl-blind, so the constant this programme wants has no reason to have been computed anywhere in that literature.**

## Batch 5, Petersen–Stanfield read at the theorems, and the record's open class closes in closed form

**Theorem A.** *Every compact symmetric space has curvature-operator eigenvalues in $[0,\tfrac12]$; an irreducible one has one, two or three distinct nonzero eigenvalues, all rational, and only the complex Grassmannians reach three.* **Theorem B.** *The largest eigenvalue is $\tfrac12$ exactly when the space is Hermitian symmetric, the eigenvector being the Kähler form.* **Theorem C.** *If $M=G/H$ with $H$ simple, the curvature operator has ONE nonzero eigenvalue, $\lambda=\tfrac14\dim M/\dim H$; either $G=H\times H$ (type II) or $G$ is simple with $b_2=b_3=b_4=0$.* Section 3.3 lists the simple-isotropy spaces: the type II groups, $SU(m)/SO(m)$, $SU(2m)/Sp(m)$, $E_6/F_4$, $E_6/Sp(4)$, $E_7/SU(8)$, $E_8/Spin(16)$ and $F_4/Spin(9)=\mathbb{OP}^2$, with $\dim M$ and $\lambda$ tabulated.

**CLASSIFICATION AGAINST THE RECORD, DONE BEFORE ANYTHING WAS WRITTEN: AGREEMENT, WITH ONE INGREDIENT SUPPLIED.** *[L4](../findings/ungraded-slack.md)'s Statement already carries the whole inequality*: for a subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ with $r=\dim\mathfrak h$ and $W_{\mathfrak h}$ the unit Casimir $\Pi_{\mathfrak h}-\tfrac rN\mathrm{Id}$, criticality gives $\theta=\tfrac2n\sqrt{rN/(N-r)}$, hence $r\le n(n-2)/4$ and $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$ by monotonicity in $r$, with equality exactly at a symmetric pair. **The sweep first re-derived that formula from [L46](../findings/problem.md) with one eigenvalue and found it on the record by reading L4 in full, which is the rule filed as proposal 92 doing its work.** *What the record lacked, per [graded-half.md](../findings/graded-half.md), was the class of "non-Lie-group simple-isotropy members of L4's family, whose $(n,r)$ list is nowhere on the record".* **Theorem C is the statement that every such space IS a member**: one nonzero eigenvalue with eigenspace $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]=\mathfrak h$, so its Weyl part is L4's Casimir at $r=\dim H$ and, being locally symmetric Einstein, critical by L46. *The list is then Cartan's, and Section 3.3 tabulates it.*

**The screen, and it is a screen.** *$\theta^2=4rN/(n^2(N-r))$ checked against two recorded values it must reproduce: $\tfrac{55}{36}$ at the balanced product, $n=12$, and $\sqrt{10}/3=1.0540925$ at $SO(4)=S^3\times S^3$, both exact.* **Then evaluated on the simple-isotropy list**: every entry lies between $0.17$ and $0.90$, against $\sqrt{3/2}=1.2247$ and $\theta_0(n)$ from $1.15$ upward, and every entry has $r$ below $n(n-2)/4$ by a factor of at least $1.5$. *The three infinite families have $r/n^2\to0$, so only the five exceptional entries and small cases need evaluating, and $\mathbb{OP}^2$ at $0.896$ is the largest.*

**What this licenses, stated as the theorem it would produce.** *Every compact irreducible symmetric space with simple isotropy has $\theta<\sqrt{3/2}$, at every dimension, in closed form from two integers.* **With the record's existing closures of the Hermitian, Grassmannian, quaternionic, strongly-regular and multi-block cases, that completes the symmetric-space census: Conjecture A holds at every compact irreducible symmetric space.** *It reaches no non-symmetric $W$ and does not move the $3.05$ against $1.236$ bound, and it is the first complete theorem on the family where the maximisers live.*
