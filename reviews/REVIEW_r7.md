### Reviewer 7: the diagonal case at n=12,14 and Jäger's threshold

**Method.** Audited the claims made after reading Florian Jäger's 2016 Münster thesis (the source of the assumed diagonal case), in particular the load-bearing claim that the diagonal statement is *already true* at $n=12,14$, and that Jäger's $n\ge16$ is accumulated proof slack rather than a real phase transition. Wrote three from-scratch scripts ([adversarial_check7_reduction.py](scripts/pipelines/adversarial_check7_reduction.py), [adversarial_check7_diagmax.py](scripts/pipelines/adversarial_check7_diagmax.py), [adversarial_check7_jaeger_quartics.py](scripts/pipelines/adversarial_check7_jaeger_quartics.py)) with no import from the reviewed agent's code: own $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ pair-index bookkeeping, own constrained-subspace basis via SVD nullspace, own analytic gradient (checked against finite differences), and `sympy` plus `numpy.roots` independently for the algebra.

**1. The vertex-matrix reduction, CONFIRMED.** $(W^2)_{ij}=\Lambda_{ij}^2$, $(W^\#)_{ij}=(\Lambda^2)_{ij}$, Weyl $\iff$ zero row sums (checked in both directions, including a control matrix with nonzero row sums giving genuinely nonzero Ricci $=2.47$), and $\Theta(\Lambda)=P(W)/\|W\|^3$, all to $\le1.3\times10^{-15}$ at $n=5,6,7,8$ against `Q_op`. *Methodological note worth recording:* this reviewer's own first pass carried a real bug, computing $\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$ as a quadratic form rather than the Hilbert–Schmidt contraction, caught because the two routes disagreed outright rather than at noise level. The same class of index-contraction slip has now been caught in fresh reviewer code three times in this file; checking two routes against each other before trusting either is doing real work.

**2. The headline claim, CONFIRMED.** Global maximization of $\Theta$ over the constrained subspace, 800–1500 restarts per $n$: the maximum equals $\theta_0(n)$ at every even $n\in\{4,\dots,20\}$ and $\tilde\theta_0(n)$ at every odd $n\in\{5,\dots,15\}$, agreeing to $7\times10^{-16}$–$1.3\times10^{-15}$, **including $n=12$ and $n=14$**, reproducing the reviewed agent's figures exactly. Maximizer identity was verified by comparing sorted eigenvalue spectra (permutation- and scale-invariant) rather than by a row-permutation search, which broke on ties at balanced blocks.

**3. The critical-value catalogue, CONFIRMED, no unexplained attractor.** At $n=12$, 1500 restarts give exactly four clusters, matching $S^p\times S^{12-p}$ for $p=3,4,5,6$ to $\le4\times10^{-16}$; pushing to 6000 restarts still gives exactly four. Cross-validated with a structurally unrelated method, a gradient-free fixed-point/power iteration $\Lambda\mapsto\mathrm{Proj}(\Lambda^{\circ2}+\Lambda^2)$, which found the identical value set at $n=12$ and $n=14$ with basin fractions within ~2 points. (A third attempt with Nelder–Mead in 54 dimensions failed to converge; reported for honesty, excluded from the confirmation, and attributable to the method's known behavior at that dimensionality rather than to the problem.)

**4. The quartics producing $n\ge16$, CONFIRMED, with one honest nuance.** Both polynomials were located verbatim in the thesis text (grepped, not taken on trust), with real roots $14.811308$ and $14.962808$ (sympy and numpy agreeing to $10^{-15}$), positive at $n=12,13,14$ and negative at $n=15,16,17$. The thesis's own inline checkpoints reproduce exactly ($g(15)=-25497$, $h(15)=-59616$, and both concavity claims). **Nuance disclosed rather than glossed:** Jäger's Assumption 1 already stipulates $n\ge16$ as a standing hypothesis for the whole chapter, so while §3.5's argument genuinely and independently requires $n\ge15$, this review did not verify that no *other* lemma in Chapter 3 imposes a stronger bound. So these polynomials are demonstrably *a* binding constraint and very likely *the* operative one (15 rounding up to 16 among even integers matches Theorem A exactly) but uniqueness was not proven.

**5. The quantified slack, CONFIRMED.** Reconstructed the thesis's own dominant-term split (verified to re-expand to $g,h$ exactly under `sympy`) and solved for the strengthening factor needed on that term alone: $1.0682$/$1.0717$ to reach $n=14$, $1.2838$/$1.2876$ to reach $n=12$, matching the reviewed figures to within $0.0005$ and confirming the "nearly identical factors between the two cases" characterization that motivates the single-shared-bottleneck inference. A robustness check under the naive alternative reading of "dominant term" (bare leading monomial) gives visibly less consistent factors, supporting the compound-term reading as the correct, non-arbitrary one.

**Overall verdict.** All five claims **CONFIRMED** by independent code, with the most load-bearing one (the critical-value catalogue) confirmed twice by structurally different search algorithms. **No error found.** The conclusion this supports is consequential for the project: Jäger's $n\ge16$ is proof-technical slack, not a phase transition, and the diagonal Böhm–Wilking statement is numerically true already at $n=12,14$, the very dimensions the conjecture's threshold turns on, and precisely where the published proof does not reach.
