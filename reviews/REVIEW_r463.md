## L32, a bivector's centraliser collapses to a line, **REFUTED as stated ($m=3$); CONFIRMED for $m\ge4$**

**Where.** [findings/isotropy-cap.md](../findings/isotropy-cap.md) line 64; canon at [results.md](../results.md) line 430 and summary line 150.

**1. Restated with hypotheses.** Let $x\in\mathfrak{so}(m)$, $x\ne0$, and $\mathfrak c=\mathfrak z(x)\cap x^\perp$ (centraliser and orthogonal complement both inside $\mathfrak{so}(m)$, with the ad-invariant form). Claim: $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)\cap\mathfrak c^\perp=\mathbb Rx$, **except** when $x$ is a multiple of a complex structure on a $4$-dimensional subspace, where it is a $3$-dimensional $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. **The only hypothesis is "$x\ne0$", there is no lower bound on $m$.** That is the defect.

**2. Heading vs content.** Yes, the block states and argues the claim. Statement, proof and a scope note are all present.

**3. Rebuilt from the definitions.** The easy inclusion is unconditional: $x$ is central in $\mathfrak z(x)$ so $[x,\mathfrak c]=0$, and $x\perp\mathfrak c$, hence $\mathbb Rx\subseteq\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)\cap\mathfrak c^\perp$ always. The content is the reverse inclusion, and the block's chain is sound where it applies: $\mathfrak t\subseteq\mathfrak z(x)$ gives $\mathfrak c\supseteq\mathfrak t\cap x^\perp=a^\perp$, hence $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)\subseteq\mathfrak z(a^\perp)=\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus\{\mathfrak g_\beta:\beta\propto a\}$, and $\mathfrak c\supseteq a^\perp$ gives $\mathfrak c^\perp\subseteq(a^\perp)^\perp$, so the intersection sits inside $\mathfrak t\cap(a^\perp)^\perp=\mathbb Rx$. I re-derived each step; all four are correct.

**Hand checks, each built from the definition of $\mathfrak z$ rather than from the lemma.**
- $m=4$, $a=(a_1,a_2)$ distinct: $\mathfrak z(x)=\mathfrak t=\langle e_{12},e_{34}\rangle$, $\mathfrak c=\mathbb R(a_2e_{12}-a_1e_{34})$; a root $\pm e_1\pm e_2$ annihilates $(a_2,-a_1)$ only if $a_1=\pm a_2$, so $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)=\mathfrak t$ and the answer is $\mathbb Rx$ ✓.
- $m=4$, $x=a(e_{12}+e_{34})$ (the exception): $\mathfrak c=\mathbb R(e_{12}-e_{34})$, killed by the root $e_1+e_2$, so $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)=\mathfrak t\oplus\mathfrak g_{\pm(e_1+e_2)}$ (dim $4$) and the intersection with $\mathfrak c^\perp$ is $\mathfrak{su}(2)_+$, dim $3$ ✓.
- $m=6$, $x$ a rank-$4$ complex structure padded: $\mathfrak z(x)=\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2)_{56}$, $\mathfrak c=\mathfrak{su}(2)_-\oplus\mathbb Re_{56}$, $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)=\mathfrak{su}(2)_+\oplus\mathbb Re_{56}$, answer $\mathfrak{su}(2)_+$, dim $3$ ✓, reproduces the scope note's "returns $3$ every time".
- $m=5$ and $m=6$, $x$ of rank $2$ ($x=ae_{12}$): $\mathfrak c$ contains $\mathfrak{so}(\ker x)=\mathfrak{so}(3)$ resp. $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, whose centraliser in $\mathfrak{so}(m)$ is $\mathfrak{so}(2)_{12}=\mathbb Rx$ by Schur, answer $\mathbb Rx$ ✓. The degenerate clause is doing real work here, and it is right.

**4. THE COUNTEREXAMPLE, $m=3$.** Take $x=e_{12}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)$, nonzero, so the hypothesis holds. Then $\mathrm{ad}(x)$ on $\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong(\mathbb R^3,\times)$ has kernel exactly $\mathbb Rx$, so $\mathfrak z(x)=\mathbb Rx$ and
$$\mathfrak c=\mathfrak z(x)\cap x^\perp=\mathbb Rx\cap x^\perp=\{0\}.$$
Therefore $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)=\mathfrak z(\{0\})=\mathfrak{so}(3)$ and $\mathfrak c^\perp=\mathfrak{so}(3)$, giving
$$\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)\cap\mathfrak c^\perp=\mathfrak{so}(3),\qquad \dim=3\ne1 .$$
And $x$ is **not** a multiple of a complex structure on a $4$-dimensional subspace (there is no $4$-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb R^3$) so the stated exception does not cover it. The conclusion is false as written.

*Why the proof misses it, exactly.* The proof splits on $p\ge3$ and $p=2$ and **never treats $p=1$**. At $m=3$ the effective rank is $p=1$: $\mathfrak t\cap x^\perp=a^\perp=\{0\}$, so the inclusion $\mathfrak c\supseteq a^\perp$ is vacuous and the root argument has nothing to bite on. The degenerate clause does not rescue it either: $\ker x$ is $1$-dimensional and $\mathfrak{so}(1)=0$, so "additionally centralising $\mathfrak{so}(\ker x)$" is also vacuous. $m=3$ is precisely the configuration where both of the proof's two levers are empty.
*The near-miss that hides it.* The answer at $m=3$ **is** a $3$-dimensional $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ ($\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)$) so the failure agrees with the exception's dimension and isomorphism type while contradicting its description. A dimension-only check of the exceptional set would pass at $m=3$.

*$m=2$ is fine by accident:* $\mathfrak c=\{0\}$ there too, but $\mathfrak z(\{0\})=\mathfrak{so}(2)=\mathbb Rx$ ✓. And $m=1$ is vacuous.

**Completeness of the exceptional set for $m\ge4$.** The reverse inclusion fails only when some root $\beta\propto a$ (the full singular-value vector, zeros included). $D$-type roots $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ force $a$ to have exactly two nonzero entries of equal magnitude, a rank-$4$ complex structure, the stated exception. Short roots $\pm e_i$ (odd $m$, type $B_p$) force $a$ to have exactly one nonzero entry ($x$ of rank $2$) but there $\mathfrak c\supseteq\mathfrak{so}(\ker x)$ with $\dim\ker x=m-2\ge3$, whose centraliser is already $\mathbb Rx$, so the case does not survive. At $m=3$ that last escape closes ($\dim\ker x=1$) and the counterexample appears. So for $m\ge4$ the exceptional set is exactly as stated, and I could construct no second exception.

**5. Norm.** The lemma is normalisation-free: $\mathfrak z$, $\perp$ and $\mathbb Rx$ are all scale-invariant in $x$, and any positive multiple of the ad-invariant form gives the same $\perp$. **No convention trap here**, the factor-$2$ discrepancy between the $\Lambda^2$ and $n\times n$ pairings ([conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md)) rescales the form and leaves every orthogonal complement fixed. The scope note's "rank $4$ with equal singular values" is stated in the singular-value language, consistent with conventions.md's "singular values in pairs $s_1,s_1,s_2,s_2,\dots$".

**6. What cites it, and whether within hypotheses.**
- [findings/stratum-two-negative.md](../findings/stratum-two-negative.md) line 153, the proof of **L33**, the only substantive use. It applies L32 to the minority eigenvector $x$ of a $W\in\mathrm{Weyl}_m$ and concludes $y_i\in\mathbb Rx$, then $y_i\perp x\Rightarrow y_i=0$. **Within hypotheses**: $\mathrm{Weyl}_m=0$ for $m\le3$, so $m\ge4$ throughout, and L33's statement carries the complex-structure exception explicitly ("If that eigenvector is not a multiple of a complex structure on a $4$-dimensional subspace"). The $m=3$ defect does not reach it.
- [results.md](../results.md) lines 150 and 430, restate the same over-broad quantifier, so **the canon carries the defect too**. The summary at line 150 additionally compresses the exception to "a complex structure on a $4$-plane, where it is an $\mathfrak{su}(2)$", dropping the dimension $3$; harmless but looser.
- **The defect propagates one step sideways.** [Lemma L81](../findings/isotropy-cap.md) (line 223) states the sharp criterion as "$\dim(\mathfrak z(\mathfrak c)\cap\mathfrak c^\perp)>1$ **iff** the singular-value vector is proportional to a root of $D_p$". At $m=3$, $p=1$ and $D_1$ has no roots, yet the dimension is $3$, so L81's "iff" is false at $m=3$ by the same counterexample. L81 is outside my seven, but the repair is the same one hypothesis.

**Verdict: REFUTED as stated; CONFIRMED for $m\ge4$.** The hypothesis it actually needs is **$m\ge4$** (equivalently: $\mathfrak c\ne\{0\}$, equivalently $x$ has effective rank $p\ge2$ or $\dim\ker x\ge2$). The proof is otherwise correct and its degenerate clause is genuinely load-bearing. The repair is one clause in the statement, in [isotropy-cap.md](../findings/isotropy-cap.md), [results.md](../results.md) lines 150 and 430, and [Lemma L81](../findings/isotropy-cap.md); no downstream result changes.

## L48, the reducible case, and the symmetric-space theorem, **PLAUSIBLE** (lemma correct; the Theorem's quantifier is too wide and its equality case is wrong)

**2. Heading vs content.** Yes, and more than the heading promises, the block also carries the Theorem.

Setting $t^2=t^{*2}=15/32$ gives $96\varepsilon=15-45\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon=15/141$, $\alpha=\tfrac13-\varepsilon=\tfrac{47-15}{141}=\mathbf{32/141}=0.2269504$ ✓.
- **The overlap arithmetic is consistent.** Nienhaus's branch runs to $0.234970$ at $n=100$; the frontier starts at $0.226950$; margin $0.008$, tending to the stated $+0.0074$.

**4. The quantifier, which is where it fails, four separate ways, each fatal to "for every $n\ge12$".**
- **(i) Four unproven vanishings, not one.** The block's warning box names [Observation L102](../findings/route-a.md) alone. **Line 370 of the same file is worse than the box:** *"The four vanishings the bound rests on are exhaustively verified numerics, not proofs, and proving them is blocked. - **(ii) The $n$-extension is a lower bound pointing the wrong way.** "$n=20,24$ are checked by zero-padding, which gives monotonicity in $n$ **as a lower bound**." - **(iii) The overlap needs a monotonicity nobody has.** Coverage requires $\alpha_N(n)\ge32/141$ for every $n\ge12$. Three sample points ($0.322271,\,0.290168,\,0.234970$ at $n=12,16,100$) and a limit with margin $+0.0074$ establish it only if $\alpha_N$ is monotone decreasing, and no closed form or monotonicity proof for $\alpha_N(n)$ is given. **A $+0.0074$ margin on an unproven monotone descent is not a covering statement.**

And the block records that the failure margin *grows with $c_n$*, the ratio $\gamma_{\max}/\text{requirement}$ reaching $0.825$ at $n=12$ and extrapolating to $\approx0.93$, so the conditional is load-bearing rather than cosmetic: a true constant even modestly above $\theta_0(n)$ breaks the estimate.
