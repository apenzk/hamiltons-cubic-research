### Reviewers 54 and 55: the $C_5$ blow-up, and why a corrector cannot see past it

**They agree on every verdict, and both replaced the cycle's evidence with a third-order argument.** Own implementations in twenty and fourteen scripts respectively. Reports in [review_r54_report.md](agent_logs/review_r54_report.md) and [review_r55_report.md](agent_logs/review_r55_report.md).

**K1 and K2 CONFIRMED, made exact.** $\theta^2=9/20$ in `Fraction` arithmetic, $\theta_0(10)=6/5$, ratio $\sqrt5/4$; orbit $40\subset E(\tfrac12)$ with all forty principal cosines $1.000000000$, $\dim E(\tfrac12)=60$, $\lambda_{\max}=1.206011$. A fact the record missed: **$W$ has no zero eigenvalue on $\Lambda^2$** ($-0.149$ twenty times, $+0.0745$ twenty times, $+0.298$ five times), so $\dim\mathfrak m=20$ is stable and $S$ is locally continuous here, unlike padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$.

**K3: the conclusion holds and the evidence for it was refuted, with a structural reason why the test was blind.** The corrector sweep is close to vacuous. It walks back to the base point, median displacement $0.020$ after pushes of $0.4$, and the recorded bound $|dc|\le0.046$ is wrong, the maximum being $0.1268$. **$S>0$ critical points do sit nearby**: a shell of eight, residuals $4\times10^{-15}$ to $10^{-13}$, $\theta\in[0.669762,0.671452]$, within $0.07\%$ to $0.16\%$, and $S\in[0.041,0.102]$, at orbit distance $0.1026$ to $0.2226$. "Same $\theta$" is not a same-point test either: pushes from outside $E(\tfrac12)$ return $\theta=0.670820$ with a **different** $\Lambda^2$ spectrum.

**Why the method could not have worked.** The symmetric three-tensor $\langle F_i,F_j\circ F_k\rangle$ vanishes **identically** on $E(\tfrac12)$ at this point, $3.9\times10^{-17}$, confirmed by a second route at $1.7\times10^{-15}$, against $6.6\times10^{-2}$ at $(3,3)$ and $2.2\times10^{-2}$ at $(2,2,4)$. So every $E(\tfrac12)$ direction passes the second-order test automatically and a corrector started at $W+tv$ begins with residual $O(t^3)$ **whatever is nearby**: the $59/60$ return measures the base point's attractiveness, not the absence of a neighbour.

**What settles it is third order.** With $\Psi(v)=\Pi_{E(1/2)}\big(v\circ(2L_e-1)^+(v\circ v)\big)$: $\Psi$ vanishes on the whole forty-dimensional orbit subspace and satisfies $\Psi(o+x)=\Psi(x)$ for every orbit direction $o$ (worst $5.6\times10^{-13}$ over 300 pairs), so the cone is sixty homogeneous cubics in twenty unknowns. Minimising over the excess unit sphere with an analytic Jacobian gives $9.03\times10^{-4}$ from 4000 starts and $6.06\times10^{-4}$ from an independent implementation, with **zero starts below $10^{-6}$** in either; 12000 uniform samples give a minimum of $0.219$. Since the tangent cone is invariant under translation by the orbit tangent, a nonvanishing $\Psi$ on the excess sphere forces **tangent cone $=$ orbit tangent**, so $\mathrm{Crit}(P)$ near the point is exactly the orbit. Cross-validated two ways: a Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction over 709 complementary dimensions, and a gauge-frozen corrector whose residual floor along $v$ is $2\|\Psi(v)\|t^3$ to three digits across five directions.

**K4 closed in closed form.** For the equal-block $C_5$ blow-up at $n=5m$, $\theta/\theta_0=\tfrac12\sqrt{10(m-1)/(5m-2)}$, strictly increasing, **supremum $1/\sqrt2$, never attained**, reproducing $0.5590$, $0.6202$, $0.6455$ exactly. More generally the $n\to\infty$ vertex system collapses to $K^2=\tau K$, giving $\theta/\theta_0\to1/\sqrt r$, and $r=1$ forces a complete-bipartite negative graph, which is the two-block sphere product. Larger triangle-free graphs do **worse**: $1/2$ for Petersen, $1/\sqrt5$ for Clebsch, about $0.21$ for Higman–Sims; $C_7$, $C_9$, $K(7,3)$ and $K(9,4)$ admit no $S=0$ blow-up at all. The question must be stratified, since unrestricted every two-block sphere product has ratio $\to1$. **And independently of all of it: every blow-up is coordinate-diagonal**, so the granted diagonal case already caps the class and item F's falsifier cannot fire.

**K5: the trap is real and the filter is wrong.** The coarse-partition artefacts reproduce, $0.988212$, $0.993073$ and exactly $1.000000$, each verified as the two-block sphere product to $2.8\times10^{-17}$, and at every size vector tested **none of the top six** is genuinely five-block. But the "all rows distinct" filter has two holes: at sizes $(2,2,1,1,1)$ the top solution has five distinct rows, $S=0$ and ratio $0.989949$ while being $S^3\times S^4$, because a **singleton block has no diagonal entry to compare**; a second at $0.952579$ passes and is padded $S^3\times S^3$. The right test is a merge test together with a null-block check.

**Two corrections found off-brief.** The recorded $0.5960$ at $(2,2,2,3,3)$ has $S=54$, not $S=0$; the $S=0$ maximum there is $0.580508$. More seriously, [plan.md](plan.md)'s retired route **T1 rests on a false statement**: "$A_c(\tfrac12)A_c(\tfrac12)\subseteq A_c(\tfrac12)^\perp$ holds at the ungraded points too" is **false** at $(S^3)^3$ (relative $0.335$) and at padded $S^3\times S^3$ (relative $0.259$), measured two independent ways. The fusion rule is a property of particular points rather than a general fact, and at the $C_5$ blow-up it is precisely what makes the corrector blind.
