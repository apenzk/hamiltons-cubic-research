## M1, the character argument, attacked step by step

**The trivial character space really is exactly the diagonal entries.** $\Gamma=\{\operatorname{diag}(\pm1)\}$ acts on $\Lambda^2$ by $e_{ab}\mapsto\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_be_{ab}$, so identifying characters of $(\mathbb Z/2)^n$ with subsets, $e_{ab}$ carries $\chi_{\{a,b\}}$ and $e_{ab}\odot e_{cd}$ carries $\chi_{\{a,b\}}\chi_{\{c,d\}}=\chi_{\{a,b\}\triangle\{c,d\}}$. A symmetric difference is empty iff the two pairs coincide, so the trivial isotypic space of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ is spanned by the $\binom n2$ elements $e_{ab}\odot e_{ab}$ and nothing else. Distinct pairs give distinct characters, so no accidental extra trivial component can hide in an off-diagonal entry. The basis $\{e_\alpha\odot e_\beta\}$ is orthogonal for the operator pairing, so the trivial-isotypic projection of any symmetric operator is exactly its diagonal part, and $R=W-\operatorname{diag}(M)$ has zero trivial component by construction. **This step is right.**

**The equivariance step is right, and the reason is weaker than "equivariance" makes it sound.** The statement in use is the bilinear one: $\gamma\cdot(A\circ B)=(\gamma\cdot A)\circ(\gamma\cdot B)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma\subset O(n)$, acting on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ by conjugation with $\Lambda^2\gamma$. This follows from $O(n)$-equivariance of $Q$ by polarising $X\circ Y=\tfrac12(Q(X+Y)-Q(X)-Q(Y))$. What makes the character conclusion immediate is that $\Gamma$ acts on each character space **by a scalar**: if $\gamma\cdot R_\chi=\chi(\gamma)R_\chi$ and $\gamma\cdot X=\chi_X(\gamma)X$ then $\gamma\cdot(R_\chi\circ X)=\chi(\gamma)\chi_X(\gamma)\,(R_\chi\circ X)$, so $R_\chi\circ X$ lies in the $\chi\chi_X$ character space on the nose, no isotypic decomposition of the target is needed. **Right.**

**The full sign group is required and is available.** On $\Gamma\cap SO(n)$ the argument collapses at $n\le8$ exactly as [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md)'s warning says, since $\chi_S=\chi_{S^{\mathsf c}}$ there and a $4$-set character can equal the trivial one ($n=4$) or a pair character ($n=6$). I12 uses the full $\Gamma$, and $\circ$ is $O(n)$-equivariant rather than merely $SO(n)$-equivariant because $\#$ is built from the structure constants of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, which are $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant under every $g\in O(n)$. **Right.** A second potential fusion does not occur either: $-\mathrm{Id}\in\Gamma$ acts trivially on $\Lambda^2$, so the effective group is $\Gamma/\{\pm\mathrm{Id}\}$, but the characters that occur are the even-subset ones, which are precisely those trivial on $-\mathrm{Id}$, and they stay pairwise distinct in the quotient.

**The isotypic spaces are orthogonal for the inner product actually in use.** The pairing is $\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ up to a fixed constant, invariant under conjugation by any orthogonal map of $\Lambda^2$, and $\Lambda^2\gamma$ is orthogonal. Given $\chi\ne\chi'$, pick $\gamma$ with $\chi(\gamma)=1$, $\chi'(\gamma)=-1$; then $\langle u,v\rangle=\langle\gamma u,\gamma v\rangle=-\langle u,v\rangle$. **Right**, and it needs nothing about $\mathcal W_n$.

**$\chi\chi_X=\chi_X$ forces $\chi$ trivial.** The characters form a group (the dual of $(\mathbb Z/2)^n$) so cancellation is available and there is no nontrivial stabiliser. The concrete form: $S\triangle T=T$ iff $S=\varnothing$. **Right.**

**$X$ in a single character space is a genuine hypothesis, not automatic.** For a general $X\in\mathcal W_n$ the cross terms $\langle X_{\chi_1},R_\chi\circ X_{\chi_2}\rangle$ with $\chi_1=\chi\chi_2$ survive, and there is no reason for them to cancel. The $4$-set Bianchi plane does satisfy it: all three pairings $e_{ab}\odot e_{cd}$, $e_{ac}\odot e_{bd}$, $e_{ad}\odot e_{bc}$ carry the single character $\chi_{\{a,b,c,d\}}$, and the Bianchi relation cuts a $2$-plane inside that $3$-dimensional character space, so every direction in it is a character vector. **The hypothesis is stated correctly and is met.**

**Two attacks the brief did not list, both of which M1 survives.** *(i)* If $\circ$ does not preserve $\mathcal W_n$ and $L_W$ is really $\Pi_{\mathcal W_n}\!\circ(W\circ\cdot)$, the record itself flags at [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) that computing $L_e$ without the Weyl projection measures a different operator, the lemma is unaffected: $\Pi_{\mathcal W_n}$ is $O(n)$-equivariant, hence preserves character spaces, and $\langle X,\Pi(R\circ X)\rangle=\langle\Pi X,R\circ X\rangle=\langle X,R\circ X\rangle=0$. *(ii)* The lemma is stronger than I12 uses it: it applies to a direction in **any** single character space, so it also transports [Lemma L122](../findings/second-order.md)'s pair-block form to an arbitrary frame, and it says the frame-diagonal directions themselves see nothing of $R$ at second order.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** The screen reported in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md), $\langle X,L_RX\rangle$ at machine zero while $\lVert L_RX\rVert$ has median $0.23$, is consistent, and the control on $\lVert L_RX\rVert$ is the right one, since it rules out the trivial reason.

## M2, where I12 fails, and it is not M1

**The failure is the hypothesis $X\perp W$ in [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md).** L24 reads $\lambda_{\max}(L_e|_{W^\perp})\le\tfrac12$, and the restriction to $W^\perp$ is not cosmetic, it is where the constraint $\lVert W\rVert=1$ enters the second variation. In the eigenframe this costs nothing: a diagonal $W$ has no $4$-set component, so the whole Bianchi plane sits inside $W^\perp$ and the top eigenvector is admissible, which is why [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) is sound as stated. **In any other frame $W$ has a component in that same Bianchi plane**, because $\langle X,W\rangle=\langle X,R\rangle$ for $X$ in the $4$-set character space, and the $4$-set part of $R$ is the $4$-set part of $W$.

**I12's own derivation knows this and drops it.** The intermediate reduction theorem in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) states the hypothesis "*$X$ a unit vector in the Bianchi plane of that $4$-set, orthogonal to $W$ and to the orbit*". The final I12 proof then says only that the test direction lies in a single character space and applies L24 to the top eigenvector, without checking that eigenvector against the hypothesis its own theorem statement carries.

**And the programme's plan already contained the missing step.** [Route step 2 of I12](plan-maximizer.md) reads: *"Remove the admissibility constraint by projection: $N'=N-cW$ with $c=\langle N,W\rangle$ satisfies $\langle N',L_WN'\rangle=\langle N,L_WN\rangle-\theta c^2$ and $\lVert N'\rVert^2=\lVert N\rVert^2-c^2$"*, and the theorem the plan predicts carries $c^2$ in both numerator and denominator. The delivered derivation reports the theorem without the $c$ terms.

**The corrected criterion, derived.** Let $X$ be a unit vector with $c=\langle X,W\rangle$, $\lvert c\rvert<1$, and $X'=(X-cW)/\sqrt{1-c^2}$, which is a unit vector in $W^\perp$. Criticality gives $L_eW=W\circ W/\theta=Q(W)/\theta=W$, and $L_e$ is self-adjoint because $\langle A,B\circ C\rangle$ is totally symmetric. Hence $\langle W,L_eX\rangle=\langle L_eW,X\rangle=c$ and $\langle W,L_eW\rangle=1$, so
$$\langle X',L_eX'\rangle=\frac{\langle X,L_eX\rangle-2c^2+c^2}{1-c^2}=\frac{\langle X,L_eX\rangle-c^2}{1-c^2}\le\frac12\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\langle X,L_eX\rangle\le\frac{1+c^2}2 .$$
The form is sharp at $X=W$, where both sides equal $1$. This is the plan's step 2 in unit normalisation, and it is exactly equivalent to it.

**What I12 therefore proves.** By M1 the $4$-set quadratic form of $L_W$ equals that of $L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}$, which [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) computes as traceless with eigenvalues $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA}$. Applying the corrected criterion to the top eigenvector $X_+$ and writing $\rho^2$ for the squared norm of $W$'s component in that Bianchi plane, so that $c_+^2\le\rho^2$:
$$\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA}\ \le\ \theta\,(1+\rho^2),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA\ \le\ \theta^2(1+\rho^2)^2 .$$
**That is the honest I12.** It is still a frame-independent certificate on the frame-diagonal part alone, and the defect is controlled: the $\rho^2$ over all $\binom n4$ subsets are the $4$-set masses of a unit $W$, so $\sum_{\{a,b,c,d\}}\rho^2_{abcd}\le1$. In particular the correction factor never exceeds $4$, and at most $k$ subsets can have $\rho^2>1/k$. What it is **not** is uncorrected, and the uncorrected form is the one the plan file records as delivered.

**The defect is real, not vacuous, and it can be exhibited by hand.** Take $n=4$ and the diagonal Weyl operator with $M_{12}=M_{34}=\alpha$, $M_{13}=M_{24}=\beta$, $M_{14}=M_{23}=\gamma$, $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0$, this is all of $\mathcal D_4$. Rotate to the orthonormal frame $f_1=(e_1+e_3)/\sqrt2$, $f_2=(e_2+e_4)/\sqrt2$, $f_3=(e_1-e_3)/\sqrt2$, $f_4=(e_2-e_4)/\sqrt2$. Then $f_1\wedge f_2=\tfrac12(b_{12}+b_{14}-b_{23}+b_{34})$ and $f_3\wedge f_4=\tfrac12(b_{12}-b_{14}+b_{23}+b_{34})$ in the orthonormal coordinate-bivector basis, both unit and mutually orthogonal, and
$$\langle W(f_1\wedge f_2),\,f_3\wedge f_4\rangle=\tfrac14\big[M_{12}-M_{14}-M_{23}+M_{34}\big]=\tfrac{\alpha-\gamma}2\ \ne\ 0\quad\text{whenever }\alpha\ne\gamma .$$
So a coordinate-diagonal Weyl operator acquires a nonzero $4$-set component under rotation, and I12's test direction is not orthogonal to $W$. Note that single-plane rotations do **not** produce this (the frame above mixes two planes at once) which is one reason the omission is easy to miss.

**No screen on a sphere product could have caught it.** The plan's own step 5 records $c=0$ at the balanced product with $a,b$ in one block and $c,d$ in the other. The screen actually run in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) measured $\langle X,L_RX\rangle$, which is M1 and is genuinely zero; it did not measure $c$. **The screen confirms the claim that is true and is silent on the one that fails.**

**The other two sub-attacks in the brief clear.** *Is $\theta$ the same in every frame?* Yes, $\theta=P(W)$ with $W$ fixed and only the frame changing, and $P$ is an $O(n)$-invariant. *Must the orbit be excluded?* No, and the extra hypothesis in the derivation's reduction theorem is not merely unnecessary but harmful, since it would cut the admissible plane further. Along an orbit curve $\gamma(t)=\exp(tA)\cdot W$ with $\lVert\gamma'(0)\rVert=1$, constancy of $P$ gives $0=6\langle X_{\mathrm{orb}},W\circ X_{\mathrm{orb}}\rangle-3\theta$, so orbit directions sit at exactly $\tfrac12$ and satisfy L24's inequality with equality. **Only $X\perp W$ is load-bearing.**

**Verdict: REFUTED as proved.** The identified failing step is the application of L24 in I12's final proof; M1 itself is untouched, and the repaired statement above is CONFIRMED.

## M3, the bilinear split

$W\mapsto L_W$ is linear because $\circ$ is bilinear, so $L_W=L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}+L_R$ with no remainder, in whatever frame. **CONFIRMED**, and the same holds if $L_W$ carries a Weyl projection, since the projection is linear.

**The brief's question "does $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ lie in $\mathcal W_n$ at all" has a better answer than the derivation gives: it does.** $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ is symmetric and hollow by construction, first Bianchi is vacuous on it because a diagonal operator has no component on four distinct indices, and Ricci-flatness of $W$ reads $\sum_kW_{akak}=0$ for each $a$ (a condition on the frame-diagonal entries **only**) which is exactly $\sum_bM_{ab}=0$. So $M\in\mathcal D_n$ as [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) defines it, $\operatorname{diag}(M)\in\mathcal W_n$, and therefore $R\in\mathcal W_n$ as well. **It matters twice**: it puts the whole split inside the space where $L$ is defined, and it supplies the Ricci-flatness that L122's Laplacian rewriting needs (see M5).

## M4, the pair-block condition

**The sign is right.** $L|_{\{j,k\}}=-\tfrac12\Delta^{(jk)}-\tfrac12M_{jk}\mathrm{Id}$, so $\lambda_{\max}(L|_{\{j,k\}})=-\tfrac12\lambda_{\min}(\Delta^{(jk)})-\tfrac12M_{jk}$, and L24 in the form $\lambda_{\max}(L_M)\le\theta/2$ gives $-\lambda_{\min}(\Delta^{(jk)})-M_{jk}\le\theta$, i.e. $\lambda_{\min}(\Delta^{(jk)}|_{\mathbf 1^\perp})\ge-(\theta+M_{jk})$. **Confirmed as written.**

**$\Delta^{(jk)}$ is a Laplacian and the identification checks.** With weights $M_{im}$ on the index set $\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{j,k\}$, the degrees are $d_i=\sum_{m\notin\{i,j,k\}}M_{im}=-M_{ij}-M_{ik}$ by Ricci-flatness, so $\Delta^{(jk)}=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)-M^{(jk)}=-\big[\operatorname{diag}(M_{ij}+M_{ik})+M^{(jk)}\big]$, which reproduces the recorded identity exactly. Its row sums vanish, so $\Delta^{(jk)}\mathbf 1=0$ and $\mathbf 1^\perp$ is invariant.

**The restriction to $\mathbf 1^\perp$ is load-bearing, not decoration.** The kernel direction $\mathbf 1$ carries $L$-eigenvalue $-\tfrac12M_{jk}$ on a vector outside $\mathcal W_n$; if $-M_{jk}>\theta$ the unrestricted claim would be false while the restricted one holds. So the Ricci cut must be imposed, and it is exactly the Laplacian's own $\mathbf 1^\perp$.

**The orbit needs no separate exclusion, and the bound is attained.** The orbit direction $x_i=M_{ki}-M_{ji}$ lies in $\mathbf 1^\perp$: summing over $i\notin\{j,k\}$ gives $(-M_{kj})-(-M_{jk})=0$ by Ricci-flatness. It is an $L$-eigenvector with eigenvalue $\theta/2$, hence a $\Delta^{(jk)}$-eigenvector with eigenvalue exactly $-(\theta+M_{jk})$. **So wherever the orbit direction is nonzero the inequality is an equality**, and its content is the sharper statement that *the orbit direction minimises the Rayleigh quotient of $\Delta^{(jk)}$ on $\mathbf 1^\perp$*. Where the orbit direction vanishes (the within-factor blocks at $S^p\times S^q$, since $M_{ji}=M_{ki}$ there), it is a strict inequality with no automatic witness. **CONFIRMED**, with the equality case recommended for the record: stated as an inequality alone the lemma understates what it proves.

## M5, the scope claim

**True for the block formulae.** For a coordinate-diagonal $M$ and $u=e_\alpha\odot e_\beta$ as an operator, $\tfrac12(Mu+uM)=\tfrac12(\mu_\alpha+\mu_\beta)u$ identically, which is diagonality alone; and $(M\#X)_{\alpha\beta}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha MA_\beta X)$ is bilinear in $(M,X)$ with no side condition. Neither half of $\circ$ can see criticality, $\theta>0$, or a trace condition. **L121's $\tfrac12G$ and L122's bracket form both need diagonality alone.** The hypotheses "critical point with $\theta>0$" in L121's statement are inherited from the L24 *consequence* attached to it, not from the block computation, and the record should say so.

**One imprecision in L122's wording, worth fixing.** The sentence "*Diagonality alone is needed, not criticality, not $\theta>0$, not Ricci-flatness*" is placed after the Laplacian rewriting and after the claim that the Ricci cut is $\sum_ix_i=0$. Both of those need Ricci-flatness: without it the degrees are $\sum_{m\notin\{i,j,k\}}M_{im}$ rather than $-M_{ij}-M_{ik}$, $\operatorname{diag}(M_{ji}+M_{ki})+M^{(jk)}$ has row sums varying with $i$ and is not a Laplacian, and $\mathbf 1$ is not in its kernel. **The scope line is correct for the bracket form and over-reaches over the two sentences before it.** This is precisely the failure mode the withdrawn halved form exhibited, and it is invisible on non-Ricci-flat inputs for the same reason.

**I12's use of the scope is legitimate on this point.** It applies the block formulae to $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ in a frame where $W$ is not diagonal, and $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ is diagonal by construction and Ricci-flat by M3, so both the bracket form and the Laplacian reading are available. **The scope claim is not where I12 fails.**
