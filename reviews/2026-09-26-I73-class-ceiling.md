# I73, D6: a class-level ceiling from the value hypothesis, working note, 2026-09-26

**The target.** At a three-value critical point with $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$, a bound on $\theta$ through a whole extreme eigenspace $E_0$ ($\dim E_0=p$) that scales like $\sqrt p$ in $\kappa=\theta\sqrt{2p}$, where every per-vector ceiling on file scales like $p$ ([post F77](../findings/fence-posts.md)). **Teeth test**: padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$. **Acceptance test**: the constant, since $\theta_0(n)<\sqrt2$.

## Batch 1: summed bracket demand against an isotropy supply

**Demand.** Row (C) at unit $x\in E_0$ asks bracket mass $b_{02}(x)\ge\tfrac12(\theta\sqrt{2p}+1)$ into $E_2$ ([post F77](../findings/fence-posts.md), [L244](../findings/second-order.md)), and $\sum_{\gamma\in E_0}\lVert[x,\psi_\gamma]\rVert^2=2\sum_bb_{0b}(x)$. Summed over an orthonormal basis of $E_0$, the demand is $\tfrac p2(\theta\sqrt{2p}+1)$, of order $\theta p^{3/2}$.

**Supply, an identity for any subspace $F\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with orthonormal basis $\{x\}$ and $A_F=\sum_xx^{\mathsf T}x$** (Frobenius pairing, checked numerically on a random $7$-dimensional $F\subset\mathfrak{so}(9)$ to machine precision):
$$\sum_{x,y}\lVert[x,y]\rVert^2=2\operatorname{tr}(A_F^2)-2\sum_{x,y}\operatorname{tr}(xyxy)\ \le\ 4\operatorname{tr}(A_F^2),$$
*since $(xy)^{\mathsf T}=yx$ for skew $x,y$ gives $\lVert xy\rVert^2=\lVert yx\rVert^2=\operatorname{tr}(x^2y^2)$ and $\lvert\operatorname{tr}(xyxy)\rvert\le\lVert xy\rVert^2$.* **So the summed demand is paid for by the isotropy matrix $A_0$ of [L245](../findings/second-order.md)**, whose eigenvalues $\alpha_i$ satisfy $\sum_i\alpha_i=\operatorname{tr}A_0\propto p$.

**The scaling is right exactly when $A_0$ is spread.** *A subalgebra $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ has $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)\propto p^{3/2}$, matching the demand's order, so the argument would give $\theta=O(1)$. A star $e\wedge U$ has $\alpha_e=p$ and $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)\propto p^2$, which is the whole of post F77's window.* [L246](../findings/second-order.md) excludes both as extreme eigenspaces on the symmetric family, since its inequality $\alpha_j\le\sum_{i\ne j}\min(\tfrac12,\alpha_i)$ fails at $e$ for a star and at every support direction for $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^k$.

**Where it stops.** *L246 forces the support of $A_0$ to have at least $2\alpha_{\max}$ directions, which caps $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)\le\alpha_{\max}\operatorname{tr}A_0$ only by $O(np)$, not $O(p^{3/2})$.* **The constant, pinned in the record's normalisation** ($e_i\wedge e_j$ unit, so a decomposable unit $x$ has $-\mathrm{ad}_x^2$ eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity $2(n-2)$). *At $E_0=\mathfrak{so}(k)$, $p=\binom k2$, each basis element brackets to norm one with $2(k-2)$ others, so $\sum_{x,y\in E_0}\lVert[x,y]\rVert^2=2p(k-2)$ exactly, and $\sum_xb_{02}(x)\le\tfrac12\sum_{x,y}\lVert[x,y]\rVert^2=p(k-2)$.* **Against the demand $\tfrac p2(\theta\sqrt{2p}+1)$ with $\sqrt{2p}\approx k$ this gives $\theta\le2-O(1/k)$**, a factor near $1.5$ above $\theta_0$, and it has not yet used that only the $E_2$-component of $[x,y]$ counts. *The crude $4\operatorname{tr}(A_F^2)$ route loses more, which is why the identity is the supply to use and the trace bound only its scaling.*

**So the invention reduces to one extremal problem, stated without the programme's names**: *over $p$-dimensional subspaces $F\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ satisfying the isotropy constraints of [L245](../findings/second-order.md) and [L246](../findings/second-order.md), how large can the total bracket mass $\sum_{x,y}\lVert\pi_{F'}[x,y]\rVert^2$ into a complementary eigenspace $F'$ be, measured against $p^{3/2}$?* Stars reach $p^2$ and are excluded by L246, subalgebras reach $2p(k-2)\approx2\sqrt2\,p^{3/2}$ and are excluded by L246's support count, and the question is what L246 permits in between. **The two gaps are therefore one**: an upper bound of order $p^{3/2}$ on bracket mass under L246, with constant below the one that makes $\theta<\theta_0$.

**The unconstrained version is on the shelf and sits at the star scale.** *Ge's skew DDVV inequality ([notes](../references/ge-2014-ddvv-skew-symmetric/README.md), Theorem 1.1) gives $\sum_{r,s}\lVert[B_r,B_s]\rVert^2\le\tfrac23\big(\sum_r\lVert B_r\rVert^2\big)^2$ in the Frobenius norm, which over an orthonormal basis of $F$ is of order $p^2$, and its equality case concentrates on a four-dimensional block.* **What the extremal problem above asks is a DDVV inequality under an isotropy constraint**, the constraint being what L246 derives from $\operatorname{Ric}(W)=0$. *That is the literature question for the next batch: DDVV and Böttcher–Wenzel refinements under a bound on $A_F=\sum B_r^{\mathsf T}B_r$ (the Lu–Wenzel line, [ge-li-lu-zhou-2019](../references/ge-li-lu-zhou-2019-lu-wenzel-conjectures/README.md)).*

## Batch 2: classified against the record

**Agreement, not novelty, on the demand side.** *The summed row (C) against total bracket mass is a row of the class-level system of [post F76](../findings/fence-posts.md), which that post proves cannot close (c) on linear class data.* **And the missing supply is the ingredient F76 names**, *a $k$-subspace bracket-mass cap for every $k$*, here at $k=p$ and in the form L246 makes available: an isotropy constraint on $A_0$ rather than a linear class total. **So D6's invention is F76's successor ingredient, specialised to one extreme eigenspace.**

**The term to bound is the crossing sum.** *Since $\sum_{x,y}\lVert[x,y]\rVert^2=2\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)-2\sum_{x,y}\operatorname{tr}(xyxy)$ and the spectral projector $P_0=\sum_xx\otimes x$ is a quadratic polynomial in $W$, both terms are quartic $O(n)$-invariants of $W$.* **The next step is to bound $-\sum_{x,y}\operatorname{tr}(xyxy)$ below $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)$ by a margin L246 controls**, since the crude bound $\lvert\sum\operatorname{tr}(xyxy)\rvert\le\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)$ is what loses the constant. *At a star the crossing sum is negligible ($xy=-uv^{\mathsf T}$ for $x=e\wedge u$, $y=e\wedge v$, $u\perp v$, so $\operatorname{tr}(xyxy)=0$) and the $p^2$ sits in $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)$ itself, through $\alpha_e=p$.* **So the order $p^{3/2}$ needs the isotropy spread, and the crossing sum only moves the constant.**

## Batch 3: the isotropy spread is the spread of $\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)$

**On the symmetric family the isotropy matrix is a Ricci contraction of $W^2$.** *From [L245](../findings/second-order.md)'s identities $\sum_cA_c=(n-1)I$, $\sum_c\lambda_cA_c=\operatorname{Ric}(W)=0$ and the next one, $\sum_c\lambda_c^2A_c=\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)$, at $\lambda=(-a,0,a)$ with $A_0=A_2$ and $2pa^2=1$:*
$$A_0=\frac{\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)}{2a^2}=p\,\operatorname{Ric}(W^2),\qquad\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)=p^2\,\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2,\qquad\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)=2.$$
*Agreement with the record rather than novelty: it is L245's third weighted sum, which the record's proof uses implicitly.* **So the order $p^{3/2}$ asks exactly $\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2=O(p^{-1/2})$**: a positive semidefinite $n\times n$ matrix of trace $2$, isotropic value $4/n$, concentrated value up to $1$ at a star (which L246 excludes). *At a critical point $\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)=-\operatorname{Ric}(W^\#)$, since $\operatorname{Ric}(W^2+W^\#)=\theta\operatorname{Ric}(W)=0$.* **The crux of D6 is therefore one spread inequality for the quadratic tensor $\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)$ at a three-value critical point**, and [curvature-invariants.md](../findings/curvature-invariants.md) is where the record handles it.

## Batch 4: the product family and padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ against the spread (a screen)

*At $n=12$ the products $S^{k-1}\times S^{11-k}$ have $\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)$ in two blocks, and $\theta$ falls monotonically as $\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2$ rises from its isotropic value $\tfrac13$ at $k=6$ ($\theta_0=1.23603$) through $0.379$ ($1.22824$), $0.535$ ($1.20156$), $0.871$ ($1.14180$) to $1.624$ ($0.99499$).* **Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has $\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2=1$ at $\theta=1.22474$, above the product curve at that spread**, so no single decreasing curve $\theta\le F(\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2)$ fitted to the products holds, and one that also admits $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is nearly flat and yields only a constant bound on the spread. **So the spread inequality D6 needs cannot be a universal inequality in $\theta$ and the spread alone**: it must use criticality at three values, which is where L246 enters. *A screen on catalogued points, recorded as evidence about the form of the target and not as a result.*

## Batch 5: the crux is near-star exclusion, and it is post F77's open case

**The isotropy in one direction is capped by the dimension, with equality only at a star.** *For unit $e$ and $x\in E_0$ write $x=e\wedge v+y$ with $v\perp e$ and $ye=0$. Then $xe=-v$, the two parts are orthogonal, and $\lVert x\rVert^2=\lVert xe\rVert^2+\lVert y\rVert^2$ in the record's normalisation ($e_i\wedge e_j$ unit).* Summing over an orthonormal basis,
$$\alpha(e)=\langle A_0e,e\rangle=\sum_x\lVert xe\rVert^2=p-\sum_x\lVert y_x\rVert^2\le p,$$
**with equality exactly when every $x\in E_0$ is $e\wedge v$, that is, when $E_0$ is a star at $e$.**

**And the order $p^{3/2}$ follows from an $O(\sqrt p)$ cap on the largest isotropy**, since $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)\le\alpha_{\max}\operatorname{tr}A_0=2p\,\alpha_{\max}$. *The subalgebra $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ has $\alpha_{\max}=k-1\approx\sqrt{2p}$, the star has $\alpha_{\max}=p$.* **So D6's crux is: at a three-value critical point on the symmetric family, $\alpha_{\max}(A_0)=p\,\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\sqrt p$**, i.e. no eigenspace is close to a star.

**L246 does not reach it.** *At $e$ it asks $\alpha(e)\le\sum_{i\ne e}\min(\tfrac12,\alpha_i)$, and the remaining isotropy $p+\delta$ spread over $e^\perp$ can supply up to $\tfrac{n-1}2$, so L246 bounds $\alpha(e)$ only by $\tfrac{n-1}2$.* **That is post F77's window $p\le\tfrac{n-1}2$ exactly, and F77's own residue, "near-stars remain", is this statement.** *So the invention has converged on the obstruction the record already named, now with a quantitative target: $\alpha_{\max}\le C\sqrt p$ with $C$ small enough that batch 1's constant falls below $\theta_0$.*

## Batch 6: a perturbative near-star exclusion, and how far it reaches (a sketch, constants unpinned)

**Mechanism, the one L246 uses for stars.** *On the symmetric family $A_2=A_0$, so a near-star $E_0$ at $e$ ($\alpha(e)=p-\delta$) forces $E_2$ to be a near-star at $e$ with the same deficiency. With $Q$ the projector onto $e\wedge e^\perp$ and $P$ onto $\Lambda^2e^\perp$: $[Qx,Qy]$ and $[Px,Py]$ lie in $\Lambda^2e^\perp$, where $E_2$ has total mass $\delta$, and $[Qx,Py]$ lies in $e\wedge e^\perp$ but carries a factor $Py$ of total mass $\delta$.* **So every contribution to the bracket mass from $E_0$ into $E_2$ carries a factor of mass $\delta$**, and with $\sum_{x}\lVert xw\rVert_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(w^{\mathsf T}A_0w)\le\alpha_{\max}\lVert w\rVert_F^2$ the total is at most a constant times $\alpha_{\max}\,\delta\le p\,\delta$.

**Against the summed demand $\tfrac p2(\theta\sqrt{2p}+1)$ this excludes only $\delta\lesssim c\,\theta\sqrt p$**, i.e. $\alpha(e)\ge p-c\theta\sqrt p$, with $c$ of order $1/24$ in the crude count. **It does not reach $\alpha_{\max}\le C\sqrt p$**: the region $C\sqrt p\le\alpha_{\max}\le p-c\sqrt p$ stays open, and it is the whole width of post F77's near-star residue. *The loss is the factor $\alpha_{\max}\approx p$ in the bracket estimate, which is exactly the concentration being excluded, so the estimate is circular in the regime that matters.*

**Status of D6.** *The invention has produced three agreements with the record (the summed row (C) is F76's class system, $A_0=p\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)$ is L245's third sum, the crux is F77's near-star residue) and two new facts (the bracket identity with its crossing term, and $\alpha(e)\le p$ with equality only at a star), and it has reduced I73 on the symmetric family to one quantitative statement:* **at a three-value critical point with $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$, $\alpha_{\max}(A_0)\le C\sqrt p$ with $C$ small enough.** *No method for it is on the table.*

## Batch 7: the literature for the near-star statement (D7's search)

**One subspace-restricted DDVV inequality exists**, [Ge–Li–Zhou](../references/ge-li-zhou-2018-ddvv-bw-generalizations/README.md) Theorem 4.4, for subspaces spanned by a Clifford algebra: constant $\tfrac4l(1-\tfrac1N)$ against $\tfrac23$ for arbitrary skew matrices. *It confirms the mechanism D6 needs, that a spread isotropy lowers the bracket mass to order $p^2/n$, but a Clifford subspace has logarithmic dimension, so it does not reach $p\sim n$.* **Searched, nothing else**: DDVV stability and equality-case refinements, subspace commutator bounds with Casimir scaling, the normal-scalar-curvature (Lu) line.

**The form the target should take, read off batch 1 and this.** *The bracket mass is at most a constant times $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)=p^2\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2$, and the summed demand is of order $\theta p^{3/2}$, so what the exclusion needs is the trace form*
$$\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2\ \lesssim\ \theta/\sqrt p\qquad\text{at a three-value critical point with }\theta\ge\theta_0(n),$$
*of which the $\alpha_{\max}\le C\sqrt p$ form is a sufficient case. At a perfectly spread $A_0$, $\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2=4/n$, which meets it whenever $p\lesssim\theta^2n^2/C$.* **So the near-star statement is a spread bound on the quadratic tensor $\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)=-\operatorname{Ric}(W^\#)$ at critical points**, and no source on the shelf or found here proves one.

## Batch 9: sufficiency check, the spread target as stated does not close I73

**The chain with its constant pinned.** *In the record's normalisation ($e_i\wedge e_j$ unit, $\operatorname{tr}A_0=2p$) the bracket identity gives $\sum_{x,y\in E_0}\lVert[x,y]\rVert^2\le2\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)$ (checked at $\mathfrak{so}(k)$: $2p(k-2)\le4p(k-1)$), so $\sum_xb_{02}(x)\le\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)$.* **Exclusion by the summed row (C) therefore needs**
$$\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)<\tfrac p2\big(\theta\sqrt{2p}+1\big),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\lVert\operatorname{Ric}(W^2)\rVert^2<\frac{\theta\sqrt{2p}+1}{2p}.$$

**It cannot hold for large $p$.** *Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)\ge(\operatorname{tr}A_0)^2/n=4p^2/n$, so the requirement is satisfiable only when $8p/n<\theta\sqrt{2p}+1$, roughly $p<\theta^2n^2/32$.* **For $\theta^2n^2/32\lesssim p\lesssim n^2/4$ the needed spread lies below the isotropic minimum, so no spread bound, however strong, closes that band by this chain**, and [R39](../routes.md) reaches only $p\gtrsim n^2/4$ from above. *For $p\sim n$ the requirement is satisfiable only from $n\approx32/\theta^2\approx21$.*

**And where it can hold it asks near-isotropy.** *At $n=12$, $p=7$, $\theta=\theta_0$: the bound is $0.40$ against the isotropic $\tfrac13$, within $20\%$. [L241](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s exact point ($n=12$, $p=7$, $\theta=5/\sqrt{14}$) carries $G_{00}=\operatorname{tr}(A_0^2)=\tfrac{77}2$ ([R37](../routes.md)) against the chain's threshold $\tfrac72(5+1)=21$, so it survives the chain with its own spread.*

**Verdict: the target is insufficient as stated.** *The loss is in the chain, not only in the spread: every bracket is charged to $E_2$, and the identity's factor-$2$ loss and the crossing term are both kept at their worst.* **The fallback the plan named is the $E_2$-projection**: bound $\sum_{x,y}\lVert\pi_{E_2}[x,y]\rVert^2$ directly, which uses $A_2=A_0$ and the fact that $E_2$ has dimension $p$ in $\Lambda^2$ of dimension $\binom n2$. *Whether that projection gains the order of magnitude the large-$p$ band needs is the next question, and it is a question about the geometry of two $p$-dimensional subspaces, which is where the outside input would help.*

## Batch 10: the $E_2$ projection, by the same estimate, returns R37's $G_{02}$

**The refinement batch 9 named.** *By invariance of the structure constants, $\sum_{x,y\in E_0}\lVert\pi_{E_2}[x,y]\rVert^2=\sum_{z\in E_2}\lVert\pi_{E_0}\,\mathrm{ad}_z\,\pi_{E_0}\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$. Dropping the outer $\pi_{E_0}$ and using $\sum_{x\in E_0}\lVert zx\rVert_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(z^{\mathsf T}A_0z)$ gives at most a constant times $\sum_z\langle A_0,z^{\mathsf T}z\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(A_0A_2)=G_{02}$.* **On the symmetric family $A_2=A_0$, so $G_{02}=G_{00}$ and the projection gains nothing by this estimate.** *$G_{ab}=\operatorname{tr}(A_aA_b)$ is [R37](../routes.md)'s coordinate, closed on its abandon condition, so the refinement lands on a closed route.*

**What a gain would need.** *The quantity that matters is the doubly compressed $\sum_{z\in E_2}\lVert\pi_{E_0}\mathrm{ad}_z\pi_{E_0}\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$, and bounding it below $G_{02}$ depends on how $E_0$ and $E_2$ sit relative to each other beyond their isotropy matrices.* **That is a statement about the relative position of two $p$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ under the bracket, which no result on the shelf addresses**, and it is the question to put to outside input:

> *Given orthogonal subspaces $E_0,E_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)$ of dimension $p$ with equal isotropy $\sum_{x\in E_0}x^{\mathsf T}x=\sum_{z\in E_2}z^{\mathsf T}z$, bound $\sum_{x,y\in E_0,\,z\in E_2}\langle[x,y],z\rangle^2$ by $c\,p^{3/2}$, or exhibit families where it grows faster.*

## Batch 13: where the $\sqrt p$ has to come from

*Write $C_z=\pi_{E_0}\mathrm{ad}_z\pi_{E_0}$ for $z\in E_2$ and $K=\sum_{z\in E_2}C_z^{\mathsf T}C_z$ on $E_0$. Then $S=\operatorname{tr}K$ and $\langle Kx,x\rangle=2b_{02}(x)$, so row (C) at every unit $x$ says $K\succeq(\theta\sqrt{2p}+1)I$: each $M_x(y,z)=\langle[x,y],z\rangle$ must have Hilbert–Schmidt mass of order $\sqrt p$, and since $\lVert\mathrm{ad}_x\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=O(1)$, rank of order $\sqrt p$.* **Every estimate tried drops a projection and lands on a known ceiling**: dropping $\pi_{E_0}$ gives the per-vector Ky Fan caps of [L244](../findings/second-order.md) ($O(p)$ per vector), dropping it in trace form gives [R37](../routes.md)'s $G_{02}$. **The exclusion lives in the doubly compressed trilinear form $\langle[x,y],z\rangle$ on $E_0\times E_0\times E_2$ under equal isotropy, and no inequality for that object is known here.** *Recorded as I73's obstruction in [findings/INDEX.md](../findings/INDEX.md).*

## Batch 14: an internal-bracket-mass reduction, right order, constant short

*$S$ counts the triples of $F=E_0\oplus E_2$ with two entries in $E_0$ and one in $E_2$, so $S\le\mathcal M(F):=\sum_{x,y,z\in F}\langle[x,y],z\rangle^2$, the internal bracket mass of a $2p$-dimensional subspace.* **At a subalgebra $\mathfrak{so}(k)$, $d=\binom k2$, $\mathcal M=2d(k-2)\approx2\sqrt2\,d^{3/2}$; a star has $\mathcal M=0$; mixed families checked by hand stay $O(d)$.** *If $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ maximised $\mathcal M$ among $d$-dimensional subspaces, $S\le2\sqrt2(2p)^{3/2}=8p^{3/2}$: the right order, but the demand is $\sqrt2\theta_0p^{3/2}\approx1.75p^{3/2}$, so the constant is short by a factor near $4.6$, and the screen's $S\le1.5p^{3/2}$ says the loss is in dropping the $(0,0,2)$ restriction and equal isotropy.* **So the order $p^{3/2}$ has a candidate source, "subalgebras maximise internal bracket mass at fixed dimension", itself an open extremal statement, and closing I73 this way still needs the partial count.**

## Batch 15: an explicit equal-isotropy family whose ratio tends to 1 from below

*On $\mathbb R^{4k}=A\oplus B\oplus C$, $\lvert A\rvert=\lvert B\rvert=k$, $\lvert C\rvert=2k$: $E_0=A\otimes B\oplus\operatorname{span}\{c_{2i-1}\wedge c_{2i}\}$ and $E_2=\Lambda^2A\oplus\Lambda^2B\oplus\operatorname{span}\{a_i\wedge c_i,\,b_i\wedge c_{k+i}\}$. Both have dimension $p=k^2+k$, they are orthogonal, and their isotropies agree ($k$ on $A\cup B$, $1$ on $C$), checked exactly at $k=2,\dots,5$.* **$S=2k^2(k-1)$** *(only $[a\wedge b,a\wedge b']$ and $[a\wedge b,a'\wedge b]$ reach $E_2$)*, **against the demand $p(\theta_0(4k)\sqrt{2p}+1)$: ratio $0.27,0.43,0.53,0.60$ at $n=8,12,16,20$, and $\to1$ from below as $k\to\infty$**, since $\sqrt2\,\theta_0(n)=2-O(1/n)$ and $\sqrt{2p}\approx\sqrt2(k+\tfrac12)$.

**So the margin is intrinsic, not a defect of the screen.** *The equal-isotropy relaxation reaches the demand's leading term along a product-like family, which is the conjecture's own equality pattern, so an analytic bound on $S$ must be sharp to leading order and win on the lower-order terms: roughly $S\le2p^{3/2}-c\,p$ with $c$ beating the demand's $+p$ and the $O(1/n)$ in $\theta_0$.* **That is the precise form of I73's obstruction on the symmetric family.**

## Batch 17 (derivation at effort `xhigh`, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): the cap on subalgebras, and where the general cap stands

**The subalgebra cap.** *For a compact Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n)$ acting through a real representation $\rho$, the ambient form is $\langle X,Y\rangle=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\rho(X)\rho(Y)=\tfrac12\ell_\rho(X,Y)$ on each simple ideal, with $(\,,)$ normalised to long roots of length $\sqrt2$ and $\ell_\rho$ the Dynkin index (complex fundamental of $\mathfrak{su}$ index $1$, realification doubling it). $$\mathcal M(\mathfrak h)=\sum_i d_i\,C_i,\qquad\frac{\mathcal M(\mathfrak h)}{d^{3/2}}\le\max_i\frac{C_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}\quad(\text{superadditivity of }d^{3/2};\ \text{the centre carries }0).$$

## Batch 19 (derived at effort `xhigh`, recorded at `medium`): the cap follows from a projection-free quartic bound on $\Pi\#\Pi$

**Cauchy–Schwarz on the compressed operator.** *For a subspace $V$ with orthonormal basis $v_i$ (record normalisation) and projector $\Pi$, let $K_V=\pi_V(2\,\Pi\#\Pi)\pi_V$, so for $x\in V$, $\langle x,K_Vx\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle[v_i,v_j],x\rangle^2=\lVert\pi_V\mathrm{ad}_x\pi_V\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$, and $\operatorname{tr}K_V=\mathcal M(V)$. $$\mathcal M(V)\le2\sqrt d\,\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F,$$
*the first step Cauchy–Schwarz on the $d\times d$ matrix $K_V$, the second $\lVert\pi_VB\pi_V\rVert_F\le\lVert B\rVert_F$. Both are equalities on every simple subalgebra, and on any subalgebra whose ideals share one Casimir value: at $\mathfrak{so}(k)$, $\Pi\#\Pi=(k-2)\Pi$.*

*(equality at $\Pi=I$): with [L255](../findings/second-order.md), $\theta\le\mathcal M/(2d^{3/2})\le\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F/d\le\sqrt2(n-2)/\sqrt{n(n-1)}<\theta_0(n)$.* **No projection onto $V$ appears**: $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F^2=\tfrac14\sum_{i,j,k,l}\langle[v_i,v_j],[v_k,v_l]\rangle^2$ is the norm of the algebraic curvature tensor $\langle[x,y],[u,w]\rangle$ restricted to $V$ (Jacobi gives Bianchi), so leaks and stars do not enter. *On simple subalgebras it equals [L256](../findings/second-order.md)'s condition and on semisimple ones it follows from L256's table ($\sum c_i^2d_i\le\max_i(c_i^2/d_i)\,d^2$); at a star it is $\sqrt{d(d-1)/2}$. **It fails at $n\le8$**: $\Lambda^2_+$ gives ratio $\sqrt{2n(n-1)/3}/(n-2)$, above $1$ exactly for $n\le8$ ($1.018$ at $n=8$, $0.990$ at $n=9$), the same threshold as L256 ([r1214](REVIEW_r1214.md), [r1215](REVIEW_r1215.md)).*

## Batch 20 (standing level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `medium`): the quartic bound, attempted, and the obstacle

**The object, rewritten.** *$\Pi\#\Pi=AA^{\mathsf T}$ for the restricted bracket map $A:\Lambda^2V\to\mathfrak{so}(n)$, $x\wedge y\mapsto[x,y]$, so $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F=\lVert\widehat R_V\rVert_F$ with $\widehat R_V=A^{\mathsf T}A\succeq0$ the curvature operator of $\langle[x,y],[u,w]\rangle$ on $\Lambda^2V$. ## Batch 22 (derivation at effort `xhigh`, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): the quartic bound as a principal-angle inequality

**The Casimir tensor on $\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g$, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{so}(n)$.** *$\Omega=\sum_a\mathrm{ad}_{e_a}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{e_a}$ acts on each irreducible summand by $\tfrac12(2C_{\mathrm{adj}}-C_\lambda)$, record normalisation ($C_{\mathrm{vec}}=n-1$, $C_{\mathrm{adj}}=2(n-2)$). $\Lambda^2\mathfrak g=A\oplus H$ with $A\cong[1,1]$ the adjoint copy $\{\varphi_z(x,y)=\langle[x,y],z\rangle\}$ and $H\cong[2,1,1]$, so*
$$\Omega|_{\Lambda^2\mathfrak g}=(n-2)\,P_A,\qquad\Omega|_{\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak g}=2(n-2),\ n-4,\ -2,\ 4\ \text{ on trace, traceless Ricci, Weyl, }\Lambda^4,$$
*the second list being $2(I\#)$, matching [r1214](REVIEW_r1214.md)'s fourth scalar. Checked by diagonalising $\Omega$ at $n=5,6,7$: on $\Lambda^2\mathfrak g$ the eigenvalue $n-2$ has multiplicity exactly $N$ and every other eigenvalue is $0$; on $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak g$ the multiplicities are $1$, $\binom{n+1}2-1$, $\dim\mathcal W_n$, $\binom n4$.*

**The quartic bound, restated.** *With $\beta:\Lambda^2\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ the bracket, $\beta\beta^{\mathsf T}=(n-2)I$ and $\beta^{\mathsf T}\beta=(n-2)P_A$, and $\Pi\#\Pi=\beta(\Lambda^2\Pi)\beta^{\mathsf T}$. Hence*
$$\frac{\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F^2}{(n-2)^2}=\operatorname{tr}\big(\Lambda^2\Pi\,P_A\,\Lambda^2\Pi\,P_A\big)=\sum_k\cos^4\phi_k,$$
*the principal angles between $\Lambda^2V$ (dimension $\binom d2$) and the adjoint copy $A$ (dimension $N$).* **So L257's quartic bound is: $\sum_k\cos^4\phi_k(\Lambda^2V,A)\le d^2/N$ for $n\ge9$**, with equality at $V=\mathfrak g$ ($N$ angles zero); at $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ the angles have $\cos^2=\tfrac{k-2}{n-2}$ on $d$ directions and $\cos=0$ elsewhere. *A zero angle means a $z$ with $\varphi_z$ supported on $V\times V$, i.e. $\operatorname{im}\mathrm{ad}_z\subseteq V$, which needs $d\ge2(n-2)$ at $n=5$ and $n\ge7$ (at $n=6$ a complex structure has centraliser $\mathfrak u(3)$ and $\operatorname{rank}\mathrm{ad}_J=6$, at $n=4$ a self-dual $z$ has rank $2$); the bound says such alignment is rare in $\ell^2$ unless $V$ is most of $\mathfrak g$.*

**Two identities it yields** (for the compressed adjoints $M_a=\pi_V\mathrm{ad}_{e_a}\pi_V$, $a$ over an orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak g$): *$\langle M_a,M_b\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=2(\Pi\#\Pi)_{ab}$, and since $\Omega$ vanishes on $H$, $\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}\big((M_aM_b)^2\big)=2\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F^2$: of the Frobenius mass $4\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert^2$ of $\sum_aM_a\otimes M_a$ on $V\otimes V$, exactly a quarter sits on $\Lambda^2V$.*

**Where it stops.** *The eigenvalues $(n-2)\cos^2\phi_k$ lie in $[0,n-2]$ and sum to $\operatorname{tr}\Pi\#\Pi\le(n-2)d$, which again gives only $(n-2)^2d$; the bound needs the $\ell^2$ mass of the cosines to scale like $d^2/N$, a statement that $\Lambda^2V$ cannot align with $A$ along many directions at once. On subalgebras this is L256's index table.* **Open, in its cleanest form so far: a Schatten-$4$ bound on the angles between $\Lambda^2V$ and the adjoint copy of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ in $\Lambda^2\mathfrak{so}(n)$.**

## Batch 23 (standing level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `medium`): the quartic bound on coordinate subspaces is a codegree inequality

**Reduction.** *For $V=\operatorname{span}\{e_i\wedge e_j:ij\in E\}$ with $E$ the edge set of a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, $[e_i\wedge e_j,e_j\wedge e_k]=\pm e_i\wedge e_k$, so $\Pi\#\Pi$ is diagonal in the coordinate basis with entry $\operatorname{codeg}(i,k)$ at $e_i\wedge e_k$. With $e=\lvert E\rvert=d$, [L257](../findings/second-order.md)'s quartic bound on $V$ reads*
$$S(G)=\sum_{i<k}\operatorname{codeg}(i,k)^2\ \le\ \frac{(n-2)^2e^2}{N},\qquad\text{equivalently}\quad\operatorname{tr}A^4-\sum_id_i^2\le\frac{(n-2)^2(\operatorname{tr}A^2)^2}{n(n-1)},$$
*with equality at $K_n$ (every codegree $n-2$), where $\operatorname{tr}A^4=\sum d_i^2+2S$.*

**Screen.** *Every graph on $5$ and on $6$ vertices ($2^{10}$ and $2^{15}$ edge sets), and $20000$ random graphs plus all cliques at $n=7,9,12$: the largest ratio is exactly $1$, at the complete graph.* **So the coordinate case holds on everything screened, at every $n$ from $5$, with the complete graph extremal** (the small-$n$ failure of the general bound is $\Lambda^2_+$, which is not a coordinate subspace).

**Two proof attempts.** *(i) $\operatorname{codeg}\le n-2$ and de Caen's $\sum d_i^2\le e\big(\tfrac{2e}{n-1}+n-2\big)$ give $2S\le(n-2)e\big(\tfrac{2e}{n-1}+n-4\big)$, which meets the target only at $e=N$: the cap $n-2$ is too crude for sparse graphs. (ii) $\operatorname{tr}A^4\le\lambda_1^2\operatorname{tr}A^2$ with Stanley's $\lambda_1\le\tfrac12(\sqrt{8e+1}-1)$ and $\sum d_i^2\ge4e^2/n$ prove the bound for $e=\binom m2$ with $m\le n(n-1)/(2n-3)$, about $n^2/8$ edges; denser graphs need the negative eigenvalues, which the first inequality discards.* **Next: the dense regime of the codegree inequality**, a question in extremal graph theory (sums of squared codegrees at fixed edge count), to be searched before it is derived.

## Batch 24 (standing level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `medium`): the quartic bound holds on every coordinate subspace

**Theorem.** *For every graph $G$ on $n\ge3$ vertices with $e$ edges, $S(G)=\sum_{i<k}\operatorname{codeg}(i,k)^2\le(n-2)^2e^2/N$; among graphs with an edge, equality holds exactly at $K_n$ (the edgeless graph gives $0=0$). Hence [L257](../findings/second-order.md)'s quartic bound holds on every coordinate subspace $V=\operatorname{span}\{e_i\wedge e_j:ij\in E\}$, at every $n\ge3$.*

**Proof.** *With $A$ the adjacency matrix, $t=\operatorname{tr}A^2=2e$ and degrees $d_i$: $\operatorname{tr}A^4=\sum_{i,k}(A^2)_{ik}^2=\sum_id_i^2+2S$. $A$ is symmetric with $\operatorname{tr}A=0$, and for real $x_1,\dots,x_n$ with $\sum x_i=0$ the maximum of $\sum x_i^4/(\sum x_i^2)^2$ is $c_n=\tfrac{n^2-3n+3}{n(n-1)}$, attained at one value against $n-1$ equal ones (the classical maximum of sample kurtosis: at a constrained maximum every $x_i$ solves one cubic $4x^3=2\mu x+\nu$, the second-order condition leaves at most two values, and the two-value family gives $\tfrac n{k(n-k)}-\tfrac3n$, largest at $k=1$; proof in [r1218](REVIEW_r1218.md)), so $\operatorname{tr}A^4\le c_nt^2$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sum d_i^2\ge t^2/n$. Then $2S\le(c_n-\tfrac1n)t^2=\tfrac{(n-2)^2}{n(n-1)}t^2$, which is $S\le(n-2)^2e^2/N$ (`coordinate_quartic`).* $\square$ *Equality forces the extremal spectrum and equal degrees, which among graphs with an edge is $K_n$ (Perron–Frobenius).*

**What it gives.** *Stars, cliques $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ in coordinates, and every edge subspace satisfy the quartic bound, at every $n$; the general bound's failure at $n\le8$ comes from $\Lambda^2_+$, which is not a coordinate subspace.* **The mechanism, a fourth-moment bound for a traceless operator whose square carries the codegrees, is the one to lift**: for general $V$ the codegree matrix is replaced by $\Pi\#\Pi$ and the adjacency matrix by the realigned map $\widetilde Z=\sum U_i\otimes U_i$ ($\operatorname{tr}\widetilde Z=0$, $\operatorname{tr}\widetilde Z^2=d$), with $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F^2=8\big(\operatorname{tr}\widetilde Z^4-\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde Z^2(\widetilde Z^2)^{\Gamma})\big)$, $\Gamma$ the partial transpose. *Open: which traceless operator plays $A$'s role in general.*

## Batch 26 (standing level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `medium`): lifting L258, approach B, perturbation from the whole algebra (a sketch)

**Expansion.** *With $\Pi=I-\Pi'$, $\operatorname{rank}\Pi'=w=N-d$: $\Pi\#\Pi=(n-2)I-2I\#\Pi'+\Pi'\#\Pi'$, and the quartic bound's target is $(n-2)^2(N-w)^2/N=(n-2)^2(N-2w+w^2/N)$. The first two terms contribute $(n-2)^2N-4(n-2)^2w$ exactly, so the bound asks*
$$4\lVert I\#\Pi'\rVert^2+2(n-2)\operatorname{tr}(\Pi'\#\Pi')-4\langle I\#\Pi',\Pi'\#\Pi'\rangle+\lVert\Pi'\#\Pi'\rVert^2\ \le\ (n-2)^2\big(2w+w^2/N\big).$$
*The isotypic scalars give $4\lVert I\#\Pi'\rVert^2\le4(n-2)^2w^2/N+(n-4)^2w$, and $(n-4)^2w$ sits under $2(n-2)^2w$ with room about $(n^2-4)w$. The quadratic terms are of order $(n-2)w^2$ ($\operatorname{tr}\Pi'\#\Pi'\le w(w-1)$ by the single-bracket cap), so the room is spent at $w$ of order $n/2$.* **So the quartic bound holds near the whole algebra, for codimension up to order $n$** (constants to be pinned before this is recorded as a result). **Obstacle: the regime I73 needs is $d\le(n-5)^2/\theta_0^2$, codimension about $3n$ and more, where the quadratic terms are no longer small and their signs are what decide; at the other end, the operator-norm bounds cover only $d\le3$.** *With batch 25 these are two approaches in this round, each ending at a named obstacle, and the middle range of $d$ is untouched by both.*

## Batch 28 (derivation at effort `xhigh`, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): what the escalated pass settled about lifting L258

**One extension, proved.** *L258's argument never used that the weights are $0$ or $1$: for every real symmetric $W$ with zero diagonal, $\sum_{i<k}\big((W^2)_{ik}\big)^2\le(n-2)^2\big(\sum_{i<k}W_{ik}^2\big)^2/N$, since $\sum_{i<k}(W^2)_{ik}^2=\tfrac12\big(\operatorname{tr}W^4-\sum_i(W^2)_{ii}^2\big)$, $\operatorname{tr}W^4\le c_n(\operatorname{tr}W^2)^2$ for traceless $W$, and $\sum_i(W^2)_{ii}^2\ge(\operatorname{tr}W^2)^2/n$ (screen: $3000$ random $W$ at $n=4,7,12$, largest ratio $0.983$, $0.779$, $0.696$).* **So the codegree inequality is a statement about any traceless symmetric matrix; subspaces enter only through which matrix is used.**

**Three mechanisms ruled out.**
1. **The frame-diagonal weight matrix.** *For a general $V$ the natural weights are the diagonal of $\Pi$ in a frame, $W_{ik}=\langle e_i\wedge e_k,\Pi\,e_i\wedge e_k\rangle$. At $\Lambda^2_+$ in the standard frame $W=\tfrac12(J-I)$ gives $1.5$ against $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert^2=12$: the diagonal loses the coherence of non-decomposable elements by a factor $8$, so no frame choice built on it can dominate.*
2. **Completely positive norms.** *$\Pi\#\Pi=\tfrac12\Psi_V(\Pi)$ with $\Psi_V(X)=\sum_{x\in V}\mathrm{ad}_xX\,\mathrm{ad}_x^{\mathsf T}$, whose $2\to2$ norm is at most $\lVert C_V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2(n-2)$; this gives $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F\le(n-2)\sqrt d$, short of $(n-2)d/\sqrt N$ by $\sqrt{N/d}$, because the Casimir cap is attained by elements of any subspace that contains a full star.*
3. **Monotone growth.** *Adding $y\perp V$ changes $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert^2$ by $4\langle y,(\Pi\#\Pi\#\Pi)y\rangle+4\lVert\Pi\#yy^{\mathsf T}\rVert^2$, whose average over $V^\perp$ is $4\big(\tfrac12\langle\Pi\#\Pi,C_V\rangle-\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert^2\big)+4\sum_y\lVert\Pi\#yy^{\mathsf T}\rVert^2$; neither sign is controlled, so no induction on $d$ follows.*

**The cleanest form of what is open.** *For every orthonormal basis $\{z_k\}$ of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ and every index set $K$ of size $d$: $\sum_{k,l,m,p\in K}\langle[z_k,z_l],[z_m,z_p]\rangle^2\le4(n-2)^2d^2/N$ ($n\ge9$). It is a densest-subset statement for the non-negative $4$-tensor $\langle[z_k,z_l],[z_m,z_p]\rangle^2$ normalised by $\lvert K\rvert^2$, true at the whole set with equality, on every coordinate subset (L258), on every subalgebra (L256), and in every screen.* **A proof needs an inequality that sees coherence across non-decomposable elements, which the fourth-moment argument sees only through a matrix, and no matrix carrying it for general $V$ has been found.**

**Search for the open core (batch 28's densest-subset form).** *Searched: norms of structure constants restricted to subspaces, fourth-power commutator sums over a basis, densest subsets of the bracket tensor. The one relevant source, Mehta–Normand–Gupta 1983 ([on the shelf](../references/mehta-normand-gupta-1983-contracted-products/)), proves identities for products of structure constants contracted over the WHOLE algebra (one free index: zero; two: Cartan–Killing; three: proportional to $f$); the open bound contracts over a subspace, which those identities do not reach. ## Batch 29 (standing level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `medium`): approach D, the complement split

**Identity.** *With $\Pi'=I-\Pi$ and $I\#I=(n-2)I$, $\Pi\#\Pi=(n-2)I-2\,\Pi\#\Pi'-\Pi'\#\Pi'$, so*
$$\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F^2=(n-2)\operatorname{tr}(\Pi\#\Pi)-2\langle\Pi\#\Pi,\Pi\#\Pi'\rangle-\langle\Pi\#\Pi,\Pi'\#\Pi'\rangle,$$
*every term non-negative ($\#$ of positive operators is positive).* **Screen at $n=9$**: *on every extremal case the mixed term vanishes and the complement term carries the whole gap: $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ $90=210-0-120$; $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ $1008=1176-0-168$; $\Lambda^2_+$ $12=42-0-30$; the star $28=196-0-168$. ## Batch 30 (derivation at effort `xhigh`, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): the three open statements are one norm, that of Hamilton's $\#$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak{so}(n)$

**The norm.** *On $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathfrak g$, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{so}(n)$, with the Frobenius norm and the record normalisation, $F(A,B,C)=\langle A\#B,C\rangle$ is a symmetric trilinear form (batch 21). Let $\kappa_n=\sup_{X\ne0}F(X,X,X)/\lVert X\rVert^3$. By Banach's theorem on symmetric multilinear forms over a real Hilbert space, $\kappa_n=\sup\lVert X\#Y\rVert/(\lVert X\rVert\lVert Y\rVert)=\sup\lVert X\#X\rVert/\lVert X\rVert^2$.* **So every open statement of this note is a case of one bound:**
- *[L257](../findings/second-order.md)'s quartic bound is $\lVert\Pi\#\Pi\rVert_F\le\kappa_nd$ on projectors;*
- *the internal-mass cap of [L255](../findings/second-order.md) is $F(\Pi,\Pi,\Pi)=\mathcal M(V)/2\le\kappa_nd^{3/2}$;*
- *and I73's symmetric family needs neither: $W=a(P_2-P_0)$ has $\operatorname{tr}W^3=a^3(p-p)=0$, so at a unit critical point $\theta=P(W)=F(W,W,W)\le\kappa_n$ directly.*

$$\textbf{Statement H.}\qquad \kappa_n=\frac{n-2}{\sqrt N}\qquad(n\ge9),$$
*which closes all three at once, since $(n-2)/\sqrt N<\theta_0(n)$ at every $n$ ([L256](../findings/second-order.md)'s `whole_below_theta0`). At $n=3$ it holds, since $X^\#=\operatorname{cof}X$ there and $\kappa_3=1/\sqrt3$ ([r1220](REVIEW_r1220.md), [r1221](REVIEW_r1221.md)).*

**Two equality families, proved.** *At $X=I$, $I\#I=(n-2)I$ gives the value $(n-2)/\sqrt N$. **Approach 1, idempotents.** *A maximiser $X$ is critical, $X\#X=\kappa_nX$, so $C=X/\kappa_n$ satisfies $C\#C=C$ with $\lVert C\rVert=1/\kappa_n$, and H says every nonzero $\#$-idempotent has $\lVert C\rVert^2\ge N/(n-2)^2$. $$s=\frac{(n-2)s^2}N+\frac{n-4}2\lVert C_1\rVert^2-\lVert C_2\rVert^2+2\lVert C_3\rVert^2,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\lVert C\rVert^2=\frac{2s^2}n-s+\frac{n-2}2\lVert C_1\rVert^2+3\lVert C_3\rVert^2,$$
*exact on $I/(n-2)$, $\Lambda^2_+/2$, $\mathfrak{so}(3),\mathfrak{so}(5),\mathfrak{so}(7)$ and $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_4$ at $n=9$ ([check_I73_sharp_norm_identities.py](../scripts/misc/check_I73_sharp_norm_identities.py), residuals $10^{-15}$). **Approach 2, realignment and partial transpose.** *Write $Z=\iota(X)=\sum_i\mu_iv_i\otimes v_i$ on $\mathbb R^n\otimes\mathbb R^n$ ($v_i$ record-orthonormal skew matrices, $X=\sum\mu_iv_iv_i^{\mathsf T}$). $$\textbf{U.}\quad\text{on a compact semisimple Lie algebra with an invariant metric, the positive-definite }\#\text{-idempotents are exactly }\textstyle\bigoplus_i\Pi_i/c_i.$$
