## G1, the index inclusion is not necessary, and here is a Lie algebra that shows it

**What Jacobi actually gives.** With B(i,j) = {k : {i,j,k} not in Z} the containment [E_i, E_j] contained in the sum of E_m over m in B(i,j) holds, so [[E_i,E_j],E_k] is contained in the span of E_l over l in the union of B(m,k) for m in B(i,j), and that is an **upper bound on the support, not a description of it**. The Jacobi identity gives [[E_i,E_j],E_k] contained in [E_i,[E_j,E_k]] + [[E_i,E_k],E_j], hence contained in the span of E_l over l in the right-hand union. Both sides are upper bounds, and an inclusion between two upper bounds does not follow from an inclusion between the spaces they bound. The claimed index inclusion is therefore an additional hypothesis (that bracket supports **compose exactly**) and it fails in two independent ways: the contributions of different m in B(i,j) can cancel in E_l, and, even with a single m, [E_i,E_j] is generally a **proper subspace** U of E_m, so [U,E_k] can miss E_l while [E_m,E_k] hits it.

**The counterexample.** Take g = so(4) = su(2)_A + su(2)_C with the orthonormal basis A_1, A_2, A_3, C_1, C_2, C_3, brackets [A_i,A_j] = eps_ijk A_k and [C_i,C_j] = eps_ijk C_k and [A_i,C_j] = 0, and the ad-invariant inner product in which that basis is orthonormal, so &lt;[X,Y],Z&gt; is totally antisymmetric, exactly as [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) requires. Decompose orthogonally as E_1 = span(A_1,A_2), E_2 = span(A_3,C_1,C_2), E_3 = span(C_3). Then, checking all ten types by hand: t_112 = &lt;[A_1,A_2],A_3&gt; is nonzero and t_223 = &lt;[C_1,C_2],C_3&gt; is nonzero, and **every other type vanishes**, t_111 and t_113 because [E_1,E_1] = span(A_3) lies in E_2; t_122 and t_123 because [E_1,E_2] lies in E_1; t_133 because [E_1,E_3] = 0; t_222 because [E_2,E_2] = span(C_3) lies in E_3; t_233 because [E_2,E_3] lies in E_2; t_333 because E_3 is one-dimensional.

**The exact vanishing set is Z = {111, 113, 122, 123, 133, 222, 233, 333}, and the test rejects it.** The table is B(1,1) = {2}, B(1,2) = {1}, B(1,3) = empty, B(2,2) = {3}, B(2,3) = {2}, B(3,3) = empty. At (i,j,k) = (1,1,2) the left union is B(2,2) = {3} and the right union is B(1,1) = {2}, so {3} is not contained in {2} and the inclusion fails. It fails again at (1,1,3), (2,3,1) and (3,2,1). Jacobi holds identically (g is a Lie algebra) so **the failure of the inclusion does not contradict Z being the exact vanishing set**, which is precisely the sentence the enumeration rests on. *Screened*: the ten types, the B-table, the four failures and a zero Jacobi residual were all reproduced in seconds on the structure constants I wrote down for this purpose; the derivation above is by hand and does not depend on the run.

**Why it fails, in one line, and why the same line reaches a four-subset.** [E_1,E_1] is the *proper* subspace U = span(A_3) of E_2, and [U,E_2] = 0 has no E_3 component, while [E_2,E_2] does. The identical step is what rejects the four-subset {111, 113, 122, 123}: there B(1,1) = {2} and B(1,2) = {1} and B(2,2) = {2,3}, so the test fails at (1,1,2) for exactly the reason it fails above. I have not exhibited a *realisable* four-subset counterexample (my witness has eight zeros) so the honest statement is that the argument is invalid and one of the four-subsets it rejects is rejected by the invalid step, not that a specific one of the 89 is wrongly rejected.

**A second, independent frame error: "exact" excludes the degenerate vertices.** A vertex of a four-dimensional polyhedron has **at least** four active constraints, so its exact vanishing set may have five or more members; a support with more zeros is invisible to an enumeration that builds B from a four-subset and declares the other six types nonvanishing. This is not a corner case here, [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) attains its optimum at exactly such a degenerate vertex, the balanced product where t_112 and t_222 vanish together, and [DERIVE-sp3](DERIVE-sp3.md) says so in its own consistency check. My eight-zero witness is one instance: no four-subset of it is its exact vanishing set, so the enumeration never sees the configuration at all.

**And a third thing the list cannot do, which is a scope point rather than an error.** Pruning vertices by a realisability test does not lower theta*(lambda). The bound in force is theta &lt;= theta*(lambda) = max over **all** vertices of Pi(lambda); a maximum taken over a Jacobi-filtered sublist is smaller and bounds nothing, unless one first proves the unfiltered maximiser is realisable. [DERIVE-sp3](DERIVE-sp3.md) flags the realisability gap and [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) repeats that the list is a candidate list, so this is agreement rather than a new objection, but it means the number that eventually comes off the list needs an argument that the discarded vertices were discarded legitimately, and that argument currently runs through the refuted inclusion.

**What survives.** The count itself is reproducible: my own independent implementation of the stated inclusion returns 121 of 210, passes the split symmetric pair {113, 123, 223, 333}, rejects the replacement negative control {111, 112, 122, 123}, finds no full grading and finds 21 supports with some [E_i,E_j] vanishing entirely, every figure [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) reports. So the arithmetic is sound and the transcription is faithful; it is the interpretation of the number that is wrong. **121 is the number of four-subsets passing a heuristic filter, not an upper bound on the admissible supports.**

## G2, the retraction is right, and it is right for the right reason

**Total antisymmetry, and what it does to a grading.** &lt;[X,Y],Z&gt; is totally antisymmetric because the inner product on so(n) is ad-invariant ([conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md)), so T_abc is a function of the multiset and a type is nonzero only if every reading of it is permitted. For a Z_m-grading with an invariant form, the form pairs g_a with g_{-a}, so B([g_a,g_b],g_c) can be nonzero only when a + b + c = 0 mod m, symmetric in the three indices, as it must be. The condition a + b = c is the same statement read against a form that pairs g_a with g_a, and it is not symmetric; **the retraction has identified the error correctly.**

**And the real obstruction is exactly as stated.** On a real space with a positive-definite invariant form the eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal and each pairs with itself, so a nonzero t_abc needs c = a + b **and** a = b + c **and** b = a + c, which forces 2a = 2b = 2c = 0 mod m. At m = 2 that is vacuous and the symmetric pair is a genuine real orthogonal grading; at m = 3 only a = 0 survives. Concretely, if E_0, E_1, E_2 were orthogonal real parts of a Z_3-grading then t_112 = &lt;[E_1,E_1],E_2&gt; equals &lt;[E_1,E_2],E_1&gt; by antisymmetry, and [E_1,E_2] lies in E_0, which is orthogonal to E_1, so t_112 = 0 and hence [E_1,E_1] = 0; the same reading kills t_011, forcing [E_0,E_1] = 0. The three-part real Z_3-grading degenerates to nothing. **An order-3 element of SO(n) gives so(n) = g_0 + m with m = (g_omega + g_omegabar) intersected with the real form, a single subspace**, and m is not bracket-orthogonal to itself ([g_omega, g_omega] lies in g_omegabar, which pairs with g_omega) so the two-part decomposition is not a symmetric pair either. Every consequence the note withdraws follows from a claim that is false, and the withdrawal is correct.

**The centraliser count, checked.** An order-3 element of SO(n) is conjugate to a block with eigenvalue 1 of multiplicity p and the pair omega, omegabar of multiplicity q each, n = p + 2q, and its centraliser algebra is so(p) + u(q) of real dimension p(p-1)/2 + q^2. At n = 12 the six classes are (p,q) = (10,1), (8,2), (6,3), (4,4), (2,5), (0,6), giving dim g_0 = 46, 32, 24, 22, 26, 36 and the complementary halves (66 - dim g_0)/2 = 10, 17, 21, 22, 20, 15. **Those are exactly the six triples, so the arithmetic is right and the retraction's description of it (correct as complex dimensions), is right.** Note also that the two equal entries in every triple are forced, being conjugate complex eigenspaces, which is the structural reason the triples could never have excluded [DERIVE-t11](DERIVE-t11.md)'s witness (30, 20, 16) with three distinct multiplicities: the comparison was between a real spectrum and a complex dimension count.

**The surviving half, with two caveats.** For a symmetric pair h + p with h split as E_1 + E_2 and p = E_3: t_113, t_223 and t_123 vanish because [h,h] lies in h, orthogonal to p; t_333 vanishes because [p,p] lies in h. That is four zeros and the reading is correct. **Caveat one: "exactly four" is a genericity statement, not a theorem.** If the split of h happens to be by ideals then t_112 and t_122 vanish too and the support has six zeros; the four-zero count needs the split to be a genuine non-ideal splitting, which is where the eigenvalue structure would have to come in and does not. **Caveat two: "a genuine vertex" is not what the enumeration confirmed.** Passing the index inclusion is a statement about brackets; being a vertex of Pi(lambda) requires the four active nonnegativity constraints to be independent against the four-dimensional affine set, which is a linear-algebra check nobody has run. The control is a control for the Jacobi filter, not a vertex certificate.

## G3, the identity re-derived, and the cut is sound

**The reduced rows, checked first.** With a in class i and mu_j = m_j - delta_ij, the three row families of [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) come straight out of (A), (B), (C) by counting ordered pairs (b,c) with b, c distinct from a and from each other: 2 mu_j mu_k of them carry t_ijk for j &lt; k and mu_j(mu_j - 1) carry t_ijj, and in (B) the two orders of a mixed pair carry nu_j and nu_k, which is where the factor (nu_j + nu_k) comes from. **I re-derived all three rows before opening the note and they agree.**

**The multiplier and the identity.** For phi(x) = x^2 + p x + q the combination (C_i) + p (B_i) + q (A_i) gives the type coefficients mu_j(mu_j - 1) phi(nu_j) on {i,j,j} and 2 mu_j mu_k psi(nu_j, nu_k) on {i,j,k}, where psi(x,y) = (phi(x) + phi(y))/2 - (x - y)^2 / 2 is the polarisation, and the right-hand side is 2 nu_i theta - 2 phi(nu_i) + 2(n - 1) q. With i = 1 and phi(x) = (x - nu_1)(x - nu_2) the four surviving coefficients come out as minus mu_1 mu_2 (nu_1 - nu_2)^2 on t_112, minus mu_1 mu_3 (nu_1 - nu_2)(nu_1 - nu_3) on t_113, plus mu_2 mu_3 (nu_1 - nu_2)(nu_2 - nu_3) on t_123 and plus mu_3(mu_3 - 1)(nu_1 - nu_3)(nu_2 - nu_3) on t_133, against 2 nu_1 [(n-1) nu_2 + theta]. **Every one matches [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) exactly, sign included.**

**The sign reasoning.** Under nu_1 &gt; nu_2 &gt; nu_3 every bracket factor is positive, so moving the two negative terms across leaves t_123 and t_133 with strictly positive coefficients and a right-hand side of 2 nu_1[(n-1) nu_2 + theta] plus nonnegative multiples of t_112 and t_113. At nu_1 &gt; nu_2 &gt; 0 and theta &gt; 0 that is strictly positive, so **t_123 and t_133 cannot both vanish**, and the conclusion holds at every feasible point, not only at vertices, which is stronger than the note claims for it. Two small scope notes: nu_3 &lt; 0 is not used (it is automatic by tracelessness), so the real hypotheses are nu_2 &gt; 0 and theta &gt; 0; and t_133's coefficient needs mu_3 &gt;= 2, which the standing m_i &gt;= 3 supplies. The count of four-subsets containing both types is 8-choose-2 = 28, as stated.

## G4, the identities are right, the accounting is not, and the cut that does the work is the unproved one

**All nine identities check.** The general right-hand side is 2 nu_i theta - 2 phi(nu_i) + 2(n-1) nu_a nu_b. When i is one of the two roots, phi(nu_i) = 0 and the right-hand side is 2 nu_i [(n-1) nu_j + theta] with j the other root, six of these, two per row. When i is the third value, phi(nu_i) is the product of the two gaps and the right-hand side is the quadratic 2[nu_i theta - nu_i^2 + nu_i(nu_a + nu_b) + (n-2) nu_a nu_b], three of these, one per row. **I generated all nine four-subsets and all nine right-hand sides independently and every row of the table is correct**, including the observation that every one of the nine contains type 123, which follows because the annihilated types {i,a,a} and {i,b,b} always carry a repeated index and so are never {1,2,3}.

**"Exactly four survive" is right, and it needs m_i &gt;= 3.** For each of the nine, the four unannihilated coefficients are nonzero: the mixed ones because psi(nu_j, nu_k) reduces to half a product of two distinct gaps in every case, the diagonal ones because mu_j(mu_j - 1) phi(nu_j) needs mu_j &gt;= 2. In the three identities with i outside the root pair the surviving t_iii coefficient carries mu_i(mu_i - 1) with mu_i = m_i - 1, so **m_i &gt;= 3 is load-bearing there** and m_i = 2 would collapse that identity to three surviving types. The forbidding argument itself is valid and does not depend on signs: the identity holds at every feasible point, so four vanishing types force the right-hand side to vanish.

**Defect one, the Pi-saturation reading is false at nu_2 &lt; 0.** The six simple right-hand sides vanish when theta = -(n-1) nu_j. For nu_j &gt; 0 that is negative and excluded by theta &gt; 0. For j = 3 it is theta = (n-1)|nu_3|, and that **is** the Pi-bound saturated, which I verified is a bound at d = 3, not merely at d = 2, by taking row 1 against phi(x) = (x - nu_1)(x - nu_3): all four surviving coefficients, on t_112, t_113, t_122 and t_123, are then negative, so the left side is at most zero and nu_1 &gt; 0 gives theta &lt;= (n-1)|nu_3|. But for j = 2 with nu_2 &lt; 0 the degeneracy sits at theta = (n-1)|nu_2|, which is **strictly inside** the Pi-bound since |nu_2| &lt; |nu_3|, and nothing on the record excludes it; the same multiplier trick at row 3 against (x - nu_2)(x - nu_3) gives mixed signs and no bound at nu_2. **So "vanishing exactly at Pi-bound saturation" is right for three of the six and wrong for the other three**, and the correct hypothesis is theta different from (n-1)|nu_2| as well.

**Defect two, the six proved exclusions cut nothing, and the three that cut are the unproved ones.** Running the stated index inclusion over the nine four-subsets, exactly **three** pass it, and they are {111,112,113,123}, {122,123,222,223} and {123,133,233,333}, precisely the three whose right-hand sides are the quadratics. The six with the clean Pi-bound reading were all already rejected by the Jacobi filter. **So the entire 121 -> 118 step is carried by the three identities whose right-hand side the note never shows to be nonzero**, and "the Pi-bound strict" is not a hypothesis that covers them. Each of those three supports says structurally that E_i is abelian (t_iii, t_iij and t_iik all vanish) together with t_123 = 0, and their right-hand sides vanish on a codimension-one locus in the spectrum that no recorded bound rules out. Screening the three at [DERIVE-t11](DERIVE-t11.md)'s witness, n = 12, multiplicities (20, 16, 30) on values (1.2m, 0.375m, -m) with m = 61.05^(-1/2) and theta = 1.2917, returns 0.1011, -0.1356 and -0.1338, all comfortably nonzero, so nothing is wrong at that point; but one witness is not the proof the RESULT sentence asserts. **The honest cut, on what is proved, is 210 -> 121 -> 112, not 110.**

**Defect three, the prose contradicts its own arithmetic.** [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) writes "3 of the nine were already excluded by Jacobi, so the count falls from 121 to 118". If three had been excluded, six would remain and the count would fall to 115. ## G5, the action is right, the conclusion is right, the counting is not the reason

**The action of rho(E_jk), verified.** With E_jk = e_j tensor e_k - e_k tensor e_j acting on Lambda^2 by the derivation rule, rho(E_jk) sends e_k ^ e_i to e_j ^ e_i and e_j ^ e_i to minus e_k ^ e_i for i outside {j,k}; it kills e_j ^ e_k, because the two terms cancel, and it kills every e_a ^ e_b with a and b both outside {j,k}. **That is exactly the stated action.**

**The orbit tangent, and it is confined.** With M diagonal on coordinate bivectors, the commutator X = [rho(E_jk), M] sends e_ki to (M_ki - M_ji) e_ji and e_ji to (M_ki - M_ji) e_ki, and kills e_jk and every bivector disjoint from {j,k}. So X is the symmetric operator with coordinates x_i = M_ki - M_ji in the basis f_i = e_ji odot e_ki, and it lies **entirely** in the pair block of {j,k}. I also checked it satisfies that block's Ricci relation, which the note does not: the relation is that the coordinates sum to zero, and Ricci-flatness of M gives the sum of M_ji over all i not equal to j is zero, hence the sum of x_i over i outside {j,k} is M_jk - M_kj = 0. So the orbit direction genuinely lies in the (n-3)-dimensional block inside W_n, not merely in the ambient (n-2)-dimensional character space.

**The counting argument is unnecessary and, as stated, wrong.** The conclusion needs no dimension count at all: the map A goes to [rho(A), M] is linear, the E_jk form a basis of so(n), and each basis vector's image lies in its own pair block, so **the whole orbit tangent space is contained in the direct sum of the pair blocks**, which is what "the 4-set blocks and the diagonal block contain no orbit direction" asserts. By contrast the stated reason, that there are n-choose-2 pair blocks and dim so(n) = n-choose-2 so the orbit is "exhausted", is not valid: it needs the images to be independent, and they are not in general, because the orbit's dimension is n-choose-2 minus the dimension of the isotropy. **At S^3 x S^3 the orbit is 9-dimensional, not 15**, exactly because the six generators E_jk with j and k in the same factor lie in the isotropy and give X = 0; the record's own figure of a 9-dimensional orbit is the counterexample to the counting. The right statement is: **each pair block contains at most one orbit direction, and exactly one when M_ji and M_ki differ for some i.**

**The consequence for the nine eigenvectors is confirmed, and the arithmetic closes.** Since the 4-set blocks meet the orbit trivially, the nine plus-one-half eigenvectors [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) places in the (2,2)-split 4-set blocks at S^3 x S^3 are genuinely off-orbit. The count is consistent: 3-choose-2 times 3-choose-2 is nine (2,2)-split 4-subsets, and nine orbit directions from the nine mixed pair blocks, giving the recorded dim E(1/2) = 18 with no residue. **One correction to the corollary drawn in [DERIVE-df1](DERIVE-df1.md)**: the criterion on a pair block is L_e &lt;= 1/2 on an explicit (n-4)-dimensional complement only where the orbit direction in that block is nonzero; in the six within-factor blocks at S^3 x S^3 the orbit direction vanishes and the complement is the full n-3.

## What I would put to the session

**Withdraw the necessity claim for the index inclusion, keep the number as a heuristic filter, and say so wherever the number is used.** The counterexample is small and checkable by hand, and the honest form of the enumeration's output is "121 four-subsets pass a support-composition heuristic", with the 89 rejections carrying no proof.

**Change 110 to 112 and mark the three quadratic conditions as owed.** They are the only part of the nine-identity cut that does any work, and proving them nonzero (or exhibiting a spectrum where one vanishes), is a self-contained piece of analysis on a codimension-one locus that needs no computation.

**Correct the sentence "3 of the nine were already excluded by Jacobi" to six, and correct "vanishing exactly at Pi-bound saturation" to name the nu_2 case.** Both are transcription-level and both are cited downstream.

**Replace the pair-block counting sentence with the linearity argument, and drop "exhausted".** The conclusion is stronger and cheaper than the argument offered for it, and the argument offered for it is contradicted by the record's own orbit dimension at S^3 x S^3.
