## Observations

**O1. `L221b_split` generalises (b) step 1 faithfully.** ([L221Ops.lean:107-114](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L221Ops.lean)) The hypotheses are `c ∈ Q₁ \ Q₂` and `e ∈ Q₂ \ Q₁`. Four-sets with $\lvert Q_1\cap Q_2\rvert=2$ are non-nested, since $2<4$ leaves two indices of each outside the other, so the record's case is an instance. The proof uses only three facts: `foxProd2_char2` multiplies characters, `setChar_symmDiff` gives $\chi_{Q_i}\chi_{Q_j}=\chi_{Q_k}$ on sign vectors, and the flips at $c$, at $e$ and at both take the values $(-1,1,-1)$, $(1,-1,-1)$, $(-1,-1,1)$ on $(\chi_1,\chi_2,\chi_3)$ ([L221Ops.lean:164-171](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L221Ops.lean)), which I checked by hand from `setChar_sgn` and `setChar_mul` (with $c\in Q_3$ and $e\in Q_3$ at lines 162-163). None of these needs Weyl, unit norm or $\theta>0$: the split of $Q(W)=\theta W$ is a statement about sign-equivariance of the bilinear product, so it is true in the stated generality, exactly as r1790 found for `L220_split`. The Lean route bypasses the record's duality argument and does not state $\mathcal S$ as a subalgebra. Non-nestedness is what makes the four characters pairwise distinct: at $Q_1=Q_2$ the character of $\nu_3$ is $1$ and the split into four equations fails, which the hypotheses exclude. **Falsifier**: operators $D,\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3$ of the stated characters at non-nested $Q_1,Q_2$ with $Q(W)=\theta W$ and $2D\circ\nu_1+2\nu_2\circ\nu_3\ne\theta\nu_1$. The kernel check excludes it.
