## The route, as stated

At a unit global maximiser $W$ with $Q(W)=\rho W$, choose an orthonormal frame maximising $\lVert\pi_{\mathcal D}W\rVert^2$ and write $W=A+B$, $A=\pi_{\mathcal D}W$, $t=\lVert A\rVert$, $b=\lVert B\rVert$, $t^2+b^2=1$. **Note the normalisation: the author's $t$ is $\lVert A\rVert$, where [L214](../findings/second-order.md)'s $t$ is $\lVert D\rVert^2$.** The mixing gap is
$$(\mathrm{MG})\qquad\rho\le\max\Big\{\sqrt{3/2},\ \frac{P(A)}{\lVert A\rVert^3}\Big\},$$
and with [L57](../findings/problem.md) ($P(A)\le\Theta_n\lVert A\rVert^3$ for $A$ diagonal in an orthonormal frame) and $\Theta_n>\sqrt{3/2}$ at $n\ge12$ it gives $\rho\le\Theta_n$, which is Conjecture A. At even $n$, $\Theta_n=\theta_0(n)$.

## Identity (86), derived here from the definitions

Put $z=(A/t-tW)/b$, $\mathcal U=\tfrac12I+\tfrac12W\otimes W-\rho^{-1}L_W$, $\eta=\langle z,\mathcal Uz\rangle$, $C=(\mathcal U-\eta)z$. Decompose $B=\sum_UB_U$ over the nontrivial sign characters of the frame, $p_U=\lVert B_U\rVert^2$, and write $Q(A)-\tfrac{P(A)}{t^2}A=q_\perp$ and $Q(A,B_U)=\lambda_UB_U+Y_U$ with $Y_U\perp B_U$.

1. **$z$ is a unit vector orthogonal to $W$.** Using $A\perp B$, $A/t-tW=\tfrac{b^2}tA-tB$, whose squared norm is $b^4+t^2b^2=b^2$, and $\langle A/t-tW,W\rangle=t-t=0$.
2. **$C\perp W$ and $C\perp z$, hence $C\perp A$ and $C\perp B$.** $\mathcal UW=\tfrac12W+\tfrac12W-\rho^{-1}Q(W)=0$ uses criticality, and $\langle C,W\rangle=\langle z,\mathcal UW\rangle$ uses the self-adjointness of $\mathcal U$, which holds because $L_W$ is self-adjoint; $\langle C,z\rangle=\langle\mathcal Uz,z\rangle-\eta=0$ is the definition of $\eta$. Since $b\ne0$ and $t\ne0$, $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{W,z\}=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{A,B\}$.
3. **The components of $C$.** $\mathcal Uz=\tfrac12z-\rho^{-1}Q(W,z)$ because $z\perp W$, and $Q(W,z)=\big(Q(A)+\sum_UQ(A,B_U)\big)/(tb)-\rho tW/b$ by criticality. The trivial character of the sign group on operators on $\Lambda^2$ is exactly the diagonal operators, $Q$ is equivariant, so $Q(A)$ is diagonal and $Q(A,B_U)$ lies in the character $U$. Projecting:
$$\pi_{\mathcal D}C=\Big(\big(\tfrac12-\eta\big)\tfrac bt+\tfrac tb\Big)A-\frac{Q(A)}{\rho tb},\qquad \pi_UC=\frac{t(\tfrac12+\eta)}{b}B_U-\frac{\lambda_UB_U+Y_U}{\rho tb}.$$
4. **The two scalars are forced.** $\langle\pi_{\mathcal D}C,A\rangle=\langle C,A\rangle=0$ gives $(\tfrac12-\eta)b^2+t^2=P(A)/(\rho t^2)$, so $\pi_{\mathcal D}C=-q_\perp/(\rho tb)$. $\sum_U\langle\pi_UC,B_U\rangle=\langle C,B\rangle=0$ gives $\bar\lambda:=\sum_Up_U\lambda_U/b^2=\rho t^2(\eta+\tfrac12)$, which is (86)'s companion, so $\pi_UC=-\big(Y_U+(\lambda_U-\bar\lambda)B_U\big)/(\rho tb)$.
5. **Pythagoras over the orthogonal characters**, with $Y_U\perp B_U$:
$$\rho^2t^2b^2\lVert C\rVert^2=\lVert q_\perp\rVert^2+\sum_U\lVert Y_U\rVert^2+\sum_Up_U(\lambda_U-\bar\lambda)^2 .$$

## The shape of $q=P(f)$ on the surviving modes

**Derived here, unreviewed.** Index a $2+2$ four-set by $(\pi,\kappa)$, $\pi$ an edge of the complete graph on $P$ and $\kappa$ one on $Q'$, and write $f=\sum\sigma_{\pi\kappa}\hat\nu_{\pi\kappa}$ with $\hat\nu$ the unit $+c_*$ modes. Each $\hat\nu\circ\hat\nu$ is diagonal and so orthogonal to $f$, so $q=P(f)$ collects only triangles $S,T,S\triangle T$ of active characters. **For three $2+2$ four-sets this forces $\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3$ to be the edges of a triangle in $P$ and $\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3$ those of a triangle in $Q'$, matched in any of the $3!$ ways**: $S\triangle T=(\pi_1\triangle\pi_2)\cup(\kappa_1\triangle\kappa_2)$ is a $2+2$ four-set exactly when each pair of edges shares one vertex, and $\pi_1=\pi_2$ would give a four-set inside $Q'$, which vanishes. So $q=6\sum_{\text{hyperedges}}\kappa_{STU}\,\sigma_S\sigma_T\sigma_U$ over a $3$-uniform hypergraph in which each vertex has degree $2(m-2)^2$ at the balanced product.

- **Why the crude bound fails.** The unit-mode constant $\kappa_{STU}$ is independent of $m$, so a degree bound on the cubic grows like $m$, while the sufficient condition asks $q^2\le d^2\tfrac{m^2(m-3)}{(m-1)^3}\to d^2$. **Closedness has to do the work**: $\sum_S\hat\nu_S\circ\hat\nu_S\,\sigma_S^2=\tau e$ with $\tau=c_*$ small, and every product $f_S\circ f_T$ with $\lvert S\cap T\rvert=3$ lands in a pair character and must cancel, since $f\circ f=\tau e+qf$ has no pair component.
- **Next**: the constant $\kappa_{STU}$ and its signs from the author's action, then the two closedness constraints as linear conditions on the $\sigma^2$ and $\sigma\sigma$, to see whether they confine $f$ to few four-sets, where the per-mode frame bound then limits $r$.

## The surviving cubic: an exact model, a screened maximum of $\tfrac56$, and a candidate lemma

- **The triangle constant.** For unit $+c_*$ modes on a hyperedge, $\langle\hat\nu_S\circ\hat\nu_T,\hat\nu_U\rangle=\pm\tfrac{5\sqrt3}{24}$, the sign the parity of the edge matching ([screen_I121_triangle_constant.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_triangle_constant.py), whose $Q$ reproduces $\theta_0(6)=1.054093$ at $S^3\times S^3$ with criticality residual $2\times10^{-16}$). So on the surviving modes, **$P(f)=\tfrac{5\sqrt3}4F(\sigma)$ with $F(\sigma)=\sum_{t,s}\det\sigma[\mathop{\mathrm{edges}}t,\mathop{\mathrm{edges}}s]$**, $\sigma$ the $\binom m2\times\binom m2$ matrix of mode coefficients and $t,s$ triangles in $P$ and $Q'$; the model matches the true $P(f)$ to $8\times10^{-17}$ at $n=8$. At $m=3$, $F=\det\sigma$.
- **Screen, not evidence.** The maximum of $P(f)$ on the unit sphere of the surviving modes, from forty random starts, is $\tfrac5{12}$ at $m=3$ (the determinant's $\tfrac1{3\sqrt3}$) and **$\tfrac56$ at $m=4,5,6$**, independent of $m$ ([screen_I121_mode_cubic_max.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_mode_cubic_max.py)), against the sufficient bound $d\,m\sqrt{m-3}/(m-1)^{3/2}$: $0.882$, $1.061$, $1.149$, increasing to $\sqrt2$. The maximiser at $m=4$ has full support built on the perfect matchings of $K_4$.
- **Candidate lemma, REFUTED at $m\ge9$ by the identity.** *The uniform bound $\lvert F(\sigma)\rvert\le\tfrac2{3\sqrt3}\lVert\sigma\rVert_F^3$ is false*: with $P$ identified with $Q'$, $\sigma=I$ has $F(I)=\binom m3$ and $\lVert I\rVert_F^2=\binom m2$, so $F/\lVert\sigma\rVert^3=\binom m3/\binom m2^{3/2}$, $0.378$ at $m=8$ and $0.389>\tfrac2{3\sqrt3}=0.385$ at $m=9$, tending to $\tfrac{\sqrt2}3=0.471$. The screen's $\tfrac56$ at $m\le6$ was a constant read off too few dimensions. **What the argument needs is only the endpoint bound**, $q^2\le d^2\tfrac{m^2(m-3)}{(m-1)^3}$, and the identity satisfies it: $q=0.842$ against $1.262$ at $m=9$, about $1.02$ against $\sqrt2$ in the limit. **If $\max q$ stays below the endpoint bound at every $m\ge4$, the balanced closed branch with $b>0$ is empty**: the closed algebra's maximum on the circle is then attained only at $e$, and $W$ with $b>0$ is not a global maximiser.
- **Why $\tfrac56$ at small $m$, and what replaces it.** Read $\sigma\in\mathfrak{so}(m)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(m)$; then $F$ is the contraction of the two structure tensors through $\sigma$, the determinant on one $\mathfrak{so}(3)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(3)$. The self-dual $\mathfrak{so}(3)\subset\mathfrak{so}(4)$ has, in the unit Frobenius basis, structure constants $\sqrt2$ times a coordinate $\mathfrak{so}(3)$'s, which gives $\tfrac56$, twice the determinant's $\tfrac5{12}$; the sharp constant for skew-symmetric commutators is Bloch–Iserles's, $\lVert[X,Y]\rVert_F\le\lVert X\rVert_F\lVert Y\rVert_F$ at $n\ge4$ ([Theorem 6](../references/bloch-iserles-2005-skew-commutators/notes.md)). **But the identity $\sigma=I$, the Casimir of $\mathfrak{so}(m)$, overtakes it at $m=9$**, and the screen at $m=6,8,9,10,12$ ([screen_I121_mode_cubic_max_large.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_mode_cubic_max_large.py)) finds $\max q=0.833,\,0.833,\,0.842,\,0.861,\,0.888$, equal to the identity's value from $m=9$ on, against the endpoint bound $1.149,\,1.237,\,1.262,\,1.281,\,1.308$. *The double-sharp route of the previous version fails for the same reason: $\lVert\nabla F(\sigma)\rVert\le\tfrac2{\sqrt3}\lVert\sigma\rVert^2$ is violated by the identity at large $m$.*
- **The inequality the argument needs, with room.** $\sup P(f)$ over unit surviving modes stays below the endpoint bound $d\,m\sqrt{m-3}/(m-1)^{3/2}$ at every $m\ge4$; on the screen the room is at least $38\%$, and the identity's value tends to $\tfrac{5\sqrt6}{12}\approx1.02$ against $\sqrt2$. **A proof may therefore afford crude steps**: an $m$-uniform bound of about $1.1$ on $P$ over these modes would do for every $m\ge6$.
- **Attempt 1 at the needed bound, and why it fails.** In the Lie normalisation $\Phi=3F$, $P(f)=\tfrac{5\sqrt3}{12}\Phi$, and for $S:\mathfrak{so}(m)'\to\mathfrak{so}(m)$ the map of $\sigma$, $\Phi(S)=\sum_{i,j}\langle[Sb_i,Sb_j],S[b_i,b_j]\rangle$ over an orthonormal basis $b_i$. Cauchy–Schwarz over the pairs, Bloch–Iserles on the first factor ($\sum\lVert[Sb_i,Sb_j]\rVert^2\le\lVert S\rVert_F^4$) and the Casimir on the second ($\sum_{i,j}\lVert S[b_i,b_j]\rVert^2=(m-2)\lVert S\rVert_F^2$) give $\lvert\Phi\rvert\le\sqrt{m-2}\,\lVert S\rVert_F^3$, which grows with $m$. Bounding the first factor instead by $(m-2)\lambda_{\max}(SS^{\mathsf T})\lVert S\rVert_F^2$ gives $(m-2)\lVert S\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert S\rVert_F^2$, exact at the identity ($\tfrac{m-2}{\sqrt{\binom m2}}\to\sqrt2$) and loose on a concentrated $S$. **The needed bound is $\Phi\le\tfrac{12}{5\sqrt3}\times$ the endpoint bound, $1.59$ at $m=6$ rising to $1.96$**; a proof must interpolate between the spread extreme (the identity) and the concentrated one (the self-dual $\mathfrak{so}(4)$), for instance by splitting $S$ along its singular values.
- **Attempt 2: a reduction to one algebra (proved), and the conjecture it leaves.** With a Schmidt decomposition $\sigma=\sum_is_iA_i\otimes B_i$, $s_i\ge0$, $A_i$ and $B_i$ orthonormal, $\Phi=\sum_{ijk}s_is_js_ka_{ijk}b_{ijk}$ where $a_{ijk}=\langle[A_i,A_j],A_k\rangle$, $b_{ijk}=\langle[B_i,B_j],B_k\rangle$, so AM–GM on $\lvert a_{ijk}b_{ijk}\rvert$ gives
$$\Phi\le\tfrac12\big(T(s;A)+T(s;B)\big),\qquad T(s;A)=\sum_{i,j,k}s_is_js_k\langle[A_i,A_j],A_k\rangle^2\ \ge0 .$$
**So the balanced closed branch is empty at every $m\ge6$ if $T(s;A)\le1.59$ for every orthonormal system in every $\mathfrak{so}(m)$ and every unit $s\ge0$**, since then $P(f)=\tfrac{5\sqrt3}{12}\Phi\le1.149$, the endpoint bound at $m=6$, which increases with $m$. Equivalently $T=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(G\,\beta(G\otimes G)\beta^{\mathsf T}\big)$ for the positive operator $G=\sum_is_iA_iA_i^{\mathsf T}$ on $\mathfrak{so}(m)$ with $\lVert G\rVert_F=1$, $\beta$ the bracket. **Conjecture: $T<\sqrt2$.** On subalgebras with uniform weights, $T$ is the Casimir constant over the square root of the dimension: $\tfrac{k-2}{\sqrt{\binom k2}}$ for $\mathfrak{so}(k)$, increasing to $\sqrt2$ and never reaching it; $\tfrac k{\sqrt{k^2-1}}$ for $\mathfrak{su}(k)\subset\mathfrak{so}(2k)$, $\tfrac2{\sqrt3}$ at the self-dual $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; $\tfrac4{\sqrt{14}}$ for $\mathfrak g_2\subset\mathfrak{so}(7)$; $\tfrac5{\sqrt{21}}$ for $\mathfrak{spin}(7)\subset\mathfrak{so}(8)$. **The obstacle** is attempt 1's in a cleaner form: Bloch–Iserles bounds each commutator and the Casimir each row, and neither alone stops a large orthonormal system from being pairwise far from commuting; what is needed is a bound of order $r^{3/2}$ on the commutator energy $\sum_{i,j}\lVert[A_i,A_j]\rVert^2$ of an $r$-dimensional subspace of $\mathfrak{so}(m)$, which the subalgebras $\mathfrak{so}(k)$ saturate.
