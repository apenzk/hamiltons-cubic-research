## 1. [innerOp2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L264) and [normSq2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L268) against the record's

**The two definitions, side by side.** [innerOp](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L135) is `(1/2) * ∑ i : ι, ∑ j : ι, inner2 (R (phi i j)) (S (phi i j))`, and [innerOp2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L264) is `(1/2) * ∑ i : ι, ∑ j : ι, inner2 (applyPhi R i j) (applyPhi S i j)` with [applyPhi](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L260) the coercion of `R ⟨phi i j, _⟩`. **The outer `½` is the same constant, the summation is over the same `Finset.univ ×ˢ Finset.univ` of ORDERED pairs, and the inner pairing is the same [inner2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L39)**, which carries its own `½` in the half-trace. Term for term the only change is that `R (phi i j)` becomes the coercion of `R` applied to `phi i j` packaged with its membership proof. **So the `½` is not doubled, not dropped, and not moved.**

**Where both are defined, they agree, and the sense of *both* is the one section 0 fixes.** For any bare `Rb` and `Sb` extending `R` and `S`, `innerOp Rb Sb = innerOp2 R S`. I checked it rather than asserted it: `congr 1` on the outer `½`, then `Finset.sum_congr` twice, then the extension hypothesis at `⟨phi i j, phi_mem_skewSub i j⟩` in each slot. The theorem compiles. **The reason it needs no hypothesis about `Rb` off `Λ²` is that `innerOp` never reads its argument there**, `phi i j` being skew at every `i, j`, so every extension of the same operator returns the same number. `normSq Rb = normSq2 R` is that at `S = R`.

**The control.** The same proof script against the same statement with the outer `½` DROPPED is rejected, so the check is sensitive to the constant it was written to test. This matters because a factor is exactly the failure mode the brief names, and a check that passes under either normalisation would have tested nothing.

## 2. [normSq2_eq_zero](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L273)

**The derivation, step by step.** `normSq2 R = 0` unfolds to `(1/2) * Σ = 0`, and `linarith` gives `Σ = 0` where `Σ = ∑_i ∑_j ⟨R φ_ij, R φ_ij⟩`. [eq_zero_of_phi_sq_sum](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L112) takes that to `(R : skewSub ι →ₗ[ℝ] skewSub ι) = 0`, and [ContinuousLinearMap.ext](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Module/LinearMap.html#ContinuousLinearMap.ext) with `congrArg` at each point lifts the linear equality to the bundled one. Every step is the obvious one and the whole is on the standard three axioms.

**What it would take for the hypothesis to be met non-trivially: nothing can.** By [inner2_eq_sum](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L58) each summand is `½ ∑_{a,b} (R φ_ij)²_ab`, a sum of squares over `ℝ`, so `Σ = 0` forces every `R φ_ij = 0`, and [eq_zero_of_phi](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L68) turns that into `R = 0` because the `φ` family spans `Λ²` at finite `ι`. **A definiteness theorem is exactly one whose hypothesis only the zero vector satisfies**, so the absence of a non-trivial witness is its content. The two directions to check are whether the hypothesis is ever FALSE, which is what stops the theorem being vacuous, and it is: at `R = id` the sum is `n(n-1)`, so `normSq2 id = ½ n(n-1) = N = dim Λ²`, strictly positive at `n ≥ 2`. At `n ≤ 1` the space is zero and the statement reads `0 = 0`.

**The contrast, checked rather than taken: [normSq](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L173) IS genuinely degenerate on [weylModule](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylModule.lean#L91).** [symOp](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/WeylRadical.lean#L43) is `X ↦ X + Xᵀ`, which at skew `X` is `X + (-X) = 0`, so it kills `Λ²` pointwise. [mem_weylModule_of_kills_skew](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/WeylRadical.lean#L55) puts every such map in the module, every clause of [IsWeyl](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L126) reading its operator on skew arguments or on the `φ` family alone. [symOp_normSq](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/WeylRadical.lean#L105) then gives `normSq = 0` and [symOp_ne_zero](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/WeylRadical.lean#L115) gives `symOp ≠ 0` at `single a a 1`, whose image has `(a,a)` entry `2`. Both are kernel-checked here on the standard three axioms. **And it is not one witness but a subspace**, every element of `Hom(Sym, M)` extended by zero doing the same, of dimension `n² · n(n+1)/2`, which is [r1004](REVIEW_r1004.md)'s count.

**So the two halves of the contrast are asymmetric in the right direction**: on `CurvOp2` the hypothesis has exactly one solution and on the bare type it has a positive-dimensional space of them, and the difference is the domain, since an operator on `Λ²` has nowhere to hide.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 3. [br_mem_skewSub](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L284), and what `sharpQF` further needs

**The transposition argument, in closed form.** `(XY - YX)ᵀ = (XY)ᵀ - (YX)ᵀ = YᵀXᵀ - XᵀYᵀ`. At skew `X` and `Y` that is `(-Y)(-X) - (-X)(-Y) = YX - XY`, the two minus signs cancelling in EACH product, and `YX - XY = -(XY - YX) = -br X Y`. **So the bracket of two bivectors is a bivector, which is `𝔰𝔬(n)` closed under its own bracket, and the file's script is that argument and nothing else**: `transpose_sub`, `transpose_mul` twice, the two skewness rewrites, then `neg_mul`, `mul_neg`, `neg_neg` and `abel`. Both hypotheses are used, one per factor, and dropping either leaves a product of a skew and a non-skew matrix, whose bracket is not skew in general.

**Is it enough to express [sharpQF](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L102) on this domain? Yes, and I wrote the transport to be sure.** The record's form is `-(1/4) ∑_i ∑_j ⟨ br η (R (br η (R φ_ij))), φ_ij ⟩`, and reading it over `CurvOp2` needs four things, of which this lemma is the only new one. `R φ_ij` is in `Λ²` by the codomain. `br η (R φ_ij)` is in `Λ²` by this lemma at `η.2` and `(R φ_ij).2`, which is what lets the second `R` be applied at all. `R` of that is in `Λ²` again by the codomain, and the outer `br η (-)` by the lemma a second time. **The transported `sharpQF2` elaborates with those two membership proofs and nothing else, and `sharpQF Rb ↑η = sharpQF2 R η` at every bare `Rb` extending `R`.** Both compile. The `-(1/4)` and the ordered-pair range carry across untouched, so the normalisation that [Sharp.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Sharp.lean)'s `n = 4` anchor pins is the same one.

**What IS further needed, named, and it is above `sharpQF` rather than at it.** [sharpBil](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L147) is three values of `sharpQF` plus a `½`, so it transports free. [sharpOp](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L153) is `X ↦ ½ ∑_i ∑_j sharpBil R X (φ_ij) • φ_ij`, and its VALUE lands in `Λ²` for free, [Submodule.smul_mem](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Module/Submodule/Defs.html#Submodule.smul_mem) and [Submodule.sum_mem](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Module/Submodule/Defs.html#Submodule.sum_mem) closing it, which I checked. **What it does not get for free is being a `CurvOp2`**, which needs the map linear in `X` and continuous. Continuity is then free by [LinearMap.continuous_of_finiteDimensional](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Normed/Module/FiniteDimension.html#LinearMap.continuous_of_finiteDimensional), as [r1005](REVIEW_r1005.md) established, so the one genuine obligation is linearity of `sharpBil R X` in `X`, which is the SAME obligation [WeylReal.lean:263](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L263) already records as owed at the bare type. ## 4. [compOp2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L294) and [trCube2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L297)

**Is composition of continuous linear maps the record's [compOp](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L139)?** [compOp](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L139) is `fun X => R (R X)`. [ContinuousLinearMap.comp](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Module/LinearMap.html#ContinuousLinearMap.comp) has underlying function `fun x => R (R x)` by [ContinuousLinearMap.coe_comp'](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Module/LinearMap.html#ContinuousLinearMap.coe_comp'), which is a `rfl` lemma. **So the map is the same map, and the only change is that the composite is bundled**, which the bare type cannot do because it has no arrow to bundle.

**Is [trCube2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L297) its [trCube](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L142)?** Yes. `trCube R = innerOp (compOp R) R` and `trCube2 R = innerOp2 (compOp2 R) R`, the same expression with the same pairing, which section 1 shows is the record's. I checked the agreement: `trCube Rb = trCube2 R` at every bare `Rb` extending `R`, and the theorem compiles.

**And the proof of that agreement exhibits something the file does not claim, which is a point in its favour.** To rewrite `Rb (Rb ↑X)` as `↑(R (R X))` one applies the extension hypothesis twice, and the SECOND application needs `R X` to lie in `Λ²`. On `CurvOp2` that is the codomain. On the bare type it is not available: `compOp Rb` reads `Rb` at `Rb (φ_ij)`, which need not be skew, so **on the bare type `trCube` is not a function of the operator's restriction to `Λ²` at all**, and two bare functions agreeing on `Λ²` can have different `trCube`. It becomes one only under [IsWeyl](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L126)'s first clause, which is the clause the same file's docstring records as load-bearing and once absent. **The change of domain makes that clause type-level and buys well-definedness of the cubic term, which is a second payoff alongside the definiteness the file does claim.**

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**, with the extra payoff recorded.
