## Batch 1: the two constraints have names in the literature, and the record has never searched under either

**Isotropy $\sum_\alpha\Pi_{P_\alpha}=\kappa\,\mathrm{Id}$ is a TIGHT FUSION FRAME of $2$-planes**, and the frame constant is forced to $C=MN/D$, which at $M=2$, $N=a$, $D=n$ is exactly the record's $\kappa=2a/n$. *Sources: [Fusion Frames: Existence and Construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.5606), [Tight p-fusion frames](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.1798), [equi-chordal and equi-isoclinic tight fusion frames](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.08879), [Robust Dimension Reduction, Fusion Frames, and Grassmannian Packings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.2340).* **That field studies exactly the principal angles between the subspaces of a tight fusion frame**, so the record's extra condition, one right principal angle per pair from $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality, is a statement in its own vocabulary. *The bound $n\le\binom{d+1}2$ on equiangular subspaces and the "generalized simplex bound" are the kind of packing result that could cap a configuration.* **Screen against the record**: the record's own Gram matrix $S$ is the fusion-frame operator's Gram, and nothing in the record uses the field's existence theory, which classifies which $(M,N,D)$ admit tight fusion frames at all.

**Coordinate-diagonality is a PURE CURVATURE OPERATOR.** *"Riemannian manifolds whose curvature tensor is diagonalized at every point by some orthogonal basis are referred to as having pure curvature operator"*, and an orthogonal basis diagonalises an algebraic curvature tensor iff all nonzero exterior products of its vectors are eigenvectors. *Sources: [Curvature-homogeneous indefinite Einstein metrics](https://people.math.osu.edu/derdzinski.1/preprints/rarlg03.pdf) (Derdzinski), [The classification of simple Jacobi-Ricci commuting algebraic curvature tensors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.2080), [Higher dimensional bivectors and classification of the Weyl operator](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.1160).* **So [I36](../plan.md) reads, in the literature's words, "a maximiser of $P$ has pure curvature operator", and that phrase has never been searched.** *The Weyl-operator alignment classification (Coley and collaborators) files eigenvalue multiplicity as algebraic type, which is the vocabulary for "a maximiser has two eigenvalues" ([I73](../plan.md)).*

**Verdict of the batch**: two vocabularies to search next, both older and better developed than anything the record built by hand this week.

## Batch 2: the names hold up, isotropy is nearly free, and $P$ is a HARMONIC cubic

**"Pure curvature operator" is the literature's term for coordinate-diagonal, and the record's competitor is its textbook obstruction.** *"In dimensions two and three every Riemannian manifold has pure curvature operator, because all 2-vectors are decomposable; starting in dimension four, curvature operators without this property emerge, such as the Fubini-Study metric on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ where the Kähler 2-form is not decomposable."* Sources: [On the curvature operator in dimensions $4n$](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19050) (2025), [Decomposition of the curvature operator and applications to the Hopf conjecture](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15237) (2025), [Andrejić, On certain classes of algebraic curvature tensors](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-b/b5f0a8539e0497333e1b3e3f79389918). **So the record's [I36](../plan.md) is "a maximiser has pure curvature operator" and the two 2025 papers are the live literature on the property itself, unread.**

**Tight fusion frame existence is "completely resolved" at equal dimensions and the requirements are "very mild"** ([Constructing tight fusion frames](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1063520310000850), Casazza, Fickus, Mixon, Wang, Zhou; [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.5606)). *Which explains what this cycle found the hard way: isotropic configurations abound, and it is the $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality, one right principal angle per pair, that carries all the content.* **A construction exists giving $N(M-1)$ subspaces forming a tight fusion frame from any family of $m$-dimensional ones**, and [Radon-Hurwitz Grassmannian codes](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2404.06417) is the Clifford-structure end of the same field, which is where the record's isoclinic exclusion lives.

**THE LEAD: [Fox, The commutative nonassociative algebra of metric curvature tensors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.04012)** ([Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/15155E41E20AB76E7EED7A5FD64A3E49/S2050509421000694a.pdf/the-commutative-nonassociative-algebra-of-metric-curvature-tensors.pdf)). *The tensors of metric curvature type form a commutative nonassociative algebra with a nondegenerate invariant trace form, determined by the $O(n)$-orbit of a homogeneous cubic polynomial; the Weyl tensors form a subalgebra whose cubic polynomial is HARMONIC; and in dimension four the self-dual Weyl tensors form a five-dimensional subalgebra whose cubic is the five-variable Cartan isoparametric cubic.* **The record's $P(W)=\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$ IS that cubic polynomial, and its harmonicity on $\mathcal W_n$ is a structural fact this sweep has not seen used anywhere in the position file.** *The Cartan cubic's maximum on the sphere is classical and equals the record's $\sqrt{3/2}$ at $n=4$, which is a consistency check the record can run in a line.* **What harmonicity buys**: a harmonic cubic restricted to the unit sphere is a degree-three spherical harmonic, whose supremum is bounded by its $L^2$ norm times a constant depending only on the dimension, and whose $L^2$ norm is computable from the trace form. *That is a bound on $\theta$ from a mechanism the record has never touched, and it reasons about the OPERATOR and the whole space rather than about a basis or a marginal, which is what the position file asked for.*

**Also seen**: [On some new Ricci flow invariant curvature conditions](https://pith.science/paper/2412.13633) uses Huisken's algebraic identities to bound the Weyl part inside $F(R)=\lVert R\rVert^2/\lVert\mathrm{Ric}\rVert^2$, and the Osserman-Jacobi literature ([Gilkey, Nikolayevsky](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0204258v2)) carries eigenvalue-multiplicity results for the Jacobi operator, a different operator from the curvature operator and so not directly the record's object.

## Batch 3: the harmonic-cubic structure is ON THE SHELF and UNUSED, and it carries a bound the record has never derived

**Screen first.** *The Fox folder's [notes.md](../references/fox-2019-nonassociative-curvature-algebra/notes.md) already transcribes Theorem 3.2 under the heading "the two identities this project had not used": $\operatorname{tr}L_\circ(x)=0$ for every $x$, and $\operatorname{tr}(L_\circ(x)L_\circ(y))=\kappa\,h(x,y)$ with $\kappa>0$, both by irreducibility of the $O(n)$ action. Fox's Theorem 1.3 states both for the Weyl algebra directly, Remark 1.4 leaves $\kappa$ uncomputed for $\dim V>4$ and says computing the eigenvalues of $L_e$ at ONE idempotent suffices, and Theorem 1.5 gives $\kappa=21/16$ at $\dim V=4$ in Fox's metric, which is four times the Frobenius one.* **So harmonicity is not new to the record. What is new is that nothing in the position file uses it, and the scoreboard's bound comes entirely from the $T$-calculus.**

**The bound the two identities give, derived here and owed a check on Opus.** *Let $e=W/\theta$ be the idempotent at a critical point, $|e|=1/\theta$ in the record's units, acting on $\mathcal W_n$ of dimension $D$. $L_e$ has eigenvalue $1$ on $e$; on the orbit tangent, of dimension $d$, it has eigenvalue $\tfrac12$, because the orbit consists of critical points of an invariant cubic; and on the remaining $D-1-d$ directions it has eigenvalues $\mu_j$.* **Then $\operatorname{tr}L_e=0$ reads $\sum_j\mu_j=-1-d/2$, and the Killing identity reads $1+d/4+\sum_j\mu_j^2=\kappa/\theta^2$, so Cauchy-Schwarz gives** $$\theta^2\ \le\ \frac{\kappa}{1+\tfrac d4+\dfrac{(1+d/2)^2}{D-1-d}}.$$ *The denominator increases in $d$, so the bound is WEAKEST at the most symmetric critical points, which are exactly the ones the record already knows by value; and at $n=12$ with $d=36$ for the balanced product and $D=1638$ the denominator is about $10.2$, against $1$ for the crude version that ignores the orbit.* **Everything hinges on $\kappa$, and $\kappa$ is one computation away: the record's second-order apparatus already block-diagonalises $L_e$ at the balanced product ([L120](../findings/second-order.md) to [L122](../findings/second-order.md) per the position file), so $\kappa=\theta_0^2\,(1+\tfrac d4+\sum_j\mu_j^2)$ there in minutes.** *The normalisation trap the notes flagged, idempotents at $|e|=1/\theta$ and Fox's metric at $4\times$ Frobenius, is exactly where a factor of two would enter, and the $n=4$ anchor $\kappa=21/16$ is the check.*

**Two sources for the second-order half, one of them already on the shelf.** *[Tkachev, Spectral properties of nonassociative algebras and breaking regularity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.02765)* proves that in the relevant class the spectrum of $L_c$ on $c^\perp$ at an idempotent lies in $\{-1,\tfrac12\}$ and that every idempotent is primitive, and *[The universality of one half in commutative nonassociative algebras with identities](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1808.03808)* is the general statement that $\tfrac12$ sits in the Peirce spectrum. **At a MAXIMISER the record's own [R11](../routes.md) condition, $L_e\le\tfrac\theta2$ on the tangent, caps every $\mu_j$ at $\tfrac12$, which combined with the trace identity is a second constraint on the same spectrum.** *And [Fox, Harmonic cubic homogeneous polynomials such that the norm-squared of the Hessian is a multiple of the Euclidean quadratic form](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00071) ([Anal. Math. Phys. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13324-020-00462-4)) is the classification paper for exactly the class $P$ belongs to, with solutions constructed in all dimensions and classified up to dimension four.* **The Hsiang-eigencubic literature ([Nadirashvili, Tkachev, Vlăduţ](https://www.ams.org/books/surv/200/surv200-endmatter.pdf)) carries the cubic trace identity $\operatorname{tr}\mathrm{Hess}^3(f)=3(n_1-1)\lambda f$, a THIRD identity on the same spectrum, if $P$ is an eigencubic, which is to be checked.**

### Route 1, analytical, recommended: the Killing-constant bound on every critical point

**Mechanism.** *At an idempotent $e=W/\theta$, $\operatorname{tr}L_e=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}L_e^2=\kappa|e|^2$ are on the shelf ([Fox 2019](../references/fox-2019-nonassociative-curvature-algebra/notes.md), Theorem 1.3, filed 2026-09-08 as "the two identities this project had not used"). $L_e$ is $1$ on $e$, $\tfrac12$ on the orbit tangent of dimension $d$, and $\mu_j$ elsewhere, so Cauchy-Schwarz on the $\mu_j$ gives* $$\theta^2\le\frac{\kappa}{1+\tfrac d4+\dfrac{(1+d/2)^2}{D-1-d}}$$ *at every critical point, weakest at the most symmetric ones, whose values the record already has.* **What it takes**: compute $\kappa$ from the balanced product's $L_e$ spectrum, which [L120](../findings/second-order.md) to [L122](../findings/second-order.md) block-diagonalise, minutes on Opus; then check the $n=4$ anchor $\kappa=21/16$ in Fox's metric against the record's normalisation, which is where the factor-of-two hazard lives. **What it delivers**: a bound on $\theta$ from a mechanism the record has never used, reasoning about the operator on the whole space. **What falsifies it**: $\kappa$ making the bound no better than $3.0509$ at $n=12$, decided by the same computation. *Sharpening available at a maximiser: [R11](../routes.md)'s $L_e\le\tfrac\theta2$ caps every $\mu_j$, and Tkachev's linearisation of $W\circ W=\theta W$ may supply a third identity on the same spectrum.*

### Route 2, analytical: the pure-curvature-operator literature for [I36](../plan.md)

**Mechanism.** *"Coordinate-diagonal" is the literature's "pure curvature operator", and [two 2025 papers](../references/) on the property itself, [2512.19050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19050) and [2507.15237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15237), are unread.* **What it takes**: read both for a characterisation of purity usable at a critical point, and restate I36 in their vocabulary. **What it delivers**: either a known criterion for purity that the value hypothesis can be checked against, or a recorded absence. **What falsifies it**: the papers characterising purity only geometrically, with no algebraic criterion. *Lower than Route 1 because it is a reading, not a derivation.*

### Route 3, structural: tight-fusion-frame packing bounds on eigenplane multiplicities

**Mechanism.** *The eigenplanes of a critical point on the decomposable locus form a tight fusion frame of $2$-planes with one right principal angle per pair, and the Grassmannian packing bounds of that field cap how many such planes can coexist, which bounds multiplicities and so the number of distinct eigenvalues, feeding [I73](../plan.md) and, through the Vandermonde relations, [I75](../plan.md).* **What it takes**: the simplex and equiangular bounds from [1708.08879](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.08879) and [0709.2340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.2340) specialised to one right angle per pair. **What falsifies it**: the bounds saturating only above $N$, which would make them vacuous here. *Lowest because it is speculative and lives on the decomposable locus, which the first link does not reach.*

It also spends two identities that have sat unused on the shelf for four days, which is the cheapest kind of route there is.*
