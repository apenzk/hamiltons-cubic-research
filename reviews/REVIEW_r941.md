## 1. (a)'s subalgebra

**Link by link.**

- *Four-set $Q'$ with an index $p\in Q'\setminus\bigcup_iQ_i$.* The flip at $p$ lies in every $\ker\chi_{Q_j}$ since $p\notin Q_j$, and $\chi_{Q'}$ is $-1$ on it. Follows.
- *Four-set $Q'\subset\bigcup_iQ_i$, $Q'$ not among the $Q_i$.* $Q'$ meets some $Q_i$, and $Q'\cap Q_i=Q_i$ would force $Q'=Q_i$ as both have four elements, so an $i$ with $Q'\cap Q_i$ proper and nonempty exists, which the proof asserts without this one-line reason. With $a\in Q'\cap Q_i$, $b\in Q_i\setminus Q'$, the element $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$ lies in $\ker\chi_{Q_i}$ (two indices of $Q_i$) and in $\ker\chi_{Q_j}$ for $j\ne i$ (no index of $Q_j$, by disjointness), and $\chi_{Q'}(\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b)=-1$. Follows. This covers $Q'$ meeting several $Q_i$, since only one $i$ is needed.
- *Pair character $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$ with $a\notin\bigcup_iQ_i$.* The flip at $a$ is in every kernel and gives $-1$. Follows.
- *Pair character with $a\in Q_i$.* $c\in Q_i\setminus\{a,b\}$ exists (at least two elements), $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$ is in every kernel as above, and $\chi_{\{a,b\}}(\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c)=-1$ since exactly $a$ is shared. This holds whether $b\in Q_i$, $b\in Q_j$ for $j\ne i$, or $b\notin\bigcup Q_j$, so both indices in one set and indices in two different sets are covered. The split on $a$ is exhaustive for any labelling. Follows.
- *$\chi_{Q_i}\chi_{Q_j}$.* It is $\chi_{Q_i\cup Q_j}$, an eight-set character, which occurs in no summand of L120. This is what gives $\nu_i\circ\nu_j=0$: $\circ$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, so the product carries the product character and has zero component everywhere. Follows.
- *Subalgebra.* The fixed space of $H=\bigcap_i\ker\chi_{Q_i}$ is the sum of the summands whose character is trivial on $H$, which by the witnesses is $\mathcal D_n\oplus\bigoplus_iV_{Q_i}$, and a fixed space of algebra automorphisms is a subalgebra. Follows.
- *$\nu_i\circ\nu_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$.* L220's step 2 verbatim. Follows.

**A shorter exact argument, offered as a repair.** The characters of the full sign group are the $\chi_S$, $S\subseteq[n]$, pairwise distinct. The characters trivial on $H$ form the annihilator of $H$, which is the group generated by the $\chi_{Q_i}$, that is $\{\chi_{\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_i}\}$, of sizes $4\lvert I\rvert$. Only $I=\emptyset$ and $\lvert I\rvert=1$ have sizes occurring in L120. This replaces the witness list and also gives the vanishing of $\nu_i\circ\nu_j$.

**On "critical point of $P$ in the space".** Either reading (critical on the unit sphere of the subspace, or a critical point of $\mathcal W_n$ lying in it) gives $W\circ W=\theta W$, since the subalgebra property puts $W\circ W$ in the subspace.

## 2. (a)'s chain

The split equations, which the proof leaves implicit in "step 3", are $D\circ D+\sum_j\nu_j\circ\nu_j=\theta D$ and $2D\circ\nu_i=\theta\nu_i$ for each $i$, since $D\circ\nu_i\in V_{Q_i}$ (L120, $D$ is $\Gamma$-fixed) and the cross products vanish (item 1).

- *Step 3.* $L_D\nu_i=\tfrac\theta2\nu_i$ for the fixed $i$. Follows.
- *Step 4.* $r_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$ by definition of $q_i$, and for $\nu'\in V_{Q_i}$, $\nu_i\circ\nu'\in\Pi_{Q_i}$ by polarising step 2, so $\langle L_{r_i}\nu_i,\nu'\rangle=0$ and $L_{r_i}\nu_i\in V_{Q_i}$ is zero. The other blocks do not enter. Follows.
- *Step 5.* $L_{q_i}\nu_i=\tfrac\theta2\nu_i$, $\nu_i\ne0$, $\theta>0$ and L121's spectrum $\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_i$ give $u_i^2=\theta^2/3$. Follows.
- *Step 6.* Pairing the $\mathcal D_n$ equation with $q_i$ gives $P(q_i)+\kappa_i+\tfrac\theta2N_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle=\theta u_i^2$. Each extra term is an inner product of a matrix supported on pairs inside $Q_j$ with one supported on pairs inside $Q_i$, and disjoint four-sets share no pair, so it is zero in the diagonal inner product. Follows.
- *Steps 7 and 8.* Statements about $q_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$ and $r_i\in\mathcal D_n\ominus\Pi_{Q_i}$ only. Follow.
- *Step 9.* The inequality is $\tfrac{\theta^3}6-\tfrac\theta2N_i\le\tfrac\theta3\lVert r_i\rVert^2$. Unit norm and orthogonality give $\lVert r_i\rVert^2=1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-\sum_jN_j\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-N_i$, and the coefficient $\tfrac\theta3$ is positive, so replacing $\lVert r_i\rVert^2$ by the bound yields exactly L220's step 9 inequality. The second use, $N_i\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3$, holds as $N_i\le1-\lVert D\rVert^2\le1-u_i^2$. Hence $\theta^2\le\tfrac32$. Follows.

**Repair.** "Which only strengthens it" reads as if the extra $-\sum_{j\ne i}N_j$ were substituted and discarded afterwards. Say: the bound $\lVert r_i\rVert^2\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-N_i$ reduces the inequality to L220's step 9 verbatim.

## 3. (b)1

- *Characters.* With $Q_1=\{a,b,c,d\}$, $Q_2=\{a,b,e,f\}$, $Q_3=\{c,d,e,f\}$, the annihilator of $\ker\chi_{Q_1}\cap\ker\chi_{Q_2}$ is $\langle\chi_{Q_1},\chi_{Q_2}\rangle=\{1,\chi_{Q_1},\chi_{Q_2},\chi_{Q_3}\}$ by the argument of item 1. No pair character is among them (the pair $\{a,b\}=Q_1\cap Q_2$ included). The proof states the list without a witness, and the double-annihilator line is the repair. Follows.
- *Split equations.* $W\circ W=D\circ D+\sum_i\nu_i\circ\nu_i+2\sum_iD\circ\nu_i+2(\nu_1\circ\nu_2+\nu_1\circ\nu_3+\nu_2\circ\nu_3)$. The characters multiply as a Klein group, so $\nu_i\circ\nu_i\in\mathcal D_n$, $D\circ\nu_i\in V_{Q_i}$, and $\nu_j\circ\nu_k\in V_{Q_i}$ for $\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}$. Projecting $W\circ W=\theta W$ gives $D\circ D+\sum_i\nu_i\circ\nu_i=\theta D$ and $2D\circ\nu_i+2\nu_j\circ\nu_k=\theta\nu_i$, the factor $2$ on $\nu_j\circ\nu_k$ correct as each unordered pair appears once with coefficient $2$. Follows.
- *$\tau=0$ at $\nu_3=0$.* The $\chi_{Q_3}$ equation gives $\nu_1\circ\nu_2=0$, which is more than needed, since $\tau=\langle\nu_1\circ\nu_2,\nu_3\rangle$ vanishes at $\nu_3=0$ directly. By symmetry of $T$, $\tau=\langle\nu_2\circ\nu_3,\nu_1\rangle=\langle\nu_1\circ\nu_3,\nu_2\rangle$, so $\tau=0$ whenever any one $\nu_i$ is zero. Follows.

## 4. (b)2

- *$\langle D\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle$.* $\langle D\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\langle D,\nu_i\circ\nu_i\rangle$ and $\nu_i\circ\nu_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}\perp r_i$. Follows from step 2 and the definition of $q_i$, the eigen-equation of L220's step 3 being unused, which is right since it fails here.
- *Pairing the $\chi_{Q_i}$ equation.* $2\langle D\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle+2\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_k,\nu_i\rangle=\theta N_i$, and the middle term is $2\tau$ for each of the three $i$ by symmetry of $T$ (item 3). So $\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau$ with the same sign of $\tau$ in all three. Follows.
- *Direction.* $L_{q_i}$ is self-adjoint and preserves $V_{Q_i}$, its spectrum there is $\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_i$ (L220 step 5, from L121, valid for any $q\in\Pi_Q$), so $\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_iN_i$. With $\tau\le0$ the left side is at least $\tfrac\theta2N_i$, and dividing by $N_i>0$ gives $u_i\ge\theta/\sqrt3$. A positive $\tau$ would weaken this to $u_i\ge\theta/\sqrt3-2\tau/(\sqrt3N_i)$, which is why the open case is $\tau>0$. Follows.

## 5. (b)3

- *Norm identity and normalisation.* The Lean identity is $\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert^2=\tfrac34\lVert\nu\rVert^4$ once $\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert^2=2(x^2+y^2+z^2)$ and $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)$. The first is the $\mathcal D_n$ norm. The second is confirmed against L220's equality point: $a=2t$, $b=c=-t$ gives $\nu\circ\nu=3t\,q$ with $q=t(2,-1,-1)$, and $\langle q,\nu\circ\nu\rangle=36t^3=\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ at $\theta=6t$ only if $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=12t^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)$. **The needed direction does not even depend on the normalisation**: $\nu\circ\nu\in\Pi_Q$, so $\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert=\sup_{\hat q\in\Pi_Q\text{ unit}}\langle L_{\hat q}\nu,\nu\rangle\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ by L121 alone. Follows.
- *Shared pairs.* Pairs of $Q_1$: $ab,cd,ac,bd,ad,bc$. Of $Q_2$: $ab,ef,ae,bf,af,be$. Of $Q_3$: $cd,ef,ce,df,cf,de$. $Q_1,Q_2$ share only $ab$, $Q_1,Q_3$ only $cd$, $Q_2,Q_3$ only $ef$. Exactly one pair for every pair of the three. Follows.
- *Entry bound.* For a unit pattern, $y+z=-x$ gives $y^2+z^2\ge\tfrac{x^2}2$, so $1=2(x^2+y^2+z^2)\ge3x^2$ and $x^2\le\tfrac13$, likewise for $y,z$, sharp at $(2,-1,-1)/\sqrt{12}$. This holds for every shape. **Citation repair**: L220's step 8 bounds $\max(x,y,z)\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, which is one side only, and the square bound needs the same argument at $-\hat q$ or the two-line derivation above. The inner product of patterns on different four-sets is the product of their entries on the shared pair, so $\lvert\langle\hat q_i,\hat q_j\rangle\rvert\le\tfrac13$. Follows.
- *Cross-term bound.* $\lvert\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle\rvert=\lVert\nu_j\circ\nu_j\rVert\,u_i\,\lvert\cos\rvert\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2N_j\cdot u_i\cdot\tfrac13=\tfrac{u_i}{2\sqrt3}N_j$. Follows.

## 6. (b)4

**The equality.** Pairing $D\circ D+\sum_j\nu_j\circ\nu_j=\theta D$ with $q_i$: $\theta\langle D,q_i\rangle=\theta u_i^2$. $\langle D\circ D,q_i\rangle=P(q_i)+2\langle r_i,q_i\circ q_i\rangle+\langle L_{q_i}r_i,r_i\rangle$ with the middle term zero as $q_i\circ q_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$ (L220 step 2). $\langle\nu_i\circ\nu_i,q_i\rangle=\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau$ (item 4). The remaining terms are $\sum_{j\ne i}\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle$. The displayed equality follows, with the sign of $\tau$ matching item 4.

**The bounds, term by term.**

| Term | Bound used | Source | Valid here |
|---|---|---|---|
| $P(q_i)$ | $\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_i^3$ | L220 step 7, homogeneous | yes, upper bound on a possibly negative quantity |
| $\langle L_{q_i}r_i,r_i\rangle$ | $\le\tfrac{u_i}{\sqrt3}(t-u_i^2)$ | L220 step 8 scaled by $u_i$ | yes, $r_i\in\mathcal D_n\ominus\Pi_{Q_i}$ and $\lVert r_i\rVert^2=\lVert D\rVert^2-u_i^2=t-u_i^2\ge0$ |
| $\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau$ | $\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_iN_i$ | Rayleigh upper bound from item 4 | yes, for every sign of $\tau$, so $\tau\le0$ is spent only in the pinning |
| $\sum_{j\ne i}\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle$ | $\le\tfrac{u_i}{2\sqrt3}(N-N_i)$ | item 5 | yes, absolute value bound |

$t=1-N$ with $N=N_1+N_2+N_3$ holds by unit norm and orthogonality of $\mathcal D_n$ and the three blocks.

**The division.** $u_i\ge\theta/\sqrt3>0$ for $\nu_i\ne0$. Dividing: $\theta u_i\le\big(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2-\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\big)u_i^2+\tfrac{1-N}{\sqrt3}+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2N_i+\tfrac{N-N_i}{2\sqrt3}$. The $u_i^2$ coefficient is $\tfrac{3-2}{2\sqrt3}=\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$, the $N_i$ coefficient is $\tfrac{3-1}{2\sqrt3}=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, the $N$ coefficient is $\tfrac{-2+1}{2\sqrt3}=-\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$. So $\theta u_i-\tfrac{u_i^2}{2\sqrt3}\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}(1-\tfrac N2+N_i)$, as written. Follows.

**Monotonicity.** $g(u)=\theta u-\tfrac{u^2}{2\sqrt3}$ has $g'(u)=\theta-\tfrac u{\sqrt3}\ge0$ on $u\le\sqrt3\theta$. $u_i\le\lVert D\rVert\le1$, and $1\le\sqrt3\theta$ when $\theta\ge\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ (the Lean hypothesis is the non-strict one, which suffices). So $g(u_i)\ge g(\theta/\sqrt3)=\tfrac{\theta^2}{\sqrt3}\big(1-\tfrac16\big)$, giving $\tfrac56\theta^2\le1-\tfrac N2+N_i$. At $\theta<\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ the conclusion $\theta^2\le\tfrac65$ holds outright, and the per-block inequality is then not claimed, which item 7 respects since the sum is only needed in the other case. The Lean theorem per_block settles this step from exactly these hypotheses. Follows.

## 7. (b)5

- *Three nonzero.* Summing: $\tfrac52\theta^2\le3-\tfrac32N+N=3-\tfrac N2\le3$, so $\theta^2\le\tfrac65$. Follows.
- *Two nonzero, $\nu_k=0$.* Then $N_k=0$, $N=N_i+N_j$, and $\tau=0$ automatically (item 3), so the hypothesis $\tau\le0$ holds and the pinning is available for both $i$ and $j$. In each chain the term for block $k$ is $\tfrac{u}{2\sqrt3}N_k=0$ and its $q_k$ never appears. Summing the two: $\tfrac53\theta^2\le2-N+N=2$. Follows, and it does not matter which of the three indices vanishes, since the configuration is symmetric under relabelling ($Q_1=Q_2\triangle Q_3$ and so on, each pair sharing two indices).
- *A block with $\nu_k=0$ is not summed.* Correct and necessary: its per-block inequality needs $u_k\ge\theta/\sqrt3$, which the pinning gives only for $\nu_k\ne0$.

## 8. Scope

- *Hypotheses.* Every step uses only unit norm, criticality, $\theta>0$, the four-set configuration, and (in (b)) $\tau\le0$ with two nonzero components. $n\ge6$ is what (b) needs for six indices, and (a) with $m=1$ at $n=5$ is L220 itself, so the range loses nothing. The full sign group, not its determinant-one subgroup, is what makes the characters distinct at $n=6,8$, and L120 is stated for the full group.
- *Claims beyond the proof.* (a) claims no equality case and (b) claims the bound $\tfrac65$, both as proved. "(a) and (b) settle every stratum of the far region" is true only where $\sqrt{3/2}<\Theta_n$, that is $n\ge12$ per L220, and a critical point with exactly one nonzero $\nu_i$ in $\mathcal S$ is handled by L220 in $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{Q_i}$, not by (b). **Repair**: write "at $n\ge12$" and "with L220 for one component" into that sentence. The "$\lvert\tau\rvert\le\tfrac12\sqrt{N_1N_2N_3}$" in the open list is marked as a screen and is not used.
- *Can $\tau\le0$ be normalised?* An element $g\in\Gamma$ acts on $V_{Q_i}$ by $\chi_{Q_i}(g)$, and $\tau\mapsto\chi_{Q_1}\chi_{Q_2}\chi_{Q_3}(g)\tau=\tau$ since $\chi_{Q_3}=\chi_{Q_1}\chi_{Q_2}$. Permutations preserving the configuration preserve $T$. $W\mapsto-W$ sends $\tau\mapsto-\tau$ and $\theta\mapsto-\theta$, which leaves the hypothesis $\theta>0$. An orthogonal map acting as $-1$ on one block and fixing $\mathcal D_n$ pointwise would have to lie in the stabiliser of a generic diagonal operator, which is $\Gamma$. So the sign of $\tau$ is an invariant of the critical point within the available symmetries, and $\tau\le0$ is a genuine split.
