## 0. The calculus, rebuilt from the definitions before reading the derivations

A multiplier at row $i$ is a quadratic $\varphi(x)=c_2x^2+c_1x+c_0$ applied as $\tfrac12[c_0(\mathrm A_i)+c_1(\mathrm B_i)+c_2(\mathrm C_i)]$. Reading off [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s reduced system, the coefficient on $t_{ijk}$ ($j<k$) is $\mu_j\mu_k\,\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)$ and on $t_{ijj}$ is $\tfrac12\mu_j(\mu_j-1)\varphi(\nu_j)$, with $\psi(y,z)=c_2yz+c_1\tfrac{y+z}2+c_0$ the polarisation; the right-hand side is $c_0(n-2)-c_1\nu_i+c_2\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)$. I checked this against the one worked instance the record carries: at $d=2$, [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) takes $\varphi=(x-u)(x+w)$ and reports $\psi(u,-w)=-\tfrac12(u+w)^2$, which is what the formula gives, so the convention is fixed and the orbit counts are the ones in the lemma.

**Everything in this apparatus is one line in one plane.** Write $\ell(X,Y)=c_0+c_1X+c_2Y$. Then $\varphi(\nu_j)=\ell(P_{jj})$ with $P_{jj}=(\nu_j,\nu_j^2)$ on the parabola $Y=X^2$, and $\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)=\ell(P_{jk})$ with $P_{jk}=\big(\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2,\nu_j\nu_k\big)$, the intersection of the tangents at $\nu_j$ and $\nu_k$, the configuration [post F43](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) already records. And the right-hand side is $(n-2)\,\ell(R_i)$ for the single **row point** $R_i=\big(\tfrac{-\nu_i}{n-2},\ \tfrac{\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)}{n-2}\big)$, since $c_0(n-2)-c_1\nu_i+c_2\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)=(n-2)\big[c_0+c_1\tfrac{-\nu_i}{n-2}+c_2\tfrac{\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)}{n-2}\big]$.

**So the whole per-row calculus at $d=3$ is six points, one row point, and the lines between them.** A multiplier annihilates exactly the types whose point lies on its line; its right-hand side is nonzero exactly when its line misses $R_i$. That single reformulation settles U1, U2 and U3 at once, and it is the thing the record's derivations do not have.

## 1. U1, the span claim is right, the exhaustion drawn from it is not

**CONFIRMED, the linear-algebra half.** Row $i$'s multiplier space is the space of quadratics, dimension $3$, and the map to coefficient-plus-right-hand-side is injective because the six points affinely span the plane. The three double roots at that row have coefficient vectors $(c_0,c_1,c_2)=(\nu_r^2,-2\nu_r,1)$, and $\det[(\nu_r^2,-2\nu_r,1)]_{r=1,2,3}=-2\prod_{r<s}(\nu_s-\nu_r)\ne0$ for distinct eigenvalues. They are independent and they span. I also confirm the maximum-annihilation half independently: the only lines meeting three of the six points are the three tangents $Y=2\nu_rX-\nu_r^2$, which carry $P_{rr},P_{r1},P_{r2},P_{r3}$ and are exactly $\varphi=(x-\nu_r)^2$; no line meets four; the chord through $\nu_a,\nu_b$ misses $P_{ab}$ and every $P_{ac}$; the three points $P_{12},P_{13},P_{23}$ are non-collinear with determinant $(a-c)(a-b)(c-b)/2$; and $P_{rr},P_{rs},P_{st}$ are non-collinear with determinant $-\tfrac12(a-b)^2(a-c)$. **So "nine double roots, unique maximum annihilation, three per row spanning the row" is confirmed in full, and confirmed with a proof rather than a check.**

**REFUTED, the inference.** "Spans, therefore the two-root family is a combination of them, therefore the eighteen are the whole apparatus" is not valid, and the record's own use of the two-root family shows why. Spanning is a statement about linear content; the forbidding argument reads the *surviving set*, and the surviving set is not a linear invariant, every multiplier in the row's $3$-space is a combination of the three double roots, including ones with surviving sets no double root has. If spanning implied redundancy it would make the two-root family redundant too, and the record counts the two-root family as cutting supports the double roots do not. **The premise proves too much; taken at its own word it contradicts the eighteen it is offered to justify.**

**And the omission is concrete, not hypothetical.** The lines that can forbid a four-subset are exactly those through at least two of the six points, because a line through $k$ points leaves $6-k$ surviving types and a four-subset can contain the surviving set only when $6-k\le4$. There are nine such lines per row: three tangents, and six secants, of which the record's two-root family supplies only the three chords $P_{11}P_{22},P_{11}P_{33},P_{22}P_{33}$. **The three secants $P_{11}P_{23}$, $P_{22}P_{13}$, $P_{33}P_{12}$ are multipliers of exactly the same form, leave four surviving types exactly as the chords do, and are absent from the eighteen.** The apparatus is **twenty-seven** identities, nine per row.

**A witness, checkable by hand.** Take the secant through $P_{11}$ and $P_{23}$ at row $1$: it annihilates $t_{111}$ and $t_{123}$ and leaves $\{112,113,122,133\}$, so it forbids the zero-set $Z=\{112,113,122,133\}$. None of the eighteen forbids it. At row $1$ the complementary points are $P_{11}$ and $P_{23}$, and no tangent and no chord contains both, tangent $1$ has $P_{11}$ without $P_{23}$, tangents $2$ and $3$ have $P_{23}$ without $P_{11}$, and each chord carries two parabola points. At rows $2$ and $3$ the set $Z$ meets only two row types each ($\{112,122\}\mapsto\{P_{11},P_{12}\}$ and $\{113,133\}\mapsto\{P_{11},P_{13}\}$), leaving four points off, which no line covers. **So $Z$ is one of the $141$ and it dies to a nineteenth identity of the same family.**

**Its right-hand side is as good as the ones the record uses.** For *any* $\varphi=(x-\nu_i)(x-z)$ at row $i$ the right-hand side is $\nu_i\big[(n-1)z+\theta\big]$, so the secant through $P_{11}$ and $P_{23}$ at row $1$ carries $\nu_1[(n-1)z+\theta]$ with $z=\big[2\nu_2\nu_3-\nu_1(\nu_2+\nu_3)\big]/\big(\nu_2+\nu_3-2\nu_1\big)$, one codimension-one vanishing condition, exactly like the $j=3$ bracket. When the denominator vanishes the multiplier degenerates to the linear $\varphi=x-\nu_1$, whose right-hand side is $-(n-1)\nu_1\ne0$ unconditionally.

## 2. U2, the floor is real, $141$ is not it, and the bar for a successor is set at the wrong place

**A floor exists, and that much is provable in one line.** Any $Z$ meeting each row in at most two types survives every annihilation identity, because the four or more points it leaves off lie on no line; $Z=\{111,222,333,123\}$ is such a set. **So the annihilation method cannot cut everything, with no enumeration and no appeal to a number.** That is the part of F45 that survives.

**The floor is $132$, not $141$, and the gap is exactly the nine omitted secants.** The criterion is uniform: $Z$ dies at row $i$ exactly when the points *not* used by $Z$ at that row lie on a line missing $R_i$. Granting every right-hand side nonzero, this gives three cases. If $Z$ has four types at row $i$ (that is, $Z$ lies inside one row's six types), the two remaining points always determine a line, so $Z$ dies: $\binom64=15$ per row, and the three rows are disjoint here because two rows share only three types, giving $45$. If $Z$ has three types at row $i$, it dies exactly when the complementary triple is one of the three tangent triples; that is $3\times4=12$ per row, and the three families overlap in exactly one set apiece, $\{111,112,122,222\}$, $\{111,113,133,333\}$, $\{222,223,233,333\}$, each lying in two of them and none in three, for $36-3=33$. If $Z$ has at most two types at a row, that row cannot kill it. The two families are disjoint by intersection size, so $78$ die and $\mathbf{132}$ survive.

**That reconstruction reproduces the record's own number, which is why I trust it against the record's.** Of the fifteen pairs of the six points, nine lie on tangents, three on chords and three on the omitted secants. Dropping the omitted secants therefore spares exactly three of the fifteen four-type sets per row, nine in all, and $210-(45-9)-33=141$. **So $141$ is precisely the count of what the eighteen leave, and $132$ is the count of what the family actually leaves, the record's arithmetic is right and its family is not.** F45's "no choice of spectrum, no additional non-degeneracy hypothesis and no sharper analysis of any right-hand side can take the per-row multiplier apparatus below $141$" is false as stated: nine more identities of the same form take it to $132$.

**And the deeper objection: the annihilation criterion is the affine shadow of a per-row condition the record already holds.** With $\ell$ ranging over all affine functions, the row-$i$ equations say $\sum_{jk}w_{jk}P_{jk}=(n-2)R_i$ with $w_{jk}\ge0$ summing to $n-2$, that is, **$R_i$ is a convex combination of the points the support uses.** The annihilation identities detect only $R_i\notin\operatorname{aff}(\text{used points})$; the true row condition is $R_i\notin\operatorname{conv}(\text{used points})$, which is strictly stronger and is [Theorem B of DERIVE-t11](DERIVE-t11.md)'s row point in its triangle, restated. **So the answer to "could a per-row constraint that is not of multiplier form exist" is yes, and the record has one.** F45's stated bar, *"exhibit a constraint that is not a weighted combination of one row's $(\mathrm A_i),(\mathrm B_i),(\mathrm C_i)$"*, is therefore the wrong bar twice over: it is cleared by sign certificates that are weighted combinations of exactly those three rows, and clearing it is not what a successor needs.

**What survives of the post's conclusion, and where it comes from.** "The per-row multiplier method cannot close $d=3$ alone" is true, but it is [post F43](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s statement, resting on the $n=12$ spectrum with multiplicities $(30,20,16)$ that passes every row at $\theta=1.2917$. **F45 adds no independent support for it.** A surviving zero-set is a candidate vertex and nothing more, so neither $141$ nor $132$ bears on whether $\theta\le\theta_0$ follows; the closure question is decided by the witness, not by the count.

## 3. U3, the factorisation holds for nine of the eighteen and the blanket statement is false

**The general fact, which is cleaner than the recorded one and contains it.** At row $i$, a multiplier with a root at $\nu_i$, $\varphi=(x-\nu_i)(x-z)$, has right-hand side $(n-2)\nu_iz+(\nu_i+z)\nu_i-\nu_i^2+\nu_i\theta=\nu_i\big[(n-1)z+\theta\big]$. **The eigenvalue factor is $\nu_i$, nonzero by kernel-freeness, and the bracket is $(n-1)z+\theta$ where $z$ is the other root.** Setting $z=\nu_i$ gives the diagonal double root $\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta]$; setting $z=\nu_b$ gives the two-root identity at row $a=i$, namely $\nu_a[(n-1)\nu_b+\theta]$. **Both recorded factorisations are confirmed, and both are the same statement.**

**But the remaining nine identities have no such root and do not factor.** The off-diagonal double root $\varphi=(x-\nu_r)^2$ at row $i\ne r$ gives $(n-2)\nu_r^2+2\nu_i\nu_r-\nu_i^2+\nu_i\theta$, and the two-root $\varphi=(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$ at the third row $i=c$ gives $(n-2)\nu_a\nu_b+(\nu_a+\nu_b)\nu_c-\nu_c^2+\nu_c\theta$; in each case the $\theta$-coefficient forces the eigenvalue factor to be $\nu_i$ and then the bracket would have to be $\big[(n-2)\nu_r^2+2\nu_i\nu_r-\nu_i^2\big]/\nu_i$, which is not $(n-1)\nu_j+\theta$ for any $j$. **Six off-diagonal double roots and three two-root identities are genuine quadratics in $(\nu,\theta)$.** The file says so itself two paragraphs earlier (*"six more: quadratic conditions in $(\nu,\theta)$, not analysed here"*) and then the corrected-classification prose says both families factor. **The table is right and enumerates exactly the nine that do; the sentence above it is wrong.** I also re-derived the two $\rho_1$ conditions and they stand: at row $3$ with double root $\nu_1$ the substitution of $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert$ gives $(\rho_1+1)\big[(n-2)\rho_1-n\big]\lvert\nu_3\rvert^2$, positive for $\rho_1>\tfrac n{n-2}$; at row $1$ with double root $\nu_3$, dropping $\nu_1\theta\ge0$ gives $\big[(n-2)-2\rho_1-\rho_1^2\big]\lvert\nu_3\rvert^2$, positive for $\rho_1<\sqrt{n-1}-1$; and $\sqrt{n-1}-1>\tfrac n{n-2}$ at $n\ge7$ with a genuine gap at $n=6$.

**The factor never obstructing: CONFIRMED.** In every case the eigenvalue factor is $\nu_i$ for the row's own class, nonzero by kernel-freeness.

## 4. U4, the dichotomy is sound, with nothing hidden in it

**CONFIRMED, derived independently.** If $(n-1)\nu_2+\theta\le0$ then $\nu_2\le-\theta/(n-1)<0$ because $\theta>0$, so $\lvert\nu_2\rvert\ge\theta/(n-1)$, i.e. $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$. The ordering $\nu_1>\nu_2>\nu_3$ with $\nu_2<0$ gives $\lvert\nu_3\rvert=-\nu_3>-\nu_2=\lvert\nu_2\rvert$, **that is the whole of "$\lvert\nu_2\rvert<\lvert\nu_3\rvert$", and it is forced by the ordering alone, needing no further hypothesis.** Hence $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert<(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert$, which is the $\Pi$-bound strict, and the $j=3$ bracket $(n-1)\nu_3+\theta=\theta-(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert$ is strictly negative, so nonzero. **The comparison goes the right way**: the $\Pi$-bound is $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$ and $\lambda_{\min}=\nu_3$, so a bound in terms of $\lvert\nu_2\rvert$ is the stronger one and strictness follows in the correct direction. The two alternatives are a negation pair, so the dichotomy is exhaustive with no hypothesis on the sign of $\nu_2$ anywhere. The withdrawal of "second-$\Pi$-strictness" is correct on both halves for the reasons the file gives.

**One scope note.** The dichotomy buys three identities in each branch, but which three differs, and the branch-$2$ identities are the $j=3$ ones whose *own* non-vanishing was previously the $\Pi$-strictness condition, so the dichotomy is a genuine reshuffle rather than a strengthening of both branches at once. The file states this correctly; I record it because a reader may take "either way three identities are bought" to mean the same three.

## 5. U5, what depends on a number

**Yes, and it is F45's own headline.** "The per-row multiplier method floors at $141$ supports" is a claim about a number, the number is wrong for the family it names, and the post's boxed corollary, *"no choice of spectrum, no additional non-degeneracy hypothesis and no sharper analysis of any right-hand side can take the per-row multiplier apparatus below $141$"*, is refuted by nine identities of exactly that form. The label "*a computation, and labelled as one; the argument this post rests on is the exhaustion below*" does not save it, because **the exhaustion is the part that fails**: the count is arithmetically correct given the eighteen, and the eighteen are not the apparatus.

**And a second claim depends on it: the retroactive conviction of the withdrawn $130$ row.** F45 argues *"$130<141$ is below the floor, so that row was drawing on something outside the family"*. That inference is void as stated, it reads a bound on an incomplete subfamily as a bound on the family. It survives arithmetically by two, since $132>130$, but it does not survive as reasoning, and it cannot be repaired by substituting $132$: the *complete* per-row condition is the convex one, whose floor is spectrum-dependent and is not bounded below by either number. **If the $130$ row is to stay withdrawn it needs a reason that is not "below the floor".**

**The other counts.** $194$ and $178$–$179$ are reached from four and from a handful of the eighteen; nothing in the record's arguments turns on their values, and both would need recounting against twenty-seven, though the nine additions each carry a codimension-one vanishing condition, so they are unlikely to move the *hypothesis-free* figure. **The classification of right-hand sides, the dichotomy and the geometry are what carry, exactly as the file says; it is the exhaustion, not the counts, that this review takes down.**

## 6. On L121 and L123

**They bear on none of U1–U5 and I flag one trap.** [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md)'s "one necessary condition per $4$-subset" is a condition on $4$-subsets of *frame indices* in the second-order apparatus; F45's $210$ are $4$-subsets of the ten *class-multisets*. The two have nothing to do with each other and $\binom{10}4=210$ against $\binom n4$ is a coincidence of vocabulary. Both lemmas read correctly as stated and neither supports nor undermines the floor.

## 7. What I would put to the session

**The record should keep the six-point picture and drop the eighteen.** Stating the per-row calculus as *"$R_i$ must lie in the convex hull of the points $P_{jk}$ the support uses"*, with the annihilation identities as its affine shadow, replaces four sections of case work, produces the nine double roots and their uniqueness as a two-line fact about tangents, produces the factorisation $\nu_i[(n-1)z+\theta]$ in one line, and makes the omission of the three secants per row visible on sight. **Post F45 as written should be withdrawn or restated**; the honest residue is that the annihilation method has a positive floor, that the floor is a property of which subsets of six points are collinear, and that [post F43](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s witness (not any count), is what closes the per-row route.
