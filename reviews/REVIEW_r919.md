**One line.** The four steps are true and the lemma stands as stated, and five things it leans on are not on the record: two hypotheses of the cited definition, the orthogonality of the summands, the cross terms in step 3, the positivity of $\lambda$ that L47's census actually needs, and one false claim in the Lean row about Mathlib.

## 1. Proposition 3.1.2, its sign, and its hypothesis

Gorodski fixes $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ at line 2122, immediately above 3.1.2, and his proof derives $R(X,Y)X=[X,[X,Y]]=-[[X,Y],X]$ from Lemma 3.1.1, then extends by the curvature symmetries. So the quoted identity is his, in his convention, with no transcription slip.

**No sign can propagate through this block, because every use of 3.1.2 concludes a vanishing.** Steps 1 and 2 conclude $R(X,Y)Z=0$, step 3 concludes $R=0$ and then $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=0$, and step 4 reads off a restriction of an equation both sides of which are already fixed. A global sign flip on $R$ changes none of those four statements. That is the one respect in which this lemma is cheap to audit, and it is worth saying explicitly, because the record's usual convention hazards are all about magnitudes and signs of nonzero quantities.

**The record's convention, and where the agreement actually has to be checked.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) carries no $R(X,Y)Z$ convention at all. What it carries is the operator-level pair, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}$ as the adjoint of $\cdot\wedge\mathrm{id}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{scal}}=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}$ on $\Lambda^2$, so the brief's premise that the record's tensor convention is on file there is not borne out. The agreement is checkable anyway, through the sphere anchor: the record's own [L208](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L208RicciFoxSquare.lean) proves $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)\delta$, positive, and L47 runs at $R=\tfrac16I$ on $S^4$, also positive, while Gorodski's Corollary 3.1.4 gives $K(X,Y)=\lambda\beta([X,Y],[X,Y])\ge0$ at compact type from $\lambda<0$ and $\beta|_{\mathfrak k}$ negative definite. Both conventions therefore make a round sphere positively curved with positive Ricci, which is the only comparison this lemma needs.

**The hypothesis is LOCALLY symmetric and the block needs no more.** 3.1.2 asks for a locally symmetric $M$ and a point, and its proof runs on one geodesic inside a normal neighbourhood. No finding.

## 2. The imported-hypothesis check, and what the transcription dropped

This is the item with a finding, and it has two halves.

**Half one, the definition.** The transcription at [notes.md](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md) renders an OIL-algebra as a triple with "$B$ an $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariant nondegenerate form". Gorodski's own definition, at line 621 of the stored text, is:

> An abstract orthogonal involutive Lie algebra is a triple $(\mathfrak g,s,B)$, where $\mathfrak g$ is a real finite-dimensional Lie algebra, $s$ is an involutive automorphism of $\mathfrak g$, **the fixed point set $\mathfrak k$ of $s$ does not contain nontrivial ideals of $\mathfrak g$** and $B$ is an $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariant **inner product** on the $-1$-eigenspace $\mathfrak p$ of $s$.

So two hypotheses are stated as one weaker one. $B$ is an inner product on $\mathfrak p$, hence positive definite and living on $\mathfrak p$ alone rather than a nondegenerate form on $\mathfrak g$, and there is an effectiveness clause that the transcription omits entirely. Lemma 2.1.2(b), which 2.1.3's proof uses, consumes both: it argues from "$B$ is positive-definite" and closes with "the multiples of $X$ form an ideal of $\mathfrak g$ contained in $\mathfrak k$, hence $X=0$".

**Does every compact Einstein symmetric space supply them? Yes, and by construction rather than by assumption.** $B$ is $g_x$ under $\mathfrak p\cong T_xM$, which is positive definite because the metric is Riemannian, and $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariant because $\mathfrak k$ is the isotropy algebra acting by isometries (Gorodski 1.5.1(iv) for the global case). Effectiveness holds because $\mathfrak k$ is represented faithfully, an ideal $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak k$ satisfying $[\mathfrak a,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak a\cap\mathfrak p=0$ and so acting trivially. **So the failure mode the brief recalls, a cited theorem wanting a unit that the object lacks, does not recur here.** Nondegeneracy is automatic, not assumed and not false anywhere in the Theorem's range.

**Half two, and this is the part the block owes.** The block opens "A symmetric space carries an orthogonal involutive Lie algebra $(\mathfrak g,s,B)$" and cites nothing for it, while the lemma's hypothesis is the weaker *locally* symmetric. The construction for a locally symmetric space is Gorodski's §1.6, whose Proposition 1.6.1 builds $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak k+\mathfrak p$ out of the local isotropy algebra and the infinitesimal transvections on a normal neighbourhood, proves $[\mathfrak k,\mathfrak k]\subseteq\mathfrak k$, $[\mathfrak k,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak p$, $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak k$, and produces $s$. **None of §1.6 is transcribed, and [notes.md](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md)'s "Not transcribed" list does not mention it.** This matters more than a missing pointer, because the composition of the two inputs requires that the algebra 2.1.3 decomposes is the same algebra whose brackets 3.1.2 computes with. That identification is exactly what §1.6 supplies. **Verdict GAP**: true, available in the cited source, absent from the record, and one transcribed paragraph would close it.

**The effectiveness clause is load-bearing rather than decorative.** See §6: without it, $\mathfrak p_0=0$ does not give $\mathfrak g_0=0$, and the lemma's phrase "no Euclidean factor" outruns what step 3 proves.

## 3. Is Gorodski's "irreducible" the notion L47 uses?

**They coincide, on the compact side, and the qualification is the finding.** Gorodski's Proposition 2.3.2 sorts the irreducible OIL-algebras into four classes: class 1, $\mathfrak g$ compact simple; class 2, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak h$ with $\mathfrak h$ compact simple and $s$ the swap; classes 3 and 4, the noncompact duals of those. Classes 1 and 2 are precisely of compact type. [L47](../findings/problem.md)'s index set is the type I and type II dichotomy it cites from [Holland-Ion](../references/holland-ion-2012-symmetric-spaces/), type II being the compact simple Lie groups, which is class 2 verbatim, plus the classical families and the twelve exceptional pairs, which is class 1. So an irreducible OIL-algebra of compact type is an object L47's census ranges over, and there is no third kind of compact irreducible factor for L210 to hand it.

L47 nowhere defines "irreducible", so the match is between Gorodski's definition and L47's *enumeration*. That is the honest reading, and on it they agree. **The gap is not in the notion, it is in the type**, and it is booked at item 9: L210's $\lambda\ne0$ permits classes 3 and 4, which L47 covers nowhere. At $r=1$ with $\lambda>0$ the factor is class 1 or class 2 and L47 applies.

## 4. Step 1, and do distinct ideals of an internal direct sum commute?

**They do.** Let $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak g_i$ with each $\mathfrak g_i$ an ideal, and take $i\ne j$. Then $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]\subseteq\mathfrak g_i$ because $\mathfrak g_i$ is an ideal, and $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]\subseteq\mathfrak g_j$ for the same reason applied to $\mathfrak g_j$, so $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]\subseteq\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak g_j=0$, the last equality being directness of the sum. The only hypothesis used is that both summands are ideals, which 2.1.3 states, and internality, which the display $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_0\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak g_r$ states. Nothing about $s$, $B$ or semisimplicity enters.

Step 1 then follows: $X\in\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak g_i$ and $Y\in\mathfrak p_j\subseteq\mathfrak g_j$ give $[X,Y]=0$, hence $[[X,Y],Z]=0$ for every $Z\in\mathfrak g$, hence $R(X,Y)Z=0$ by 3.1.2. **CONFIRMED**, and corroborated independently inside 2.1.3's own proof, whose equation (2.1.4) reads $[\mathfrak p_i,\mathfrak p_j]=0$ for $i,j>0$, $i\ne j$, derived there from Lemma 2.1.2(c) without the ideal argument at all.

## 5. Step 2, and whether the triples are the operator statement

**The triple claim is right.** $\mathfrak g_i$ is an ideal, hence a subalgebra, so $X,Y\in\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak g_i$ gives $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak g_i$, and then $[[X,Y],Z]\in[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$ for $Z\in\mathfrak p_j$.

**The operator claim is not the same statement, and the difference is a real gap.** L48 consumes "$R$ is block-diagonal on $\Lambda^2$ and zero on the mixed terms", a statement about $\mathcal R:\Lambda^2\mathfrak p\to\Lambda^2\mathfrak p$ relative to the splitting $\Lambda^2\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_i\Lambda^2\mathfrak p_i\oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}\mathfrak p_i\wedge\mathfrak p_j$. Writing $R(X,Y,Z,W)=g(R(X,Y)Z,W)$, three things are needed and the steps as written give two:

1. $\mathcal R$ kills $\mathfrak p_i\wedge\mathfrak p_j$: step 1 gives $R(X,Y)Z=0$ for all $Z$, hence $R(X,Y,Z,W)=0$ for all $Z,W$. Clean.
2. $\mathcal R(\Lambda^2\mathfrak p_i)$ has no component with a slot outside $\mathfrak p_i$: for $Z\in\mathfrak p_j$ step 2 gives $R(X,Y)Z=0$, killing every $Z\wedge W$ with $Z\notin\mathfrak p_i$. Clean.
3. The remaining case $Z\in\mathfrak p_i$, $W\in\mathfrak p_j$: here $R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]\in\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_i$, so the component vanishes **only if $g(\mathfrak p_i,\mathfrak p_j)=0$**. The block never says the summands are orthogonal, and the splitting of $\Lambda^2$ into the listed pieces is itself a direct sum only under that orthogonality.

**The gap is one line wide and has two independent repairs.** The first is to cite 2.1.3's proof, which constructs the decomposition $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_0+\cdots+\mathfrak p_r$ as a $B$-orthogonal one, from $B$-orthogonal $A$-eigenspaces refined into $B$-orthogonal $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-irreducibles. The second is to derive it: with $\mathfrak k_i=\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak k$, $A\in\mathfrak k_i$, $X\in\mathfrak p_i$ and $Y\in\mathfrak p_j$, $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariance of $B$ gives $B([A,X],Y)=-B(X,[A,Y])=0$ because $[A,Y]\in[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$, and $[\mathfrak k_i,\mathfrak p_i]=\mathfrak p_i$ for an irreducible factor, since $[\mathfrak k_i,\mathfrak p_i]$ is an $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k_i}$-submodule of $\mathfrak p_i$ and its vanishing would make $\mathfrak p_i$ abelian and the factor Euclidean. Either repair is cheaper than the sentence claiming the two statements are already identical. **Verdict GAP**, and the claim "this is exactly L48's block-diagonality, now derived rather than assumed" is the sentence to weaken.

## 6. Step 3, the load-bearing one

**From $R=0$ on $\mathfrak p_0$ alone, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}$ does not vanish on $\mathfrak p_0$.** $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)$ for $X\in\mathfrak p_0$ is a trace over a full orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak p$, so it reads $\sum_a g(R(X,e_a)e_a,X)$ with $e_a$ running over every summand. The terms with $e_a\in\mathfrak p_0$ die from $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$, which is what the block's sentence supplies. **The terms with $e_a\in\mathfrak p_j$, $j\ge1$, die from step 1**, that is from $[\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_j]=0$, and the block's step 3 does not say so. The conclusion is true and the written inference is incomplete, which is the definition of GAP this brief gives. The repair is a clause, and a second one is available from the source: 2.1.3's proof establishes $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p]=0$ outright at (2.1.4), stronger than the $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$ the block quotes, and that alone kills every term of the trace. Neither (2.1.4) nor the $B$-orthogonality of §5 is in [notes.md](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md), so both repairs want a line of transcription first.

**Given $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}|_{\mathfrak p_0}=0$, does $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda g$ with $\lambda\ne0$ force $\mathfrak p_0=0$?** Yes. For $X\in\mathfrak p_0$, $0=\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)=\lambda g(X,X)$ with $\lambda\ne0$ gives $g(X,X)=0$, and $g$ is positive definite, so $X=0$. This is where $B$ being an inner product rather than merely nondegenerate does visible work, and it is the hypothesis §2 found dropped from the transcription. Under mere nondegeneracy the same conclusion is still available from $B|_{\mathfrak p_0}$ being nondegenerate, which 2.1.3(b) names, so the step survives either reading.

**Corollary 2.1.5 is neither needed nor, strictly, corroboration.** It says $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple if and only if there is no Euclidean factor, so applied here it converts the conclusion into a statement about $\mathfrak g$. That is a consequence of step 3, not independent evidence for it, and the block's word "corroborated" overstates a citation that is logically inert. **And it is the point where effectiveness bites.** Step 3 proves $\mathfrak p_0=0$. The lemma's headline says "no Euclidean factor", which is $\mathfrak g_0=0$. These differ: $\mathfrak g_0$ is Euclidean as soon as $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$, which holds vacuously when $\mathfrak p_0=0$, so a nonzero $\mathfrak g_0\subseteq\mathfrak k$ would be a Euclidean factor surviving the Einstein condition. It is excluded by Gorodski's effectiveness clause, an ideal inside $\mathfrak k$ being trivial, and by nothing the record writes down. So the headline's "no Euclidean factor" and the appeal to 2.1.5 both rest on the hypothesis the transcription omitted. Nothing downstream depends on it, since L47 and L48 consume $\mathfrak p$ and never $\mathfrak g_0$.

## 7. Step 4, and v192 §7a

**CONFIRMED, and the reason is step 1 rather than a general fact about products.** For $X,Y\in\mathfrak p_i$, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,Y)=\sum_a g(R(X,e_a)e_a,Y)$ and every $e_a\notin\mathfrak p_i$ contributes zero by step 1, so the ambient trace equals the trace over $\mathfrak p_i$, which is the Ricci tensor of the factor whose OIL-algebra is $(\mathfrak g_i,s|_{\mathfrak g_i},B|_{\mathfrak p_i})$. Hence $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda g$ restricts to $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_i=\lambda g_i$, at the one $\lambda$, and the off-block components $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,Y)$ for $X\in\mathfrak p_i$, $Y\in\mathfrak p_j$ vanish identically, so the global condition is equivalent to the family of factorwise ones.

**This is what [v192](../verification/VERIFY_v192.md) §7a asks for, and the match is exact.** §7a's point is that $\lVert W\rVert^2=\sum_a\lVert W_a\rVert^2+\sum_af(n_a)-f(n)$ needs the same $\lambda=\tfrac12$ on every factor at once, because $f(n_a)=c_{n_a}^2\binom{n_a}2$ is computed at that constant, and that rescaling one factor breaks the identity. Step 4 delivers a single $\lambda$ common to all factors, so one global homothety normalises every factor simultaneously to $\tfrac12$. §7b's converse direction, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_M=\lambda g$ if and only if every $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_{M_a}=\lambda g_a$ at one $\lambda$, is the same statement read at the manifold, and the two agree. **One thing to note in the lemma's favour**: it supplies this hypothesis without ever claiming the space is a Riemannian product, so L48's pointwise identity is reachable even where no global splitting exists.

## 8. Is $\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak p_i$?

**Yes, and $s$-invariance is exactly what it takes.** Each $\mathfrak g_i$ is $s$-invariant by 2.1.3(a), so $s|_{\mathfrak g_i}$ is an involution of $\mathfrak g_i$ and $\mathfrak g_i=(\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak k)\oplus(\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p)$. For $X\in\mathfrak p$ write $X=\sum_ix_i$ with $x_i\in\mathfrak g_i$, uniquely. Then $\sum_is(x_i)=s(X)=-X=\sum_i(-x_i)$ with $s(x_i)\in\mathfrak g_i$, and uniqueness of components in a direct sum gives $s(x_i)=-x_i$ for each $i$, that is $x_i\in\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_i$. So $\mathfrak p\subseteq\sum_i\mathfrak p_i$, the reverse inclusion is immediate, and the sum is direct because the $\mathfrak g_i$ are. **CONFIRMED.** The same argument gives $\mathfrak k=\bigoplus_i(\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak k)$, which the block does not need. Note that this derivation gives the vector-space splitting alone, and the orthogonality §5 needs is a separate fact.

## 9. The reach, and whether the lemma covers the Theorem's quantifier

The Theorem ranges over compact Einstein symmetric spaces of dimension $n$ with $W\ne0$. The lemma hypothesises locally symmetric with $\lambda\ne0$. Three things stand between them.

**First, is $\lambda\ne0$ available? Yes, and by the lemma's own steps 1 and 2, which never use it.** If $\lambda=0$ then $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=0$, so every factor has $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_i=0$ by step 4's argument, which is impossible for $i\ge1$: an irreducible factor has $B=\lambda_i\beta|_{\mathfrak p_i}$ with $\lambda_i\ne0$ (Gorodski 3.1.4), so $K(X,Y)=\lambda_i\beta([X,Y],[X,Y])$ has one strict sign wherever $[X,Y]\ne0$ by Lemma 2.1.2(b), and for each $X\ne0$ some $Y$ has $[X,Y]\ne0$, since $\{X:[X,\mathfrak p_i]=0\}$ is an $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-submodule whose being all of $\mathfrak p_i$ would make the factor Euclidean. Hence $r=0$, so $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]=0$, so $R=0$ and $W=0$, excluded by hypothesis. **So $W\ne0$ delivers $\lambda\ne0$ on the record's own material, and the [L46](../findings/problem.md) parenthetical's route through Cartan-Ambrose-Hicks with de Rham is not the only one.** That parenthetical is also worth flagging for what it is: at $\lambda=0$ it invokes de Rham, the very step L210 exists to replace, so the two blocks between them still send the reader to the geometric theorem for one case.

**Second, and this is the finding, $\lambda\ne0$ is not enough for L47.** L47's census covers compact irreducible symmetric spaces, that is Gorodski's classes 1 and 2. A factor of class 3 or 4 has $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}<0$ and is covered by nothing in the record. The sign of $\lambda$ is what separates them: with $B=\lambda_i\beta|_{\mathfrak p_i}$ and $\beta|_{\mathfrak k}$ negative definite, $\lambda>0$ forces every factor to be of compact type and $\lambda<0$ forces every factor to be of noncompact type. **So the chain L210 to L47 needs $\lambda>0$, not $\lambda\ne0$.** The block's closing paragraph says compactness is retained as a hypothesis of the Theorem "for their own reasons, compactness excluding noncompact-type factors and fixing the sign of $\lambda$", which names the need and derives nothing. The derivation owed is short, that a globally symmetric space of noncompact type is diffeomorphic to a Euclidean space and so noncompact, hence compact plus symmetric plus $\lambda\ne0$ gives $\lambda>0$, and the record holds no source for it. A compact *locally* symmetric Einstein space with $\lambda<0$ and $W\ne0$ exists, a compact quotient of an irreducible noncompact-type space, and it satisfies every hypothesis L210 states.

**Third, a transfer step.** L47's numbers are computed for global compact irreducible symmetric spaces, while L210 delivers an irreducible OIL-algebra and hence a locally symmetric factor. The transfer is sound because $\theta$ and $\lVert W\rVert$ are pointwise algebraic invariants of $R_x$, and $R_x$ is determined by the algebra through 3.1.2, which is exactly Gorodski's Corollary 3.1.5. It is also written nowhere in the record.

**On the quantifier as a whole**: L210 plus item 9's first paragraph do exhaust it, once $\lambda>0$ is supplied. Nothing further is needed, in particular no statement that the space is a Riemannian product, since L48's identity is pointwise.

## 10. Is the claimed advantage over the geometric route real?

**Substantially yes, with one overstatement.** The geometric statement the record would otherwise use is the one Gorodski puts in his introduction as prose with no theorem number, "Every simply-connected symmetric space is a Riemannian product of irreducible symmetric spaces", and it does need simple connectivity, so reaching $\mathbb{RP}^{n/2}\times\mathbb{RP}^{n/2}$ through it does mean passing to the universal cover and then wanting that cover's compactness. **The block is right that $\mathbb{RP}^{n/2}$ puts non-simply-connected spaces in scope**: it is the equality case L48 states after [r501](../reviews/REVIEW_r501.md) and [r502](../reviews/REVIEW_r502.md) repaired it, and $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^m)=\mathbb Z/2$ for $m\ge2$. It is also right that both algebraic inputs are topology-free, 2.1.3 being linear algebra on $\mathfrak p$ and 3.1.2 asking only for local symmetry.

**The overstatement.** There is a standard statement that avoids simple connectivity, the local de Rham decomposition: a parallel splitting of the tangent bundle gives a local isometric splitting, with no completeness and no simple connectivity, and that is all a pointwise invariant such as $\theta$ needs. So "the geometric de Rham theorem needs simple connectivity" is true of the global product statement and false of the local splitting. **It is not a straw man**, because the record carries no source for the local version either, so between the two available routes the algebraic one is the one whose inputs are on file. The fair wording is that the geometric route as the record could cite it needs simple connectivity, not that the geometric route as such does.

In this specific case the cover step would also have been cheap, $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ being visibly compact, but a Theorem quantifying over every compact Einstein symmetric space cannot be argued at its equality case, so the objection to the route is the right shape.

## 11. The Lean file

**`curv` matches 3.1.2.** `def curv (x y z : L) : L := -⁅⁅x, y⁆, z⁆` is the identity's right-hand side, and the docstring names the source and its $\nabla$-convention. No mismatch.

**The three theorems assume the mixed-term vanishing rather than establishing it.** All three reduce to `curv_eq_zero_of_bracket_eq_zero`, which takes `⁅x, y⁆ = 0` as a hypothesis and closes by `simp`. `curv_eq_zero_of_mem_of_mem` takes `hIJ : ∀ a ∈ I, ∀ b ∈ J, ⁅a, b⁆ = 0`, so the mathematical content of step 1, that distinct ideals of an internal direct sum commute, is a binder and not a proof, and `curv_eq_zero_of_abelian_part` is the same theorem with `I = J = P`. The word "ideal" occurs in no statement, the arguments being `Set L`. They are non-vacuous, in that the hypotheses are satisfiable by nontrivial objects such as any abelian algebra, and they are trivial, in that what is proved is $[x,y]=0\Rightarrow-[[x,y],z]=0$. **The file's own docstring claims more than the file proves**, saying the theorem holds "for the one reason that ideals of an internal direct sum commute", a reason it assumes.

**The row's refusal to claim `statement: typed` is the honest call**, and the row explains what is missing accurately, steps 3 and 4 needing a Ricci contraction the layer has no object for.

**One claim in the row is false.** It states that "the ideal hypothesis is stated over subsets because `LieIdeal` is not in the vendored Mathlib under that name". The vendored Mathlib is pinned at `v4.33.1` in [lean/lakefile.toml](../lean/lakefile.toml), and it carries `abbrev LieIdeal` at `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Ideal.lean:46`, together with `LieSubmodule.lie_le_inf : ⁅I, J⁆ ≤ I ⊓ J` for `I J : LieIdeal R L` at `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/IdealOperations.lean:142`. The file imports `Mathlib.Algebra.Lie.Submodule`, which is where `LieIdeal` used to live and no longer does, so the likely history is a grep of the wrong module. **The consequence is not cosmetic**: with `lie_le_inf` plus `I ⊓ J = ⊥`, step 1's actual content is provable in a few lines over `LieIdeal`, which would turn the load-bearing hypothesis into a theorem. The row's stated reason for taking it as a hypothesis does not hold.

**`Lean: blocked -- no lieRic` names a genuine absence, and the word overstates it.** No declaration anywhere in the repository mentions `lieRic` or any Ricci contraction of a Lie-algebra curvature, and the layer's `Ric` is the adjoint of $\cdot\wedge\mathrm{id}$ on $\Lambda^2$ operators, a different object. So there is nothing to reuse. But stating step 3 needs only a finite orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak p$ and a sum, which the layer does elsewhere, so the obstacle is work not yet done rather than an unavailable primitive, and "blocked" reads as the latter.

## 12. What would break it, and what I did to look

**The objects that would falsify it, in the order of how much they would cost.**

- **A nonzero Euclidean ideal surviving the Einstein condition.** This is the live one, and it is not a hypothetical: $\mathfrak g_0\subseteq\mathfrak k$ with $\mathfrak p_0=0$ is Euclidean by Gorodski's definition and is killed by his effectiveness clause alone, which the record's transcription dropped. It would falsify the headline's "no Euclidean factor" and the appeal to 2.1.5, and it would leave steps 1, 2 and 4 and every downstream consumer untouched. I looked for it by reading the definition at source rather than in the transcription, which is how it surfaced.
- **A pair of summands that are not $g$-orthogonal.** That would break the $\Lambda^2$ block-diagonality L48 quotes while leaving the triple statements of steps 1 and 2 true. I looked in 2.1.3's proof, which constructs the decomposition $B$-orthogonally, and then derived the orthogonality a second way from $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariance (§5), so it holds twice over and only the record's wording is at fault.
- **An irreducible factor with $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}=0$.** That would break item 9's $\lambda=0$ exclusion and let a flat direction hide inside a non-Euclidean factor. Refuted above from 3.1.4 and 2.1.2(b), the sectional curvature of an irreducible factor having one strict sign wherever the bracket is nonzero.
- **A noncompact-type factor inside the Theorem's range.** Not a falsifier of L210, which admits it honestly at $\lambda<0$, and a falsifier of the covering claim L210 advertises. Named at item 9.
- **A convention mismatch in 3.1.2.** Refuted structurally at item 1: every use is a vanishing.

I ran no numerics. The two candidates worth a screen, a flat factor inside an Einstein symmetric space and a Euclidean factor with $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}\ne0$, are both settled by the identity $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}|_{\mathfrak p_0}=0$ rather than by a computation, and $S^2\times T^2$ is the standard witness that a flat factor breaks Einstein rather than surviving it.

## Verdict for L210 as a whole

**CONFIRMED as a statement, GAP as written, and the covering claim it advertises needs one more step than it takes.**
