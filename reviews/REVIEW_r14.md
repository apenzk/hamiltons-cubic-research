### Reviewer 14: the tube lemma, Theorem 4.17's dimension range, and the Hessian kernel

Rebuilt the algebra from definitions, structure constants by literal commutators, $\#$ as a bilinear map validated against Hamilton's diagonal formula, the Weyl basis as $\mathrm{Bianchi}\cap\mathrm{Ricci\text{-}flat}$, using this repo's modules only as cross-checks.

**The tube lemma, confirmed.** The step that could have sunk it is whether $\alpha\le1/3$ really applies on $(\mathbb{R}u\oplus T_u(\mathrm{orbit}))^\perp$. It holds tightly: $T_u(SO(n).u)$ and the $\tfrac12\theta^*$ eigenspace do not merely have equal dimension, they **coincide**, with $\|\Pi_E-\Pi_T\|_2\le1.4\times10^{-14}$ at $n=8,10,12,14$. So the complement's top eigenvalue is exactly $\tfrac13\theta^*$ and the bound is attained rather than slack. Orbit directions are already Bianchi and Ricci-flat exactly, so projecting into $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ is a no-op; the induced $SO(n)$ action is orthogonal to $1.5\times10^{-15}$ and preserves $P$ to $10^{-16}$, so the rotation step is safe; $\psi\le1$ exactly on $[0,\varphi_c]$ with $\varphi_c=0.574826297515555$, the root of $\sin^3\varphi=1-\cos\varphi$. Three independent hunts inside the tube at $n=12$ found nothing above $\sqrt{3/2}$, global ascent from 40 starts tops at $\theta_0(12)$, tube-constrained ascent converges to $\varphi=0$, and a fixed-$\varphi$ sweep over the full $1603$-dimensional complement gives $\max f=0.9147$ against $\psi=0.99999$. The margin is wide because $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ trade off, so the worst case the proof guards against is not realisable on any single $V$.

**Theorem 4.17, the proof does not extend, and the premise dies to a classical witness.** At $n=12$ the balanced $S^6\times S^6$ Weyl operator for the split $\{0,\dots,5\}\mid\{6,\dots,11\}$, whose first factor contains the $\mathbb{CP}^2$ block, is exactly orthogonal to $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ ($0.0$) and to the orbit tangent ($4.8\times10^{-16}$), with $P=1.236033081$. So $f(\pi/2)=1.0092168>1$ for an admissible competitor, and no weaker "local to the curve" reading escapes. Ascent over the whole complement tops at the same value, so $c_{12}=1.009216785$ is the sharp replacement constant. The minimiser algebra was verified symbolically: $(1-\alpha)k^2-ck+\alpha=0$ and $\gamma_{\max}(c{=}1)=(1+\alpha)\sqrt{1-2\alpha}$ exactly. **Wording correction:** his *proof* does not extend; his *theorem* is not shown false, for that witness $f$ is still strictly decreasing on $(0,\pi/6)$ with $f(\pi/6)=0.7757$.

**The Hessian kernel, confirmed exactly.** $\ker(6F_u-3\theta^*)=T_u(SO(n).u)$ of dimension $4n-14$, threshold-independent across tolerances $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-12}$ and proved *exactly over a finite field* at $n=6,8,10$.

**Corrections it forced.** Theorem 4.14 has cases (a)–**(g)**; the $-\sqrt{3/2}$ eigenvalue on $\mathrm{Weyl}^{-,2}_4$ is case (g), not an omission, and this project's claim of a discrepancy is withdrawn. $\psi$ is not monotone. The parity restriction on the tube lemma is unnecessary, odd $n\ge13$ also clears $\sqrt{3/2}$, so it holds for all $n\ge12$.

**The risk it left open, since closed.** $\alpha\le1/3$ rested on Theorem 4.14's Schur argument, valid only on multiplicity-free isotypic components. That dependency was subsequently removed by the $SO(n-4)$-equivariant decomposition recorded in [findings/INDEX.md](../findings/INDEX.md), which absorbs multiplicities into the multiplicity spaces and needs no such hypothesis.
