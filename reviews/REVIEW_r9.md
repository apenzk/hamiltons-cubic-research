**2. **3. The Fox first-order protection lemma, CONFIRMED as a theorem, with TWO corrections to the surrounding claims.** Every ingredient re-derived by hand and machine-checked at $n=8$ and $n=12$. The structure is exact: spectrum $(2,-1,-1,0,\dots)/\sqrt6$, $P=\sqrt{3/2}$ to 10 digits with criticality residual $4\times10^{-16}$, $\mathrm{Ric}(W)=\mathrm{Alt}(W)=0$ identically, and $\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)=a\star$ with $a=1/(3\sqrt6)$ at **zero** residual, which this reviewer also derived by hand ($\mathrm{Alt}(\hat\omega_i\hat\omega_i^{\mathsf T})=\tfrac16\star$ for each self-dual $\hat\omega_i$, so $\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)=\tfrac{2}{6\sqrt6}\star$), giving $\delta^2=6a^2=1/9$ analytically rather than numerically. The Daleckii–Krein divided differences are $0$ on $(+,+)$, $-1$ on $(-,-)$ and $(-,0)$, $-\tfrac13$ on $(+,-)$, $0$ on $(+,0)$, and the $(0,0)$ block contributes the kink $(P_0HP_0)_-$; since $\Pi_{ASD}\subset\ker W$ and $\star=\Pi_{SD}-\Pi_{ASD}$, this yields $d^+\delta^2(H)=-2a\,F(H)$ exactly as claimed. Verified against one-sided finite differences on random, engineered-$B\prec0$, engineered-$B\succ0$, mixed and $O(n)$-orbit-tangent directions: worst error $3\times10^{-5}$ at truncation level. Ingredient (d) holds to $5\times10^{-16}$ ($\langle H,\star\rangle=0$ and $2H_{11}=H_{22}+H_{33}$, giving $H_{22}+H_{33}=\tfrac23\operatorname{tr}B$); ingredient (c) is exactly Hansen–Pedersen's Jensen trace inequality with $f(x)=x_-$ convex and $f(0)=0$ applied to the isometry $\Pi_{ASD}\le P_0$, and its margin was positive on all 800 sampled directions. The chain $F\ge\tfrac23\operatorname{tr}B_++\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}B_-\ge0$ then follows algebraically. **The lemma is correct.** Two corrections to what is said around it:

- **ERROR (minor): "adversarial minimization of $F$ ... finds min $F=+0.15>0$" is a sampling artifact, not the minimum.** Over 2000 *random* unit tangent directions this reviewer gets exactly that number ($0.1507$ at $n=8$, $0.1160$ at $n=12$), so $+0.15$ is the random-direction floor. But $F$ is **convex and 1-homogeneous** on the tangent space ($H\mapsto H_{22}+H_{33}$ is linear and $H\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{ASD}(P_0HP_0)_-)$ is convex, being a convex matrix function traced against a PSD projection), so projected subgradient descent certifies the sign globally rather than locally. Doing that drives $F$ to $4\times10^{-5}$ ($n=8$) and $7\times10^{-5}$ ($n=12$), i.e. to $0$, not to $0.15$. The sentence is also self-contradictory as written, since it reports in the same breath that $F$ vanishes on $O(n)$-orbit tangents. **Consequence:** the number should be struck; the correct statement is $\inf F=0$, attained, which is what the lemma's own equality analysis already implies.
- **ERROR (substantive for the stated next step): the equality manifold $\{F=0\}$ is far larger than the two families named.** FINDINGS lists the $O(n)$-orbit and the hyper-Kähler subalgebra tangents. This is a linear subspace of dimension $\dim\mathcal W_{n-4}$, **10 at $n=8$ and 300 at $n=12$**, against an orbit dimension of 34. Independently, the directed minimisers of $F$ found above have only $0.12$–$0.24$ overlap with the orbit-tangent space and $\|P_0HP_0\|\approx0.91$–$0.96$, so they are not orbit directions either. **Consequence:** Result 6's "what this does not yet prove" understates the remaining work, the second-order argument on $\{F=0\}$ must control a $\ge300$-dimensional flat at $n=12$, not a handful of symmetry directions. This is the single most important finding of this review.

**5. The lifted Lemma 3.4.6 (Result 8), CONFIRMED, proof logic sound, with a stronger reduction and one sign slip.** Both flagged steps hold. $\mathrm{Ric}\wedge\mathrm{Ric}$ and $\mathrm{Ric}\wedge2\,\mathrm{id}$ are diagonal in the basis induced by any $\mathrm{Ric}$-eigenbasis (off-diagonal norms $\le1.3\times10^{-11}$), so $\operatorname{tr}(RD)$ really does see only $R$'s diagonal there, verified against the basis-free trace at $n=5,6,7$ on PSD operators with $\|R_{\text{offdiag}}\|$ up to $105$, so the check is not vacuous. The Ricci-annihilates-$\Lambda^4$ step holds: $\mathrm{Ric}(\mathrm{Alt}X)=0$ and $\mathrm{Ric}(\mathcal W)=0$ exactly, and any coordinate-diagonal comparison operator $\tilde R$ is automatically Bianchi ($\|\mathrm{Alt}\|=0$ exactly), hence $\Lambda^4$-free, so $\langle R,\tilde R\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Ric}(R),h\rangle$ via Result 6a's $\langle R,h\wedge2\,\mathrm{id}\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Ric}(R),h\rangle$, which this reviewer confirmed for *every* symmetric $R$ (residual $\le3.6\times10^{-15}$). Numerically the inequality held on 600 random PSD operators at $n=5,6,7,8$ carrying Bianchi-defect fractions to $0.41$ (min normalised margins $+0.027$ to $+0.052$), and $R=\mathrm{Id}$ gives **exact** equality $n(n-1)^4$ at every $n$, which also pins the convention as $\mathrm{Ric}\wedge\mathrm{Ric}$ having diagonal $\lambda_i\lambda_j$ and $\mathrm{Ric}\wedge2\,\mathrm{id}$ having $\lambda_i+\lambda_j$. Two additions:

- **The statement reduces exactly to a scalar inequality, and PSD is stronger than it needs.** Since the LHS sees only $\operatorname{diag}(R)$ in the $\mathrm{Ric}$-eigenbasis, the RHS only $\mathrm{Ric}(R)$, and $\lambda_i=\sum_{j\ne i}R_{(ij)(ij)}$ automatically, the claim "for all PSD $R$" is *equivalent* to: for every hollow symmetric $d\ge0$ with row sums $\lambda$, $\sum_{i<j}d_{ij}\lambda_i\lambda_j(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)\ge n\bar\lambda^4+\bar\lambda^2(2-\epsilon)\|\lambda_0\|^2-\bar\lambda(1+\epsilon)\sum\lambda_{0,i}^3$. Verified to agree with the operator formula to $3.6\times10^{-15}$. This makes the test far sharper than random PSD sampling: adversarial minimisation over $d\ge0$ at $n=4,5,6,8,12$ returns min margin exactly $0$, attained only at $d\equiv\mathbf1$ ($R=\mathrm{Id}$), with the margin ranging over $[+0.003,+50]$ (median $\approx+1.5$–$2.1$) on random $d$ and a strictly positive Hessian at the equality point. **No violation exists.**
- **The $\epsilon\ge(\sqrt{10}-3)/2$ hypothesis does not bind.** The same minimisation at $\epsilon=0.99\epsilon_0$, $\epsilon=0.05$ and $\epsilon=0$ still returns min margin $0$, and the Hessian at $d\equiv\mathbf1$ stays strictly positive down to $\epsilon=-0.5$. So $\epsilon_0$ is a sufficient condition inherited from Jäger's Lemma 3.4.5 route, not a necessary one for the assembled inequality, worth knowing, since it is slack the §3.5 margins could in principle spend.
- **Sign slip in the write-up (cosmetic).** Result 8 displays "$R_{ijji}=\langle R(b_i\wedge b_j),b_i\wedge b_j\rangle\ge0$". In fact $R_{ijji}=-R_{ijij}=-\langle R(b_i\wedge b_j),b_i\wedge b_j\rangle\le0$ for PSD $R$ (checked: $-10.126$ vs $+10.126$). The neighbouring "$\operatorname{tr}(RD)=\sum_{i<j}R_{ijji}D_{ijji}$" is fine because both signs flip, and the argument is correct read in the $ijij$ order throughout, but as printed the two clauses are inconsistent, and a reader checking "may be multiplied through" against a negative $R_{ijji}$ will stall. Change the index order to $R_{ijij}$, $D_{ijij}$.
