## 2. [isWeyl_extend](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L235), and the converse the brief asks for

**The forward direction is correct and its proof reads as it claims.** The first clause comes from the codomain, and the other three come from [extend_apply](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L214) rewriting `extend R (φ_ij)` to `applyPhi R i j` in each summand. The self-adjointness clause is the only one that needs the rewrite at a general element rather than at a `φ`, and the proof does exactly that, instantiating at `⟨X, hX⟩` and `⟨Y, hY⟩`. It is kernel-clean on the standard three axioms.

**The converse is the item's real question, and it holds.** If `IsWeyl2` held of fewer operators than the record's `IsWeyl` does, [I36Shape](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L276) would be a weaker proposition than the item, and it would still type-check. The obstruction to be feared is linearity: `IsWeyl` is stated at a BARE function and asks for no linearity at all, while `CurvOp2` is continuous-linear by construction, so a record-Weyl bare operator that is not linear would be outside the new class.

**No such operator exists, because linearity on `Λ²` is a CONSEQUENCE of the record's own first two clauses.** For skew `X` and `Y`, put `Z = R(X+Y) - R X - R Y`, which is skew by clause one applied three times. Then

`⟨Z, Z⟩ = ⟨R(X+Y), Z⟩ - ⟨R X, Z⟩ - ⟨R Y, Z⟩ = ⟨X+Y, R Z⟩ - ⟨X, R Z⟩ - ⟨Y, R Z⟩ = 0`,

the middle step being clause two at `(X+Y, Z)`, `(X, Z)` and `(Y, Z)`, each with both arguments skew, and the last being bilinearity of [inner2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean#L39). Since `⟨Z, Z⟩ = ½ ∑ Z_ab²`, [inner2_self_eq_zero](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/Bivectors2.lean#L91) gives `Z = 0`. The same argument with `Z = R(cX) - c·R X` gives homogeneity. **I proved both in Lean and they compile**, and the control, the identical script asked to conclude `R (X + Y) = R X - R Y`, is REJECTED at the final `sub_eq_zero.mp`.

**So the restriction exists and lands in the new class.** With `restrictL hW X = ⟨R ↑X, hW.1 _ X.2⟩` a linear map, continuous by [LinearMap.continuous_of_finiteDimensional](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Normed/Module/FiniteDimension.html#LinearMap.continuous_of_finiteDimensional), `restrictCL hW : CurvOp2 (Fin n)` satisfies `IsWeyl2` by the three projections of section 1. **And the restriction loses nothing [I36Shape](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L276) reads**: `toTensor2 (restrictCL hW) = toTensor R` and `normSq2 (restrictCL hW) = normSq R`, the second by `rfl`. All five declarations compile.

**The two directions together.** Every `IsWeyl2` operator extends to a record-Weyl bare operator agreeing with it on `Λ²` ([isWeyl_extend](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L235)), and every record-Weyl bare operator restricts to an `IsWeyl2` operator agreeing with it on `Λ²` (here). Since every quantity in the statement is a function of the operator's action on `Λ²`, the quantifier of [I36Shape](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L276) ranges over the record's class read on the record's domain. *What the change of domain removes is the radical [WeylRadical.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/WeylRadical.lean) describes, the operators differing only off `Λ²`, which the record's own quantities cannot see.*

**The opposite reading, argued.** One could hold that `CurvOp2` is narrower because the record's `IsWeyl` predicate also holds of bare functions that are wild off `Λ²`, and those have no counterpart here. That is true and it is not a narrowing of the CLASS the item is about: a bare operator and its restriction have the same `P`, the same norm and the same tensor, so the wild ones are duplicates under every quantity the item mentions, and [r1004](REVIEW_r1004.md) already records that the whole `Hom(Sym, M)` of them is inert.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with the converse holding and having no argument on file.** The finding to carry is that `restrictCL` and `isWeyl2_restrict` are absent from the layer, so the file's *"the native class is not a weaker thing wearing the same name"* is currently supported in one direction only.

## 3. [skewProj](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L192), [extend](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L210) and [toTensor2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L221)

## 6. [I36Budget](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L283) as the budget form

**The two are equivalent, and the proof is three lines rather than an assumption.** [L174](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L174.lean#L58)'s `shape_iff_budget` takes `L94` as a HYPOTHESIS over abstract types, and its docstring names the sense of "unit" and the per-frame/extremal swap as the two risks its abstraction cannot see. At this domain neither is abstract: [kappaSq_eq_iff](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L94Statement.lean#L502) is the real equality case, proved, and it applies to `toTensor2 R` through `isWeyl_toTensor (isWeyl_extend hw)`. **I proved `I36Shape n t ↔ I36Budget n t` from it directly**, and the control with `3 * normM2` in place of `2 * normM2` is REJECTED by the identical script. *So the file's pairing is tighter than the one L174 states, and the swap risk L174 names is discharged rather than carried.*

**And the right-hand side is L174's `2`.** `I36Budget` writes `kappaSq (toTensor2 R) = 2 * normM2 (toTensor2 R)` where L174 writes `kappaSq W = 2`. Under the stated hypotheses the two agree: [normM2_toTensor](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L140) gives `normM2 (toTensor2 R) = normSq (extend R)`, and `normSq (extend R) = normSq2 R` holds by [extend_apply](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/TensorBridge.lean#L214) under the two sums, **which I proved in four lines**, so `2 * normM2 (toTensor2 R) = 2` follows from `normSq2 R = 1`. That composite compiles.

**The finding is that the middle link is missing from the layer.** No declaration states `normSq (extend R) = normSq2 R`, so as committed, `I36Budget`'s right-hand side is not visibly `2` under its own hypotheses, and a reader comparing it with [L174](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L174.lean#L53) has to supply the step. The control for my version, the same script asked for `normSq (extend R) = 2 * normSq2 R`, is REJECTED, the `congr 1` leaving a stuck goal.

**The unit-norm hypothesis is idle in the equivalence and essential in the comparison.** `I36Shape ↔ I36Budget` needs only `IsWeyl2`, since `kappaSq_eq_iff` is an identity at every Weyl tensor, so the `normSq2 R = 1` in both definitions is carried for the item's sake exactly as [L174](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L174.lean#L27) says its own maximiser hypotheses are. **That is a strength of the form chosen here and not a defect**: writing `= 2` instead would have made the statement depend on the norm convention, and writing `2 * normM2` makes it independent of it.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and it is the better of the two forms.**
