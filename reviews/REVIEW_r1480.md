## A. The model

- **Coordinates.** `phi i j = single j i 1 - single i j 1`, so $(\varphi_{ij})_{ij}=-1$ and $(\varphi_{ij})_{ji}=+1$. `inner2_phi` gives $\langle Y,\varphi_{ij}\rangle=-Y_{ij}$ for skew $Y$, hence `coords X p = -X_{ij}` for skew $X$.
- **Parseval.** `inner2_eq_sum` gives $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}X_{ab}Y_{ab}$, `sum_pairs` turns this into $\tfrac12\cdot2\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}Y_{ij}$, and the Euclidean inner product of the coordinates is $\sum_{i<j}(-X_{ij})(-Y_{ij})$. The two agree term by term. This is the Parseval identity $\sum_{i<j}\langle X,\varphi_{ij}\rangle\langle Y,\varphi_{ij}\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle$ for the orthonormal family $\{\varphi_{ij}\}_{i<j}$ ($\langle\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{ij}\rangle=\tfrac12\cdot2=1$), which spans the skew matrices. So `coords` restricted to skew matrices is a linear isometry into `Biv n`, and since both spaces have dimension $\binom n2$ it is onto. Surjectivity is true but not stated in Lean, and the file does not claim it.
- **`sum_pairs`.** Hypotheses `f i i = 0` and `f j i = f i j`. Sufficient: the ordered double sum splits into $i<j$, $i=j$, $i>j$, the middle vanishes and the last reindexes to the first. Both are needed: an antisymmetric $f$ has double sum $0$ with nonzero pair sum, and $f=\delta_{ij}$ breaks the identity through the diagonal. In `inner_coords` they come from $X_{ii}=0$ and $X_{ji}Y_{ji}=X_{ij}Y_{ij}$, both from skewness.

## B. `defectSq_eq`

- **It is the layer's `defectSq`.** `defectSq lam om = altNormSq (negPartOp lam om) = fourNormSq (alt4 (comp (specOp (negPart ∘ lam) om)))`, and `altNormSqOf X = fourNormSq (alt4 (compOf X))`. After `specFn_rankSum` rewrites $g(W)$ to `rankSum (negPart ∘ lam) (coords ∘ om)`, the proof reduces to `comp_specOp_eq` entrywise inside the same `fourNormSq ∘ alt4`. Both sides use the same `alt4` (with its $\tfrac13$) and the same `fourInner` (with its $\tfrac14$), so no normalisation is lost.
- **`specFn_rankSum` and `g(0) = 0`.** Via `specFn_unique`: for an eigenpair $Tv=\mu v$, each $\lambda_\gamma\langle w_\gamma,v\rangle=\mu\langle w_\gamma,v\rangle$, so $g(\lambda_\gamma)\langle w_\gamma,v\rangle=g(\mu)\langle w_\gamma,v\rangle$, and the right side is $g(\mu)p$ with $p$ the projection of $v$ on the span. From $\mu(v-p)=0$: either $v=p$, done, or $\mu=0$, where `g0` gives $g(\mu)v=0=g(\mu)p$. So `g0` is used exactly in the branch where the family does not span and $v$ has a kernel component, which is where it must be. It is necessary: with $g(0)\ne0$, $g(W)$ acts as $g(0)$ on the orthogonal complement of the span and the rank sum acts as $0$. `defectSq_eq` discharges it by `simp [negPartScalar]`, $(\lvert0\rvert-0)/2=0$.
- **Orthonormality is needed** (as the only orthogonality hypothesis). At $n=4$ put $\psi=(\varphi_{01}+\varphi_{23})/\sqrt2$, unit and skew, $\omega_1=\omega_2=\psi$, $\lambda=(1,-1)$. Then $W=0$ and the right side is $0$. The left side has $\mathrm{comp}=\langle\psi,\varphi_{ij}\rangle\langle\psi,\varphi_{kl}\rangle$, and $\mathrm{alt4}$ at $(0,1,2,3)$ is $\tfrac13(\tfrac12+0+0)=\tfrac16\ne0$, so $\mathrm{defectSq}>0$.
- **Skewness is needed.** Same $\psi$, $S$ symmetric with $\langle S,S\rangle=1$ (then $\langle S,\varphi_{ij}\rangle=0$), $\omega_{1,2}=(\psi\pm S)/\sqrt2$, which are orthonormal under `inner2`, and $\lambda=(-1,1)$. Both have `coords` equal to `coords ψ`$/\sqrt2$, so the rank sum is $0$ and the right side is $0$. The left side has $\mathrm{comp}=\tfrac12\langle\psi,\varphi_{ij}\rangle\langle\psi,\varphi_{kl}\rangle$, with $\mathrm{alt4}(0,1,2,3)=\tfrac1{12}\ne0$.
- **Orthonormality is sufficient but not minimal.** For $\omega_\gamma$ orthogonal and nonzero, the eigenvalue on $\omega_\gamma$ is $\lambda_\gamma\lVert\omega_\gamma\rVert^2$ and $x_-$ is positively homogeneous, so $g(W)=\sum_\gamma(\lambda_\gamma)_-\,\omega_\gamma\omega_\gamma^{\mathsf T}$ still holds. The Lean statement does not cover that case.
- **G1, a scope limit that 5c must bridge.** `omCP2 = ![bivA, bivB, bivC]` with `bivA = φ01 + φ23` etc., so $\langle\omega_\gamma,\omega_\gamma\rangle=\tfrac12\cdot4=2$ and `horth` fails. The same holds for `omU = pad ∘ omCP2`, the competitor's presentation in `defect_u`. So `defectSq_eq` does not reach the layer's value $\delta^2(u)=1/9$ as proved. 5c needs either the orthonormal presentation $(2\lambda,\omega/\sqrt2)$ with a proof that `negPartOp` agrees (by `negPartScalar_smul`), or `defectSq_eq` for orthogonal presentations.
- **Hand check at $\mathbb{CP}^2$.** `comp_negPart` states $\tfrac12B_{ij}B_{kl}+\tfrac12C_{ij}C_{kl}$. In the model, with $b=$ `coords bivB` $=e_{02}-e_{13}$, $c=$ `coords bivC` $=e_{03}+e_{12}$, $a=$ `coords bivA` $=e_{01}+e_{23}$ (read off `bivB_eq`, `bivC_eq` and `coords = -X_{ij}`), the operator is $W=\lambda_A aa^{\mathsf T}+\lambda_B bb^{\mathsf T}+\lambda_C cc^{\mathsf T}$ with eigenvalues $2,-1,-1$ on $a/\sqrt2,b/\sqrt2,c/\sqrt2$ and $0$ on the anti-self-dual $3$-plane, so $\lVert W\rVert^2=6$. Its negative part is $1\cdot\tfrac12bb^{\mathsf T}+1\cdot\tfrac12cc^{\mathsf T}$, and $\mathrm{compOf}$ of it is $\tfrac12\langle b,\text{coords}\,\varphi_{ij}\rangle\langle b,\text{coords}\,\varphi_{kl}\rangle+(c)=\tfrac12(-B_{ij})(-B_{kl})+\tfrac12(-C_{ij})(-C_{kl})$, which is `comp_negPart`. Continuing, $\mathrm{alt4}(0,1,2,3)=\tfrac13(0+\tfrac12+\tfrac12)=\tfrac13$ (using $B_{02}B_{31}=1$, $C_{03}C_{12}=1$), and a fully antisymmetric array with that component has `fourNormSq` $=\tfrac14\cdot24\cdot\tfrac19=\tfrac23$, which is `defect_WCP2`. Passes.

## C. The chain rule and `hasDerivWithinAt_defect`

- **Factor.** `fourNormSq A = ¼ Σ A·A`. Each `compOf (F t) i j k l` is `compCLM i j k l` (continuous linear) applied to $F(t)$, so it has derivative `compOf D`, and `alt4` is linear, so `alt4 (compOf (F t))` has derivative `alt4 (compOf D)`. The product rule gives $\tfrac14\sum(A'A+AA')=2\cdot\tfrac14\sum AA'=2\,\mathrm{fourInner}(A,A')$. The $\tfrac14$ sits inside `fourInner` and the $2$ outside, correctly.
- **$D$.** `hasDerivWithinAt_defect` instantiates `hasDerivWithinAt_specFn_negPart` at `E = Biv n`, `g = negPartScalar` (hypotheses by `negPartScalar_of_nonneg`, `_of_nonpos`, `_smul`), and its derivative expression is character for character the one in [NegPartDeriv.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/NegPartDeriv.lean) (r1479 CONFIRMED). With `rotGen = schur rotφ` ($K_{ij}=H_{ij}/(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$ off the blocks), `negBlkφ a b = [a = b ∧ a < 0]` and `zeroφ a b = [a = 0 ∧ b = 0]` it is $[K,u_-]-\sum_{\lambda<0}P_\lambda HP_\lambda+(P_0HP_0)_-$. The one remaining step rewrites `specFn (hW 0)` to `specFn hu`, since `u + 0 • H = u` and the symmetry proof is irrelevant.
- **Orientation.** `comp R i j k l = inner2 (R (phi i j)) (phi k l)`, $\langle R\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{kl}\rangle$. `compOf X i j k l = inner (coords (phi k l)) (X (coords (phi i j)))`, $\langle\varphi_{kl},X\varphi_{ij}\rangle$, the same quantity by symmetry of the real inner product, with $X$ applied to $\varphi_{ij}$ in both. `comp_specOp_eq` confirms it on rank sums, where both sides are $\sum_\gamma\lambda_\gamma\langle\omega_\gamma,\varphi_{ij}\rangle\langle\omega_\gamma,\varphi_{kl}\rangle$. The orientation matters only for non-symmetric $X$, and $D$ is symmetric anyway.
- **Hypotheses.** `hW` follows from `hu` and `hH` and is a true proposition carried as a proof argument, as in r1479. The derivative is within `Set.Ici 0` at `0`, one-sided as claimed.
