#### Lemma L229, $G(2)$ at fixed total norm on the common-eigenvector stratum: the phase maximum is one squared distance, and the value is affine where it is attained

*Gate 2 in flight. Derived 2026-09-16 for [I106](../plan.md) ([derivation](../reviews/2026-09-15-I106-m3-derivation.md), batch 14), on Opus at the user's instruction. It supersedes that derivation's batch 12, whose closed form was three cases of calculus, and it removes the four-amplitude comparison batch 13 left owed. The identity, the cap, the diagonal deficits, the boundary attainment and the affineness are kernel-checked in [L229Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L229Statement.lean), with the closing step standing as a named `sorry`.*

**Setting.** $G(2)$ at fixed total norm is the problem $\sup\Phi$ over traceless symmetric $3\times3$ matrices $\hat U,V$ with $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2=T$, where $$\Phi(\hat U,V)=\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat U^3+\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UA)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC),\qquad\alpha=\tfrac{15}4,\quad\beta=\tfrac94,$$ and $A,C$ are diagonal in an orthonormal frame $F$ of $\mathbb R^J$. It is the step of [R27](../routes.md) that frees two outer blocks while the rest stay diagonal in $F$, with $\hat U$ and $V$ the shifted sum and the difference of the two blocks and only the total norm held ([derivation](../reviews/2026-09-15-I106-m3-derivation.md), batch 10). **The common-eigenvector stratum** is the set where $\hat U$ and $V$ share an eigenvector that is a vector of $F$, say $e_2$. There, with $R(\vartheta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\vartheta&\sin\vartheta\\\sin\vartheta&-\cos\vartheta\end{pmatrix}$ on $e_2^\perp$, $\hat U=-\tfrac{u_2}2I+pR(2\varphi)$ and $V=-\tfrac{v_2}2I+rR(2\chi)$ with $p,r\ge0$, and $d_A=\tfrac12(A_1-A_3)$, $d_C=\tfrac12(C_1-C_3)$, $\tilde a=A_2-\tfrac12(A_1+A_3)$, $\tilde c=C_2-\tfrac12(C_1+C_3)$. By the symmetries of the stratum take $d_A,d_C,v_2>0$, the case in which the phase problem is frustrated ([derivation](../reviews/2026-09-15-I106-m3-derivation.md), batch 11). Write $$\Lambda=\frac{d_Ad_C}{\beta v_2},\qquad\rho_1=\frac{\beta v_2\,r}{d_A},\qquad\rho_2=\frac{\beta v_2\,p}{d_C},\qquad\mathcal A=\{z\in\mathbb C:\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert\le\lvert z\rvert\le\rho_1+\rho_2\}.$$

**Statement.** *On the common-eigenvector stratum, $\sup\Phi$ at fixed $T$ is attained with $\hat U$ and $V$ both diagonal in $F$.* The four steps:
1. *The phase sum of batch 11 is $g=2\Lambda\rho_2\cos\vartheta_1+2\Lambda\rho_1\cos\vartheta_2-2\Lambda\rho_1\rho_2\cos(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_2)$ with $\vartheta_1=2\varphi$, $\vartheta_2=2\chi$, and at every pair of phases $$\Lambda\big(1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2\big)-g=\Lambda\big\lvert1-\rho_2e^{i\vartheta_1}-\rho_1e^{i\vartheta_2}\big\rvert^2\ \ge\ 0 .$$*
2. *$\max_\vartheta g=\Lambda\big[1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2-\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(1,\mathcal A)^2\big]$, with $\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(1,\mathcal A)=\max\big(0,\,1-\rho_1-\rho_2,\,\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert-1\big)$.*
3. *A pair of blocks diagonal in $F$ is $\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2\in\{0,\pi\}$, carrying signs $\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{\pm1\}$, where $g=2\Lambda(\epsilon_1\rho_2+\epsilon_2\rho_1-\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\rho_1\rho_2)$ and the deficit is the exact square $\Lambda(1-\epsilon_1\rho_2-\epsilon_2\rho_1)^2$. So the best diagonal value is $\Lambda\big[1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2-\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(1,\{\pm\rho_1\pm\rho_2\})^2\big]$, and it equals $\max_\vartheta g$ exactly when $1\notin\mathop{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal A$.*
4. *At fixed $u_2$ and $v_2>0$, in the variables $x=p^2$ and $y=r^2$, the value with the cap in place is affine, $$\Phi=\Big(\tfrac34\alpha u_2^3+\tilde au_2+\tilde cv_2+\tfrac34\beta u_2v_2^2+\frac{d_Ad_C}{\beta v_2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\beta v_2d_A}{d_C}-3\alpha u_2\Big)x+\Big(\frac{\beta v_2d_C}{d_A}-\beta u_2\Big)y,$$ while the norm constraint is the segment $3x+y=B$, $x,y\ge0$, with $B=T-\tfrac94u_2^2-\tfrac34v_2^2$.*

**Proof.**
1. **Item 1.** Expanding $\lvert1-\rho_2e^{i\vartheta_1}-\rho_1e^{i\vartheta_2}\rvert^2$ gives $1+\rho_2^2+\rho_1^2-2\rho_2\cos\vartheta_1-2\rho_1\cos\vartheta_2+2\rho_1\rho_2\cos(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_2)$, the cross term because $\cos(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_2)=\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2+\sin\vartheta_1\sin\vartheta_2$. Multiplying by $\Lambda$ and comparing with $g$ gives the identity, and $\Lambda>0$ gives the inequality.
2. **Item 2.** The set $\{\rho_2e^{i\vartheta_1}+\rho_1e^{i\vartheta_2}:\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2\}$ is the sum of two circles, which is the closed annulus $\mathcal A$, and by item 1 maximising $g$ is minimising the squared distance from $1$ to a point of $\mathcal A$. The distance from a point of modulus $1$ to $\mathcal A$ is $0$ when $\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert\le1\le\rho_1+\rho_2$, and otherwise the gap to the nearer boundary circle.
3. **Item 3.** At $\vartheta_j\in\{0,\pi\}$, $\cos\vartheta_j=\epsilon_j$ and $\cos(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_2)=\epsilon_1\epsilon_2$, which is the stated value of $g$, and item 1 with $\sin\vartheta_j=0$ gives the deficit $\Lambda(1-\epsilon_1\rho_2-\epsilon_2\rho_1)^2$. The four reals $\epsilon_1\rho_2+\epsilon_2\rho_1$ are $\pm(\rho_1+\rho_2)$ and $\pm(\rho_1-\rho_2)$, so they include both radii of $\mathcal A$. Since $\mathcal A$ is invariant under rotation about $0$ and $1$ is real and positive, the point of $\mathcal A$ nearest $1$ is the real number $\min\big(\rho_1+\rho_2,\max(1,\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert)\big)$, which is one of those four exactly when $1\notin\mathop{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal A$. Inside the interior the distance to $\mathcal A$ is $0$ while the distance to the four points is positive.
4. **Item 4.** $\Lambda\rho_2^2=\frac{\beta v_2d_A}{d_C}x$ and $\Lambda\rho_1^2=\frac{\beta v_2d_C}{d_A}y$, so the cap $\Lambda(1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2)$ is affine in $(x,y)$, and batch 11's phase-free part contributes $-3\alpha u_2x-\beta u_2y$ and terms in $u_2,v_2$ alone. The norm is $\tfrac94u_2^2+3x+\tfrac34v_2^2+y=T$.
5. **The conclusion.** Fix $u_2$ and $v_2>0$, write $I=\{3x+y=B,\ x,y\ge0\}$ for the norm segment, and let $E\subseteq I$ be where $1\in\mathcal A$ and $E^\circ\subseteq I$ where $1\in\mathop{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal A$. **At every point of $I$ outside $E^\circ$ the value is already a diagonal value**, by item 3. **And every point of $E^\circ$ is dominated by one such point**: $E$ is closed in $I$ because $\rho_1,\rho_2$ are continuous in $(x,y)$ and the two defining inequalities are weak, each of its connected components is a closed subinterval, the value is affine on $E$ by item 4 and so attains its maximum over that component at one of its two endpoints, and such an endpoint is either an endpoint of $I$, where $\rho_1\rho_2=0$ and $\mathcal A$ is a circle with empty interior, or a boundary point of $E$ in $I$, where $1\in\partial\mathcal A$. Either way it lies outside $E^\circ$. Every configuration of the stratum is therefore matched, at the same $T$, $u_2$ and $v_2$, by one with $\hat U$ and $V$ diagonal in $F$. $\square$

## Batch 10, $G(2)$ at fixed total norm as one cubic on a sphere of $6\times6$ matrices, and the condition a non-diagonal critical point must meet

**The embedding.** Put $c=3\pm2\sqrt2$, the roots of $c^2-6c+1=0$, and $\lambda^2=c$, which is $(1+c^2)/6$ there. For traceless symmetric $U,V$ let $M=\begin{pmatrix}U&\lambda V\\\lambda V&cU\end{pmatrix}$. Then
$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=(1+c^3)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+3\lambda^2(1+c)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2),\qquad\lvert M\rvert^2=(1+c^2)\lvert U\rvert^2+2\lambda^2\lvert V\rvert^2=4c\big(\tfrac32\lvert U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2\big),$$
the first by cyclicity (the terms odd in $V$ vanish), and the ratio of its coefficients is $\frac{1-c+c^2}{c}=5=\frac{15/4}{9/4}\cdot3$, so $\tfrac{15}4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac94\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)=\kappa\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3$ with $\kappa=\frac{\beta}{3\lambda^2(1+c)}=\frac3{4c(1+c)}$, which is $0.018845$ at $c=3+2\sqrt2$. The diagonal fields embed in the same shape: with $F=\begin{pmatrix}A'&C'\\C'&cA'\end{pmatrix}$, $A'=A/(1+c^2)$, $C'=C/(2\lambda)$, $\langle M,F\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UA)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$. **So $G(2)$ at fixed total norm is the maximisation of $\kappa\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3+\langle M,F\rangle$ on a sphere of the ten-dimensional space $\mathcal M$ of such $M$, with $F\in\mathcal M$ and its blocks diagonal**, and the norm trade that batch 9's screen found decisive is built into $\lvert M\rvert$. Screened to $10^{-13}$ ([screen_I106_G2_embed6.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_G2_embed6.py)).

**Why the obvious relaxation is loose.** Over all traceless symmetric $6\times6$ matrices of the same norm, the maximum of $\kappa\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3+\langle M,F\rangle$ is attained at $M$ diagonal in an eigenbasis of $F$, by L228's alignment argument, and $F$ splits into three $2\times2$ blocks, one per coordinate of $\mathbb R^3$. That maximiser has the block form of $F$, which would give diagonal $U,V$, but it is generally not in $\mathcal M$: the relaxation frees the lower-right block from $cU$ and the traces of $U$ and $V$ from vanishing separately. *So the relaxation bounds the value and does not locate the maximiser.*

**A necessary condition at a non-diagonal critical point.** At a critical point of $\Phi$ on $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2=T$ with multiplier $\mu$, the gradients give $A\equiv3\mu\hat U-3\alpha\hat U^2-\beta V^2$ and $C\equiv\mu V-\beta\{\hat U,V\}$ up to multiples of $I$ ($\alpha=\tfrac{15}4$, $\beta=\tfrac94$). The fields commute, and with Cayley–Hamilton ($[Z^3,Y]=\tfrac12\lvert Z\rvert^2[Z,Y]$ for traceless $3\times3$ $Z$) this reads
$$0=[A,C]=\Big(3\mu^2+\tfrac32\alpha\beta\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2\Big)[\hat U,V]-3\mu(\alpha+\beta)[\hat U^2,V]+3\alpha\beta\,\hat U[\hat U,V]\hat U-\beta^2\,V[\hat U,V]V,$$
screened to $10^{-13}$. Diagonal pairs satisfy it trivially. **$G(2)$ at fixed total norm follows if every non-diagonal solution of this equation, with the two Lagrange forms, is not a global maximum**, and batch 9's screen found none that is. *The next step is to classify its non-commuting solutions: it is one $3\times3$ skew equation in $\hat U,V$ and $\mu$, and in an eigenframe of $\hat U$ it is three scalar equations for the three off-diagonal entries of $V$.*

## Batch 11, the stratum where the two blocks share an eigenvector is a three-coupling phase problem

**The reduction.** Let $\hat U$ and $V$ share the eigenvector $e_2$ of the frame $F$ in which $A,C$ are diagonal, as the witness of [post F68](../findings/fence-posts.md) does. On $e_2^\perp=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(e_1,e_3)$ write $\hat U=-\tfrac{u_2}2I+pR(2\varphi)$ and $V=-\tfrac{v_2}2I+rR(2\chi)$, with $R(\vartheta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\vartheta&\sin\vartheta\\\sin\vartheta&-\cos\vartheta\end{pmatrix}$, $p,r\ge0$, and eigenvalues $u_2,v_2$ on $e_2$. With $d_A=\tfrac12(A_1-A_3)$ and $d_C=\tfrac12(C_1-C_3)$, since $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(R(\vartheta)R(\vartheta')\big)=2\cos(\vartheta-\vartheta')$ and the eigenvalues of $\hat U$ are $u_2,-\tfrac{u_2}2\pm p$,
$$\Phi=\alpha\big(\tfrac34u_2^3-3u_2p^2\big)+\beta\big(u_2v_2^2-u_2\big(\tfrac{v_2^2}4+r^2\big)\big)+A_2u_2-\tfrac{u_2}2(A_1+A_3)+C_2v_2-\tfrac{v_2}2(C_1+C_3)+g,$$
$$g=K_{12}\cos(2\varphi-2\chi)+K_1\cos2\varphi+K_2\cos2\chi,\qquad K_1=2p\,d_A,\quad K_2=2r\,d_C,\quad K_{12}=-2\beta p\,v_2\,r,$$
with norms $\lvert\hat U\rvert^2=\tfrac32u_2^2+2p^2$ and $\lvert V\rvert^2=\tfrac32v_2^2+2r^2$. Screened to $10^{-14}$ ([screen_I106_G2_commonaxis.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_G2_commonaxis.py)). **All of the frame dependence is the three-coupling phase sum $g$.**

**Unfrustrated signs give diagonal blocks at the same amplitudes.** $g\le\lvert K_1\rvert+\lvert K_2\rvert+\lvert K_{12}\rvert$, with equality at phases $2\varphi,2\chi\in\{0,\pi\}$ exactly when signs $s_1,s_2$ exist with $s_1K_1\ge0$, $s_2K_2\ge0$ and $s_1s_2K_{12}\ge0$, that is when $K_1K_2K_{12}\ge0$. At those phases $R(2\varphi),R(2\chi)=\pm\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(1,-1)$, so $\hat U$ and $V$ are diagonal in $F$. **So on this stratum a non-diagonal maximiser needs frustration, $K_1K_2K_{12}<0$, which is $d_A\,d_C\,v_2>0$**, and then the phase maximum can be interior. *This is the fixed-norms witness's regime: at fixed $\lvert\hat U\rvert,\lvert V\rvert$ the amplitudes cannot move to escape it.*

**What the total norm adds.** Flipping $v_2\mapsto-v_2$ removes the frustration and changes only the $v_2$-odd terms, $C_2v_2-\tfrac{v_2}2(C_1+C_3)$ and $K_{12}$, while the amplitudes $p,r$ and $u_2$ can be moved between the two blocks at fixed $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2$. **$G(2)$ on this stratum is therefore a comparison between the frustrated interior phase maximum and the best unfrustrated diagonal configuration over the norm ellipsoid**, a finite problem in $(u_2,p,v_2,r)$ and two phases. Batch 9's screen, which included this stratum, found the diagonal side always at least as large. *The next computation is the frustrated phase maximum in closed form, $\max g$ for $K_1,K_2>0>K_{12}$, and the comparison.*
