# I104, the pair-block gap: two eigenvectors derived, and the all-critical-points gap is false, 2026-09-14, on Opus

**Written while [L222](../findings/second-order.md) is in Gate 2** ([r943](REVIEW_r943.md), [r944](REVIEW_r944.md)), in a file its brief does not link. Derivations only, by the user's instruction.

## Two eigenvectors of the pair block at every diagonal critical point, derived

Let $D$ be a unit coordinate-diagonal critical point with sectional matrix $K$, $Q(D)=\theta D$, and fix $\{j,k\}$. Twice the pair block is $M=\operatorname{diag}(d)-\kappa\,\mathrm{Id}+S$ on the indices $a\notin\{j,k\}$, with $d_a=K_{ja}+K_{ka}$, $\kappa=K_{jk}$ and $S$ the hollow submatrix off $\{j,k\}$ ([L122](../findings/second-order.md): $M=-\Delta^{(jk)}-\kappa\,\mathrm{Id}$).

- **$M\mathbf1=-\kappa\mathbf1$.** Row sums of $K$ give $(S\mathbf1)_a=-d_a$, so $M\mathbf1=d-\kappa\mathbf1-d$. *This is L122's $\Delta^{(jk)}\mathbf1=0$.*
- **$My=\theta y$ for $y_a=K_{ja}-K_{ka}$, the orbit direction.** The criticality equation at the entries $(j,a)$ and $(k,a)$ reads $K_{ja}^2+\sum_cK_{jc}K_{ca}=\theta K_{ja}$ and likewise for $k$. Subtracting, the $c=k$ and $c=j$ terms give $\kappa(K_{ka}-K_{ja})=-\kappa y_a$, the terms $c\notin\{j,k\}$ give $(Sy)_a$, and $K_{ja}^2-K_{ka}^2=y_ad_a$. So $(Sy)_a+d_ay_a-\kappa y_a=\theta y_a$, which is $My=\theta y$. *Adding instead gives $Sd=(\theta-\kappa)d-(K_{ja}^2+K_{ka}^2)_a$.* **This is already on file**: L122's "two consequences the record can use" state that the orbit direction $M_{ki}-M_{ji}$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\tfrac\theta2$, and it is [post F37](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s orbit eigenvector in pair-block coordinates. *So this item is a re-derivation agreeing with the record, not a new result.* $\square$

**So the gap question at a diagonal critical point is the spectrum of $M$ on $\{\mathbf1,y\}^\perp$**, and [L222](../findings/second-order.md)'s item 6 writes the Rayleigh quotient there as $\tfrac1\theta\big[Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2\big]$.

## The gap stated for all diagonal critical points above $\sqrt{3/2}$ is false

**[Post F48](../findings/second-order.md), already on file**: pad $S^6\times S^6$ to $n=14$ and take the two padding indices as the pair. Then $d=0$, $\kappa=0$, $y=0$ (rotating in a padding plane moves nothing), and $M=S$ is the vertex matrix of $S^6\times S^6$, whose top eigenvalue on $\mathbf1^\perp$ is $\mu=\tfrac{12}{11}\theta$ by [L57](../findings/problem.md)'s step 1 at equality. **So the pair block has eigenvalue $\tfrac\mu2=\tfrac6{11}\theta>\tfrac\theta2$ at a diagonal critical point with $\theta=\Theta_{12}>\sqrt{3/2}$**, with no orbit direction to remove. *The inequality the [pair-block note](2026-09-14-I104-pair-block.md) reduced the stratum to, a gap $\le\tfrac\theta2-\gamma$ off the orbit direction at every diagonal critical point above $\sqrt{3/2}$, therefore fails at every padded balanced product with at least two padding indices, so from $n=14$.*

**What this corrects.** *The pair-block stratum was being attacked as a statement about every critical point in $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$, which is strictly harder than the root node needs and, by this example, carries a false sub-step.* **The root node is about global maximisers, which supply [L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s $L_W\le\tfrac\theta2$ on $W^\perp$**, and that is exactly the hypothesis L122's own inequality needs and F48 shows cannot be dropped. **So the stratum's statement should read: a global (or local) maximiser in $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$ at its minimising angle has $\nu=0$**, with H3 available on the pair block and on all of $W^\perp$, and the small-$\nu$ regime handled by H3 rather than by a gap at every critical point. *Padded $S^6\times S^6$ at $n=14$ is not a local maximum, which is what F48's violation of L24 says, so it is no counterexample to that statement.*

## At a local maximiser in $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$: H3 splits by block, derived

Let $W=D+\nu$ be a unit local maximiser of $P$ on the sphere of $\mathcal W_n$ with $\theta>0$, $D\in\mathcal D_n$, $\nu\in V_{jk}$, so [L24](../findings/second-order.md) gives $\langle L_WX,X\rangle\le\tfrac\theta2\lVert X\rVert^2$ for $X\perp W$. For any $X$ in a single character block $V_\chi$ of [L120](../findings/second-order.md), $X\circ X$ has character $\chi^2=1$, so $X\circ X\in\mathcal D_n\perp\nu$ and $\langle L_\nu X,X\rangle=\langle\nu,X\circ X\rangle=0$. Hence $\langle L_WX,X\rangle=\langle L_DX,X\rangle$ for every such $X$, and:

1. **On $V_{jk}$: $\tfrac\theta2$ is the top eigenvalue of $L_D$, attained at $\nu$.** $L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$ is $V_{jk}$'s critical equation, and every $X\in V_{jk}$ orthogonal to $\nu$ is orthogonal to $W$, so $\langle L_DX,X\rangle\le\tfrac\theta2\lVert X\rVert^2$. *In [L122](../findings/second-order.md)'s coordinates at $D$: the top eigenvalue of $\operatorname{diag}(d)-\kappa+S$ on $\mathbf1^\perp$ is exactly $\theta$, with eigenvector $x$.*
2. **On $\mathcal D_n\cap D^\perp$: $L_D\le\tfrac\theta2$.** Such $Y$ is orthogonal to $W$.
3. **On every other block $V_\chi$, $\chi\ne\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$: $L_D\le\tfrac\theta2$.** Such $X$ is orthogonal to $W$.

**So $D$, which is not itself critical ($D\circ D=\theta D-G_x$), satisfies the second-order condition of a maximiser on every block, with equality exactly along $\nu$.** $\square$

## What that does to the small-$\nu$ regime

*This is the regime every chain with constants lost.* As $\lVert\nu\rVert\to0$ along a family of GLOBAL maximisers (a limit of local maximisers need not be one), $W\to D_0$ with $D_0$ a diagonal global maximiser, hence the balanced product by [L57](../findings/problem.md) at even $n$, and item 1 passes to the limit: $\tfrac\theta2$ is the top eigenvalue of $L_{D_0}$ on $V_{jk}$, attained on the limit of $\hat\nu$, which at the minimising angle is orthogonal to the orbit direction $y_0$ ([L222](../findings/second-order.md) item 5). Since $y_0$ is itself a $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvector ([L122](../findings/second-order.md)), **$\tfrac\theta2$ has multiplicity at least two on $V_{jk}$ at $D_0$, or $y_0=0$.** *At the balanced product both alternatives are excluded by [L27](../findings/nondegeneracy.md), which makes the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace exactly the orbit tangent at $n=2m\ge8$: with $j,k$ in different factors $y_0\ne0$ and a second $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvector on $V_{jk}$ would lie outside the orbit tangent, and with $j,k$ in the same factor $y_0=0$ and $V_{jk}$ meets the orbit tangent trivially, so its top eigenvalue is below $\tfrac\theta2$, by [L216](../findings/second-order.md)'s explicit gap $\gamma=\tfrac1{n-2}$ in both cases.* **So qualitatively no family of global maximisers at their minimising angles has $\nu\to0$ with $\nu\ne0$.** **What is owed to make it quantitative is one derivation**: how far a global maximiser's $D$ can be from the balanced orbit when $\lVert\nu\rVert$ is small, so that the gap $\gamma=\tfrac1{n-2}$ at the balanced product survives the perturbation. *That is the stability form of [L57](../findings/problem.md) named as I103, and within [L217](../findings/second-order.md)'s tube $\arcsin\frac3{n-2}$ the value is already $\Theta_n$. The odd $n$ case needs the unbalanced product in place of the balanced one.*

## A quantitative route for small $\nu$ without a stability lemma: L57's chain with the forcing $G_x$ and the value $\theta\ge\Theta_n$

**Derived.** At a global maximiser $W=D+\nu$ in $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$, the $\mathcal D_n$ equation is $D\circ D+G_x=\theta D$, so in matrix form $K^{\circ2}+K^2=\theta K-G_x+\operatorname{diag}$ with $\operatorname{tr}K^2=2t$, $t=1-N$, $N=\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2\lVert x\rVert^2$. Pairing with $v\otimes v$ for the top unit eigenvector $v$ of $K$ (eigenvalue $\mu$), exactly as [L57](../findings/problem.md)'s step 1:
$$\theta\mu=\mu^2-\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^2(v_a-v_b)^2+v^{\mathsf T}G_xv\ \le\ \tfrac{n-1}n\mu^2+v^{\mathsf T}G_xv,$$
*the forcing $-G_x$ on the right of the matrix equation moving to $+v^{\mathsf T}G_xv$ after pairing, as $F$ did in the four-set chain*, and step 2 scaled gives $\tfrac{n-1}n\mu\le\sqrt t\,\Theta_n$. **With $\theta\ge\Theta_n$ (H1) this is $\Theta_n\big(1-\sqrt{1-N}\big)\mu\le v^{\mathsf T}G_xv$.** *The left side is $\tfrac12\Theta_nN\mu+O(N^2)$ and positive, so a small-$\nu$ maximiser needs $v^{\mathsf T}G_xv\ge\tfrac12\Theta_nN\mu$, a definite positive value of the forcing's quadratic form at $v$.*

**The forcing's form, derived.** With $p_1=\lVert x\rVert^2=\tfrac N2$,
$$v^{\mathsf T}G_xv=2(v_j+v_k)\sum_ax_a^2v_a-2p_1v_jv_k+2\Big(\sum_ax_av_a\Big)^2-2\sum_ax_a^2v_a^2 .$$
**At the balanced product with $j,k$ in different factors**, $v$ is two-valued ($\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt n}$ by factor), so $v_j+v_k=0$ and $v_jv_k=-\tfrac1n$, and the orbit direction $y_a=K_{ja}-K_{ka}$ is proportional to $v$ on the indices off $\{j,k\}$; the first-order rotation condition $x\perp y$ ([L222](../findings/second-order.md) item 5) is then $\sum_ax_av_a=0$, and **$v^{\mathsf T}G_xv=\tfrac{2p_1}n-\tfrac{2p_1}n=0$ exactly**. *So at the balanced product the forcing contributes nothing at the minimising angle, while H1 demands $+\tfrac12\Theta_nN\mu$ from it: the chain excludes $\nu\ne0$ to first order in $N$.* **With $j,k$ in the same factor, $v_j+v_k=\pm\tfrac2{\sqrt n}$, $y=0$, and the term $2(v_j+v_k)\sum x_a^2v_a$ is what must be controlled**, using that $x$ is the top eigenvector of the pair block (item 1 above).

**What is owed to turn this into an inequality for small $N$**: the perturbation of $v$, $\mu$ and the two-valued structure from the balanced product to $D$, which costs $O(N\cdot\mathrm{dist}(D,\mathcal O))$ in $v^{\mathsf T}G_xv$ and must be beaten by $\tfrac12\Theta_nN\mu$. *That needs $\mathrm{dist}(D,\mathcal O)$ small when $N$ is small, which within [L217](../findings/second-order.md)'s tube is given, and outside it is the far region again, so this closes the small-$\nu$ regime inside the tube and hands the rest back to the far region.*

## The same-factor case, and where the small-$\nu$ regime now stands

**Derived.** At the balanced product with $j,k$ in the same factor $A$ ($\lvert A\rvert=\tfrac n2$), $v_j=v_k=\tfrac1{\sqrt n}$, $y=0$, and with $A'=A\setminus\{j,k\}$, $p_A=\sum_{A'}x_a^2$, $p_B=\sum_Bx_a^2$, $s_A=\sum_{A'}x_a=-\sum_Bx_a$:
$$v^{\mathsf T}G_xv=\tfrac4n(p_A-p_B)-\tfrac{2p_1}n+\tfrac8ns_A^2-\tfrac{2p_1}n=\tfrac8n\big(s_A^2-p_B\big),$$
which can be positive, up to $\tfrac8n(\tfrac n2-1)p_B$ by Cauchy--Schwarz on $B$. **So the chain alone does not exclude the same-factor case**: its largest value $\tfrac{2(n-2)}nN$ exceeds the $\tfrac{n-2}nN$ H1 demands by a factor two. *It is excluded instead by item 1 and the gap: at the balanced product the whole of $V_{jk}$ lies off the orbit tangent, [L216](../findings/second-order.md)'s gap puts its top eigenvalue at most $\Theta_n(\tfrac12-\gamma)$, and item 1 needs $\tfrac\theta2\ge\tfrac{\Theta_n}2$ there.*

**Where the small-$\nu$ regime stands, stated plainly.**
- **Near the balanced orbit it is excluded**, by item 1 against L216's gap in the same-factor case and by the chain (or the same gap, with the orbit direction as the second eigenvector) in the different-factor case. Quantitatively, with $\lvert L_D-L_{D_0}\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\le c\lVert D-D_0\rVert$ for a constant $c$ from [L215](../findings/second-order.md), a pair-block maximiser with $\nu\ne0$ at its minimising angle lies at distance at least of order $\Theta_n\gamma/c$ from the orbit, which is inside [L216](../findings/second-order.md)'s own isolation radius and so adds nothing new there.
- **Away from the balanced orbit with $\nu$ small**, $D$ is a nearly critical diagonal tensor of value about $\theta\ge\Theta_n$, and **placing it near the balanced orbit is exactly the stability form of [L57](../findings/problem.md), I103.** *So the pair-block stratum's small-$\nu$ regime reduces to I103, and I103 is now the bottleneck for the whole stratum, not a side tool.*
