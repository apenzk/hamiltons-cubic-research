# Review r355, the mass budget and the projection correction (I1–I5)

**I1 (the corrected criterion), CONFIRMED**, with two corrections to how it is stated: the step is $L_eW=W$, never $L_eW=\theta W$; and "sharp at $X=W$" both understates the sharpness and mis-scopes it.

**I2 (the mass budget), CONFIRMED**, and stronger than claimed: the orthogonality holds at **every** $n\ge4$ and needs no representation theory. **The $n\ge9$ restriction offered as the repair is REFUTED**, it is not the right repair, and no repair is needed.

**I3 (the unconditional corollary), CONFIRMED.** The chain is sound, and it does not use I2 at all.

**I4 (the sharpened corollary), CONFIRMED**, and the record already proves the step the derivation settled by screen: [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) and [target.md](../findings/target.md) each carry the identity in a different guise.

**I5 (the soundness condition), CONFIRMED** as reasoning, with the caveat that the record's own definition of $t$ is ambiguous between the per-frame and the extremal reading, and I5's force depends on which is meant.

## I1, the derivation, done from the definitions

Let $W$ be a critical point, $\theta=P(W)>0$, $e=W/\theta$, $X$ a unit vector of $\mathcal W_n$, $c=\langle X,W\rangle$ and $X=cW+X^\perp$ with $\lVert X^\perp\rVert^2=1-c^2$. Since $Q$ is quadratic and homogeneous, $W\circ W=\tfrac12(Q(2W)-2Q(W))=Q(W)=\theta W$, so $L_eW=(W/\theta)\circ W=W$, eigenvalue exactly $1$, and $e\circ e=e$. Expanding, $\langle X,L_eX\rangle=c^2\langle W,L_eW\rangle+2c\langle W,L_eX^\perp\rangle+\langle X^\perp,L_eX^\perp\rangle$. The cross term vanishes because $L_e$ is self-adjoint and $L_eW=W$: $\langle W,L_eX^\perp\rangle=\langle L_eW,X^\perp\rangle=\langle W,X^\perp\rangle=0$. Since $W$ is an eigenvector, $W^\perp$ is $L_e$-invariant, so [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) applies as a restriction rather than a compression and gives $\langle X^\perp,L_eX^\perp\rangle\le\tfrac12(1-c^2)$. Hence $\langle X,L_eX\rangle\le c^2+\tfrac12(1-c^2)=\tfrac{1+c^2}2$, which is I1.

**It needs $L_eW=W$, and the alternative is not a variant but an error.** $L_WW=\theta W$; dividing by $\theta$ is what turns $\theta$ into $1$. Had the derivation used $L_eW=\theta W$ it would have reached $\langle X,L_eX\rangle\le\theta c^2+\tfrac12(1-c^2)$ and hence $\Sigma\le\theta^2\big(1+(2\theta-1)\rho^2\big)^2$, which differs numerically wherever $\theta\ne1$, and $\theta_0(12)=1.236031>1$, so the two forms disagree in the regime the programme works in. The derivation uses the right one.

**Self-adjointness of $L_e$ is load-bearing and is nowhere stated.** It holds, $\langle X\circ Y,Z\rangle$ is the totally symmetric polarisation of the cubic $P$, which is what makes $6\theta(L_e-\tfrac12\mathrm{Id})$ a Hessian in [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s own argument, but the cross-term cancellation is the one place I1 consumes it, and it should be said.

**"Sharp at $X=W$" is wrong in scope and weaker than the truth.** Equality holds iff $X^\perp/\lVert X^\perp\rVert$ is a $\tfrac12$-eigenvector of $L_e$. Such a vector exists at every critical point, the orbit directions are exact $\tfrac12$-eigenvectors, as [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) records, so the bound is attained at **every** $c\in[-1,1]$, not only at $c=1$. Conversely $X=W$ is not an admissible test direction for the use I3 makes of the inequality: there $X$ ranges over the Bianchi plane of one $4$-subset, and $X=W$ would put the whole of $W$ in a single $4$-set plane. So as a statement about the abstract criterion the sharpness claim is true but understated, and as a statement about the $4$-set family it is vacuous. Whether a $4$-set direction attains it at a given $c$ is a separate and unproven question, and it is where the real slack sits, the derivation's own screen reporting $\max\rho^2$ of $0.03$–$0.12$ against a permitted $0.84$–$0.96$.

**One paraphrase in circulation is contradicted by the record.** The brief, and [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md)'s second section, both state L24 as a bound "on $W^\perp$ **off the orbit**". [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) has no such qualifier and the record explicitly denies it: "the orbit needs no separate exclusion, Lemma L24's bound holding on all of $W^\perp$ with equality along it", and the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace at $S^3\times S^3$ is $18$-dimensional against a $9$-dimensional orbit, so strictness off the orbit is false. This matters because [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md)'s REDUCTION theorem carries "orthogonal to $W$ and to the orbit" as a hypothesis.

## I2, the attack the brief asked for, and the answer

**Two $4$-subsets sharing three indices do not threaten the orthogonality, and the reason is elementary rather than representation-theoretic.** A basis element of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ is $\varphi_\alpha\odot\varphi_\beta$ for $2$-subsets $\alpha,\beta$, and it lies in a $4$-set block exactly when $\alpha\cap\beta=\varnothing$, in which case $\alpha\sqcup\beta$ **recovers the $4$-set**. So the map from a $4$-set basis element to its subset is well defined, and distinct $4$-subsets span **disjoint** sets of basis elements. Those basis elements are matrix units of a symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2$ and are mutually orthogonal in the trace pairing. Orthogonality follows with no group, no character and no equivariance.

**Take the case the brief names.** $S=\{1,2,3,4\}$ and $S'=\{1,2,3,5\}$ share three indices and share the three $2$-planes $\varphi_{12},\varphi_{13},\varphi_{23}$ as **factors**, which is what makes the case look dangerous. But $S$'s block is spanned by $\varphi_{12}\odot\varphi_{34}$, $\varphi_{13}\odot\varphi_{24}$, $\varphi_{14}\odot\varphi_{23}$ and $S'$'s by $\varphi_{12}\odot\varphi_{35}$, $\varphi_{13}\odot\varphi_{25}$, $\varphi_{15}\odot\varphi_{23}$: in $S$ the plane $\varphi_{12}$ is paired only with $\varphi_{34}$, in $S'$ only with $\varphi_{35}$, and $\varphi_{34}\ne\varphi_{35}$. The two blocks share **zero** basis elements while sharing three of four indices. The shared indices never meet because the pairing, not the index set, indexes the basis.

**The character version also holds at every $n$, and the $n=4,6,8$ fusion cannot touch it.** The dual group of $\Gamma=\{\mathrm{diag}(\pm1)\}\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^n$ has all $2^n$ characters $\chi_S$ pairwise distinct, so $\chi_S=\chi_{S'}$ forces $S=S'$ for **any** subsets, of any sizes, overlapping or not. The fusion caveat in [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) is a statement about $\Gamma\cap SO(n)$, where $\chi_{[n]}$ is trivial and $\chi_S=\chi_{S^{\mathsf c}}$. Restricted to the pairs the budget cares about, that can bite only when the complement of a $4$-set is a $4$-set, i.e. only at $n=8$; at $n=4$ the complement is empty and at $n=6$ it is a $2$-set, so no two $4$-set planes fuse there at all. And even at $n=8$ the two complementary planes remain orthogonal by the disjoint-basis argument above: fusion removes the character **proof**, never the orthogonality.

**So the $n\ge9$ restriction is REFUTED as the repair.** The right repair is the one [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) already prescribes in its own text, "the repair is free: $\circ$ is $O(n)$-equivariant, so the full sign group serves and its even-subset characters are pairwise distinct at every $n$", and [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md) says the same ("the full sign group is required"). [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) attaches "$n\ge9$" to the budget in the Gate-2-owed line and again to Corollary $1'$; both should be struck. The budget holds at every $n\ge4$. Nothing downstream changes at $n\ge12$, so this is a scope repair rather than a correction, but leaving it in the record would make a later reader believe a restriction exists where none does.

**Does the Bianchi cut preserve orthogonality? Trivially yes**, and the question is a non-question: orthogonality is inherited by subspaces, so cutting each $3$-dimensional character block down to its $2$-dimensional Bianchi plane cannot create overlap. The more useful observation is that the Weyl conditions respect the decomposition in the first place, because both are $O(n)$-equivariant linear maps: Ricci contraction lands in $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb R^n)$, whose characters are $\chi_\varnothing$ and the $\chi_{\{a,b\}}$, so it is identically zero on a $4$-set block; first Bianchi lands in $\Lambda^4$, whose characters are the $\chi_S$ with $\lvert S\rvert=4$, so it is identically zero on the trivial and pair blocks and cuts exactly one dimension from each $4$-set block. That reproduces [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md)'s dimension count by character bookkeeping alone, independently of its "a trace needs a repeated index" argument, and the totals check: $(\binom n2-n)+(n-3)\binom n2+2\binom n4=10$ at $n=4$, matching $\dim\mathcal W_4$.

**The budget itself.** With one unit vector $X_S$ chosen in each plane, the family is orthonormal and Bessel gives $\sum_S\langle X_S,W\rangle^2\le\lVert W\rVert^2=1$. Correct. Two remarks. First, the choice of $X_S$ is avoidable and the statement is cleaner without it: $\sum_S\lVert\Pi_{P_S}W\rVert^2\le1$ holds for the full projections and dominates every choice, which also removes the only place Corollary 2 needs the per-subset vector and the budget vector to be the same one. Second, the budget as stated is not the best available, see the sharpening under I4.

**One gap in the citation.** [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) states a **direct** sum decomposition; [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) uses it as an **orthogonal** one. The upgrade is correct, the summands are isotypic components of an orthogonal action, and the trace pairing on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ is $\Gamma$-invariant because $\Gamma\subset O(n)$ acts by conjugation, but neither file says so. It should be one clause in L120.

## I3, the chain

Fix a frame $F$ and a $4$-subset. $\circ$ is bilinear, so $W\mapsto L_W$ is linear and $L_W=L_{\mathrm{diag}(M)}+L_R$ exactly. Take $X$ a unit vector of the Bianchi plane; it lies in the single character $\chi_S$, and $R$ has zero diagonal, so [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md) kills $\langle X,L_RX\rangle$. [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md), which by the scope correction in the same file needs diagonality alone, computes the surviving term: the block is $\tfrac12G$ with $G$ the $3\times3$ opposite-pair matrix, $G\mathbf 1=(A+B+C)\mathbf 1$, and $\mathrm{tr}(G^2)=3(A^2+B^2+C^2)$, so the two eigenvalues on $\mathbf 1^\perp$ satisfy $2\lambda^2=3\sum A^2-(\sum A)^2=2\Sigma$, that is, $\pm\sqrt\Sigma$, and $\tfrac12G$ has $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt\Sigma$. The three basis elements have equal norms and are orthogonal, so $G$ is the matrix of a self-adjoint operator in an orthonormal basis and these really are eigenvalues. Taking $X$ the top eigenvector, $\langle X,L_eX\rangle=\tfrac{\sqrt\Sigma}{2\theta}$, and I1 gives $\sqrt\Sigma\le\theta(1+\rho^2)$, hence $\Sigma\le\theta^2(1+\rho^2)^2$ and, with $\rho^2\le1$, $\Sigma\le4\theta^2$.

**I3 does not use I2.** $\rho^2\le1$ is Cauchy–Schwarz between two unit vectors and nothing else. [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) places Corollary 1 under the heading "The defect is budgeted, so the certificate still bites", immediately after the budget, which invites the reading that the budget is what licenses it. Only Corollary 2 consumes the budget.

**An independent check that the whole normalisation chain is coherent, done by hand.** At $S^3\times S^3$, $n=6$: write $u$ for the within-factor sectional entry and $v$ for the cross entry; Ricci-flatness of the vertex matrix is $2u+3v=0$, so $v=-\tfrac23u$. On a $(2,2)$-split $4$-subset, $A=2u$ and $B=C=2v$, so $\Sigma=4(u-v)^2=\tfrac{100}9u^2$. The norm is $\lVert W\rVert^2=6u^2+9v^2=10u^2=1$, so $u^2=\tfrac1{10}$ and $\Sigma=\tfrac{10}9$. And $\theta_0(6)^2=\tfrac{2\cdot5\cdot4}{36}=\tfrac{10}9$. So $\Sigma=\theta^2$ **exactly**: [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) is saturated at the balanced product, the $4$-set eigenvalue of $L_e$ is $\tfrac{\sqrt\Sigma}{2\theta}=\tfrac12$ exactly, and that reproduces the record's separate claim that the nine $(2,2)$-split $4$-subsets supply the nine extra $\tfrac12$-eigenvectors in the $18$-dimensional eigenspace at $S^3\times S^3$. Three record statements written in different places (L121's radicand, the $\tfrac12$-eigenspace count, and $\theta_0(6)$), agree with one hand computation, which pins the factor $\tfrac12$ in $L_M=\tfrac12G$ and the convention $\lVert W\rVert^2=\sum_{a<b}M_{ab}^2$ simultaneously.

## I4, and the record already had it

$X$ has zero diagonal, so $\langle X,\mathrm{diag}(M)\rangle=0$ and $\rho=\langle X,W\rangle=\langle X,R\rangle\le\lVert R\rVert$. The split $W=\mathrm{diag}(M)+R$ is trivial-character against non-trivial-character, so it is orthogonal for **any** $\Gamma$-invariant pairing and the conclusion $\lVert R\rVert^2=1-\lVert\mathrm{diag}(M)\rVert^2$ is normalisation-free: scaling an inner product does not disturb orthogonality. The only place a factor of two could enter is the relation $t_F=2\lVert\mathrm{diag}(M)\rVert^2$, which is exactly the Gate 2 question [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) flags and settles by screen. **It does not need a screen. The record proves it twice.**

- [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md), the structural-consequence paragraph: $\sum d_\varphi^2=t/2$ with $d_\varphi$ the entries $K_{ab}$. That is $\sum_{a<b}M_{ab}^2=t/2$, i.e. $t=2\lVert\mathrm{diag}(M)\rVert^2$, verbatim.
- [target.md](../findings/target.md), the vertex-equation display: $\sum R=1-t/2$ with $R_{ab}=\sum_{(cd)\ne(ab)}W_{abcd}^2$. Summed over unordered pairs that is $2\sum_{\{ab\}<\{cd\}}W_{abcd}^2=\lVert W\rVert^2-\sum_{\{ab\}}K_{ab}^2$, which is I4's $\lVert R\rVert^2=1-t_F/2$ in a different notation.

So I4's "one orthogonal decomposition" duplicates an identity the record already carries, and the numerical verification at five rotations of $S^3\times S^3$ can be replaced by a citation. The relation is also forced internally: given $\lVert W\rVert=1$ and the orthogonal split, "$t\le2$ with $t=2$ iff coordinate-diagonal" is **equivalent** to $t=2\lVert\mathrm{diag}(M)\rVert^2$, since $t_F=2$ forces $\lVert R\rVert=0$ and conversely.

**Is the $t_F=2$ end really L121, or only formally so? Really.** At $t_F=2$ we have $R=0$, so $W=\mathrm{diag}(M)$ is coordinate-diagonal in $F$ and, being a maximiser, is a diagonal critical point, L121's exact hypothesis. Moreover $\rho=\langle X,R\rangle=0$, so $X\perp W$ and L24 applies unmodified: the correction is not merely small at that end, it is absent. The recovery is genuine.

**The sharpening the derivation leaves on the table.** Every $4$-set plane lies in the non-trivial characters, so $\Pi_{P_S}W=\Pi_{P_S}R$ and the budget is not $\sum_S\rho_S^2\le1$ but
$$\sum_S\rho_S^2\;\le\;\lVert\Pi_{4\text{-set}}R\rVert^2\;\le\;\lVert R\rVert^2\;=\;1-\tfrac{t_F}2 ,$$
strictly better whenever $t_F>0$, and strictly better again if the pair-block mass of $R$ is nonzero. Corollary 2 then reads: at most $\lfloor(1-t_F/2)/t\rfloor$ subsets can have $\rho_S^2>t$, and the mean of $\rho_S^2$ is at most $(1-t_F/2)/\binom n4$. I2 and I4 are stated as independent improvements when I4 in fact upgrades I2 by the same factor.

**Corollary 2's arithmetic checks.** Markov on $\sum\rho^2\le1$ gives at most $\lfloor1/t\rfloor$ subsets above $t$; $\binom{12}4=495$ and $1/495=2.02\times10^{-3}$, matching the quoted $(1+2\times10^{-3})^2$.

## I5, soundness, and an ambiguity underneath it

The reasoning is correct as far as the definitions allow. On $t_F\in[0,2]$ the factor $2-t_F/2$ lies in $[1,2]$ and is strictly decreasing, so a larger value of the argument yields a smaller right-hand side; substituting a quantity known only to satisfy $t\ge t_F$ therefore asserts an inequality strictly stronger than the one proved. At the extreme $t=2$ it asserts $\Sigma\le\theta^2$ in every frame, which is precisely the uncorrected I12 that I1 refutes and that [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md)'s "what it does not give" paragraph warns against by name. So the corollary must be read with the frame's own $t_F$, and calling that soundness rather than sharpness is right: the substitution does not weaken a valid bound, it produces an invalid one.

**But the record's $t$ does not have a single meaning, and I5's force depends on which is intended.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) writes "$t=\lVert K\rVert_F^2\le2$ and $t=2$ iff $W$ is coordinate-diagonal in **some** frame", which only parses if $t$ is extremal over $O(n)$; the same file's diagonality convention ("off-diagonal Frobenius mass minimised over $O(n)$") points the same way, and [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) writes $\mu=\max_F\mu_1(K^F)$ with an explicit frame index. Under that reading I5 is exactly right. Under a per-frame reading $t=t_F$ and I5's warning is vacuous, but then conventions.md's "in some frame" is misstated. Either way the operational instruction is the same, so I5 stands; what is owed is a one-line disambiguation in conventions.md, since a reader who takes the per-frame reading will not see why I5 is a warning at all.

**And conventions.md's own display is inconsistent with its own bound.** It writes $K=(W_{abab})_{a<b}$, indexed over unordered pairs, and then $t=\lVert K\rVert_F^2\le2$. Over unordered pairs $\lVert K\rVert_F^2=\sum_{a<b}M_{ab}^2=\lVert\mathrm{diag}(M)\rVert^2\le1$, not $\le2$. The consistent reading, the one [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) and [target.md](../findings/target.md) both use, and the one the file's own "sums over pairs are over ORDERED pairs" default supplies, is that $K$ is the $n\times n$ hollow symmetric matrix. The "$_{a<b}$" annotation is a transcription slip and should be dropped; I4's factor of two depends on dropping it.

## Defects in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) that survive the verdicts

**The REDUCTION theorem's display is wrong by $\sqrt2$ and carries an undefined symbol.** [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) gives the top block value $\tfrac12\sqrt{\tfrac12[(A-B)^2+(B-C)^2+(C-A)^2]}$; the display writes $\tfrac12[(A-B)^2+(B-C)^2+(C-A)^2]^{1/2}$, dropping the inner $\tfrac12$, and multiplies it by a placeholder "$\lvert\langle X,\cdot\rangle$-coefficient$\rvert$" naming no object. Its right-hand side $\theta(\tfrac12-\langle X,L_RX\rangle/\theta)$ is correct. The defect does not propagate, the "Equivalently" line, the corrected theorem and both corollaries are all stated in $\Sigma$ and are right, but the display should be repaired or deleted.

**That same theorem is labelled "unconditional" while assuming $X\perp W$.** Its hypothesis reads "$X$ a unit vector in the Bianchi plane, orthogonal to $W$ and to the orbit". The next section proves that $\langle X,W\rangle=\langle X,R\rangle\ne0$ off the eigenframe, which is the whole point of the note; and "orthogonal to the orbit" is a hypothesis [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) does not have. So the theorem is generically vacuous exactly where it is meant to apply.

**One clause contradicts the note's own screen.** The corrected I12 theorem says the bound "reduces to Lemma L121's inequality exactly when $c=0$, in particular in the eigenframe **and at every sphere product**". Four paragraphs earlier the note reports that over $45$ directions in a rotated frame at $S^3\times S^3$, $\lvert\langle X,W\rangle\rvert$ reaches $0.243$ and is nonzero at every one. $c=0$ holds in the eigenframe, not at sphere products frame-freely; the clause should be struck.

**Corollary 1's aside is PLAUSIBLE, not proved.** "The factor $4$ in Corollary 1 is attained only if a single $4$-set carries the whole of $W$, which no critical point does." The first half is right, $\rho^2=1$ forces $X=\pm W$, hence $W$ supported on one $4$-set plane, hence $M\equiv0$ and $t_F=0$ in that frame. The second half is asserted without argument. It does not affect either corollary's validity.

**A wording defect in the record itself.** [Lemma L123](../findings/second-order.md)'s proof in [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) reads "$\Gamma$ acts on each character space by a **scalar**, so bilinearity of $\circ$ **alone** puts $R_\chi\circ X$ in the character $\chi\chi_X$". Bilinearity does not suffice: the step is $\gamma\cdot(R_\chi\circ X)=(\gamma R_\chi)\circ(\gamma X)=\chi(\gamma)\chi_X(\gamma)(R_\chi\circ X)$, and the first equality **is** equivariance. The lemma is true, because $\circ$ is $O(n)$-equivariant, and [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md)'s own statement of the same proof says "equivariant" and is correct. Only the findings-file wording needs repair.
