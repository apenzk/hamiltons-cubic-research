## A. Fidelity

- **A-1. The SOS step** ([L254.lean:48](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean)). The hypothesis is the pointwise identity at one $R$, with $x=\langle Q(R),R\rangle$, $N=\lvert R\rvert$ and $s_k=\lvert T_k(R)\rvert$ for the block's equivariant cubic maps $T_k$, so applying it at every $R$ is the block's "hence". `sos_nonneg` gives $x^2\le\tfrac{55}{36}N^6=(\Theta_{12}N^3)^2$, and `abs_le_of_sq_le_sq'` needs $\Theta_{12}N^3\ge0$, which is exactly where $N\ge0$ enters. That hypothesis is the right one: $N=\lvert R\rvert$ always satisfies it, and without it the conclusion fails at $N<0$. The $\lvert x\rvert$ form is stronger than the block's one-sided $\langle Q(R),R\rangle\le\Theta_{12}\lvert R\rvert^3$, and correct, since the SOS bounds $x^2$.
- **A-2. The constant** ([L254.lean:38](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean), line 41). $(\sqrt{55}/6)^2=55/36$, and $2\cdot11\cdot10/144=220/144=55/36$. [conventions.md:19](../findings/conventions.md) sets $\Theta_n=\theta_0(n)$ at even $n$, so $\Theta_{12}=\theta_0(12)=\sqrt{55}/6$. It is distinct from Jäger's $\tilde\theta_0(12)^2=\tfrac{99}{65}$ ([conventions.md:73](../findings/conventions.md)), which the file does not confuse with it.
- **A-3. The ratio identity** ([L254.lean:57](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean)). At $n=12$ the record's $\frac{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)}{(n-2)pq}$ is $\frac{22(p-1)(q-1)}{10pq}=\frac{11(p-1)(q-1)}{5pq}$, so the identity holds at every split, and the proof's `rw [hpq]` makes $n=p+q$ explicit. **Gap.** The left side is the block's *quoted* description of the certificate's convention ([sym3-stability.md:79](../findings/sym3-stability.md), "the certificate's convention gives a two-sphere product the ratio $11(p-1)(q-1)/(5pq)$"). Nothing in the file computes that ratio from the outside $n=12$ SOS certificate (unpublished, held in the private record)'s $Q$ and four-index norm. So the theorem shows two printed closed forms agree at $n=12$. It does not show the two conventions agree, which is what [L254.lean:18–20](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean) ("the certificate's convention agrees with the record's on the whole two-sphere family") and [lean/INDEX.md:373](../lean/INDEX.md) ("so the conventions agree on the whole two-sphere family") say. Honest wording: "the block's quoted ratio agrees with the record's formula at every split".
- **A-4. "The block checks $p=q=6$ only."** Fair. The block prints the general ratio and then compares it with the record's constant at $p=q=6$ alone ("which at $p=q=6$ is $\tfrac{55}{36}=\Theta_{12}^2$"). It makes no comparison at another split.
- **A-5. The balanced maximum** ([L254.lean:62](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean)). With $q=12-p$, $(p-1)(q-1)=pq-11$ and $\tfrac{55}{36}-\tfrac{121(36-pq)}{180pq}=\tfrac{396}{180}-\tfrac{121}{5pq}=\tfrac{11}{5}-\tfrac{121}{5pq}$, which is the ratio, so `key` is right. $pq\le36$ from $(p-q)^2\ge0$, and equality forces $p=q$, hence $p=q=6$. The statement holds on the positive reals as written. The real domain does not matter for truth: it contains the integer splits, and at $p,q\ge2$ over $\mathbb N$ the statement follows by casting. It is a strengthening the record never needs, since only integer splits are sphere products.
- **A-6. "partial" and the certificate.** Honest. The file's header states in bold that the certificate is not formalised ([L254.lean:10–12](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean)), the owed list names the identity over the outside table, and row 254 of INDEX-results reads "the step from the certificate to the bound". The commit's `Deliverable:` line, "L254's derivation from its certificate proved in Lean", also reads correctly as the derivation *from* the certificate.
- **A-7. Attainment and the owed list** ([L254.lean:21–26](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L254.lean), [lean/INDEX.md:373](../lean/INDEX.md)). **Gap.** "Attained at $S^6\times S^6$" in the block means $\langle Q(R),R\rangle=\Theta_{12}\lvert R\rvert^3$ at the Weyl part $R$ of $S^6\times S^6$, and $M(12)=\Theta_{12}$ needs that together with the bound. `ratio_le_balanced` proves that a closed form in $(p,q)$ peaks at $(6,6)$. It evaluates no tensor. The file calls it "the attainment at `S⁶ × S⁶`" (line 22), the row calls it "the attainment at $S^6\times S^6$", and the file's title (line 6) reads "from the certificate to `M(12) = Θ₁₂`". The layer already has the value in its own convention: `theta_sq_sphere` ([L2Operators.lean:54](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L2Operators.lean)) and `L57_attained_even` ([L57Attain.lean:49](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L57Attain.lean)).
