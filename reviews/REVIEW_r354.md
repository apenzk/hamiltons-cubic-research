## M1, re-derived before reading the derivation

Write $\Gamma=\{\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon)\}\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^n$, acting on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ by $\varphi_{ab}\mapsto\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b\varphi_{ab}$ and on a symmetric operator $X$ by conjugation, so the basis element $\varphi_\alpha\odot\varphi_\beta$ carries the character $\chi_\alpha\chi_\beta$. For $\alpha=\{a,b\}$ and $\beta=\{c,d\}$ that character is $\chi_S$ with $S=\alpha\,\triangle\,\beta$, and since the symmetric difference of two $2$-sets has size $0$, $2$ or $4$ according as they are equal, meet in one index, or are disjoint, **the character is trivial precisely when $\alpha=\beta$**. So the trivial isotypic is exactly the span of the $\varphi_{ab}\odot\varphi_{ab}$, and an operator with zero diagonal in the frame has zero trivial component: step one holds, and it holds in $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ rather than only in $\mathcal W_n$, which is the stronger reading I12 needs.

The equivariance in use is $(gXg^{-1})\circ(gYg^{-1})=g(X\circ Y)g^{-1}$ for $g\in O(n)$ acting on $\Lambda^2$ through $\Lambda^2$ of the standard representation. Both halves of $\circ$ satisfy it: the anticommutator because conjugation is an algebra map, and $\#$ because it is built from $c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle[v_\alpha,v_\beta],v_\gamma\rangle$, and conjugation by any $g\in O(n)$ (determinant $-1$ included), is a Lie algebra automorphism of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ preserving the invariant metric, hence fixes $c$ as a $3$-tensor. **This is where the full sign group is needed and where the record's own warning bites**: on $\Gamma\cap SO(n)$ the character $\chi_{[n]}$ is trivial, complementary blocks fuse at $n=4,6,8$, and the argument would fail; I12 uses $\Gamma$ and is safe. Given equivariance, $\gamma\cdot(R_\chi\circ X)=(\gamma R_\chi)\circ(\gamma X)=\chi(\gamma)\chi_X(\gamma)(R_\chi\circ X)$, which is the claim that $R_\chi\circ X$ carries $\chi\cdot\chi_X$; note it needs $R_\chi$ to be a **character** vector rather than merely isotypic, which is automatic here since every $\Gamma$-character space is a sum of one-dimensional pieces with the same character.

Orthogonality of the isotypic spaces holds for the pairing actually in use (the $O(n)$-invariant Frobenius pairing on operators on $\Lambda^2$), by the one-line argument $\langle u,v\rangle=\chi(\gamma)\psi(\gamma)\langle u,v\rangle$ and the existence of $\gamma$ with $\chi(\gamma)\psi(\gamma)=-1$ when $\chi\ne\psi$; all characters here are real, so no complexification subtlety enters. The last attack in the brief has a clean answer: in $\widehat{(\mathbb Z/2)^n}$ every element is invertible, so $\chi\chi_X=\chi_X$ forces $\chi=1$, and there is no nontrivial stabiliser to worry about. The one caution worth recording is that all the characters occurring, $\chi_S$ with $\lvert S\rvert$ even, are trivial on $-\mathrm{Id}\in\Gamma$ (the kernel of the action) so they descend to $\Gamma/\{\pm\mathrm{Id}\}$ **without any two of them becoming equal**, which is what the $\Gamma\cap SO(n)$ failure would otherwise suggest.

Finally, the single-character hypothesis is not automatic and is correctly stated: for $X=X_{\chi_1}+X_{\chi_2}$ the cross term $\langle X_{\chi_1},R_\chi\circ X_{\chi_2}\rangle$ survives whenever $\chi\chi_2=\chi_1$, and $\chi=\chi_1\chi_2$ is a legitimate nontrivial character of the frame. A $4$-set direction is safe because all three pairings $\varphi_{ij}\odot\varphi_{kl}$, $\varphi_{ik}\odot\varphi_{jl}$, $\varphi_{il}\odot\varphi_{jk}$ carry the same $\chi_{\{i,j,k,l\}}$, so the whole block (and the Bianchi plane inside it), lies in one character space. **A consequence worth recording, which the derivation stops just short of**: since $L_R$ is symmetric and all its Rayleigh quotients vanish on the block, polarisation gives $P_{V}L_RP_{V}=0$ outright, so the compression of $L_W$ to the $4$-set block *is* $L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}$ restricted there, with no need to argue term by term.

## M2, where I12 fails, and what survives

The reduction theorem in [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) states its hypothesis correctly, "$X$ a unit vector in the Bianchi plane of that $4$-set, **orthogonal to $W$** and to the orbit", and the final I12 theorem then uses [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md)'s *eigenvalue*, which is attained at the top eigenvector $X_+$ of the block and at no other unit vector of it. Nothing in the derivation checks that $X_+\perp W$, and in the frames I12 is about it is false. The projection of $W$ onto $V_{ijkl}$ is $\langle X,W\rangle=\langle X,\operatorname{diag}(M)\rangle+\langle X,R\rangle=\langle X,R\rangle$, the first term vanishing by character orthogonality; the second is $W_{ijkl}$ up to normalisation, and it is nonzero for exactly the reason the frame is not the eigenframe. **In the eigenframe $R=0$ and the whole block is orthogonal to $W$, which is why L121 needs no correction there and why the gap is invisible from the diagonal case.**

The exact cost is computable and needs no computation. For any unit $X$, put $c=\langle X,W\rangle$ and $X'=X-cW$, so $X'\in W^\perp\cap\mathcal W_n$ with $\lVert X'\rVert^2=1-c^2$. Using $L_WW=Q(W)=\theta W$ and the symmetry of $L_W$,

$$\langle X',L_WX'\rangle=\langle X,L_WX\rangle-2c\langle W,L_WX\rangle+c^2\langle W,L_WW\rangle=\langle X,L_WX\rangle-\theta c^2 .$$

[Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) applied to $X'$ then gives $\langle X,L_WX\rangle\le\tfrac\theta2(1-c^2)+\theta c^2=\tfrac\theta2(1+c^2)$. Taking $X=X_+$ and using M1 to replace $L_W$ by $L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}$ on the block, L121's eigenvalue $\tfrac12\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA}$ obeys

$$\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA}\ \le\ \theta\big(1+c^2\big),\qquad c=\langle X_+,W\rangle,\ \ c^2\le\lVert P_{V_{ijkl}}W\rVert^2 .$$

**This is the theorem I12 actually proves, and it is not the theorem I12 states.** The correction cannot be removed inside this method: applying L24 across the whole projected plane $\{X-\langle X,W\rangle W:X\in V_{ijkl}\}$ returns the same bound, and the only unit direction of the block that is genuinely admissible without projection is the one-dimensional $V_{ijkl}\cap W^\perp$, on which a traceless $2\times2$ block has Rayleigh quotient $\lambda\cos2\alpha$ and therefore yields nothing about $\lambda$ when $\cos2\alpha\le0$.

**Two secondary points, both of which the derivation gets away with.** The orbit exclusion is harmless: L24 as stated in [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) is $\lambda_{\max}(L_e|_{W^\perp})\le\tfrac12$ on all of $W^\perp$, with the orbit directions being exact $\tfrac12$-eigenvectors, so a $4$-set direction carrying an orbit component, which it can, in a non-diagonal frame, since $[\rho(A),W]=[\rho(A),\operatorname{diag}M]+[\rho(A),R]$ and only the first summand is confined to the pair blocks, still obeys the bound. And $\theta=P(W)$ is a scalar, so it is the same in every frame; that attack in the brief finds nothing.

It shows $\langle X,L_RX\rangle=0$ at $4$-set directions on rotated sphere products, which is the character lemma; it does not test the inequality I12 asserts, because it never compares $A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA$ with $\theta^2$ in the rotated frame. **BLOCKED computation, logged and not run**: evaluate $\max_{4\text{-sets}}\big(A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA\big)-\theta^2$ in randomly rotated frames at the record's stored critical points. ## M3, and why $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ is better behaved than claimed

$\circ$ is bilinear, so $L_W=L_{\operatorname{diag}(M)}+L_R$ with no remainder; that half is beyond attack. The interesting part is the attack the brief names: **does $\operatorname{diag}(M)$ lie in $\mathcal W_n$ at all.** It does, in every frame. A coordinate-diagonal operator has $R_{abcd}=0$ whenever $\{a,b\}\ne\{c,d\}$, so first Bianchi is satisfied termwise and the off-diagonal Ricci contractions $\sum_bR_{abcb}$ vanish identically for $a\ne c$; the diagonal Ricci entries are the row sums $\sum_bM_{ab}$, and those are the diagonal Ricci entries of $W$ itself, which vanish because $W$ is Weyl **in every orthonormal frame**. So $\operatorname{diag}(M)\in\mathcal D_n$ and $R=W-\operatorname{diag}(M)\in\mathcal W_n$ with zero diagonal, which is precisely the hypothesis M1 wants. It happens that neither M1 nor L121 needs this (M1 lives in $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$, and L121 needs only diagonality) but the requirement that the *test direction* lie in $\mathcal W_n$ is real, and it is met because $V_{ijkl}$ is defined as the Bianchi plane inside the $4$-set block.

## M4, the sign, the restriction, and the orbit

Reading [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) on the pair block: $L_e=L_M/\theta$, so $\lambda_{\max}\big(-\tfrac12\Delta^{(jk)}-\tfrac12M_{jk}\mathrm{Id}\big)\le\tfrac\theta2$, and $\lambda_{\max}(-\tfrac12\Delta)=-\tfrac12\lambda_{\min}(\Delta)$ gives $\lambda_{\min}(\Delta^{(jk)})\ge-(\theta+M_{jk})$. **The sign is right.**

**The restriction to $\mathbf 1^\perp$ is forced, not a weakening.** L24 is the Hessian condition on the unit sphere of $\mathcal W_n$, so the test direction must be a Weyl operator; [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) cuts the $(n-2)$-dimensional pair block to the $(n-3)$-dimensional $V_{jk}$ by one Ricci relation, and that relation is $\sum_ix_i=0$. Directions off $\mathbf 1^\perp$ are simply not admissible, so no stronger unrestricted statement is available. The restriction is also well posed as an eigenvalue statement, since $\Delta^{(jk)}\mathbf 1=0$ makes $\mathbf 1^\perp$ invariant.

**The orbit needs no separate exclusion, and its presence is a check that the bound passes.** The whole $SO(n)$-orbit at a diagonal point lies in the sum of the pair blocks, and in the $\{j,k\}$ block the orbit direction is $x_i=M_{ki}-M_{ji}$ with $L_M$-eigenvalue $\theta/2$. Feeding that into the block form gives $-\tfrac12\delta-\tfrac12M_{jk}=\tfrac\theta2$, i.e. $\delta=-(\theta+M_{jk})$: **the orbit direction saturates M4's inequality exactly**, so the bound is sharp and would be contradicted by any sign error. The orbit direction also lies in $\mathbf 1^\perp$, since $\sum_i(M_{ki}-M_{ji})=(0-M_{kj})-(0-M_{jk})=0$ by Ricci-flatness, a second internal check that passes. Excluding it would be needed only to claim strictness.

## M5, the scope claims, checked from the structure constants

I computed both blocks by hand from $X\circ Y=\tfrac12(XY+YX)+X\#Y$ with $(N\#X)_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12\sum c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}c_{\beta\gamma\zeta}M_\gamma X_{\delta\zeta}$ for diagonal $N$, in the basis $\varphi_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}$ with $[\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{cd}]=\delta_{bc}\varphi_{ad}-\delta_{ac}\varphi_{bd}-\delta_{bd}\varphi_{ac}+\delta_{ad}\varphi_{bc}$.

**L121 needs diagonality alone, CONFIRMED, and I can say which half supplies what.** The anticommutator contributes exactly $\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(A,B,C)$, since the entry at $(\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{kl})$ picks up $\tfrac12(M_{ij}+M_{kl})$. The $\#$ term contributes **nothing** on the diagonal: at $(\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{ij})$ the two triangles through the edge $ij$ force $\delta=\zeta$, and the $4$-set block has zero diagonal. Off the diagonal, the common index $\gamma$ ranges over $\{(ik),(il),(jk),(jl)\}$, and the four terms assemble into $\tfrac12\big[(M_{ik}+M_{jl})x_3-(M_{il}+M_{jk})x_2\big]$ in the first row, the signs being $c_{(ij)(ik)(jk)}c_{(kl)(ik)(il)}=(-1)(-1)=+1$ and $c_{(ij)(jl)(il)}c_{(kl)(jl)(jk)}=(+1)(+1)=+1$ against $c_{(ij)(il)(jl)}c_{(kl)(il)(ik)}=(-1)(+1)=-1$ and $c_{(ij)(jk)(ik)}c_{(kl)(jk)(jl)}=(+1)(-1)=-1$, so a single sign flip $x_2\mapsto-x_2$, which is the lemma's "signed coordinates", produces exactly $\tfrac12G$ with $G$'s first row $(A,C,B)$. **Nowhere does a row sum of $M$ appear**, so neither Ricci-flatness nor criticality nor $\theta>0$ is used, and the internal consistency check passes: $\operatorname{tr}(G^2)=3(A^2+B^2+C^2)$ and $G\mathbf 1=(A+B+C)\mathbf 1$ give eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA}$ on $\mathbf 1^\perp$, which is L121's spectrum after the factor $\tfrac12$.

**The record does not state that scope, however.** [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) opens "Fix a diagonal critical point $M$ with $\theta>0$", and [DERIVE-mx1.md](DERIVE-mx1.md) asserts the opposite, "that is the whole point of stating L121 for diagonality alone rather than for criticality". The widened scope is true, and I have just proved it, but I12 currently leans on a hypothesis-set the lemma it cites does not carry. **The repair is to widen L121's statement in [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md), not to leave I12 citing it as if already widened.**

**L122's scope sentence is wrong in part, REFUTED as written.** The bracketed form is correct under diagonality alone, and I verified it the same way: the anticommutator gives $\tfrac12(M_{ji}+M_{ki})$ on the diagonal, the $\gamma=(jk)$ term of $\#$ gives $-\tfrac12M_{jk}$ there with sign $c_{(ji)(jk)(ik)}c_{(ki)(jk)(ij)}=(-1)(+1)=-1$, and the $\gamma=(im)$ terms give $\tfrac12M_{im}$ off it. But the identification with a graph Laplacian does not survive the loss of Ricci-flatness. Writing $\Delta^{(jk)}=\operatorname{diag}(s_i)-M^{(jk)}$ with $s_i=\sum_{m\ne i,j,k}M_{im}=r_i-M_{ij}-M_{ik}$, matching $-\Delta^{(jk)}$ against $\operatorname{diag}(M_{ji}+M_{ki})+M^{(jk)}$ requires $-s_i=M_{ji}+M_{ki}$, that is $r_i=0$. The same hypothesis is behind the companion claim that the Ricci cut is the Laplacian's own $\mathbf 1^\perp$: the block's row sums are $\tfrac12(r_i-M_{jk})$, constant at $-\tfrac12M_{jk}$ only when $r_i$ is constant and equal to the Laplacian's value only when $r_i=0$. **So "diagonality alone, not criticality, not $\theta>0$, not Ricci-flatness" should be attached to the bracketed matrix and withdrawn from the Laplacian form and from the $\mathbf 1^\perp$ statement.** [DERIVE-df1.md](DERIVE-df1.md) is self-aware about this in one place, its decisive refutation of the halved form is said to follow "from Ricci-flatness alone", which makes the scope sentence an overreach of the surrounding text rather than a computational error. It does not touch M4, because every $M$ M4 is applied to is Ricci-flat, and it does not touch I12, which uses L121.
