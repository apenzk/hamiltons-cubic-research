## The target, and why it is not typed

**Theorem sought.** *At a unit critical point $W$ of $P$ whose globally maximal frame has diagonal mass $t^2\le\tau_n$, $P(W)<\theta_0(n)$.* A global maximiser has $P=\Theta_n\ge\theta_0(n)$, so this empties the small-mass node. **The statement needs a globally maximal frame, an object the Lean layer does not define** (`maxFrame` is absent from [lean/](../lean/)), so it stays prose and the pieces below are typed.

**What any proof must use.** *Criticality, the value and the Hessian of $P$ are frame-invariant, so the balanced product in any frame satisfies them.* So a proof uses enough frame maximality to exclude the product in every non-maximal frame, and enough of the value to exclude the obstructed $\mathbb{CP}^m$, which sit at maximal-frame $t^2=\tfrac3{n+2}$ with $\theta<\sqrt{3/2}$ ([r1843](REVIEW_r1843.md)). At small $t$ the value sits in the four-set part $B_f$ (the [pair identity](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/PairIdentity.lean)), so the first step is to write $P(B_f)$ in coordinates that frame maximality can see.

## Coordinates on a four-set block, relative to a pairing

For a four-set $Q=\{i,j,k,l\}$ and its pairing $\pi=(e|f)$, $e=\{i,j\}$, $f=\{k,l\}$, write the raw entries $a=W_{ij,kl}$, $b=W_{ik,jl}$, $c=W_{il,jk}$, with Bianchi $a=b-c$, and set
$$J_\pi=a=W_{e,f},\qquad X_\pi=\tfrac12(b+c).$$
**$J_\pi$ is the entry of $W$ between the matched bivectors $e_{ij}$ and $e_{kl}$**, so for a perfect matching $M\ni e,f$ it is an entry of the compression of $W$ to $U_M=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_g:g\in M\}$, which every rotation in $M$'s planes fixes. **$X_\pi$ is the combination $M$ sees**: $E(M)=\sum_{e<f\in M}4X_{ef}^2$ in the [matching inequality](2026-09-28b-LIT-step8-sweep.md). With $b=X+\tfrac J2$, $c=X-\tfrac J2$,
$$\lVert\nu_Q\rVert^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)=4X_\pi^2+3J_\pi^2\qquad\text{for each of }Q\text{'s three pairings}\quad(\texttt{rect\_norm}).$$

## The rectangle identity

**Claim.** *Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be disjoint edges, $Q_{ab}=e_a\cup e_b$, and $\nu_{ab}\in V_{Q_{ab}}$ with coordinates $(J_{ab},X_{ab})$ relative to the pairing $(e_a|e_b)$. Then*
$$\tau:=\langle\nu_{12}\circ\nu_{13},\nu_{23}\rangle=\tfrac32J_{12}J_{13}J_{23}+4X_{12}X_{13}X_{23},$$
*independent of $n$, and for every four-set part $B_f=\sum_Q\nu_Q$,*
$$P(B_f)=\sum_{\text{rectangles }R}\big(9J_{12}J_{13}J_{23}+24X_{12}X_{13}X_{23}\big)_R.$$
*Here a rectangle is three disjoint edges. Reversing $e_1$ swaps $b$ and $c$ on the blocks $Q_{12},Q_{13}$, so $X_{ab}$ is independent of the edges' orientations and $J_{12},J_{13}$ change sign, which leaves $J_{12}J_{13}J_{23}$ independent of them too.*

**Proof.**

1. **Only rectangles couple** (`triple_char_zero` and `single_zero` in [RectOp.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/RectOp.lean), with the $XXX$ coefficient $4$ as `tau_matched`). $T$ is multilinear and invariant under the sign group, and $\nu_Q$ carries the character $\chi_Q$, so $T(\nu_{Q_1},\nu_{Q_2},\nu_{Q_3})=0$ unless $\chi_{Q_1\triangle Q_2\triangle Q_3}=1$, that is $Q_3=Q_1\triangle Q_2$ with $\lvert Q_1\cap Q_2\rvert=2$ ([L120](../findings/second-order.md)'s characters). A single block has $\chi_Q^3=\chi_Q\ne1$. So $P(B_f)=6\sum_R\tau_R$, the $6$ counting the orders of three distinct blocks, and the three pairwise intersections of a rectangle's blocks are its edges.
2. **The $R^2$ part.** $\langle\nu^2,\nu\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nu^3$ over the bivector basis, and the mixed terms of $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\nu_{12}+\nu_{13}+\nu_{23})^3$ are $6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\nu_{12}\nu_{13}\nu_{23})$. A term $\nu_{12}(x,y)\nu_{13}(y,z)\nu_{23}(z,x)$ needs $x$ inside $Q_{12}\cap Q_{23}=e_2$, so $x=e_2$, then $y=e_1$ and $z=e_3$, the complements within each block. **So the $R^2$ part is $6J_{12}J_{13}J_{23}$ and sees no crossing entry.**
3. **The $\#$ part.** By [Böhm–Wilking](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/notes.md) eq. 5, $2\langle R^\#,R\rangle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\langle[Rb_\alpha,Rb_\beta],Rb_\gamma\rangle\langle[b_\alpha,b_\beta],b_\gamma\rangle$ over an orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$. In the basis $e_{ab}$ the structure constant $\langle[e_{ab},e_{bc}],e_{ca}\rangle$ is nonzero exactly on index triangles, so the sum is a fixed multiple of $\sum R_{ab,a'b'}R_{bc,b'c'}R_{ca,c'a'}$, which is $I_3=\sum R_{ijkl}R_{ipkq}R_{jplq}$ after relabelling ($i=a$, $j=b$, $k=a'$, $l=b'$, $p=c$, $q=c'$). So $\langle R^\#,R\rangle=\kappa\,I_3$. On a diagonal tensor $I_3=6\sum_{\text{triangles}}K_{ab}K_{bc}K_{ca}$, and $\langle D^\#,D\rangle=3\sum_{\text{triangles}}$ ([L220](../findings/second-order.md) step 7, `P_PiQ`), so $\kappa=\tfrac12$ ([r1846](REVIEW_r1846.md), [r1847](REVIEW_r1847.md): the proportionality carries the step, since the other contraction, with third factor $R_{jqlp}$, gives $3JJJ+24XXX$).
4. **$I_3$ on a rectangle.** Consecutive factors share two indices, so the three factors lie in three blocks pairwise meeting in an edge, and in $R_{ijkl}$ the indices $i,k$ lie in one edge and $j,l$ in another. **So every factor is a crossing entry**, $b$ or $c$, and it is $b=X+\tfrac J2$ when the two edges are traversed in the same orientation and $c=X-\tfrac J2$ otherwise. Fixing which edge carries which index pair ($6$ ways) and summing over the $2^3$ orientation bits, the three signs multiply to $1$, so the sum is $8XXX+JJJ$ (`rect_bits`). Hence $I_3=48XXX+6JJJ$ and $\langle R^\#,R\rangle=24XXX+3JJJ$.
5. **Sum.** $P=6JJJ+3JJJ+24XXX$, and $\tau=\tfrac16P$. $\square$

**Restated through step 2**: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B_f^3=6\sum_RJ_{12}J_{13}J_{23}$, so $P(B_f)=\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B_f^3+24\sum_RX_{12}X_{13}X_{23}$, **the invisible part entering only through the spectrum of $B_f$ as an operator on $\Lambda^2$.** *Every raw entry of $\nu_Q$ is the $J$ of one of $Q$'s pairings, so the $J$'s over all disjoint pairs of edges are exactly $B_f$'s entries, with $\sum_\pi J_\pi^2=\tfrac12\lVert\nu_Q\rVert^2$ and $\sum_\pi4X_\pi^2=\tfrac32\lVert\nu_Q\rVert^2$ on each block.*

**Check** (Gate 1 (b), a second route sharing no algebra with the proof): [check_I121_rectangle_cubic.py](../scripts/misc/check_I121_rectangle_cubic.py) evaluates $\tau$ on all eight basis triples with the record's $Q$ ($\tfrac32$ at $JJJ$, $4$ at $XXX$, $0$ at the six mixed triples, at $n=6$ and $n=8$), and $P(B_f)$ against the rectangle sum on a random four-set tensor: $20.4282301675$ both at $n=6$ ($15$ rectangles), $-207.2176848878$ both at $n=8$ ($420$).

## What it settles on the record

- **The bilinear formula's convention, as an operator identity.** The [I104 note](2026-09-14-I104-tau-positive.md)'s formula for $\nu_1\circ\nu_2$, read off at $n=8$, was left with its coordinate convention unsettled ([r1077](REVIEW_r1077.md)): the raw and signed readings give different $\tau$. In raw coordinates, $a_1a_2a_3+(b_1b_2+c_1c_2)b_3+(b_1c_2+c_1b_2)c_3$ with $a=J$, $b=X+\tfrac J2$, $c=X-\tfrac J2$ is exactly $\tfrac32JJJ+4XXX$ (`rect_raw`). **So the raw reading is the right one, settled against a derived identity.** *And it holds for the product itself, not only for $\tau$: the formula's coefficient triple satisfies Bianchi, so it lies in $V_{Q_3}$, the character forces $\nu_1\circ\nu_2$ into $V_{Q_3}$, and agreement against every $\nu_3$ there fixes the element* ([r1846](REVIEW_r1846.md)). *The note's norm is L221's, $N=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)$.*
- **L221's product constant with its equality case.** In unit coordinates $x=2X/\sqrt N$, $j=\sqrt3J/\sqrt N$ on each block, $\tau=\sqrt{N_1N_2N_3}\,\big(\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}j_1j_2j_3+\tfrac12x_1x_2x_3\big)$, so $\lvert\tau\rvert\le\tfrac12\sqrt{N_1N_2N_3}$ (`rect_unit` at $c=1/\sqrt3$), **with equality exactly when all three blocks are purely visible**, $x_i=\pm1$. The purely invisible rectangle has $\lvert\tau\rvert=\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}\sqrt{N_1N_2N_3}$, which is $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ of the bound.

## The whole of $P$ at pair-free $W$ in these coordinates

**Claim.** *For pair-free $W=A+B_f$,*
$$P(W)=P(A)+3\sum_{(e,f)}\big(\tfrac32J_{ef}^2-2X_{ef}^2\big)(a_e+a_f)+\sum_R\big(9JJJ+24XXX\big)_R,$$
*the middle sum over unordered pairs of disjoint edges, $a_e$ the diagonal entry on the edge $e$.* **Proof.** $P(A+B_f)=P(A)+3T(A,A,B_f)+3T(A,B_f,B_f)+P(B_f)$. The second term vanishes by characters, and $T(A,B_f,B_f)=\langle A,Q(B_f)\rangle=\sum_Q\langle A,\nu_Q\circ\nu_Q\rangle$, cross terms having a nontrivial character. By `nu_sq` ([FourProd.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/FourProd.lean)) $\nu_Q\circ\nu_Q=\Pi_Q(2a^2-b^2-c^2,\dots)$ in the signed coordinates, which are $(a,-b,c)$ in the raw entries, the middle term of `spanVec` being written with $\varphi_{ki}=-\varphi_{ik}$, so the squares are the raw ones, and $2a^2-b^2-c^2=2J^2-(2X^2+\tfrac12J^2)=\tfrac32J^2-2X^2$ on the pairing $(e|f)$. By `inner_PiQ` ([FourStep5.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/FourStep5.lean)) the pairing with $A$ is $\sum_\pi x_\pi(a_e+a_f)$. $\square$ *So the diagonal couples to the invisible part with weight $+\tfrac92$ and to the visible part with weight $-6$, each against the two matched edges' entries.* **Check**: the same script, on a random Weyl diagonal plus a random four-set part, gives $7.0944991067$ both ways at $n=6$ and $-527.1291880232$ at $n=8$.

## The matching form, and what it does for the node

At even $n$ each rectangle lies in $(n-7)!!$ perfect matchings, so with $J_M$ and $X_M$ the hollow symmetric $\tfrac n2\times\tfrac n2$ matrices of $J_{ef}$ and $X_{ef}$ over $e,f\in M$,
$$P(B_f)=\frac1{(n-7)!!}\sum_M\Big(\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}J_M^3+4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_M^3\Big),\qquad E(M)=2\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2.$$

- **$J_M$ is invisible to $M$ and $X_M$ is what $M$ sees.** At [post F81](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s witness, $J_{M_0}=0$ for the factor matching (the torus of $M_0$ fixes $J_{M_0}$, and the product has none), and every nonzero four-set block is a union of two $M_0$ edges, so every contributing rectangle lies inside $M_0$ and $P(B_f)=4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_{M_0}^3$ ([r1846](REVIEW_r1846.md)). That is why the matching inequality excludes it: $E(M_0)=1-t^2$ against a slack of $t^2-\sum_{e\in M_0}a_e^2$.
- **Every $J$ is visible elsewhere.** On one block, $J_{\pi_1}=\tfrac23(\pm X_{\pi_2}\pm X_{\pi_3})$, the $X$'s of the two crossing pairings, which crossing matchings see.
- **The estimate is still averaging.** The matching inequality gives $\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2\le\tfrac{t^2}2$ for every $M$, and summing $\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2$ over matchings gives $\tfrac34(n-5)!!\lVert B_f\rVert^2$, so the visible part of $P(B_f)$ is at most $\tfrac3{\sqrt2}(n-5)\,t\,\lVert B_f\rVert^2$. That is order $nt$, the scale averaging already reached. **The obstacle is the same one**: a bound that sums over all matchings loses the factor by which a high-value tensor's visible mass concentrates on few of them, and nothing here shows it does.

## The reduction: above $\sqrt{3/2}$, the value at pair-free $W$ lives in the visible and diagonal parts

*Gate 2: [r1849](REVIEW_r1849.md), [r1850](REVIEW_r1850.md), every identity and estimate CONFIRMED, the scope corrected below.*

**Claim 1 (frame-free).** *Put $E(W)=P(W)-\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3=\langle W^\#,W\rangle-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$, an $O(n)$-invariant cubic. At every unit critical point, $\theta^2\le\tfrac32+\theta\,E(W)$. So $\theta>\sqrt{3/2}$ forces $E(W)>0$, and $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$ forces $E(W)\ge\varepsilon_n:=\theta_0-\tfrac3{2\theta_0}$*, which is $0.022473$ at $n=12$ and tends to $\tfrac1{2\sqrt2}=0.353553$, and is nonnegative exactly at even $n\ge12$. **Proof.** [L198](../findings/bounds.md)'s row inequality $\lvert\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)\rvert\le1-\lambda_k^2$ gives $\theta\lambda_k\le1$ at every $k$, so $\theta\lambda_k^3\le\lambda_k^2$ termwise and $\theta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3\le\sum\lambda_k^2=1$ at every sign of $\theta$ (`theta_trace_le`, [r1849](REVIEW_r1849.md)). Then $\theta^2=\tfrac32\theta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3+\theta E\le\tfrac32+\theta E$ (`crit_E_bound'`). The threshold follows as $\theta-\tfrac3{2\theta}$ increases on $\theta>0$ (`crit_E_lt`). $\square$

**Claim 2 (the invariant in a frame, at pair-free $W=A+B_f$).**
$$E(W)=\Big[P(A)-\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A^3\Big]-6\sum_{(e,f)}(a_e+a_f)X_{ef}^2+24\sum_RX_{12}X_{13}X_{23},$$
*every $J$ cancelling.* **Proof.** $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A^3+3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB_f^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B_f^3$, since $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^2B_f)=0$ ($B_f$ has zero diagonal), and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB_f^2)=\sum_ea_e\sum_{f\cap e=\emptyset}W_{e,f}^2=\sum_{(e,f)}(a_e+a_f)J_{ef}^2$. Subtract $\tfrac32$ of it from the whole-$P$ formula above: the $J^2$ terms cancel ($3\cdot\tfrac32-\tfrac32\cdot3=0$), and $\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B_f^3$ cancels against $P(B_f)$'s. $\square$ **Check**: $E(W)=-26.1123208173$ both ways at $n=6$ and $-212.9753387454$ at $n=8$ on the random pair-free tensor above.

**So at pair-free $W$ in a globally maximal frame at even $n$, a value above $\theta_0$ needs $E\ge\varepsilon_n$ from three sources**: the diagonal term, of size $O(t^3)$; the coupling $-6\sum(a_e+a_f)X^2$, at most $\tfrac32\sqrt{3(n-3)}\,t^2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz with the matching inequality ($S_e=\sum_fX_{ef}^2\le\tfrac{n-3}4t^2$ for each $e$ by averaging over the matchings through $e$, and $\sum_eS_e=\tfrac34\lVert B_f\rVert^2$ over ordered pairs); and **the visible cubic $24\sum_RXXX$**, which per matching is $P(D_M)$ for the diagonal tensor $D_M$ of the $\tfrac\pi4$-rotated visible part (the triangle sum of its cross pairs, the cubes cancelling in each block), so $\lvert\cdot\rvert\le\Theta_nE(M)^{3/2}$ matching by matching (checked at $n=8$ on one matching of the random four-set tensor: $P(D_M)=3.8260460188=24\sum_{R\subset M}XXX$ and $\lVert D_M\rVert^2=E(M)=7.5828792132$, $D_M$ with zero row sums). **L198 bounds the whole of $\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$, which carries every $J$** (through $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B_f^3$ and the $A$–$J^2$ coupling), so no $J$ survives in $E$, and that is where $\sqrt{3/2}$ comes from. **What is open** ([r1849](REVIEW_r1849.md), [r1850](REVIEW_r1850.md)): *the visible cubic*, whose bound summed over matchings with each $E(M)\le t^2$ is of order $nt$ (precisely $3\Theta_n(n-5)t\lVert B_f\rVert^2$); *the coupling at finite $n$*, whose bound falls below $\varepsilon_{12}$ only at $t^2<0.0029$, against $\mathbb{CP}^m$'s $t^2=\tfrac3{n+2}$ and F81's $\tfrac1n$; and *the pair part*, which the pair-free formula leaves out and which at the node has $\lVert B_p\rVert^2\le(n-1)t^2$, so its terms in $E$ are of order $\sqrt nt$. *A heuristic for the visible cubic, not derived here*: a proof would have to show that a critical point cannot spread product-like visible patterns over a $\tfrac1{cn}$ fraction of all matchings at once.

**Screen.** *Descent on the $\tfrac\pi4$ matching family plus the first-order condition of $t^2$ ([screen_I121_critical_matching.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_critical_matching.py)), six starts on the product at $n=8$ under caps $0.3$ and $0.2$, found no frame satisfying both: the near-critical frames sit at $t^2=0.005$ to $0.016$, below the frame average, with $\lvert\nabla t^2\rvert\le0.044$, and fail the matching family by $0.088$ to $0.102$. Eighty steps of a penalised descent, so a screen.*

## The visible cubic is a Kneser triangle sum, and the matching family allows it to be large (screens)

**Structure.** *The visible coordinates parametrise $B_f$ completely*: on each four-set the three $X_\pi$ sum to zero identically (each $X$ is orientation-independent), and each $J_\pi$ is $\tfrac23$ of a signed difference of the other two $X$'s ([r1851](REVIEW_r1851.md), [r1852](REVIEW_r1852.md)). So the visible cubic is $24\,T(X)$ with $T(X)=\sum_RX_{12}X_{13}X_{23}$, **a weighted triangle sum on the Kneser graph $KG(n,2)$**, whose vertices are the edges of $K_n$, whose edges are disjoint pairs, whose triangles are the rectangles and whose maximum cliques, at even $n$, are the perfect matchings. The matching inequality caps each clique's mass, $E(M)=\sum_{e<f\in M}4X_{ef}^2\le t^2$, so $24T\le24\kappa_nt^3$ with $\kappa_n=\max\{T:\max_ME(M)\le1\}$.

**Screens** ([screen_I121_visible_cubic.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_visible_cubic.py), normalised ascent from random starts, giving lower bounds on $\kappa_n$, establishing nothing positive):

| $n$ | $\kappa_n\ge$ | $\max_ME(M)$ at unit four-set mass | $24T$ there |
|---|---|---|---|
| $6$ | $0.213$ | $0.13$ | $0.24$ |
| $8$ | $1.289$ | $0.063$ | $0.48$ |
| $10$ | $2.724$ | $0.043$ | $0.59$ |

- **So the matching family alone allows a visible cubic of order one at $t^2\approx0.45/n$** at $n=8,10$, comparable to $\varepsilon_n\le0.354$, spread over many matchings (the maximisers carry $23$ to $35$ times the clique cap in total visible mass at $n=8,10$). *This is the $n^{-1/2}$ barrier in the language of the reduction.*
- **The diagonal equation does not exclude these maximisers robustly.** At a pair-free critical point $\theta A=Q(A)+D(B_f)$, with $D(B_f)_{ij}=\sum_{kl}(\tfrac32J^2-2X^2)$ over the pairings $(ij|kl)$, so $\theta t\le\lVert D(B_f)\rVert+(1+\sqrt2)t^2$, and the matching inequality needs $t^2\ge\max_ME(M)$. At the first $n=8$ maximiser ($\lVert D(B_f)\rVert=0.143$ at unit $B_f$) and $\theta=\theta_0(8)$ that pair of conditions is marginal: at unit $W$, where both carry the factor $1-t^2$, it fails at the matching threshold by $0.002$ and holds from $t^2\approx0.08$ ([r1853](REVIEW_r1853.md), rechecked here). At the second ($\lVert D(B_f)\rVert=0.198$) it holds at the threshold with $0.05$ to spare ([r1855](REVIEW_r1855.md)). So it excludes neither. *Only the norm consequence of the diagonal equation is tested here, not its solvability.*
- **The four-set value those caps allow** ([screen_I121_capped_fourset.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_capped_fourset.py), maximising $P(B_f)=9\sum JJJ+24\sum XXX$ at unit mass under $\max_ME(M)\le c/n$, local ascent, so lower bounds): $0.53$, $0.75$, $0.81$ at $c=\tfrac12$ and $n=8,10,12$, and $0.83$, $0.84$, $0.85$ at $c=2$, against the four-set values $0.833$, $0.949$, $1.033$ that unconstrained ascent reached in the [first sweep](2026-09-28b-LIT-step8-sweep.md) and $\theta_0=1.146$, $1.200$, $1.236$. *At the $c=\tfrac12$ maximisers $9\sum JJJ=0.133$ and $24\sum XXX=0.392$ at $n=8$, $0.132$ and $0.622$ at $n=10$* ([r1851](REVIEW_r1851.md), [r1852](REVIEW_r1852.md), reproduced here at $n=8$), the visible part the larger, and at $c=2$ $0.083$ and $0.750$ ($n=8$), $0.092$ and $0.746$ ($n=10$). *In these ascents the cap lowers the reachable four-set value by about a fifth at $n=12$; whether a bound follows is open.*
- **What is left unused** is the block part of criticality, $\theta\nu_Q=[Q(W)]_{\chi_Q}$ for every four-set, and frame maximality beyond the matching family.

## Large $E$ and the maximal frame (screens)

*The candidate the reduction points to is frame-free: $t_{\max}(W)^2=\max_g\lVert\pi_{\mathcal D}(gW)\rVert^2$ and $E(W)$ are both $O(n)$-invariant, and the node follows if $E(W)\ge\varepsilon_n$ at a critical point forces $t_{\max}(W)^2\ge\tau$.* **Calibration**: the balanced product has $E\approx\theta_0$ at $t_{\max}=1$, a normalised sum of $k$ products in generic frames has $E\approx\theta_0/\sqrt k$ at $t_{\max}\gtrsim1/\sqrt k$ (the cross terms being small, which needs $k\ll\dim\mathcal W_n$), and $\mathbb{CP}^m$ has $E\to0$ at $t_{\max}^2=\tfrac3{n+2}$, so the scaling the data suggest is $E\lesssim C\,t_{\max}$.

## The frame-free target on general tensors (a screen)

*If $E(W)\le C\,t_{\max}(W)$ held for every unit Weyl $W$, criticality would not be needed, and at a maximiser $E\ge\varepsilon_n$ would give $t_{\max}\ge\varepsilon_n/C$, a constant for $C$ of order $\theta_0$. ## $E$ is the second-kind cubic, and $E\le\tfrac32\sqrt{2(n-2)/(n-1)}\,t_{\max}$ on the whole unit sphere (derived)

*The frame-free target of the previous section holds for every Weyl tensor, critical or not, with a constant uniform in $n$. The frame it needs is the eigenframe of the bottom eigenvector of $\mathring W$.*

- **$E=-\tfrac12p_3(\omega)$.** With $\omega=\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(\mathring W)$ on $S^2_0$ in Li's $iklj$ order ([conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md)), [L50](../findings/bispectral.md) reads $P=\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3-\tfrac12p_3(\omega)$ on all of $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, so $E=P-\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=-\tfrac12p_3(\omega)$. *Agreement with the record: this note's $E$ is L50's second-kind term, written in another notation.* Independently, $I_3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}_{M_n}(\mathring W^3)$ by relabelling indices, and the skew half of that trace is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3$ by first Bianchi, which reproduces the identity.
- **Trace positivity: agreement with the record's one-line bound** $\theta\le\tfrac32(M_++m)$ in [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) (r290), whose $m$-half is this step. Let $\mu=-\min\omega\ge0$ (as $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathring W=0$). Then $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathring W^3+\mu\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathring W^2=\sum_s\langle(\mathring W+\mu)\mathring Ws,\mathring Ws\rangle\ge0$ over an orthonormal basis $s$ of $S^2$, so with $\sum\omega^2=3\lVert W\rVert^2$, $\ E\le\tfrac32\mu\lVert W\rVert^2$.
- **The bottom of $\mathring W$ is a frame quantity: agreement with the record.** For symmetric $h$, $-\langle\mathring Wh,h\rangle=\sum W_{abcd}h_{ac}h_{bd}$, the pairing of $W$ with $\Lambda^2h$. In the eigenframe $F$ of $h$, with eigenvalues $e$, this is $e^{\mathsf T}K_Fe$ with $K_F$ the hollow sectional matrix of $F$, and [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md) already carries $\mu=v^{\mathsf T}K^Fv\le\mu_1(K^F)\le\sqrt{t_F(n-2)/(n-1)}$ frame by frame, with $t_F=\lVert K^F\rVert_F^2$ (Gate 2 [r555](REVIEW_r555.md), [r556](REVIEW_r556.md)), where it is used with $t_F\le2$ for the absolute cap $\tfrac{n-1}n\mu\le\theta_0(n)$. **Read with $t_F\le\kappa^2=2t_{\max}^2$ instead** ([L94](../findings/bispectral.md)), it gives $\mu\le\sqrt{2(n-2)/(n-1)}\;t_{\max}$. Both constants are typed: the sharp one in [SecondKindSharp.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/SecondKindSharp.lean), spending $K_F\mathbf1=0$ and $\sum e=0$ through a Cauchy–Schwarz against $ee^{\mathsf T}-c(I-J/n)$, and the hollow-only $\sqrt{2(n-1)/n}$ in SecondKindE.lean, through the off-diagonal part of $e\otimes e$. They differ by $0.3\%$ at $n=14$ ([r1865](REVIEW_r1865.md)).
- **So, for every $W\in\mathrm{Weyl}_n$,** $$E(W)\le\tfrac32\sqrt{\tfrac{2(n-2)}{n-1}}\;t_{\max}(W)\,\lVert W\rVert^2\le\tfrac32\sqrt{\tfrac{2(n-1)}n}\;t_{\max}(W)\,\lVert W\rVert^2\le\tfrac3{\sqrt2}\,t_{\max}(W)\,\lVert W\rVert^2,$$ both typed, the first in [SecondKindSharp.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/SecondKindSharp.lean) (`E_le_tmax_sharp`, spending the zero row sums of every frame's sectional matrix at a Ricci-flat operator and the trace-free reduction of $h$) and the second in SecondKindE.lean. **Every step is on the record, and what is new is their pairing at $t_{\max}$**: the node's $E$ recognised as L50's second-kind term, the one-line bound's $m$-half as a bound on it, and the frame cap read with $t_F\le2t_{\max}^2$ where the record read it with $t_F\le2$ ([r1861](REVIEW_r1861.md)). Against the calibration, the balanced product has $E/t_{\max}$ close to $\theta_0$, tending to $\sqrt2$, and the constant is $2.12$. The loss is the trace-positivity step: at the product one eigenvalue $-\mu$ carries $\mu^2\approx2$ of $\sum\omega^2=3$ and $E\approx\tfrac12\mu^3$.
- **At the node.** At a unit critical point with $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$, $\theta^2\le\tfrac32+\theta E$ ([r1849](REVIEW_r1849.md), [r1850](REVIEW_r1850.md), `crit_E_bound`) gives $E\ge\varepsilon_n$, hence $$t_{\max}^2\ \ge\ \frac29\cdot\frac{n-1}{n-2}\,\varepsilon_n^2\ \ge\ \frac29\cdot\frac n{n-1}\,\varepsilon_n^2,$$ the first with the sharp constant and the second as `t_ge_of_crit` is applied in SecondKindBalanced.lean, which is $1.22\times10^{-4}$ at $n=12$, $1.27\times10^{-3}$ at $n=14$, $2.86\times10^{-3}$ at $n=16$, and tends to $\tfrac1{36}$. **No critical point at or above $\theta_0$ has maximal-frame mass below this**, a constant uniform in $n$ where the single-rotation and matching constraints gave $n^{-1/2}$. ## The $\Lambda^2$ loss is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^2\#W^2)$, and its index-concentration bound fails on Weyl tensors from $n=5$ (Gate 2 [r1871](REVIEW_r1871.md), [r1872](REVIEW_r1872.md))

*The $E$ route's first loss is $\theta p_3(\lambda)\le1$. Weighting [L198](../findings/bounds.md)'s rows instead of capping them lands on the record's $S_{22}$ identity, and the step from there to a bound by the index concentration $\lVert N\rVert_F$ is false on Weyl tensors from $n=5$.*

- **The weighted rows are the record's $S_{22}$ identity** (Gate 2 [r1868](REVIEW_r1868.md), [r1869](REVIEW_r1869.md), both CONFIRMED). So the AM–GM drop $\tfrac12S\le\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^2\#W^2)$ loses exactly $\tfrac14G_2\ge0$, and it is [L199](../findings/bounds.md)'s $\theta\lambda_k\le\lambda_k^2+\tfrac12R_k$ weighted by $\lambda_k^2$. ## The pair part of $E$, exact

*The reviewers listed the pair part as the piece of $E$ the pair-free formula leaves out. By characters it enters through four couplings, and unlike the $J$'s, it does not cancel.* For a pair block $\nu\in V_{jk}$, $\nu=\sum_ax_a\,e_{ja}\odot e_{ka}$ with $\sum x=0$ ([L222](../findings/second-order.md)):

- **With the diagonal, exact.** From L222's $\nu\circ\nu=G_x$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A\nu^2)=\sum_ax_a^2(a_{ja}+a_{ka})$, each block adds $-\tfrac32\sum_ax_a^2(a_{ja}+a_{ka})-3\lVert x\rVert^2a_{jk}+6\sum_{a<b}x_ax_ba_{ab}$ to $E$.
- **A triangle of pair blocks**, $x\in V_{01}$, $y\in V_{12}$, $z\in V_{02}$: the polarised $E$ is $-\tfrac12x_2y_0z_1-(x_2\langle y,z\rangle+y_0\langle x,z\rangle+z_1\langle x,y\rangle)-\tfrac12\sum_ax_ay_az_a$, sums over the outer indices $a\ge3$. *The trace half by hand: the outer cycles $e_{0a}\to e_{1a}\to e_{2a}$ give $\sum xyz$ and the cycle through $e_{02},e_{12},e_{01}$ gives $-x_2y_0z_1$, so the mixed $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$ is $6\sum xyz-6x_2y_0z_1$.*
- **Two disjoint pair blocks with their four-set**, $x\in V_{01}$, $y\in V_{23}$ and $(J,X)$ on $\{0,1,2,3\}$ at the pairing $(01|23)$: $2\langle x,y\rangle X-\tfrac12(x_2+x_3)(y_0+y_1)X-\tfrac34(x_2-x_3)(y_0-y_1)J$, the inner product over the outer indices $a\ge4$.
- **A pair block with two four-sets sharing three indices**, $x\in V_{01}$ with $(J_1,X_1)$ on $\{0,2,3,4\}$ at $(02|34)$ and $(J_2,X_2)$ on $\{1,2,3,4\}$ at $(12|34)$: $-2x_2X_1X_2-\tfrac12(x_3+x_4)X_1X_2-\tfrac98(x_3+x_4)J_1J_2+\tfrac34(x_3-x_4)(J_1X_2+X_1J_2)$.
- **Derivation.** $E=\tfrac12I_3-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$ with $I_3=\sum R_{ijkl}R_{ipkq}R_{jplq}$, so each coupling is $\tfrac1{12}$ of the mixed $I_3$ less the mixed $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$, a finite index sum on the coupling's support. **Expanded symbolically with every component a free symbol**, the three sums give exactly the three forms above ([derive_I121_pair_terms_symbolic.py](../scripts/misc/derive_I121_pair_terms_symbolic.py)), which the random evaluations had fitted ([check_I121_pair_terms.py](../scripts/misc/check_I121_pair_terms.py)). The outer indices enter only through the inner products, so the forms hold at every $n$. *The sign patterns are those forced by the transpositions inside a four-set, which flip $J$ and fix $X$.* **Unlike the pair-free part, the invisible $J$'s survive here**, coupled to the pair part's inner components.

## Next: the range above the uniform threshold

- **The block equations are closed**: summed isotropically they are $\tfrac32$ times Euler, and per matching they give nothing past the matching cap ([2026-09-28e sweep](2026-09-28e-LIT-step8-sweep.md)).
- **The frame-free bound appears to have the right order in $t_{\max}$** (a calibration, unproved). The normalised sums of $k$ products in generic frames have $E\approx\theta_0/\sqrt k$ with $t_{\max}\gtrsim1/\sqrt k$; if $t_{\max}$ is also of order $1/\sqrt k$ there, $E/t_{\max}$ stays of order $\theta_0$ as $t_{\max}\to0$, no bound of higher order in $t_{\max}$ holds on the unit sphere, and frame-free the constant can move only from $2.12$ toward $\theta_0$. The balanced product alone fixes that floor, since there $E/t_{\max}\to\theta_0$ at $t_{\max}=1$.
