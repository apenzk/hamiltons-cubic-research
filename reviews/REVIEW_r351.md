# Review r351, Gate 2 on the pair block's closed form

**K1, REFUTED as stated. The sharp part is twice what the claim says.** The correct block, proved below from the structure constants and screened bit-exact, is $L[i,i]=\tfrac12(M_{ji}+M_{ki})-\tfrac12M_{jk}$ and $L[i,m]=\tfrac12M_{im}$ in the $\sigma$-oriented basis, i.e. $L=\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(M_{ji}+M_{ki})-\tfrac12M_{jk}\mathrm{Id}+\tfrac12M^{(jk)}$. The shape, the $\sigma_i$ orientation and the identification of $M^{(jk)}$ are all correct; only the constant on the $M\#$ half is wrong, $\tfrac12$ where [DERIVE-df1.md](DERIVE-df1.md) has $\tfrac14$.

**K2, CONFIRMED for the corrected block, and it REFUTES K1.** The orbit direction $x_i=M_{ki}-M_{ji}$ is an exact $\theta/2$-eigenvector of the corrected block at every diagonal critical point, proved in closed form. Under K1's constants the same computation returns $(Lx)_i=\big[\tfrac\theta4+\tfrac{M_{ij}+M_{ik}}4\big]x_i$, which is not a multiple of $x$ unless $M_{ij}+M_{ik}$ is independent of $i$.

**K3, CONFIRMED for the corrected block, and it REFUTES K1.** At $S^p\times S^q$ the within-factor pair block of the corrected form has spectrum $\{\tfrac{p-2}{2(p-1)},\,0^{(p-3)},\,\delta^{(q-1)}\}$ with $\delta<0$, top eigenvalue exactly $(p-2)/(2(p-1))$ for $L_e$. K1's constants give $0.2276$ at $p=q=4$ where the target is $\tfrac13$, and $(p-2)/(2(p-1))$ is not in their spectrum at all.

**K4, CONFIRMED, with the corrected constants, and it is the point where K1 breaks structurally.** The criterion on a pair block is a spectral condition on $M$ and on nothing else; the Ricci cut is compatible with reading the block this way precisely because the corrected block has constant row sums $-M_{jk}/2$, so $\mathbf 1$ is an eigenvector and $\mathbf 1^\perp$ is invariant. K1's form has row sums $\tfrac14(M_{ji}+M_{ki}-M_{jk})$, which depend on $i$: it does **not** preserve the Ricci cut, and therefore contradicts [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) directly, independently of any check against a target value.

**K5, the closed form needs diagonality only.** Criticality is used nowhere in K1, $\theta>0$ nowhere; Ricci-flatness is used only for the Ricci-cut statement, criticality only for K2, and $\theta>0$ only for the direction of [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s inequality. Where the orbit direction vanishes (every within-factor block at a sphere product), the criterion binds on the whole $(n-3)$-dimensional block.

## The derivation

**Conventions, quoted.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) fixes $X\circ Y=\tfrac12(XY+YX)+X\# Y$ and $L_M=M\circ(\cdot)$; [Q_full](../scripts/lib/adversarial_check1.py) fixes $(W^\#)_{\alpha\beta}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha WA_\beta W)$ with $A_\alpha=\mathrm{ad}(u_\alpha)$ on $\Lambda^2$, in the orthonormal coordinate-plane basis $u_{ab}=e_a\wedge e_b\leftrightarrow E_{ab}-E_{ba}$ with $u_{ba}=-u_{ab}$. The one bracket needed is $[u_{ab},u_{bc}]=u_{ac}$ for distinct $a,b,c$, everything else following from antisymmetry; a diagonal $M$ has $Mu_{ab}=M_{ab}u_{ab}$.

**Step 0, the two polarisation traces are equal, and this is where the factor of two was lost.** The polarised sharp is $(M\#X)_{\alpha\beta}=-\tfrac14[\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha MA_\beta X)+\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha XA_\beta M)]$, and for symmetric $M,X$ and skew $A$ the second trace equals the first: transposing, $\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha XA_\beta M)=\operatorname{tr}(M^{\mathsf T}A_\beta^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}A_\alpha^{\mathsf T})=\operatorname{tr}(MA_\beta XA_\alpha)=\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha MA_\beta X)$. Hence $(M\# X)_{\alpha\beta}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha MA_\beta X)$. **Carrying the $-\tfrac14$ onto a single trace halves the $M\#$ half of $L_M$ and leaves the symmetrised half untouched, which is exactly the discrepancy between K1 and the block below.** The same reduction reproduces the trusted diagonal formula $(Q(W))_{ij,ij}=\lambda_{ij}^2+\sum_k\lambda_{ik}\lambda_{jk}$, so the normalisation is anchored.

**Step 1, $M\#$ on the pair block, in operator form.** Write $f_m=u_{jm}\odot u_{km}=u_{jm}u_{km}^{\mathsf T}+u_{km}u_{jm}^{\mathsf T}$ for $m\notin\{j,k\}$; this is the $\sigma$-oriented basis, $e_{jm}$ meaning $e_j\wedge e_m$ whichever of $j,m$ is smaller. Then $\operatorname{tr}(A_\alpha MA_\beta f_m)=u_{km}^{\mathsf T}A_\alpha MA_\beta u_{jm}+u_{jm}^{\mathsf T}A_\alpha MA_\beta u_{km}$, and using $A_\alpha z=\mathrm{ad}(u_\alpha)z=-\mathrm{ad}(z)u_\alpha$ (so that $\alpha\mapsto A_\alpha z$ is the matrix $-\mathrm{ad}(z)$ read column by column), each term becomes a matrix product: $\langle A_\alpha u_{km},MA_\beta u_{jm}\rangle=(a_k^{\mathsf T}Ma_j)_{\alpha\beta}=-(a_kMa_j)_{\alpha\beta}$ with $a_j=\mathrm{ad}(u_{jm})$, $a_k=\mathrm{ad}(u_{km})$. Collecting,
$$M\# f_m=-\tfrac12\big(a_kMa_j+a_jMa_k\big).$$

**Step 2, evaluate the two triple products on the basis.** $a_j=\mathrm{ad}(u_{jm})$ sends $u_{mb}\mapsto u_{jb}$ and $u_{jb}\mapsto-u_{mb}$ for $b\notin\{j,m\}$ and kills everything else; $a_k$ likewise with $k$ for $j$. Chasing every basis vector: $a_jMa_k$ sends $u_{jm}\mapsto M_{jk}u_{km}$ and $u_{kb}\mapsto-M_{mb}u_{jb}$ for $b\notin\{j,k,m\}$, and is zero elsewhere; $a_kMa_j$ is its image under $j\leftrightarrow k$, sending $u_{km}\mapsto M_{jk}u_{jm}$ and $u_{jb}\mapsto-M_{mb}u_{kb}$. So $M\#f_m$ sends $u_{jm}\mapsto-\tfrac12M_{jk}u_{km}$ and $u_{km}\mapsto-\tfrac12M_{jk}u_{jm}$, and $u_{kb}\mapsto\tfrac12M_{mb}u_{jb}$, $u_{jb}\mapsto\tfrac12M_{mb}u_{kb}$. Since $f_b$ is precisely the operator sending $u_{kb}\mapsto u_{jb}$ and $u_{jb}\mapsto u_{kb}$ and killing the rest,
$$M\# f_m=-\tfrac12M_{jk}\,f_m+\tfrac12\sum_{b\notin\{j,k,m\}}M_{mb}\,f_b .$$

**Step 3, the block.** The symmetrised half is diagonal, $\tfrac12(Mf_m+f_mM)=\tfrac12(M_{jm}+M_{km})f_m$, which is the part [DERIVE-df1.md](DERIVE-df1.md) already had by hand. Adding,
$$\boxed{\;L_M f_m=\Big[\tfrac{M_{jm}+M_{km}}2-\tfrac{M_{jk}}2\Big]f_m+\tfrac12\sum_{b\notin\{j,k,m\}}M_{mb}f_b\;}$$
that is, $L=\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(M_{ji}+M_{ki})-\tfrac12M_{jk}\mathrm{Id}+\tfrac12M^{(jk)}$ in the $\sigma$-oriented basis, and $L[i,m]=\sigma_i\sigma_m\tfrac12M_{im}$ when re-expressed in the ordered basis $\tilde f_i=\sigma_i f_i$. **Note that the two displayed forms in K1 cannot both hold in the same basis**: the $\sigma_i\sigma_m$ version and the bare $M^{(jk)}$ version are conjugate by $\operatorname{diag}(\sigma)$, so they are two readings of one matrix, and the brief attaches both to the basis $f_i$. Nothing turns on it (conjugation by a sign matrix preserves the spectrum) but the statement should name which basis carries the signs.

**The derivation shows en passant that the span of $\{f_i\}$ is $L_M$-invariant for every diagonal $M$**, critical or not, Weyl or not: that is the support argument [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) attributes to [r345](REVIEW_r345.md), and it is reproduced here independently.

## K2, the orbit direction, proved

The orbit direction of the $(j,k)$ rotation has coordinates $x_i=M_{ki}-M_{ji}$ in the $\sigma$-oriented basis ([DERIVE-df1.md](DERIVE-df1.md), step 4(ii), which I confirm). Criticality is $Q(M)=\theta M$, whose $(i,k)$ entry is $M_{ik}^2+\sum_{m\ne i,k}M_{im}M_{km}=\theta M_{ik}$, so $\sum_{m\notin\{i,j,k\}}M_{im}M_{km}=\theta M_{ik}-M_{ik}^2-M_{ij}M_{jk}$ and likewise with $j$ for $k$. Subtracting,
$$\sum_{m\notin\{i,j,k\}}M_{im}x_m=(M_{ik}-M_{ij})\big[\theta-M_{ij}-M_{ik}+M_{jk}\big]=x_i\big[\theta-M_{ij}-M_{ik}+M_{jk}\big].$$
Hence $(Lx)_i=\big[\tfrac{M_{ij}+M_{ik}}2-\tfrac{M_{jk}}2\big]x_i+\tfrac12x_i\big[\theta-M_{ij}-M_{ik}+M_{jk}\big]=\tfrac\theta2x_i$, **an exact $\theta/2$-eigenvector, with the $M$-dependent terms cancelling identically.** This is the sharpest available test of the constants and the corrected form passes it identically in $M$; K1's constants leave the residue $\tfrac14(M_{ij}+M_{ik})$ uncancelled. The orbit direction also satisfies $\sum_ix_i=0$ by Ricci-flatness, so it lies inside the Weyl block and not merely inside the $(n-2)$-dimensional span.

## K3, the sphere product, proved

Take $S^p\times S^q$ with sectional curvatures $a=1/(p-1)$ inside the first factor, $b=1/(q-1)$ inside the second and $0$ mixed, so $\mathrm{Ric}=g$ and the Weyl sectional matrix is $M_{ab}=K_{ab}-\tfrac1{n-1}$: within-factor entries $A_1=\tfrac q{(p-1)(n-1)}$ and $A_2=\tfrac p{(q-1)(n-1)}$, mixed entry $C=-\tfrac1{n-1}$. This is Ricci-flat as a sectional matrix and has $\theta=1$, since the mixed entry of $Q(M)$ is $C^2+C\big[(p-1)A_1+(q-1)A_2\big]=C^2+C\tfrac n{n-1}=C$; $\theta$ and $L_e=L_M/\theta$ are scale-invariant, so no normalisation of $\lVert M\rVert$ is needed.

For $j,k$ in the first factor the corrected block splits the remaining indices into $p-2$ of type 1 and $q$ of type 2. Its type-1 diagonal is $\tfrac12A_1$, equal to its type-1 off-diagonal, so the block is $\tfrac12A_1J$ there; its type-2 diagonal is $C-\tfrac12A_1$ with off-diagonal $\tfrac12A_2$; the coupling is $\tfrac12C$. That gives $0$ with multiplicity $p-3$ on type-1 mean-zero vectors, $\delta=C-\tfrac12A_1-\tfrac12A_2<0$ with multiplicity $q-1$ on type-2 mean-zero vectors, and a $2\times2$ block on the two mean directions. Writing $\nu=1/(n-1)$ and $\lambda_*=\tfrac{p-2}{2(p-1)}$, one computes $T_{11}-\lambda_*=-\tfrac{\nu(p-2)}2$ and $T_{22}-\lambda_*=-\tfrac{\nu q}2$, whose product is $\tfrac{\nu^2q(p-2)}4=T_{12}T_{21}$, so $\det(T-\lambda_*)=0$ and **$\lambda_*$ is an eigenvalue exactly**. The second root of the $2\times2$ is $-\tfrac{\nu q}{2(p-1)}=-\tfrac12A_1=-\tfrac12M_{jk}$, which is the $\mathbf 1$-eigenvalue the Ricci cut removes. The Weyl block therefore has spectrum $\{\lambda_*,0^{(p-3)},\delta^{(q-1)}\}$, of dimension $1+(p-3)+(q-1)=n-3$ as [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md) requires, with top eigenvalue $(p-2)/(2(p-1))$, **the value [r345](REVIEW_r345.md) and [r346](REVIEW_r346.md) located there, recovered exactly.**

**And these blocks carry no orbit direction**, since $M_{ji}=M_{ki}$ for every $i$ when $j,k$ lie in the same factor, so $x=0$: the within-factor value is genuinely off-orbit, which is consistent with [DERIVE-df1.md](DERIVE-df1.md)'s count of six orbit-free pair blocks at $S^3\times S^3$, namely $\binom32+\binom32$.

## K4, the consequence, restated with the right constants

**The Ricci cut is compatible with reading the block on $M$ alone, and the reason is a row-sum identity that only the corrected form satisfies.** With $M$ Ricci-flat, $\sum_{m\notin\{i,j,k\}}M_{im}=-M_{ij}-M_{ik}$, so the corrected block's row sums are $\tfrac{M_{ji}+M_{ki}}2-\tfrac{M_{jk}}2-\tfrac12(M_{ij}+M_{ik})=-\tfrac{M_{jk}}2$, constant in $i$. Hence $L\mathbf 1=-\tfrac12M_{jk}\mathbf 1$ and, $L$ being symmetric, $\mathbf 1^\perp$, which is exactly the Weyl block, since the only Ricci component a pair-block element carries is $\mathrm{Ric}_{jk}\propto\sum_ix_i$ and first Bianchi is empty on three indices, is invariant. **K1's form fails this**, and the failure is not a matter of degree: it means K1's matrix does not restrict to $\mathcal W_n$ at all, contradicting the block-diagonality of [Lemma L120](../findings/second-order.md), which is an indexed result and takes precedence.

**The criterion.** Put $N^{(jk)}=M^{(jk)}+\operatorname{diag}(M_{ji}+M_{ki})$, an $(n-2)\times(n-2)$ matrix built from $M$ and nothing else; Ricci-flatness gives $N^{(jk)}\mathbf 1=0$. Then $L_M$ on the pair block is $\tfrac12\big(N^{(jk)}-M_{jk}\mathrm{Id}\big)$, and [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md) reads, at every maximiser and every pair $\{j,k\}$,
$$\lambda_{\max}\big(N^{(jk)}\big|_{\mathbf 1^\perp}\big)\ \le\ \theta+M_{jk}.$$
At $S^p\times S^q$ this is $\tfrac{p-2}{p-1}+\tfrac q{(p-1)(n-1)}\le1+\tfrac q{(p-1)(n-1)}$, slack $\tfrac1{p-1}$; at $p=q=4$, $\tfrac67\le\tfrac{25}{21}$. **So K4's conclusion stands (the pair-block criterion needs nothing beyond the sectional matrix) but with the corrected constants it is twice as strong a condition as K1 would have given**, which matters if it is to be used as a necessary condition on a hypothetical maximiser. It applies on the whole block, including the orbit direction, where it holds with equality; the binding content is on the orthogonal complement of the orbit direction inside the block, of dimension $n-4$ where that direction is nonzero and $n-3$ where it vanishes.

## K5, scope

**The closed form needs diagonality and nothing else.** Steps 1–3 use only $Mu_{ab}=M_{ab}u_{ab}$ and the brackets; they never use $Q(M)=\theta M$, never use $\sum_bM_{ab}=0$, and never use $\theta>0$. So the block formula holds for every symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2$ that is diagonal in coordinate 2-planes, in particular the $(n-2)$-dimensional span of the $f_i$ is $L_M$-invariant for such $M$ whether or not $M$ is a Weyl operator. Ricci-flatness enters only at K4, to make $\mathbf 1$ an eigenvector and the Ricci-cut subspace invariant; without it the row sums are $\tfrac12(\rho_i-M_{jk})$ with $\rho_i=\sum_mM_{im}$, and $\mathbf 1$ is an eigenvector exactly when the $\rho_i$ are equal. Criticality enters only at K2. $\theta>0$ enters only through [Lemma L24](../findings/second-order.md), whose inequality reverses at $\theta<0$; the block itself is indifferent to the sign. **Where a pair block's orbit direction vanishes**, which is $M_{ji}=M_{ki}$ for all $i$ and holds for every within-factor block at a sphere product, the block simply contains no forced $\tfrac12$-eigenvector and the criterion is a genuine constraint on all $n-3$ of its directions.
