**1. Identity $\langle W^\#,W\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^3)$, CONFIRMED, including the combinatorics of the $\tfrac12$ factor.** Symbolic `sympy` proof (fully expanded, zero difference) at $n=4,5,6$; numeric agreement to $10^{-14}$ at $n=8,12$ with random $\lambda_{ij}$. Independently re-derived the combinatorics by hand: for a fixed triple $\{a,b,c\}$, the double sum $\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{ij}\sum_{k\ne i,j}\lambda_{ik}\lambda_{jk}$ picks up the product $\lambda_{ab}\lambda_{ac}\lambda_{bc}$ exactly **3** times (once per choice of which pair is $(i,j)$), while $\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^3)=\sum_{i,j,k}\Lambda_{ij}\Lambda_{jk}\Lambda_{ki}$ picks up the same product **6** times (once per permutation of $\{a,b,c\}$, all equal since $\Lambda$ is symmetric). So $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^3)=3\times(\text{triangle sum})=\text{double sum}$ exactly, no off-by-factor-of-2 error, checked both symbolically and by explicit triangle-counting. **CONFIRMED.**

**2. Ricci-flatness $\Rightarrow\Lambda\mathbf1=0$, CONFIRMED, sign and formula re-derived independently.** By hand: for $R$ diagonal in coordinate 2-planes, the standard contraction $\operatorname{Ric}_{jl}=\sum_i R_{ijil}$ is nonzero only when $\{i,j\}=\{i,l\}$ forces $j=l$ (for $j,l\ne i$), giving $\operatorname{Ric}_{jj}=\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_{ij}=$ row sum of $\Lambda$ at row $j$, with all off-diagonal Ricci entries exactly zero. Verified this hand formula against the trusted `ricci_from_mop` machinery on random diagonal $W$ (max error $4\times10^{-16}$, $n=6,8$), and verified with **exact `Fraction` arithmetic** that the sphere-product $\Lambda$ (built independently from the $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ formula) has row sums exactly zero (not just numerically small) at $(p,q)=(3,3),(4,4),(2,6),(5,7)$. **CONFIRMED**, no sign error, no missing factor.

**3. Refined bound re-derivation, CONFIRMED, including the norm relation the task specifically flagged as unverified.** Re-derived by hand from Huisken/Lemma 2.10 applied with $m=n-1$ to $\Lambda|_{\mathbf1^\perp}$ (which equals $\Lambda$ exactly in norm, since $\mathbf1$ is an exact $0$-eigenvector, no restriction loss). The step FINDINGS.md's own text does not spell out (relating $\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Frob}}$ to $\|W\|$), was worked out and checked explicitly: $\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Frob}}^2=2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{ij}^2=2\|W\|^2$ (off-diagonal entries of the symmetric $n\times n$ $\Lambda$ counted twice; $\|W\|^2$ counts each coordinate-plane pair once), so $\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Frob}}=\sqrt2\|W\|$, confirmed numerically to `ratio=2.000000` exactly at $(p,q)=(4,4),(5,6)$. Substituting: $\langle W^\#,W\rangle\le\tfrac12\cdot\frac{n-3}{\sqrt{(n-1)(n-2)}}\cdot(\sqrt2\|W\|)^3=\sqrt2\cdot\frac{n-3}{\sqrt{(n-1)(n-2)}}\|W\|^3$, **matches the claimed bound exactly**, and the equality-eigenvalue-profile formula was likewise re-derived and matches. **CONFIRMED.**

**4. Exact saturation at even $n$, near-miss at odd $n$, CONFIRMED, fresh construction.** Rebuilt the sphere-product $\Lambda$ independently (own code, own $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ formula) for $n=6,\dots,20$: ratio true/bound $=1.000000$ at every even $n$ (6 through 20), and the odd-$n$ profile matches exactly, $n=7$ gives ratio $0.9546$ on the nose, monotonically climbing to $0.9988$ by $n=19$, matching FINDINGS.md's stated numbers digit-for-digit. At $n=100$, the eigenvalue spectrum matches Huisken's extremal profile essentially to machine precision ($\mu=1.010101$ vs. computed top eigenvalue $1.010101$, diff $4\times10^{-16}$; $\nu=-0.010307$ matching the $98$ negative eigenvalues exactly; one exact zero on the $\mathbf1$-direction). **CONFIRMED**, not cherry-picked, the odd-$n$ near-miss is real and reproducible from scratch.

**5. "Not frame-invariant" claim (the crux), PLAUSIBLE, genuinely investigated, no fix found.** Spent real effort searching for a correctly-transforming reformulation before accepting FINDINGS.md's characterization. The natural candidate, a frame-covariant, $O(n)$-equivariant single linear contraction of $W$ analogous to $\Lambda$, namely $M_{ij}:=\sum_k W(e_i\wedge e_k,e_j\wedge e_k)$, was worked out by hand and confirmed numerically to collapse to $\operatorname{Ric}$ (zero content beyond what Ricci-flatness already fixes), not to $\Lambda$, because on the coordinate-diagonal locus $W(e_i\wedge e_k,e_j\wedge e_k)$ is nonzero only for $i=j$. The underlying reason is structural, not a failure of cleverness: $\Lambda_{ij}=W(e_i\wedge e_j,e_i\wedge e_j)$ is literally the value of $W$'s "sectional-curvature"-type function on the *specific* 2-plane $e_i\wedge e_j$, a point evaluation of a function on the Grassmannian of 2-planes, not a tensorial contraction of $W$ with itself, and no linear single-contraction of an operator on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ can recover a point-evaluation without picking a plane (i.e. a frame). This is independent, genuine reasoning, not a restatement of FINDINGS.md's rotation stress test, it explains *why* the natural fix fails rather than just observing that one specific fix (reading $\Lambda$ off a rotated frame) fails. No counter-construction was found. Labeled PLAUSIBLE rather than CONFIRMED because a non-existence claim of this kind cannot be exhaustively verified by one reviewer's search; FINDINGS.md's own honest framing ("not yet a proof... natural next step is either a frame-invariant reformulation... or [the lifting lemma]") is not contradicted by anything found here, and should stay as-is.

**6. Off-diagonal stress tests, CONFIRMED on rebuild, with a genuine bug caught and fixed en route (worth noting as a methodology point).** Rebuilt a $\mathbb{CP}^2$-type Weyl tensor from scratch via the standard constant-holomorphic-sectional-curvature tensor formula, independent of `cp2_tensor.py`. The first attempt (naive sign on the cross term, $+2\,J_iJ_j\!\cdot\!J_kJ_l$-type term) **violated the first Bianchi identity by a residual of exactly $4.0$**, i.e. was not a valid algebraic curvature tensor at all, and would have silently produced a meaningless $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ value had it not been checked. Flipping the sign of that one term gives Bianchi residual $0$ to machine precision, exact pair-antisymmetry, and exact pair-swap symmetry; this is exactly the kind of curvature-tensor sign-convention trap Reviewer 3 flagged for the Ishihara/Besse QK formula elsewhere in this review, caught here *before* trusting a result rather than after. With the corrected tensor: true $\langle W^\#,W\rangle=-0.597$ at $n=8$, safely below the refined bound $1.091$. Separately rebuilt 5 fully random genuine (Bianchi-closed, Ricci-flat) Weyl tensors at $n=6$ via the validated Bianchi/Ricci nullspace scaffolding (confirmed $\dim(\mathcal W_6)=84$ against the formula $(n+2)(n+1)n(n-3)/12$ before trusting the basis): all 5 trials give $|\langle W^\#,W\rangle|\in[0.002,0.151]$, comfortably below the bound $0.9487$. **CONFIRMED** in both rebuilt cases, the bound held.

**7. Internal consistency / does this suffice for Conjecture A, accurately described by FINDINGS.md, with one added quantitative caveat worth folding in.** FINDINGS.md's "Open gaps" section correctly lists three separate, non-overlapping open items (global maximality against other critical-point families; the $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ bound off-diagonal; the diagonal-lifting lemma) and does not claim that closing the $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ gap alone would complete the proof. This review adds a concrete numeric point strengthening that caution: even a fully general, frame-invariant proof of the refined $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ bound, **naively combined** with the already-sharp Huisken bound on $\operatorname{tr}(W^3)$ (applied directly to $W$ as an $N\times N$ operator, $N=\binom n2$), *overshoots* the true conjectured maximum $P(W^*)/\|W^*\|^3$ by a factor of $\approx1.7$–$1.8$ at every $n$ tested ($6\le n\le100$; e.g. at $n=8$: bound sum $0.9456+1.0911=2.0367$ vs. true max $1.1456$, factor $1.778$). This happens because the two sharp bounds are achieved by mutually incompatible eigenvalue profiles (Huisken's $\operatorname{tr}(W^3)$ extremizer is a single dominant eigenvalue; the $\Lambda$-vertex extremizer realized by the sphere product is nearly rank-deficient in a different pattern), so **a future proof would need a genuine joint/trade-off inequality on the sum, not just two separately-sharp bounds plugged in side by side**, closing the $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ gap in isolation, even if fully achieved, would not by itself yield a proof of Conjecture A without this additional coupling argument. This is consistent with, not contradictory to, FINDINGS.md's existing hedges, but is not currently spelled out anywhere in the document and is worth adding explicitly to the "Open gaps" section as a fourth, distinct point (the joint-bound/trade-off requirement), since a reader could otherwise mistake "the $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ gap" for the *only* remaining analytic obstacle to a Tachibana-trick-style proof.
