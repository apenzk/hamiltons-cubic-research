## 2. The forcing argument, it closes, and here is the whole of it

**It closes, and in two generators rather than "finitely many".**

1. **Harish-Chandra.** $\mathrm{HC}:Z(U(\mathfrak g))\to S(\mathfrak h)^W$ is an algebra isomorphism after the $\rho$-shift, and $z\in Z$ acts on $V_\lambda$ by the scalar $\mathrm{HC}(z)(\ell)$, $\ell=\lambda+\rho$. So the values of *all* central elements on $V_\lambda$ are exactly the values of *all* $W$-invariant polynomials at $\ell$.
2. **$W(B_2)$-invariants.** $W(B_2)$ is the signed permutations of $(\ell_1,\ell_2)$, so $S(\mathfrak h)^W=\mathbb R[\,e_1,e_2\,]$ in the variables $t_i=\ell_i^2$, with $e_1=t_1+t_2=p_2$ and $e_2=t_1t_2=(p_2^2-p_4)/2$. **Two generators, of degrees $2$ and $4$ in $\ell$**, the basic degrees of $B_2$, whose exponents are $1,3$. This answers the brief's question directly: **finitely many suffice, and the number is exactly $2$; the quartic is $k=2$.**
3. **Regularity of $\ell$.** For dominant $\lambda$, $\ell_1-\ell_2=\lambda_1-\lambda_2+1\ge1>0$ and $\ell_2=\lambda_2+\tfrac12\ge\tfrac12>0$, so $\ell_1>\ell_2>0$ **automatically**. The $\rho$-shift is what makes it strict; $\lambda$ itself has $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge0$ with equality allowed, and four of the twenty-four blocks ($(0,0)$, $(2,2)$, $(3,3)$, $(6,6)$), sit on that wall. *The block writes "For a dominant $B_2$ weight $\ell_1>\ell_2>0$", attaching the strict inequalities to $\lambda$'s dominance without saying that the shift supplies them; the statement is true, the reason is elided.*
4. **Closing.** Knowing $p_2$ and $p_4$ gives $e_1,e_2$, hence the multiset $\{t_1,t_2\}$; positivity and strictness then order it and take positive square roots, giving $\ell$ and hence $\lambda$. **Two blocks with the same $c_2$ and $c_4$ are the same block.** Equivalently and more sharply: distinct dominant $\lambda$ give distinct central characters, because $\lambda+\rho$ and $\mu+\rho$ are both strictly dominant and $W$-conjugate strictly dominant elements are equal.

**This is a theorem, uniform in $\lambda$, and it does not look at the twenty-four.** So "forced rather than measured" is earned, *for the pair*. The block's display of the six $p_4$ values is an illustration of the theorem, not an independent second closure: it is arithmetic on the same weight list and the same $\rho$ that produced $c_2$. **The block's support is one argument, not two.**

## 3. Why the heading is false, an explicit quartic generator that separates $18$

**"The quartic generator" presupposes a distinguished element, and there is none.** $Z(U(\mathfrak{so}(5)))=\mathbb R[z_2,z_4]$ is a polynomial ring on two generators, and $\{z_2,\ z_4'\}$ is a generating set for every choice of constants, where

$$z_4'=z_4+\alpha z_2^2+\beta z_2+\gamma,\qquad \alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb R\ \text{arbitrary}.$$

Since $\mathrm{HC}$ is an algebra map and $\mathrm{HC}(z_4)=p_4+ap_2+b$, $\mathrm{HC}(z_2)=p_2+c$ (the lower-degree $W$-invariants in degree $\le2$ being spanned by $p_2$ and $1$), the choice $\alpha=-\tfrac12$ together with suitable $\beta,\gamma$ makes

$$\mathrm{HC}(z_4')=p_4-\tfrac12p_2^2=\tfrac12(\ell_1^2-\ell_2^2)^2$$

exactly. **This is a legitimate quartic generator of the centre, and on the twenty-four blocks it takes $18$ distinct values, fusing six pairs:**

| value | blocks fused |
| --- | --- |
| $18$ | $(2,2)$, $(1,0)$ |
| $50$ | $(4,4)$, $(2,1)$ |
| $98$ | $(3,2)$, $(6,6)$ |
| $162$ | $(4,3)$, $(3,1)$ |
| $450$ | $(4,0)$, $(5,3)$ |
| $648$ | $(5,2)$, $(6,4)$ |

**Eighteen is worse than $c_2$'s twenty-one.** The reason is transparent once written: $\tfrac12(\ell_1^2-\ell_2^2)^2$ is $\tfrac1{32}\big((2\lambda_1+3)^2-(2\lambda_2+1)^2\big)^2$, a function of one combination of the two labels, and one combination cannot resolve a two-parameter family.

*Screen (permitted, seconds): the six fusions above were checked in exact rationals; $\alpha\in\{-1,-\tfrac14,1\}$ leave all twenty-four distinct, so the failure is at particular generators rather than generic. Reported as a screen.*

**The consequence for the label.** The block's own next sentence, "**The generator is what separates, not the degree.** $C_2^2$ is a legal degree-four central element and separates nothing", is trying to say this and lands on the wrong side of it. Being a generator is *not* what does the work: the $\alpha=-\tfrac12$ element above is a generator and separates less than $c_2$ does. **What does the work is taking both generators.** And "$C_2^2$ separates nothing" is itself false: $c_2\ge0$ on all twenty-four blocks, so $c_2\mapsto c_2^2$ is injective there and $C_2^2$ separates the same $21$ classes $C_2$ does. The true statement is *separates nothing further*.

**Under the pair reading the heading becomes true and forced**, so this is a wording defect rather than a mathematical one. The honest heading is: *the pair $(C_2,C_4)$ separates all $24$, and the separation is forced.*

## 4. The provenance clause is manufactured

The block reads: "*Reviewer 178; the $22$ this file carried came from a run made before $\rho$ was corrected to $(3/2,1/2)$.*" **Four independent strands of `git` refute it.**

1. **The $22$ never coexisted with a different collision set.** The commit that introduced it, `b432dfd7`, wrote in one sentence "takes $22$ distinct values across the $24$ blocks. Three pairs collide" and then named the *corrected-$\rho$* pairs at $30$, $40$, $60$. A pre-correction run would have produced a different collision list, not the right one alongside a wrong count. **The sentence contradicts itself, which is the signature of arithmetic, not of a stale run.**
2. **The record held $21$ before it held $22$.** Commit dd467c6b, earlier the same day, put the material into [plan.md](../plan.md) reading "*C alone does NOT separate the blocks: 21 distinct values across 24*", with $\rho=(3/2,1/2)$ named in the same commit body. The $22$ entered later, at b432dfd7, whose whole purpose was moving that text into [findings/](../findings/). **It is a transcription slip at a file split.**
3. **No run ever produced $22$.** [run_casimir_separation.py](../scripts/runs/run_casimir_separation.py) has four commits and has carried `rhoB2 = (F(3,2), F(1,2))` in every one; it prints $21$. Searching the whole history for any other $\rho$ in this context turns up one hit, `RHO2 = (3, 1)` in the constraint-count script, that is $2\rho$ in doubled exponents, i.e. the same $\rho$, correctly. **There is no commit in this repository that corrects a $\rho$.**
4. **The cited reviewer said something else.** [r178](REVIEW_r178.md) records "**One numerical error**: '22 distinct values' is 21", derives it as $24-3$, lists the twenty-one values, and notes that the sentence contradicts the script it cites. **This is the same failure mode the audit has been finding all week**, a note presenting itself as the outcome of a check, whose check was never run. It is also *worse than harmless*: the false cause implies the record once had a $\rho$ error in this file, which would put every other $\rho$-dependent number here under suspicion.

**Checked, per the brief: no other number in this file descends from an uncorrected $\rho$, because none ever existed.** The $\rho$-dependent figures all reproduce at $(3/2,1/2)$, $c_2(2,2)=16$ and $c_2(2,1)=12$ in the module paragraph, the full $24$-value spectrum, the six $p_4$ rationals, and the "neighbouring conventions give $32$, $8$, $8/3$ and $4$" list, which are $16$ rescaled by pairing conventions ($8/3=16/6$ against $B_K=-6B$) rather than by $\rho$.

## 6. Two further gaps, both small

**The $SO(5)$-to-$O(5)$ step is unstated, and it is the step the record's own commit message flags as the trap.** The blocks are $O(5)$-isotypic components; $c_2$ is an $\mathfrak{so}(5)$ invariant and cannot see a $\det$ twist. So the forcing argument separates $SO(5)$ *labels*, and the extra step (that $V_\lambda$ and $V_\lambda\otimes\det$ never both occur here), is needed. It holds: $-I$ acts trivially on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^5$, hence on $\mathcal W_5$ and on $\mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathcal W_5)$, so only the untwisted extension appears. **One line, absent.** dd467c6b's body calls a Gram-block fusion "*the same shape as the SO-versus-O error*"; this is that shape.

**The block's twenty-four weight labels are not independent of the count they support.** Twelve of them (the multiplicity-$1$ blocks), were *solved* from the recorded Casimir spectrum in [run_casimir_separation.py](../scripts/runs/run_casimir_separation.py), which hardcodes `SPECTRUM`. So "the degree-two Casimir takes $21$ values" restates a property of that input list rather than testing it. **This does not damage the result**, [L83](../findings/sym3-blocks.md)'s Gate 2 records four reviewers reproducing the table, one by a Freudenthal peel, and my hand table above starts from the twenty-four labels rather than the spectrum. It does mean L84 supplies **no** check on L83: L83's own note observes that swapping the two dimension-$35$ blocks' multiplicities "*leaves L84's Casimir multiset unchanged*", and that is right, because multiplicities are irrelevant to separation.
