## 0. Setting and conventions

$W$ a unit-norm critical point of $\mathcal W_n$, $Q(W)=W^2+W^\#=\theta W$. Eigenbasis $\{\varphi_a\}$ of $W$ orthonormal for $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^TY)$, eigenvalues $\lambda_a$, $c_{abc}=\langle[\varphi_a,\varphi_b],\varphi_c\rangle$ totally antisymmetric, $T_{abc}=c_{abc}^2$ totally symmetric, vanishing on repeated indices. The per-index moments are

$$m_{pq}(k):=\sum_{b,c}\lambda_b^p\lambda_c^q\,T_{kbc}=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(\mathrm{ad}(\varphi_k)\,W^q\,\mathrm{ad}(\varphi_k)\,W^p\big),$$

using $[\varphi_k,\varphi_b]=\sum_c c_{kbc}\varphi_c$ and $\mathrm{ad}^T=-\mathrm{ad}$. The census's relations are $m_{00}=2(n-2)$ (A), $m_{10}=-2\lambda_k$ (B), $m_{11}=2\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)$ (C). All three constants reproduce to $\le1.8\times10^{-15}$ at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, $n=6$ ([screen_cp2.py](../scripts/reviewers/r331/screen_cp2.py)).

One correction to the census's attribution before anything else: **(B) consumes first Bianchi *and* Ricci-flatness, not Ricci-flatness alone.** Its derivation (r324 §5.1, rebuilt below as the $S=W$ case of the master identity) needs $\mathrm{Ric}(W)=0$ *and* $b(W)=0$; the census text credits only the former. Nothing downstream changes, but Q1's "does Bianchi give an independent relation" must start from the fact that Bianchi is already spent inside (B).

## 1. Q1, the hunt for a fourth relation. CONFIRMED: all four grounds are empty

### 1.1 The master identity, and where the freeness lives

Every per-index quantity linear in $T$ with polynomial $\lambda$-weights is a combination of the $m_{pq}(k)=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{ad}_k W^q \mathrm{ad}_k W^p)$, so the complete list of universal relations is the complete list of trace identities for these. For **any** $S\in\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ (no Bianchi assumed) write $S=S_c+S_4$ with $S_4=b(S)$ the totally antisymmetric (first-Bianchi-defect) part, $b(S)_{ijkl}=\tfrac13(S_{ijkl}+S_{iklj}+S_{iljk})$, and $S_c=S-b(S)$ the curvature part. For a bivector $v$ with matrix $A$, $\mathrm{ad}(v)$ acts on $\Lambda^2$ as the derivation $D_A(e_i\wedge e_j)=Ae_i\wedge e_j+e_i\wedge Ae_j$, and expanding $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S\,D_A^2)$ termwise gives

$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S\,D_A^2)\;=\;\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(A^2\,\mathrm{Ric}(S)\big)\;+\;2\langle v,S_c\,v\rangle\;-\;4\langle v,S_4\,v\rangle. \tag{$\star$}$$

*Derivation.* $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S D_A^2)=\sum_{i<j}\langle e_i\wedge e_j,\,S[(A^2e_i)\wedge e_j+2(Ae_i)\wedge(Ae_j)+e_i\wedge(A^2e_j)]\rangle$; the outer terms contract to $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^2\mathrm{Ric}(S))$ with $\mathrm{Ric}(S)_{ip}=\sum_jS_{ijpj}$, and the middle term is $\Sigma:=\sum S_{ijpq}A_{ip}A_{jq}$. For $S_c$, first Bianchi gives the classical $\Sigma=\tfrac12\sum S_{ijpq}A_{ij}A_{pq}=2\langle v,S_cv\rangle$. For $S_4$ totally antisymmetric, antisymmetrising $A_{ij}A_{kl}$ in $(j,k)$ gives $X:=\sum S_{ijkl}A_{ij}A_{kl}=\tfrac12(X-Y)$ with $Y:=\sum S_{ijkl}A_{ik}A_{jl}$, so $Y=-X$ and $\Sigma=Y=-X=-4\langle v,S_4v\rangle$. $\square$ *Screened at random non-Weyl $S$ and non-unit $A$, $n=5$: residual $3\times10^{-14}$ at scale $72$; $b(S)$'s total antisymmetry $1\times10^{-16}$* ([screen_core.py](../scripts/reviewers/r331/screen_core.py)).

Now read off the census from $(\star)$ with $v=\varphi_k$:

- $S=I$: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(D_A^2)=-2(n-2)$, the quadratic Casimir. **(A)**, any $W$.
- $S=W$: $\mathrm{Ric}(W)=0$ and $b(W)=0$ kill both correction terms: $m_{10}=-2\langle v,Wv\rangle=-2\lambda_k$. **(B)**, using exactly Ricci-flatness *and* Bianchi.
- $m_{11}=2\langle v,W^\#v\rangle$ is the definition of $\#$, and criticality's diagonal gives **(C)**. No Weyl property enters.
- $S=W^2$: $(\star)$ gives, for every eigenvector,

$$m_{20}(k)\;=\;-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(A_k^2\,\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\big)\;-\;2\lambda_k^2\;+\;6\big\langle \varphi_k,(W^2)_4\,\varphi_k\big\rangle, \tag{$\dagger$}$$

*screened to $7\times10^{-16}$ at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, $n=6$, where both obstruction tensors are nonzero* ($\lVert\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\rVert_\infty=0.5$, $\lVert(W^2)_4\rVert_\infty=0.167$). **A fourth per-index relation is exactly a universal formula for the right side of $(\dagger)$, and no hypothesis on the record supplies one.** $W^2$ is not Bianchi and not Ricci-flat; the only operator content of criticality is $W^\#=\theta W-W^2$, whose contractions give $\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)=-\mathrm{Ric}(W^\#)$ and $(W^2)_4=-(W^\#)_4$, but those two equations hold *automatically*, because $Q$ maps $\mathcal W_n$ into $\mathcal W_n$ (the record's own free constraint), so criticality's Ric- and $\Lambda^4$-components are vacuous and the transfer is circular. This is the precise sense in which the free data begins at weight $\lambda^2$ on a bracket slot.

### 1.2 The four hunting grounds, one by one

**First Bianchi beyond Ricci-flatness, empty.** Bianchi enters the $m$-moments only through $(\star)$, where its entire content for $S=W$ is spent producing (B); for $S=W^2$ the tensors in $(\dagger)$ are the obstruction and Bianchi of $W$ says nothing about them (they are quadratic in $W$, and $b(W)=0$ does not constrain $b(W^2)$, the screen shows $(W^2)_4\ne0$ at a point where $b(W)=0$ exactly).

**Jacobi, empty at linear order in $T$.** The Jacobi tensor $J_{abce}=\sum_d(c_{abd}c_{dce}+c_{bcd}c_{dae}+c_{cad}c_{dbe})=0$ is quadratic in $c$; a linear-in-$T$ consequence needs contractions in which only squares survive. The two $\delta$-contractions that achieve this are $(c,e)=(a,b)$ and $(c,e)=(b,a)$, and both vanish **termwise**: for $(c,e)=(a,b)$ the three terms are $\sum_dc_{abd}^2+\big(-\sum_dc_{abd}^2\big)+0$ (the third has a repeated index), and $(c,e)=(b,a)$ likewise. A $\lambda$-weight cannot be inserted on the summed index $d$ without breaking the identity, and any weight on the free indices preserves the termwise cancellation, so the vanishing carries no information. The operator form $\mathrm{ad}([x,y])=[\mathrm{ad}\,x,\mathrm{ad}\,y]$ traced against polynomials in $W$ yields relations among *cubic* $c$-contractions, never linear in $T$. Where Jacobi genuinely acts is on realizability of $c$ given $T$, which is the original problem, not a relation.

**Off-diagonal criticality, averaged, empty.** Criticality's full matrix form is $M_{kl}=\sum\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta c_{k\alpha\beta}c_{l\alpha\beta}=2\lambda_k(\lambda_k-\theta)\delta_{kl}$. The $k\ne l$ entries are bilinear in $(c_k,c_l)$; the only basis-invariant linear-in-$T$ shadow of the block of $M$ on an eigenspace $E$ is its trace, $\sum_{k\in E}m_{11}(k)$, which is (C) summed. Averaging any off-diagonal entry over the orthogonal group of $E$ kills it (it sits in the traceless part of the block, whose Haar average vanishes). The same holds for the polarized (A) and (B) matrices $-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{ad}_k\mathrm{ad}_l)=2(n-2)\delta_{kl}$ and $-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W\mathrm{ad}_k\mathrm{ad}_l)=-2\lambda_k\delta_{kl}$: their off-diagonal entries constrain products, and their invariant content is the diagonal already counted.

**Higher Casimirs, empty.** The quadratic Casimir is the unique invariant quadratic in $c$; the quartic Casimir controls $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{ad}_v^4)$, which is (a) quartic in $c$, and (b) not a function of $(\lambda,\theta,\lVert v\rVert)$ even in principle: it is a polynomial in the $2$-plane invariants of the bivector $v$ itself (it separates $e_1\wedge e_2$ from $(e_1\wedge e_2+e_3\wedge e_4)/\sqrt2$ at equal norm), so a "relation" through it ties a quartic contraction to data the census does not hold. Nothing linear in $T$ with a $(\lambda,\theta)$-determined right side results.

**Scope of the verdict.** What is proved is that these four sources (which are all the brief names and all the record holds), yield no fourth relation, and that any fourth relation must pin the right side of $(\dagger)$. Non-existence against *unnamed* future sources is not provable by exhaustion; Q4 supplies the complementary evidence (the linear system genuinely has $\{2,2,0\}$ off its row span at generic spectra, so no relation valid *identically in the spectrum* can exist at all, a fourth relation could only be spectrum-conditional).

## 2. Q2, the pinned classification. (i) CONFIRMED; (ii) REFUTED AS STATED

### 2.1 Direction (i): the reductions, written out

With the ordered-sum convention $\sum_{a,b,c}$, total symmetry of $T$ puts the free index on any slot. $\{3,1,0\}$: $\sum\lambda_a^3\lambda_bT_{abc}=\sum_a\lambda_a^3\,m_{10}(a)=\sum_a\lambda_a^3(-2\lambda_a)=-2p_4$. $\{2,1,1\}$: $\sum\lambda_a^2\lambda_b\lambda_cT_{abc}=\sum_a\lambda_a^2\,m_{11}(a)=2\theta p_3-2p_4$. Cross-checks: $\{1,1,0\}$ and $\{2,1,0\}$ reproduce the record's $\sum g=4(n-1)$ and $\sum\lambda g=-4\theta$ exactly. **CONFIRMED.**

### 2.2 Direction (ii): true generically, false at the record's central points

**At any two-eigenvalue spectrum, (A),(B),(C) pin everything, one line.** On a spectrum $\{x,y\}$, $\lambda^2=(x+y)\lambda-xy$ *pointwise*, so per row $m_{20}(k)=(x+y)m_{10}(k)-xy\,m_{00}(k)=-2(x+y)\lambda_k-2xy(n-2)$ is pinned, hence each $g_k=2m_{20}(k)-2m_{11}(k)$ is pinned **per index**, hence $G_2$, $S_{22}$ and every monomial pattern are functions of $(\lambda,\theta)$. The balanced sphere products, the maximal tori and every subalgebra point $\Pi_{\mathfrak h}-\tfrac rNI$ are two-eigenvalue points. *So the census's "nothing on the record pins $\{2,2,0\}$" is false at the record's own extremal family, and the boxed "first quantity a critical point carries that its spectrum and $\theta$ do not determine" is false there too.* The general mechanism: an exponent acts only through the function $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^p$ **on the $d$ values of the spectrum**; "exponent $\le1$" must be read "affine on the spectrum", and for $d=2$ everything is affine.

**At padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ and at $\mathbb{CP}^3$, multiplicity caps pin $\{2,2,0\}$, exact rank certificates.** A functional with value-symmetric weight lies in the row span iff it lies in the span of the value-averaged rows $\bar A_x,\bar B_x,\bar C_x$ (average any representation over the permutations preserving eigenvalues; the target is invariant). These act on the space of value-*types* of triples, and multiplicity caps delete types: padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has values $(2,-1,0)$ (scaled) with multiplicities $(1,2,n(n-1)/2-3)$, leaving $6$ types, and the $9\times6$ averaged matrix has rank $6$ (full) so $\{2,2,0\}$ **is pinned** ([screen_s3s5.py](../scripts/reviewers/r331/screen_s3s5.py), exact rational elimination; also verifiable by hand, the matrix is small). $\mathbb{CP}^3$ has values $(-4,1,16)/\sqrt{360}$ with multiplicities $(6,8,1)$ (built and validated here: $\theta=\theta_0(6)$ to $3\times10^{-16}$), leaving $7$ types, rank $7$, **pinned** ([screen_cp3.py](../scripts/reviewers/r331/screen_cp3.py)).

**At $d\ge3$ distinct values with multiplicities $\ge3$, $\{2,2,0\}$ leaves the span, exact certificates.** At $S^3\times S^5$'s true spectrum $(-4,3,10)/\sqrt{630}$ with multiplicities $(15,10,3)$: $10$ types, averaged rank $7$, $F_{220}$ **not in the span** (exact rational elimination). Same at $S^5\times S^7$'s $(-12,10,21)/\sqrt{11550}$, $(35,21,10)$, and at generic rational spectra with $d=3,4,5,6$. So the linear-span half of the census is right precisely on spectra with enough distinct values *and* enough multiplicity.

**Theorem (generic freeness, proof as the brief asks, weight spaces, not inspection).** *If the spectrum has at least nine pairwise distinct eigenvalues, $F_{220}\notin\mathrm{span}\{A_k,B_k,C_k\}$.* Suppose $F_{220}(t)=\sum_{k\in t}[\alpha_k+\beta_k s_k(t)+\gamma_k p_k(t)]$ on all index-triples $t$, with $s_k,p_k$ the sum and product of the other two values. Apply the double finite difference in $a\mapsto a'$ ($x\ne x'$) and $b\mapsto b'$ ($y\ne y'$) at fixed $c$; on the left only $x^2y^2$ survives, on the right the $g_c$-terms leave $\gamma_c(x-x')(y-y')$ and the $g_a,g_b$-terms leave first differences. Dividing by $(x-x')(y-y')$:

$$(x+x')(y+y')=B_{aa'}+B_{bb'}+(\Gamma_{aa'}+\Gamma_{bb'})z+\gamma_c,\qquad B_{aa'}=\tfrac{\beta_a-\beta_{a'}}{x-x'},\ \Gamma_{aa'}=\tfrac{\gamma_a-\gamma_{a'}}{x-x'}.$$

Varying $c$ alone forces $\Gamma_{aa'}+\Gamma_{bb'}$ to be constant across disjoint pairs, hence (exchange argument on a ground set of $\ge9$ values) $\Gamma_{aa'}$ constant; then at fixed $c$, subtracting two $b$-pairs gives $(x+x')[(y_1+y_1')-(y_2+y_2')]=B_{b_1b_1'}-B_{b_2b_2'}$, whose right side is independent of the $a$-pair while the left varies with $x+x'$, contradiction. $\square$ (The exact certificates show the true threshold is already $d=3$ with multiplicities $\ge3$; the theorem covers the generic points r325's Newton searches attest.)

**Repair for the record.** The classification should read: *for the linear span of (A),(B),(C), a monomial pattern is pinned iff its weight, restricted to the spectrum's values and reduced modulo multiplicity-deleted types, lies in the span of the affine patterns*, with the practical corollaries that (a) everything is pinned at two-eigenvalue points, (b) $\{2,2,0\}$ is pinned at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ and $\mathbb{CP}^3$, (c) the stated criterion is correct at spectra with $\ge3$ distinct values of multiplicity $\ge3$. The census's "elimination test" (post F42's clearance rule) survives unchanged, it polices the pinned side, which only grew.

## 5. Q5, the $S_{22}$ object and the Cauchy–Schwarz bound

### 5.1 (i) Basis-independence: CONFIRMED, with the proof the record omits

$\sum_{k,\alpha}\lVert[We_k,We_\alpha]\rVert^2=\lVert\,[\cdot,\cdot]\circ(W\otimes W)\,\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$: the map $X\otimes Y\mapsto[WX,WY]$ is linear on $\mathfrak{so}(n)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(n)$, $\{e_k\otimes e_\alpha\}$ is an orthonormal basis of the tensor square whenever $\{e_k\}$ is orthonormal, and an orthonormal change $e\mapsto Ue$ acts by the orthogonal $U\otimes U$, so the sum is basis-independent for **any** operator $W$, orthogonal or not; nothing about $W$ is used. The identity $[W\varphi_k,W\varphi_\alpha]=\lambda_k\lambda_\alpha[\varphi_k,\varphi_\alpha]$ holds only in the eigenbasis and is used only to *evaluate* the invariant there, which is legitimate. The variant $\sum_k\lVert\mathrm{ad}(We_k)W\rVert^2$ is the trace of a quadratic form in $e_k$, likewise invariant, and equals $\sum_k\lambda_k^2m_{20}(k)=S_{22}$ in the eigenbasis. The bridge $S_{22}=2\theta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3-2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^4+\tfrac12G_2$ follows from $g_k=2m_{20}(k)-2m_{11}(k)$ and (C), and screens exactly ($2\times10^{-16}$ at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$). **CONFIRMED.**

### 5.2 (ii) The equality case: REFUTED AS STATED
