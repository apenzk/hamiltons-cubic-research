## A2, where $T(D,D,N)=0$ comes from, and post F39

**The subalgebra argument is right.** If $S$ is a linear subspace with $Q(S)\subseteq S$, polarisation gives $S\circ S\subseteq S$, so $T(D,D,N)=\langle D\circ D,N\rangle=0$ for $D\in S$, $N\perp S$. The zero-extended $\mathcal W_{n-1}$ qualifies: its operators are supported on the subalgebra $\mathfrak{so}(n-1)$, $R^2$ stays there, and $R^\#_{ab}=\tfrac12c_a^{pq}c_b^{rs}R_{pr}R_{qs}$ vanishes unless $a,b\in\mathfrak{so}(n-1)$ because $\mathfrak{so}(n-1)$ is closed under the bracket. The coordinate-diagonal locus qualifies by the diagonal $R^\#$ formula and $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$ ([r1094](REVIEW_r1094.md), C2).

**The correction to post F39 misreads the archive.** The archive's "frame" is an orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$ with $D$ the coordinate-diagonal part: an outside report archive (unpublished, held in the private record) line 33 defines "diagonal" as diagonal in the bivector basis of an orthonormal basis, and line 115 reads "In any coordinate frame write $W=D+F$, with $D$ diagonal and $F\perp\mathcal D$ ... Since $Q(D)$ is diagonal". The coordinate-diagonal locus is a $Q$-subalgebra in **every** orthonormal frame, so "in any frame" is exactly the right quantifier, and no frame split in that sense has $T(D,D,F)\ne0$. *Screen*: $W$ random Weyl read in a random orthonormal frame, $D$ its diagonal: $T(D,D,F)=0.0$ at $n=5,6,7$, and $D\in\mathcal W_n$ to $2\cdot10^{-15}$. A split along a random 3-dimensional subspace of $\mathcal W_n$, which is no frame split, gives $T(D,D,F)=1.24,\ 1.09,\ -0.98$. **The footer's real defect is that it never says what $D$ is**: it should read "in any orthonormal frame, with $D$ the coordinate-diagonal part". The Lean docstring of [I113Reflection.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I113Reflection.lean) repeats the misreading ("A split `W = D + N` in an arbitrary frame, without it, does not give the identity"). The theorem statements are unaffected.

## A3, the Casimir step

**Correct, at any $\lambda$.** The note compresses one step, supplied here. Let $\sum_\alpha A_\alpha^2=-c$ on $\mathcal W_n$ (irreducible at $n\ge5$, and at $n=4$ both halves carry the same value). Invariance applied twice moves $\sum_\alpha(A_\alpha\otimes1+1\otimes A_\alpha)^2$ acting on $X\otimes Y$ onto $\sum_\alpha A_\alpha^2U=-cU$ in the third slot. Expanding the left side gives $-2c\,T(X,Y,U)+2\sum_\alpha T(A_\alpha X,A_\alpha Y,U)$, so $\sum_\alpha T(A_\alpha X,A_\alpha Y,U)=\tfrac c2T(X,Y,U)$ for all $X,Y,U\in\mathcal W_n$. This is "applying the Casimir of $\mathcal W_n\otimes\mathcal W_n$ and contracting", made explicit. Invariance at $(W,Z,A_\alpha Z)$, summed, gives $\tfrac c2T(W,Z,Z)+\sum_\alpha T(W,A_\alpha Z,A_\alpha Z)-cT(W,Z,Z)=0$, which is the note's second display. With $K=\tfrac\lambda2-L_W$ on all of $\mathcal W_n$ and $\sum_\alpha\lvert A_\alpha Z\rvert^2=c\lvert Z\rvert^2$ (orthogonal action), the stated formula follows with no condition on $\lambda$.

**The constant.** $\mathcal W_n$ has highest weight $(2,2,0,\dots)$, and with $\rho_i=\tfrac n2-i$ the Casimir is $\langle\Lambda,\Lambda+2\rho\rangle=2n+2(n-2)=4(n-1)$ in the normalisation where the vector representation gives $n-1$, which is $\sum_{i<j}\varphi_{ij}^2=-(n-1)I$ for the $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}$-orthonormal basis. *Screen*: $\sum_\alpha A_\alpha\cdot(A_\alpha\cdot Y)=-4(n-1)Y$ to $2\cdot10^{-14}$, and $\sum_\alpha T(W,A_\alpha Z,A_\alpha Z)=\tfrac c2T(W,Z,Z)$ at random Weyl $W,Z$, $n=5,6,7$. Then $\Delta_H=\tfrac1{2(n-1)}\cdot\tfrac c2(\lambda\lvert Z\rvert^2-T)=\lambda\lvert Z\rvert^2-T(W,Z,Z)$ and $\lambda$ cancels, as stated.

## A5, what (1.5) uses

## C3, the isotropy count

**Correct.** The homogeneous system says $G\mathbf1=0$ and $G\varepsilon=0$ for symmetric $G$. With three distinct values $\mathbf1,\varepsilon$ span a plane, so the kernel is $\{s\,ww^{\mathsf T}\}$, $w=\mathbf1\times\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_3,\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_1)$: rank five, one free parameter. $G_{12}$ is a valid coordinate since $w_1w_2\ne0$. **A first Bianchi check adds nothing to $G$**: contracting $b(e)=0$ with $P_b$ gives $\sum_a\varepsilon_a(G_{ab}-\tau_{ab})=2\varepsilon_bm_b$, whose $G$-part vanishes by the Ricci row, leaving a row in $\tau$ alone. The same with $b(\mathrm{id})=0$ gives $\tau_b=2(n-2)m_b$, the adjoint Casimir.

**Sharper constraints than those listed.** The two linear relations make $A_2$ and $A_3$ affine in $A_1$ and $I$, so the free parameter is $\operatorname{tr}(A_1^2)$ and the binding constraint is joint positivity $A_1,A_2,A_3\succeq0$, which confines the spectrum of $A_1$ to an interval. Section 2 of the note itself adds $\tfrac12\tau_{ab}\le G_{ab}\le m_am_b$ for $a\ne b$, from $\lvert h\rvert^2\ge0$ and $\lVert N_4\rVert^2\ge0$ per pair.

## C4, the bounds and the per-pair inequality

**Follows.** L234 gives $\langle X,e\circ X\rangle=\langle Y_0,e\circ Y_0\rangle-\langle N_r,e\circ N_r\rangle-\langle N_4,e\circ N_4\rangle$, both corrections entering with a minus sign, so upper bounds on them are the direction needed. $h$ is traceless ($\operatorname{tr}h=-\operatorname{tr}(UV)=2\langle u,v\rangle=0$), so $\langle h,\mathring eh\rangle\le\mathring\mu\lvert h\rvert^2$ with $\mathring e=S_e$. Substituting $\lVert X\rVert^2$ from C1 and moving $\tfrac{\lvert h\rvert^2}{2(n-2)}$ and $\tfrac1{12}(1-g)$ across gives exactly the displayed inequality. At $n=5$ the $\nu$ term is zero on both sides. $\mathring\mu=\lambda_{\max}(S_e)$ is the $\mu/\theta$ of (1.5) once A4's convention is stated.

## C5, the link to R35

**The logic and direction are right.** R35's dichotomy, stated as a target and unproved, reads $\lambda\le\max\{\sqrt{3/2},\beta\mu\}$ with $\mu=-\lambda_{\min}(\mathring W_{\mathrm{Li}})=\lambda_{\max}(S)$, the same quantity as $\mathring\mu\theta$. Above $\sqrt{3/2}$ it gives $\mathring\mu\ge\tfrac n{n-1}>1$, which makes $\tfrac{\mathring\mu-1}{2(n-2)}$ positive and the right side larger, so the pair inequality weakens. **"R33 and R35 are one question at this term" overstates it**: the two routes share one quantity and pull it in opposite directions, a tension to record, and R35's own second branch (the projection cap) is where an upper bound on $\mathring\mu$ would come from.

## Summary

Both notes are sound in their mathematics, and every constant was reproduced independently. (1.5) holds as an algebraic identity at every Weyl $W$, every $\lambda$ and every eigenpair of $S$, and the Casimir step is correct once the double-invariance move is written out. Three corrections are owed. (i) The I113 note's criticism of post F39 misreads the archive: "frame" there is an orthonormal coordinate frame with $D$ diagonal, a $Q$-subalgebra in every frame, so the footer needs "$D$ the coordinate-diagonal part" added, not a narrower quantifier, and the Lean docstring carries the same misreading. (ii) The note's $\mu$ is an eigenvalue of $S=-\mathring W_{\mathrm{Li}}$, and $M=\mu I-S$ must be written, since the other sign fails. (iii) Reading $\Delta_H\ge0$ from the Hessian needs $A_\alpha\cdot Z\perp W$, true because $h$ is an $S$-eigenvector and unstated. In the I73c note the norm, count and bound steps all check, the constraint set on $G$ can be sharpened to joint positivity of the affinely related $A_a$, and the R33/R35 relation is a tension rather than one question.
