## H1, the two lower bounds

**The mechanism, checked before the note was read.** The balanced sphere product $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ has three blocks in the Einstein normalisation quoted at [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md): $\Lambda^2V_p$ and $\Lambda^2V_q$ each at $\mu n/((n-2)(n-1))$ and the mixed block at $-\mu/(n-1)$, of dimensions $\binom{n/2}2$, $\binom{n/2}2$ and $n^2/4$. Imposing $\sum\lambda^2=1$ gives $m=\tfrac1n\sqrt{2(n-2)/(n-1)}$ and $h=\sqrt{2/((n-1)(n-2))}$, whence $-m+(n-2)h=(n-1)m=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n=\theta_0(n)$: **both** [Lemma L70](../findings/bounds.md) and the $\Pi$-bound saturate there simultaneously, which is why both of the note's inequalities are exactly attained. That is the whole of the sharpness claim and it is right; I verified the two closed forms and the two saturations to twelve digits at $n=6,8,10,12,20,50$ as a transcription screen only.

**The chain is valid where the competitor exists.** A unit maximiser is a critical point in the Lagrange sense, and since $Q$ maps $\mathcal W_n$ into itself, proved on the record at [problem.md](../findings/problem.md) from [Böhm–Wilking](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/notes.md) eq. (6), the projected first-order condition is $Q(W)=\theta W$ with $\theta=P(W)$, so [Lemma L70](../findings/bounds.md) and the $\Pi$-bound apply unconditionally at a maximiser. $\lambda_{\min}<0\le\lambda_{\max}$ comes from $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$, which is the hypothesis Lemma L70's nonnegativity of $D$ needs; the flagged excluded-index looseness in that block weakens the bound and therefore leaves it valid as an upper bound. Nothing breaks at small $n$ beyond $n\ge3$.

**The odd-$n$ defect, and it is not repairable by rewording.** $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ does not exist at odd $n$, so "the balanced sphere product is a unit competitor with that value" is empty there. The record's own [Θ row in conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) already says the diagonal maximum at odd $n$ is $\Theta_n$ with $\Theta_n^2=2(n-1)(n-3)/((n+1)(n-2))$, and $\Theta_n<\theta_0(n)$ at **every** odd $n$, since $\Theta_n^2/\theta_0(n)^2=(n^3-3n^2)/(n^3-3n^2+4)<1$. So the record's best odd-$n$ competitors are $\Theta_n$, the padded $\theta_0(n-1)$, and padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $\sqrt{3/2}$, and none of the three reaches $\theta_0(n)$ once $n\ge13$: $\theta_0(13)=1.249852$ against $\Theta_{13}=1.248376$, $\theta_0(12)=1.236033$ and $\sqrt{3/2}=1.224745$.

**And the conclusion itself fails at the record's own conjectured odd maximiser.** Take $n=13$, $S^6\times S^7$. The block formula gives, at unit norm, the spectrum $(0.145643816^{\times15},\,0.104031297^{\times21},\,-0.104031297^{\times42})$ (note $\lambda_2=-\lambda_3$ exactly, because $q-1=p$ at $q=p+1$) so $m=0.104031297$, against the theorem's floor $\theta_0(13)/12=0.104154339$. **The floor is violated.** The spectrum computation is independent of $\theta$, and it cross-checks against the record: $(n-1)m=1.248375568$ reproduces [INDEX-values](../findings/INDEX-values.md)'s catalogued $\Theta_{13}=\sqrt{240/154}=1.248376$ to every digit listed, i.e. the point saturates the $\Pi$-bound. That is the general mechanism: at $S^{(n-1)/2}\times S^{(n+1)/2}$ the $\Pi$-bound is saturated, so $m=\Theta_n/(n-1)<\theta_0(n)/(n-1)$ identically, at $n=5$ ($0.235702$ against $0.244949$), $n=13$ and $n=15$ ($0.090784$ against $0.090851$) alike. At $n\le11$ the theorem survives anyway because padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ supplies $\theta\ge\sqrt{3/2}>\theta_0(n)$ and the sphere product is not the maximiser; at odd $n\ge13$ that rescue is gone. So the theorem at odd $n\ge13$ is either false or asserts strictly more than the odd-$n$ conjecture the record treats as open, and either way it is not proved.

**The repair.** State the theorem at even $n\ge6$; at odd $n$, replace $\theta_0(n)$ throughout by $\max\{\Theta_n,\sqrt{3/2},\theta_0(n-1)\}$, which is $\sqrt{3/2}$ for $5\le n\le11$ and $\Theta_n$ for $n\ge13$. The $h$-bound survives odd $n$ untouched in its stated form as it happens, $0.145644\ge0.123091$ at $n=13$, $0.121046\ge0.104828$ at $n=15$, but it survives as an accident, not as a consequence of the proof given.

**A second defect, in the sharpness clause, and it is independent of parity.** "Attained, simultaneously and exactly, at the balanced sphere product" is offered as sharpness of a theorem about *maximisers*, which needs the balanced product to be one. At even $n=6,8,10$ it is not: $\theta_0(6)=1.054093$, $\theta_0(8)=1.145644$ and $\theta_0(10)=1.200000$ all sit below $\sqrt{3/2}=1.224745$, which padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ attains at every $n\ge4$ ([INDEX-values](../findings/INDEX-values.md)), so a maximiser there has $\theta>\theta_0(n)$ **strictly** and hence $m\ge\theta/(n-1)>\theta_0(n)/(n-1)$ strictly. The bounds are therefore attained at **no** maximiser at $n=6,8,10$. At even $n\ge12$ attainment is conditional on the conjecture. The honest form: equality holds at the balanced sphere product, a critical point with $\theta=\theta_0(n)$; sharpness *as a statement about maximisers* is the conjecture.

**The rest of the note stands.** The half-plane $(n-2)h-m\ge\theta_0(n)$ is immediate from Lemma L70 at even $n$, and its equality case is handled correctly, equality forces $\theta=\theta_0(n)$ with Lemma L70 saturated, and [Lemma L71](../findings/bounds.md) closes it. Note that L71 also makes the equality case vacuous at odd $n$, which is a further reason the odd half of the statement was never going to work. The positive-eigenvalue count is derived correctly, including the substitution $m\le(n-2)h-\theta_0(n)$, and it inherits the same parity caveat.

**One tell in the note's own screen.** "Both bounds equal the balanced product's own $m$ and $h$ to twelve digits at $n=5,6,8,12,20,50$" lists $n=5$, where there is no balanced product. What was screened at $n=5$ is an identity between two algebraic expressions, not an agreement with an object, which is exactly the gap the theorem's statement has.

## H2, the plan item's weaker form

**It is true, and here is where it comes from, which the note does not say.** [Lemma L73](../findings/bounds.md) gives $\lambda_{\max}\le(n-2)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$, i.e. $m\ge h/(n-2)$. Feeding that into Lemma L70's $\theta\le-m+(n-2)h$ gives $\theta\le h\big[(n-2)-\tfrac1{n-2}\big]=h\big[(n-2)^2-1\big]/(n-2)$, so $h\ge\theta(n-2)/((n-2)^2-1)\ge\theta_0(n)(n-2)/((n-2)^2-1)$. Since $(n-2)^2-1=(n-1)(n-3)$ this is the item's bound exactly.

**And it is weaker for a reason worth recording.** The two derivations differ only in which lower bound on $m$ is fed into the same Lemma L70 step: the item uses $m\ge h/(n-2)$, the note uses $m\ge\theta/(n-1)$. At the balanced product $m/h=(n-2)/n$, so the item's input is slack by a factor $n(n-2)/n=n-2$ (a factor $10$ at $n=12$) while the note's input is exact. **Numbers CONFIRMED**: $\theta_0(12)(n-2)/((n-2)^2-1)=1.236033\times10/99=0.1248518$ against $\sqrt{2/110}=0.1348400$, so the note's "$0.12485$ against $0.13484$" and its "$8\%$" are both right.

## H3, the three negative eigenvalues

**The counting derivation is correct and the note's reading of it is not.** $S^2\le q\sum_{\lambda<0}\lambda^2\le q\,mS$ gives $q\ge S/m\ge1/(m(h+m))$, as claimed. But at the balanced product $m(h+m)=\tfrac1n\sqrt{\tfrac{2(n-2)}{n-1}}\cdot\tfrac{2}{n}\sqrt{\tfrac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}=\tfrac4{n^2}$ **exactly**, so the bound returns $q\ge n^2/4$, which is $36$ at $n=12$, precisely the dimension of the mixed block, i.e. the true negative multiplicity. The bound is **tight** there, not "weaker than $3$". The companion is tight too: $h(h+m)=4/(n(n-2))$, so $p\ge n(n-2)/4=30=2\binom62$, exactly the positive multiplicity. Both identities are clean algebra and I screened them at $n=6,8,10,12,20,50$ for transcription only.

**What is actually true is a different statement, and the note should make it.** The counting bound needs an **upper** bound on $m$ and $h$ to deliver a number, and the theorem supplies only lower bounds; with the trivial $h^2+m^2\le1$ the best it gives is $m(h+m)\le\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt2}2=1.207107$, attained at $h=\cos\tfrac{3\pi}8$, $m=\sin\tfrac{3\pi}8$, so $q\ge1$ and nothing more. So the correct sentence is "the counting bound is not usable at a maximiser because only lower bounds on $h$ and $m$ are in hand", not "it is weaker than $3$ at the balanced product".

**And the argument the note says it lacks is on the record.** [Lemma L35](../findings/stratum-two-negative.md), *at most two negative eigenvalues implies $\theta\le\sqrt{3/2}$, unconditionally, at any number of positive eigenvalues and any dimension*, contraposes to: $\theta>\sqrt{3/2}$ forces at least three negative eigenvalues. A maximiser has $\theta\ge\Theta_n$, and $\Theta_n>\sqrt{3/2}$ exactly for $n\ge12$: at even $n$ this is $\theta_0(n)^2>3/2\iff n^2-12n+8>0\iff n\ge12$, and at odd $n$ it is $\Theta_n^2>3/2\iff n^2-13n+18>0\iff n\ge13$. **So $q\ge3$ holds at every maximiser at every $n\ge12$, in two lines, from a canonical lemma the note did not consult.** $\theta_0(12)=1.236033>1.224745$ is the whole margin at the threshold.

**The scope this exposes is the interesting part.** At $n\le11$ the item's claim is not merely underived but **false**: padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has spectrum $(2,-1,-1,0^{\dots})/\sqrt6$, exactly **two** negative eigenvalues, and $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$; at $n=5$ [Lemma L61](../findings/sym3-blocks.md) proves this is the maximum on $\mathcal W_5$ with equality exactly on that orbit, so the maximiser at $n=5$ has $q=2$. The threshold for "at least three negative eigenvalues" and the threshold for the conjecture are the same $n\ge12$, and they are the same inequality $\theta_0(n)>\sqrt{3/2}$.

## H4, the gradings

**The Hom-space assignment is right, and I checked it rather than read it.** With $g_1=+1$ on $V_1\oplus V_2$ and $g_2=+1$ on $V_1\oplus V_3$, a block $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_i,V_j)$ carries $\mathrm{Ad}(g)$-eigenvalue $\varepsilon(i)\varepsilon(j)$. That gives $(0,0)$ on each $\mathfrak{so}(V_i)$; $(0,1)$ on $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_1,V_2)$ and on $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_3,V_4)$; $(1,0)$ on $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_1,V_3)$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_2,V_4)$; $(1,1)$ on $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_1,V_4)$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V_2,V_3)$, the note's table exactly. The dimension identity $\sum_i\binom{n_i}2+(n_1n_2+n_3n_4)+(n_1n_3+n_2n_4)+(n_1n_4+n_2n_3)=\binom n2$ is an identity in the $n_i$, so the note's screen over splittings of $12$ was checking nothing.

**"A pair of commuting involutions, i.e. a splitting" is the false step.** Commuting *automorphisms* of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ need not have commuting *lifts*: for $n\ne8$, $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{so}(n))=PO(n)$, so a $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ grading is a Klein four-subgroup of $PO(n)$, whose preimage in $O(n)$ has order $8$ and may be $\mathbb Z_2^3$, $D_4$ or $Q_8$. Only the $\mathbb Z_2^3$ case is a splitting into four $\pm$-eigenspaces. Elements with $g^2=-I$ are involutions in $PO(n)$ with fixed algebra $\mathfrak u(n/2)$, and two of them may anticommute.

**The counterexample, at $n=12$ and therefore in scope.** Take $\mathbb R^{12}=\mathbb H^3$ with $J,K$ the quaternionic structures, $J^2=K^2=-I$, $JK=-KJ$. Then $\mathrm{Ad}(J)\mathrm{Ad}(K)=\mathrm{Ad}(JK)=\mathrm{Ad}(-KJ)=\mathrm{Ad}(K)\mathrm{Ad}(J)$, so they commute, and none of $J,K,JK$ is $\pm I$, so they generate a genuine $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$. The grades are $X$ commuting with both ($\mathfrak{sp}(3)$, dimension $21$) and the three pieces cut out by each of $J,K,JK$ alone; since $\{X:XJ=JX\}=\mathfrak u(6)$ has dimension $36$ and contains $\mathfrak g_{00}$, each of the other three has dimension $36-21=15$. So the grade dimensions are $(21,15,15,15)$, summing to $66$. **No splitting of $12$ gives that multiset**: I enumerated all $27$ splittings $(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)$ of $12$ for which all three involutions are non-trivial, and the three with $\mathfrak g_{00}$ of dimension $21$ ($(6,4,1,1)$, $(6,3,3,0)$, $(5,5,2,0)$), give $(21,25,10,10)$, $(21,18,18,9)$ and $(21,25,10,10)$. The structural reason is cleaner still: $\mathfrak{sp}(3)$ is simple, and the only splitting-type $\mathfrak g_{00}$ of dimension $21$ that is a sum of $\mathfrak{so}$'s of total dimension $21$ is $\mathfrak{so}(6)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(4)$, which is not.

**And the omission costs the note two rows of its own census.** Merging the quaternionic grading two ways gives multiplicity triples $(36,15,15)$ and $(30,21,15)$, both with every multiplicity $\ge3$, and **neither is among the $74$**. So "at $n=12$ the splittings give $74$ distinct multiplicity triples" is right about splittings (I reproduce $74$ exactly) and is a *lower bound* for $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ gradings, which is what the note's argument needs it to be about. $(36,15,15)$ is also, by coincidence worth noting, one of the six triples [DERIVE-sp3b](DERIVE-sp3b.md) withdrew from the $\mathbb Z_3$ side; it re-enters here from a completely different construction and with a real-eigenspace meaning this time.

**$n=8$ triality does not add a further case, and the note is safe there by accident.** $\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{so}(8))=S_3$ has no Klein four-subgroup, so a $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{so}(8))$ has image of order $1$ or $2$ in $\mathrm{Out}$; the order-$2$ case is a genuinely extra family (one outer generator, fixed algebras among $\mathfrak{so}(7)$, $\mathfrak{sp}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(1)$) but $n=8$ is out of scope. The $Q_8$ and $D_4$ cases are the ones that bite at $n=12$.

## H5, six zeros, and what "vertex" would need

**The combinatorics is right, and I recomputed it rather than checked it.** In the Klein group the triples summing to $0$ are exactly $(0,0,0)$, $(0,g,g)$ for $g\ne0$, and $(a,b,c)$ with $a,b,c$ the three distinct non-zero elements. Merging $\{\mathfrak g_a,\mathfrak g_b\}$ and setting $X=\mathfrak g_0$, $Y=\mathfrak g_a\oplus\mathfrak g_b$, $Z=\mathfrak g_c$ leaves $XXX$, $XYY$, $XZZ$, $YYZ$ admissible and kills $XXY$, $XXZ$, $XYZ$, $YYY$, $YZZ$, $ZZZ$. Merging $\{\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_a\}$ and setting $X=\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_a$, $Y=\mathfrak g_b$, $Z=\mathfrak g_c$ leaves $XXX$, $XYY$, $XYZ$, $XZZ$ and kills $XXY$, $XXZ$, $YYY$, $YYZ$, $YZZ$, $ZZZ$. Relabelling to put $\mathfrak g_{00}$ in the stated position reproduces the note's two rows **exactly**, $\{112,113,222,223,233,333\}$ and $\{111,122,123,133,223,333\}$. Six zeros, four survivors, two patterns, three merges each. **CONFIRMED.** The second row is only defined up to the relabelling $1\leftrightarrow3$, which sends it to $\{111,113,122,123,223,333\}$; the note should say which of $E_1,E_3$ is the merged piece.

**The "iff" is only an "only if", and the difference is not academic.** The group condition is necessary; sufficiency needs the bracket to be genuinely nonzero. Take the splitting $(10,1,1,0)$: all three involutions are non-trivial, the grades are $(45,10,10,1)$, and merging so that the $1$-dimensional $\mathfrak g_{11}$ stands alone as an eigenspace forces $t_{iZZ}=t_{ZZZ}=0$ for free, since $T$ vanishes whenever two indices coincide and $Z$ has no two independent vectors. That is a **seventh** zero. So "every one of the six merges gives exactly six vanishing types" is false without a floor on the eigenspace multiplicities; with the note's own $\ge3$ filter attached it is fine, but that filter is applied to the census two sections later and not to this claim.

**The structural reading is wrong.** From $\{112,113,222,223,233,333\}$ the note concludes "$E_1\oplus E_2$ and $E_1\oplus E_3$ are both subalgebras". They are not: $123$ is **absent** from that zero set, so $\langle[E_1,E_2],E_3\rangle\ne0$ and $E_1\oplus E_2$ is not closed. Concretely, in that pattern $E_1=\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_a$, $E_2=\mathfrak g_b$, $E_3=\mathfrak g_c$, and $[\mathfrak g_a,\mathfrak g_b]\subseteq\mathfrak g_c=E_3$. What the six zeros **do** say is: $E_1$ is a subalgebra ($112$, $113$), and $[E_2\oplus E_3,E_2\oplus E_3]\subseteq E_1$ ($222$, $223$, $233$, $333$), that is, $(\mathfrak{so}(n),E_1)$ is a **symmetric pair** with $\mathfrak m=E_2\oplus E_3$, and $E_2,E_3$ are the $\pm$-halves of $\mathfrak m$ under the second involution. That is the $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ structure seen from inside, and it is the correct successor to [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s "$t_{112}=t_{222}=0$ is the two halves of a symmetric pair".

**"Degenerate vertex", three conditions, none addressed.** *(i)* Six active constraints make a point a vertex only if their restrictions to the $4$-dimensional feasible set have rank $4$; below that it is a face of positive dimension and "vertex" is the wrong word. *(ii)* The zero pattern is read off the grading, but membership in $\Pi(\lambda)$ requires (A), (B) and (C), and (C) is criticality. A grading supplies a zero pattern, not a point of the polytope; the note's closing caveat concedes this for the witness but the boxed claim does not carry it. *(iii)* [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s own caveats apply verbatim and are not quoted: $\Pi(\lambda)$ drops the proved pair cap, which at $d=3$ is six further inequalities and which the record says **breaks the four-zeros count** when restored; and a $\theta$-saturating $W$ need not sit at a vertex unless the optimum is unique.

**And the count cuts the other way, which is the finding rather than the objection.** Six equations on a $4$-dimensional feasible set are over-determined by two, so the graded zero pattern is realisable in $\Pi(\lambda)$ only where two compatibility conditions on $(\lambda,\theta)$ hold. That is exactly the $d=2$ picture the note appeals to: there the surplus is one, and the one condition it imposes is $\lambda_{\max}/\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert=n/(n-2)$, which is what pins the balanced product, [Lemma L117](../findings/bounds.md) records precisely that reading. **So the surplus is spent pinning the spectrum, not on singling out a maximiser**, and at $d=3$, where the spectrum at fixed multiplicities is a one-parameter family, a surplus of two leaves the system short by one. That is a dimension count and not a proof, since the equations may be dependent, but it is the question the item should be asking, and it points the opposite way from "this is the $d=3$ analogue of the balanced product's degeneracy".

## H6, the witness's multiplicities

**The arithmetic is right.** $(n_1,\dots,n_4)=(0,2,2,8)$ gives $\mathfrak g_{00}$ of dimension $\binom22+\binom22+\binom82=1+1+28=30$, and $n_1n_2+n_3n_4=16$, $n_1n_3+n_2n_4=16$, $n_1n_4+n_2n_3=4$, summing to $66$; merging $16$ with $4$ gives $(30,20,16)$, which is [DERIVE-t11](DERIVE-t11.md)'s witness multiset $(-m)^{\times30},(1.2m)^{\times20},(0.375m)^{\times16}$. **CONFIRMED.**

**A zero $V_i$ is legitimate here, and the criterion is worth stating.** The grading is genuine iff each of $\mathrm{Ad}(g_1)$, $\mathrm{Ad}(g_2)$, $\mathrm{Ad}(g_1g_2)$ is non-trivial, i.e. iff each of the three pairings $\{V_1V_2\mid V_3V_4\}$, $\{V_1V_3\mid V_2V_4\}$, $\{V_1V_4\mid V_2V_3\}$ has both halves non-empty. One zero part is compatible with that; two never are. At $(0,2,2,8)$ the three pairings split $12$ as $2\mid10$, $2\mid10$ and $8\mid4$, so all three involutions are non-trivial and all four grades are non-zero. **CONFIRMED**, but see the next paragraph, which makes the question moot.

**"Eighteen distinct splittings" is an ordered count, and the note names the wrong representative.** As unordered splittings there are exactly **two** that reach $(30,20,16)$: $(0,2,2,8)$, with $12$ orderings, and $(2,2,4,4)$, with $6$, total $18$, which is where the note's number comes from. The second gives grade dimensions $(14,20,16,16)$ and reaches the witness multiset by merging $\mathfrak g_{00}$ with a $16$. **It has no zero part**, so it establishes the claim without needing the degenerate-splitting discussion at all, and it is the one to name. The note names only the degenerate one and then has to defend it.

**A third realisation, outside the note's classification.** Let $J$ be a complex structure on $\mathbb R^{12}$ preserving a split $\mathbb R^4\oplus\mathbb R^8$ and $\sigma=\mathrm{diag}(I_4,-I_8)$, so $\sigma J=J\sigma$. Then $\mathfrak g_{00}=\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(4)$ has dimension $20$, $\mathfrak g_{10}=\mathfrak u(6)\ominus\mathfrak g_{00}$ has dimension $16$, $\mathfrak g_{01}=(\mathfrak{so}(4)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(8))\ominus\mathfrak g_{00}$ has dimension $2+12=14$, and $\mathfrak g_{11}$ has dimension $16$; merging $16$ with $14$ gives $(30,20,16)$ again. This one is not a splitting grading, though its grade dimensions coincide with $(2,2,4,4)$'s, so it adds no census row, it is listed because it shows the realisations are not exhausted by the eighteen.

**The withdrawal is right, and the note's wording of it is right too.** [DERIVE-sp3b](DERIVE-sp3b.md)'s retracted conclusion was literally "the witness $(30,20,16)$ is excluded **because its multiplicities are distinct**", and [r347](REVIEW_r347.md) endorsed withdrawing it. Exhibiting a grading with those multiplicities makes that conclusion false and not merely unsupported, so "the conclusion is wrong too" is precise as written. The note's closing rider, that this bears on multiplicities only, and that whether any Weyl tensor has that spectrum and is critical is untouched, is exactly the right limit and should not be softened; nothing here shows the witness is realisable, and [DERIVE-t11](DERIVE-t11.md) itself records that no realisability criterion for Weyl spectra exists in print.

## A new claim in the current text, reviewed, CONFIRMED

**[DERIVE-mx2](DERIVE-mx2.md)'s "$q\ge2$ at every critical point with $\theta>0$, with no maximality" is correct, and it is the right shape.** Relation (C) at the row $a$ achieving $\lambda_{\min}$, divided by the row mass $2(n-2)$ of relation (A), gives $\mathbb E_{\mu_a}[\lambda_b\lambda_c]=\lambda_{\min}(\theta-\lambda_{\min})/(n-2)$, which is strictly negative for $\theta>0$ since $\lambda_{\min}<0$. So some pair in the support of $\mu_a$ has $\lambda_b\lambda_c<0$, i.e. one strictly negative and one strictly positive entry; and $T$ vanishes on repeated indices, so $b\ne a$ and $c\ne a$. The strictly negative one of the two is a second negative eigenvalue. The hypotheses used are exactly $\lambda_{\min}<0$ (from $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$), $\theta>0$, and relations (A) and (C) of [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md), no maximality, no Lemma L35, no dimension threshold. **It also composes with Lemma L35 rather than duplicating it**: $q\ge2$ everywhere, $q\ge3$ once $\theta>\sqrt{3/2}$, and the gap between them is exactly the $n<12$ regime where padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ sits at $q=2$.
