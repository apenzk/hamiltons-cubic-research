## C2. Why $Q$ preserves $\mathcal W_n$

**Two facts about $Q(R)=R^2+R^\#$ on an algebraic curvature operator $R$, both classical, both in the normalisation $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\,\mathrm{id}$ used here.** First, $Q(R)$ satisfies first Bianchi. This is the reaction term of Hamilton's evolution $\partial_t\mathrm{Rm}=\Delta\mathrm{Rm}+2Q(\mathrm{Rm})$ in an Uhlenbeck frame ([Hamilton 1986](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214440433), §4), and it is stated for $S^2_B(\mathfrak{so}(n))$ in [Böhm–Wilking 2008](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2008.167.1079), §2. Second, its Ricci contraction is LINEAR in the Ricci tensor of $R$:
$$\operatorname{Ric}\big(Q(R)\big)_{ij}=\sum_{k,l}R_{ikjl}\operatorname{Ric}(R)_{kl},$$
which is the reaction term of Hamilton's $\partial_t\operatorname{Ric}=\Delta\operatorname{Ric}+2\sum R_{ikjl}R_{kl}$ in the same frame, the Laplacian commuting with contraction. **So $\operatorname{Ric}(R)=0$ gives $\operatorname{Ric}(Q(R))=0$, and $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$. Polarising, $e\circ Z\in\mathcal W_n$.** *The linearity is not automatic from invariant theory: $W_{iabc}W_{jabc}$ is an equivariant quadratic $\mathcal W_n\to S^2$ with a nonzero traceless part at $n\ge5$, so it is the specific combination $R^2+R^\#$ that cancels it, and the normalisation of $\#$ matters.* Screen at the record's normalisation: a least-squares fit of $\operatorname{Ric}(Q(R))$ for random algebraic $R=W+h\owedge g$ against $\{R(\operatorname{Ric}),\operatorname{Ric}^2,s\operatorname{Ric},\lvert\operatorname{Ric}\rvert^2g,s^2g\}$ returns $(1,0,0,0,0)$ with residual $2\cdot10^{-12}$ at $n=6,7$, and $Q(R)_{[ijkl]}\le3\cdot10^{-14}$. **The note is right to call this the one input not on file.** The Lean layer can reach it by typing the displayed contraction identity, which is a finite polynomial identity in the entries.

## C9. What the witness establishes

**Read against [routes.md](../routes.md)'s R33 row, $T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ is the SQUARED structure constant** ([bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md), the pair cap), so the left side of the note's display is indeed linear in $T$, and "the pair's $(\lambda,T)$ data" can mean two things.

**1. The pair's own row, $(\lambda_\alpha,\lambda_\beta,\{T_{\alpha\beta\gamma},\lambda_\gamma\}_\gamma)$: the claim HOLDS, and more widely than the witness shows.** The note's three pairs all sit in ONE eigenspace, $\ker e$. [L233](../findings/second-order.md) allows that ($\alpha\ne\beta$ as indices), but R33's row takes $u\in E_\alpha$, $v\in E_\beta$ with the sign "the eigenvalue gap", which reads as two DIFFERENT eigenspaces. **At padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ that version is not witnessed**: every commuting cross-eigenspace pair screened there ($u\in\Lambda^+$, $v\in\{\varphi_{45}\}\cup\Lambda^-$) returns the same quotient $\tfrac12\lambda_u$. **A cross-eigenspace witness does exist** at a coordinate-diagonal Weyl $e$ on $n=8$, built with $e_{23}=e_{45}$ and zero row sums (Weyl to $7\cdot10^{-15}$, 27 distinct plane values): the pairs $(\varphi_{01},\varphi_{23})$ and $(\varphi_{01},\varphi_{45})$ have equal $(\lambda_u,\lambda_v)=(0.8745,-0.5111)$, zero brackets, equal unprojected quotient $0.1817$, and projected quotients $0.5271$ and $0.0288$, each matching the four-symbol law. *That $e$ is not critical, so this witnesses the functional form only, as the note's does, since the identity holds at every Weyl $e$.* **Complementary pairs cannot serve**: at $n=8$ with $\{a,b,c,d\}$ and its complement, zero row sums force the two cross sums equal, which is why the first attempt returned equal values.

**2. The whole $(\lambda,T)$ tensor: the claim does NOT follow, and at the witness it is false.** The four-symbol law's cross symbols are $\lambda$-weighted $T$-rows of NEIGHBOURING basis pairs. At the witness, with the coordinate planes in $\ker e$ as basis elements,
$$e_{01}=\sum_\gamma\lambda_\gamma T_{(04)(14)\gamma}=\tfrac13,\qquad e_{02}=\sum_\gamma\lambda_\gamma T_{(04)(24)\gamma}=-\tfrac16,$$
screened exactly, since $[\varphi_{04},\varphi_{14}]=\pm\varphi_{01}$. So the three projected quotients ARE separated by a function of the whole $(\lambda,T)$ tensor that reads rows other than the pair's own. With SIGNED structure constants the general statement is a theorem: the basis, $\lambda$ and the bracket fix $(e,u,v)$ up to an isometric automorphism of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, and the projected quotient is invariant under those, $\mathcal W_n$ being the unique isotypic summand of its dimension in $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathfrak{so}(n)$ (at $n=8$ triality permutes the three $35$s and fixes the $300$). With SQUARED constants, which is what the record's $T$ is, whether the whole tensor always determines it is open, and this review did not settle it.

**The corrected conclusion.** R33's registered step, "one inequality in $(\lambda,T)$ per pair" read as a function of the pair's own row, is false, and that is established across eigenspaces as well as within one. **"Sub-step (c) cannot run inside the pinned $(\lambda,T)$ calculus" is not established**: the admissible direction's inequality for a coordinate pair is a $(\lambda,T)$ expression that couples the pair to the four planes $ac,ad,bc,bd$, which are $T$-neighbours of the pair's members. The note's own sentence "for coordinate pairs these are the frame-diagonal entries $e_P$, which are not spectral data" is true and beside the point: they are not SPECTRAL, but in an eigenbasis containing the neighbouring planes they are $(\lambda,T)$ data. *So R33 loses its per-pair locality, which is a real loss for sub-step (c) as the plan phrases it, but the note's inference that R33 is left "without a working instrument in its registered form" should be restated as "the instrument survives only as a non-local $(\lambda,T)$ inequality", and plan.md's grade change should wait on that reading.*
