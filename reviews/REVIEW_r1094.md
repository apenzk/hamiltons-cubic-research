## C2, $e\circ\mathcal W_n\subseteq\mathcal W_n$

**True, and classical.** Two facts give it. $Q$ maps algebraic curvature operators to algebraic curvature operators, and $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(Q(R))_{ij}=\sum_{kl}\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(R)_{kl}R_{ikjl}$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(R)=0$ forces $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(Q(R))=0$. Polarisation then gives $e\circ Z=\tfrac12(Q(e+Z)-Q(e)-Q(Z))\in\mathcal W_n$. Both facts are stated in [Bhattacharya 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4618), section 3, the sentence before eq. (3.1) and eq. (3.1) itself, citing [Hamilton 1982](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214436922) and [Hamilton 1986](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214440433). The Ricci identity is also the reaction term of $\partial_t\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}$ under Ricci flow in an Uhlenbeck frame, which is pointwise and so holds for every algebraic curvature tensor.

## C5, the $\Lambda^4$ constant

Correct, with one reduction the note does not show. Complete contractions of $W\otimes\omega\otimes\omega$ must send exactly two indices of $W$ into each $\omega$ (three or more into one vanishes by Bianchi, and one into each leaves a Ricci trace). That leaves $A=W_{abcd}\omega_{abpq}\omega_{cdpq}$ and $B=W_{abcd}\omega_{acpq}\omega_{bdpq}$, and Bianchi gives $B=\tfrac12A$ (antisymmetrise $W_{abcd}$ in $a,c$ and use $W_{abcd}+W_{bcad}+W_{cabd}=0$). So the space is at most one-dimensional, equal to one at $n\ge6$ and zero at $n=5$, which the note states. The test point is right: at $\omega=s\,\mathrm{vol}_B$, $\omega^2=s^2\Pi_B$, and $\omega^\#=2s^2\Pi_B$ **computed in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ agrees with the $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ value** because $\omega$ kills $(\Lambda^2B)^\perp$ and brackets of $\Lambda^2B$ with mixed planes leave $\Lambda^2B$. The note uses this support fact silently. $\sum_{ab}\varepsilon_{ijab}\varepsilon_{klab}=2(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})$ gives $8s^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(e\vert_{\Lambda^2B})$, so $\gamma=\tfrac38$. *Screen*, random $\omega\in\Lambda^4$ and random Weyl $e$: equal to $10^{-14}$ at $n=6,7$, both sides $O(10^{-15})$ at $n=5$.
