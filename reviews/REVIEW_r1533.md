## A. Fidelity

- **A-1. The tensor** ([L95.lean:54](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L95.lean), line 57). r101's $R_{ijkl}=g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk}+J_{ik}J_{jl}-J_{il}J_{jk}+2J_{ij}J_{kl}$ ([REVIEW_r101.md:41](REVIEW_r101.md)) is $G+T$ in the file's notation, index for index. At an orthonormal pair $(X,JX)$ it gives $R_{abab}=1+3J_{ab}^2=4$, so the holomorphic sectional curvature is $4$. Contracting by hand, $\sum_aG_{abad}=(n-1)\delta_{bd}$ and $\sum_aT_{abad}=0+\delta_{bd}+2\delta_{bd}=3\delta_{bd}$ (using $J_{aa}=0$, $J^2=-1$ and $J^{\mathsf T}J=1$), so $\mathrm{Ric}=(n+2)g=2(m+1)g$. For an Einstein metric $W=R-\tfrac{s}{n(n-1)}G$ with $s=n(n+2)$, so $W=G+T-\tfrac{n+2}{n-1}G=T-\tfrac3{n-1}G$. The file's constant is the right one, and `isWeyl_Wcp`'s trace line ([L95.lean:99](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L95.lean)) contracts the same slots as `IsWeyl.traceless` ([L94Statement.lean:72](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L94Statement.lean)). `IsCx` (skew, $J^2=-1$) forces $J^{\mathsf T}J=1$, so it is an orthogonal complex structure, and `IsCx.row`/`IsCx.col` say so.
- **A-2. The three closed forms** ([L95.lean:116](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L95.lean), 191, 224). By hand, $K_{ab}=T_{abab}-cG_{abab}=3J_{ab}^2-c(1-\delta_{ab})$ with $c=\tfrac3{n-1}$, r101's form off the diagonal and $0$ on it. With $\sum J^4=\sum J^2=n$, $\lVert K\rVert_F^2=9n-6cn+c^2n(n-1)=9n(n-2)/(n-1)$, which is $36m(m-1)/(2m-1)$ at $n=2m$. For the norm, $\sum T^2=6n^2+6n$, $\sum TG=6n$, $\sum G^2=2n^2-2n$ give $\sum W^2=6n(n+1)-18n/(n-1)=6n(n^2-4)/(n-1)$, which is $48m(m^2-1)/(2m-1)$. **Normalisation.** `frobK` sums $K_{ab}^2$ over all ordered $(a,b)$ ([L94Statement.lean:84](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L94Statement.lean)), as r101's $\lVert K\rVert_F^2$ does, and `normM2` counts $a<b$, $c<d$, which `normM2_quarter` makes $\tfrac14\sum W^2$. So $\lVert K\rVert_F^2/\lVert M\rVert_F^2=4\lVert K\rVert^2/\lVert W\rVert^2$, r101's quotient, and $36/(6(n+2))=6/(n+2)=3/(m+1)$. `kappaSq` is the unnormalised supremum ([L94Statement.lean:98](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L94Statement.lean)), so the record's scale-free $\kappa^2=3/(m+1)$ reads as $\kappa^2=\tfrac3{m+1}\lVert M\rVert_F^2$, which is how the file states it.
- **A-3. The lower bound** ([L95.lean:238](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L95.lean), line 299). `le_csSup` at the identity frame, with boundedness from `frame_bound`, gives $\mathrm{frobK}(\mathrm{id})\le\kappa^2$, and `ratio_std` rewrites the left side. That is exactly a lower bound. At $m=1$ ($n=2$) both sides are $0$, since the Weyl tensor vanishes in dimension $2$, so "every $m\ge1$" is true and trivial at its first value.
- **A-4. `stdJ`** ([L95.lean:269](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L95.lean)). $J_{2k,2k+1}=1$, $J_{2k+1,2k}=-1$, zero elsewhere, so $J$ is skew, each row has one entry $\pm1$ and $J^2=-1$ blockwise. `stdJ_isCx` and `stdJ_four` prove this, and `h4` ($J^4=J^2$) is exactly "entries in $\{0,\pm1\}$".
