**5. ERROR FOUND: `full_hessian.py` (the "Second-variation Hessian attempt" section's Pipeline A) omits the unit-norm normalization the Hessian formula requires, producing a table of numbers that is not just imprecise but qualitatively wrong at unbalanced splits.** FINDINGS.md's own text already flags a discrepancy, "the balanced-split Hessian is only semi-definite, not strictly negative-definite as the other section's table suggests", but does not diagnose why, and does not notice a second, more serious symptom of the same root cause. This review pinned down the exact mechanism: [scripts/full_hessian.py](scripts/full_hessian.py)'s `weyl_sphere_product()` builds $W^*$ directly from the $(a,b,c)$ formula without dividing by $\|W^*\|$ (e.g. $\|W^*\|\approx1.90$ at $n=6$, $\approx4.05$ at $n=12$), and `lam` is computed from this same unnormalized tensor. The second-variation formula $6T(x^*,H,H)-3\lambda|H|^2$, independently re-derived and confirmed correct by both Reviewer 1 and FINDINGS.md itself, is valid only at $|x^*|=1$, for $|x^*|=R\ne1$ the correct formula picks up an extra $R^2$ factor on the cubic term only (worked out from the same Taylor-expansion argument: $x(t)=\cos(t)x^*+\sin(t)RH$ for unit $H$, giving $f''(0)=6R^2T(x^*,H,H)-3\lambda$), so the two terms are weighted incorrectly relative to each other whenever $R\ne1$, not merely rescaled by a harmless constant.

The consequence is directly demonstrable with a test that needs no eigendecomposition at all: $P$ is exactly $O(n)$-invariant along the orbit of $W^*$ under conjugation, so for $H$ tangent to that orbit (e.g. $H=[\mathrm{ad}(\phi_{i,j}),W^*]$ for $i$ in the $P$-block, $j$ in the $Q$-block, genuinely Bianchi/Ricci-flat and orthogonal to $W^*$ by construction), the second variation $d^2P(W^*)[H,H]$ **must** be exactly $0$. Running `full_hessian.py`'s own `sharp_operator`/`QWH` code (copied verbatim, not reimplemented) on this direction gives $-15.6$ at $n=6$, $-52.7$ at $n=8$, $-230.5$ at $n=12$, comfortably negative, not zero. Re-running the identical formula with $W^*$ properly normalized to unit norm gives $0$ to machine precision ($<2\times10^{-15}$) in every case. Script: [scripts/adversarial_check3_orbit_tangent_bug.py](scripts/pipelines/adversarial_check3_orbit_tangent_bug.py).

This explains why `full_hessian.py`'s table never reports any zero eigenvalues (the mathematically-forced $O(n)$-orbit kernel is present but the formula assigns it a spuriously negative value instead of zero), but the more serious consequence is that **the table's claim of comfortable negative-definiteness at unbalanced splits is wrong**: FINDINGS.md's own later, correctly-normalized computation (Pipeline B, and this review's independently bug-fixed reconstruction in point 3 above) proves unbalanced splits are genuine **saddle points**, e.g. $n=6$, $(2,4)$ has 6 strictly positive eigenvalues (each exactly $=\lambda$), $n=8$, $(2,6)$ has 15, yet `full_hessian.py`'s table reports these same cases as "top tangent eigenvalue $=-4.8000$" and "$-8.2857$" respectively, with no hint of instability. **Fix:** normalize `Wstar` to unit Frobenius norm (`Wstar = Wstar / np.linalg.norm(Wstar)`) and recompute `lam` from the normalized tensor before building the Hessian; this also removes the need for the ad hoc `overlap<0.9` masking heuristic used to (unsuccessfully) exclude the $W^*$ direction, which does not correctly identify the tangent space in the current code either. This error does not change FINDINGS.md's final conclusions, since the document already correctly identifies Pipeline B (`hessian_pipeline_b.py`/`rsharp_basis_free.py`/`hessian_fast.py`, and now this review's independent reconstruction) as the trustworthy source and flags Pipeline A's table as superseded, but the table in the "Second-variation Hessian attempt" section itself should be read as unreliable in its specific numeric entries, not merely "imprecise about semi- vs strict definiteness," and its claim of stability at unbalanced splits is actively wrong, not just incomplete.
