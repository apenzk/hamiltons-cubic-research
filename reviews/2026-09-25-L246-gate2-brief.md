## Givens (quoted from the record, take as proved)

- **Setting.** $W$ is a self-adjoint operator on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ (inner product $\langle x,y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(x^{\mathsf T}y)$ on skew matrices) with three eigenvalues $\lambda_0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2$, eigenspaces $E_c$, and isotropy matrices $A_c=\sum_{\psi\in E_c}\psi^{\mathsf T}\psi$ over an orthonormal basis of $E_c$ (so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_c=2\dim E_c$, and for $\psi=e\wedge u$ with $e,u$ orthonormal, $\psi^{\mathsf T}\psi=ee^{\mathsf T}+uu^{\mathsf T}$).
- **L245's identities**: $\sum_cA_c=\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)I$ and $\sum_c\lambda_cA_c=\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(W)=0$ for a Weyl tensor $W$.
- **$S^5\times S^7$ data** (confirmed by two earlier reviewers): its three classes have multiplicities $10,21,35$ and $A_c=4P_5$, $6P_7$, $7P_5+5P_7$ respectively, $P_5,P_7$ the projectors onto the two factors of $\mathbb R^{12}$.

## The block under audit, verbatim

#### Lemma L246, the isotropy of two eigenspaces together is capped by the directions where it is small

*Gate 2 owed. The scalar steps are typed in [L246PairIsotropy.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L246PairIsotropy.lean); the isotropy matrices and the Loewner steps enter as hypotheses.*

**Statement.** Let $W$ be a Weyl tensor with three eigenvalues $\lambda_0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2$ and isotropy matrices $A_c$ as in [L245](../findings/second-order.md#lemma-l245-the-isotropy-of-each-eigenspace-is-capped-direction-by-direction-and-a-star-eigenspace-must-be-small). The $A_c$ commute, and for any two classes $a\ne b$, with $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n$ the eigenvalues of $A_a+A_b$ on a common eigenbasis,
$$\beta_j\le\sum_{i\ne j}\min(1,\beta_i)\qquad\text{for every }j.$$
**Corollary.** If an extreme eigenspace is a star, $E_0=e\wedge U$ with $\dim U=p\le n-2$, then $\lambda_1<0$ (and $\lambda_1>0$ for a star $E_2$). **So on the symmetric family, and whenever $\lambda_1=0$, no extreme eigenspace is a star, at every $n$.**

**Proof.** $\sum_cA_c=(n-1)I$ and $\sum_c\lambda_cA_c=0$ make each $A_c$ affine in $A_0$, so they commute. Fix a common unit eigenvector $e$ and put $M_c(e)=\sum_{\psi\in E_c}(\psi e)(\psi e)^{\mathsf T}$ on $e^\perp$. Summing over an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2$ gives $\sum_cM_c(e)=I-ee^{\mathsf T}$, so $X=M_a(e)+M_b(e)\preceq I$. For $z\perp e$, skew-symmetry and Cauchy–Schwarz give $\langle\psi e,z\rangle^2=\langle e,\psi z\rangle^2\le\lvert\psi z\rvert^2$, so $M_c(e)\preceq\hat A_c$, the compression to $e^\perp$, and $X\preceq\hat A_a+\hat A_b$, whose eigenvalues are the $\beta_i$, $i\ne j$. Let $Q$ project onto those with $\beta_i\ge1$: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}QXQ+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(I-Q)X(I-Q)\le\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q+\sum_{\beta_i<1}\beta_i$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X=\langle(A_a+A_b)e,e\rangle=\beta_j$. *Corollary*: on $Z=(e\oplus U)^\perp\ne0$, $A_0=0$, so $(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)A_2=-\lambda_1(n-1)I$ there, and $A_2\succeq0$ forces $\lambda_1\le0$. At $\lambda_1=0$, $A_2=0$ on $Z$, the pair $(0,2)$ has $\beta=0$ on $Z$ and $\min(1,\beta)\le1$ on $U$, while at $e$, $\beta=p+\tfrac{\lvert\lambda_0\rvert}{\lambda_2}p>p$, which contradicts the inequality. $\square$

**Sharp** at $S^5\times S^7$: with $A_c$ equal to $4P_5$, $6P_7$ and $7P_5+5P_7$ ([L245](../findings/second-order.md#lemma-l245-the-isotropy-of-each-eigenspace-is-capped-direction-by-direction-and-a-star-eigenspace-must-be-small)), the pair of classes of multiplicities $10$ and $35$ has $\beta=11$ on $\mathbb R^5$, $5$ on $\mathbb R^7$, and $11=4+7$. The pair $21,35$ is equal on $\mathbb R^7$ in the same way. It is also sharp at $n=4$ for the Hodge star, where $A_\pm=\tfrac32I$ and $3=3$. *Gate 1: (a) and (c) by these two points, (d) against L245's ceiling, which the single-class form $\alpha_j\le\sum_{i\ne j}\min(1,\alpha_i)$ does not exceed.*

**What it does.** *It is the first ingredient on file that couples two eigenspaces, and it holds at every $n$ with only $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(W)=0$.* **It removes every configuration in [F77](../findings/fence-posts.md#post-f77-r39s-per-eigenspace-ingredients-leave-a-window-of-symmetric-spectra-open-at-every-n-from-19)'s window**, the stars there being symmetric. **Owed**: its reach beyond stars, where the per-vector caps admit near-decomposable eigenspaces. A near-star at $\lambda_1=0$ has $\beta_e\approx(1+r)p$ against a capped remainder near $p$, a margin of $rp$ rather than L245's $p-h_c$.

## Attack items

1. Does the proof establish the displayed inequality for EVERY pair $a\ne b$ and every $j$, step by step from the givens? In particular: that the $A_c$ commute; $\sum_cM_c(e)=I-ee^{\mathsf T}$; $M_c(e)\preceq\hat A_c$; that the compression's eigenvalues are the $\beta_i$, $i\ne j$; the capped-trace step; $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X=\beta_j$.
2. The corollary: is $\lambda_1\le0$ correctly derived for a star $E_0$, what is needed of $p$ (the block says $p\le n-2$), and is the $\lambda_1=0$ contradiction complete? Is the $E_2$ statement right by symmetry?
3. Sharpness: check the two equality claims ($S^5\times S^7$ pairs; the $n=4$ Hodge star with $A_\pm=\tfrac32I$) from the givens.
4. Scope: is anything beyond $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(W)=0$ used? Is "on the symmetric family" (spectrum $(-a,0,a)$) correctly covered by $\lambda_1=0$?
5. Is the inequality trivial, i.e. implied by L245's per-direction ceiling or by $\beta_i\le n-1$? Name a configuration it excludes that L245 does not (the block claims stars at $\lambda_1=0$ with $p\le(n-1)/2$).
6. The "What it does" paragraph: is the claim that it removes F77's window accurate (F77: a star $E_0=e\wedge U$, $9\le p\le\tfrac{n-1}2$, on the symmetric family), and is the near-star margin heuristic labelled as such?
