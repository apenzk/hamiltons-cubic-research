## 1. Target A, the Lean repair

### 1.1 The falsifier now falsifies, and the proof is right

[L191Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L191Statement.lean)'s `falsifier_at_dim4` is a seven-fold conjunction: `inner2 (unitSD 0) (unitSD 0) = 1`, the same at `unitSD 1`, `inner2 (unitSD 0) (unitSD 1) = 0`, the two coincidences, and then `unitSD 1 ≠ unitSD 0` with `unitSD 1 ≠ -unitSD 0`. **The last two clauses are the negation of L191's conclusion at the pair, verbatim**, which is what [r810](REVIEW_r810.md) called *"one matrix entry"* and [r811](REVIEW_r811.md) called *"typed nowhere in the file"*.

The route taken is cheaper than the entry route and is correct. From `h00 : inner2 (unitSD 0) (unitSD 0) = 1` and `h01 : inner2 (unitSD 0) (unitSD 1) = 0`, rewriting `unitSD 1 = unitSD 0` into `h01` gives `(1 : ℝ) = 0`. Rewriting `unitSD 1 = -unitSD 0` gives `-1 = 0` through the new `inner2_neg_right`, whose one-line `simp` over `Matrix.mul_neg` and `Matrix.trace_neg` is the right unfolding for [WeylReal.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean)'s `inner2`. **Both discharges rest only on `inner2_unitSD`, which was already on file**, so the repair costs one lemma and one theorem.

*Screen, reported as a screen.* The two `.olean` files carry the new names (`falsifier_at_dim4` in [L191Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L191Statement.lean)'s, `sdCombo_independent` in [SelfDual.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/SelfDual.lean)'s), and both were written after their sources were last modified. That is evidence the declarations elaborated at the author's build and is not a re-verification today.

### 1.2 The self-wedge at one component IS enough, and the file says why it is not typed

L191 compares `ω ∧ ω` in `Λ⁴`. At `dim V = 4` that space is one-dimensional, so equality at `(0,1,2,3)` is equality of the forms **provided the components really are alternating**, and that is typed: `wedgeSelf_swap01`, `wedgeSelf_swap12`, `wedgeSelf_swap23` and `wedgeSelf_diag01` in [SelfDual.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/SelfDual.lean) give both halves on a skew argument. The remaining step, that an alternating function on four `Fin 4` indices is determined by its value at `(0,1,2,3)`, is elementary and untyped, and the file's *"What is NOT typed here"* names it: *"Only its `(0,1,2,3)` component is proved, which with the alternating lemmas above determines the rest."* **Disclosed, not credited.**

### 1.3 The finding: the common-support hypothesis is the one clause nobody typed and nobody named

L191 reads: **two `2`-forms with a common six-dimensional support**, equal squares and equal self-wedges satisfy `ω₂ = ±ω₁`. To falsify its dim-4 analogue one needs a pair meeting the analogue of all three hypotheses and failing the conclusion. **The typed conjunction supplies the two coincidences and the failure, and replaces the support hypothesis with orthonormality**, which is not a hypothesis of L191 at all: it belongs to the corollary about orthonormal pairs.

Dropping a hypothesis makes a statement stronger, so falsifying the support-free version does not by itself falsify L191-at-four. The gap is small and it is real:

- **The support is met.** A nonzero self-dual `2`-form on `ℝ⁴` satisfies `ω² = -½‖ω‖²Id`, hence is invertible, hence has support all of `ℝ⁴`. Both members of the pair therefore have the same four-dimensional support.
- **It is typed twice in the imports.** `unitSD_sq` at [SelfDual.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/SelfDual.lean) gives `unitSD k * unitSD k = -(1/2) • 1` outright, and `sdCombo_nondecomposable` in [L190Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L190Statement.lean) reads the general case as invertibility.
- **It is in neither the conjunction nor the prose.** The file header, [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md)'s row and [findings/INDEX-results.md](../findings/INDEX-results.md)'s Lean column all list orthonormality, the coincidences and the distinctness, and none of the three mentions support.

*The strongest defence, and it mostly holds.* This is the same species as the defect the round found, one rewrite from a typed theorem in the same import chain, but it is a category weaker: `pair_coincidences_at_dim4` was consistent with L191 being true at four, whereas `falsifier_at_dim4` is inconsistent with any statement that does not add a hypothesis the objects satisfy. **The witness is right, the assembly is one clause short, and the shortfall is in what the row promises rather than in what the mathematics supports.** So the target is CONFIRMED and the residual is a one-line repair: conjoin `unitSD_sq` and say that the support hypothesis is what it discharges.

### 1.4 `sdCombo_independent` gives a basis, and the row's claim about it is accurate

`sdCombo_independent (x y z : ℝ) (h : x • sdForm 0 + y • sdForm 1 + z • sdForm 2 = 0) : x = 0 ∧ y = 0 ∧ z = 0` is linear independence of a three-element family written out. It is read at `(0,1)`, `(0,2)` and `(0,3)`, which is correct: `sdForm 0` is the only one of the three with a nonzero `(0,1)` entry, `sdForm 1` the only one at `(0,2)`, `sdForm 2` the only one at `(0,3)`, each carrying `-1` there because `phi i j` does. **With `selfDual_expand`, which writes an arbitrary member of the submodule in the three, and `sdForm_mem`, which puts them in it, the three are a basis of `selfDualForms`.**

## 3. Target C, the compaction hook

### 3.1 The finding: the verification half names a file that does not exist

[skill_reread.py](../.claude/hooks/skill_reread.py) keys on two flags:

```
LOOPS = {
    ".claude/research-loop-active": ".claude/skills/research-loop/SKILL.md",
    ".claude/verification-loop-active": ".claude/skills/verification-loop/SKILL.md",
}
```

[loop_flag.py](../.claude/hooks/loop_flag.py) defines `FLAG = "research-loop-active"`, `AUDIT_FLAG = "audit-active"` and **`VERIFY_FLAG = "verification-active"`**. The string `verification-loop-active` occurs in this repository exactly once, at [skill_reread.py](../.claude/hooks/skill_reread.py) line 31, and [.gitignore](../.gitignore) ignores `.claude/verification-active`, which is the name the rest of the code uses. **So `os.path.exists` on that key is False in every session, and a compacted verification-loop session gets no marker and no message.**

The rule this hook enforces, [research-loop-reread.md](../.claude/rules/research-loop-reread.md), names both loops by their skills. **The hook covers one of the two.** The audit loop, whose flag is `audit-active` and whose skill is [audit-results](../.claude/skills/audit-results/SKILL.md), is outside the rule and outside the hook, consistently.

*The strongest defence.* The verification loop may never arm a flag at all: its own [SKILL.md](../.claude/skills/verification-loop/SKILL.md) says only *"Do not arm `.claude/research-loop-active` for this loop"* and never tells the reader to write `.claude/verification-active`. **If no flag is ever armed, the key is harmless because nothing would have matched under either name.** That defence makes the dead key invisible rather than correct, and it turns the docstring's claim, that the hook is *"inert unless a loop flag names this session"*, into a description of a hook that is inert for that loop in every session. Either way the second row of `LOOPS` is not a working branch, and the one-line fix is to import the constant rather than to retype the filename, which is [proposal 40](../.claude/skill-proposals.md)'s rule about a pattern verified against a real filename applied to a flag.

### 3.2 The marker is session-bound at write and unbound at read

[skill_reread.py](../.claude/hooks/skill_reread.py) checks `session not in armed` before writing. [loop_stop.py](../.claude/hooks/loop_stop.py) checks `os.path.exists(owed)` and nothing else. The marker file carries a skill path and no session. **So a marker written by one session and never deleted blocks the next loop session in the repository**, telling it to read a skill it may not be running, which is the failure [loop_flag.py](../.claude/hooks/loop_flag.py)'s own docstring says the session binding exists to prevent. Three ways to reach that state: the writing session ends without deleting it, the flag it matched is older than `TTL_HOURS` so `armed_for` returns False and [loop_stop.py](../.claude/hooks/loop_stop.py) never fires to clear it, or the marker is written for a session whose payload has no `session_id`, where `if session and session not in armed` short-circuits and the guard is skipped.

**The reimplementation is the cause.** `skill_reread.py` does its own substring match instead of calling `loop_flag.armed_for`, and so drops the TTL, the `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` fallback and the anchored `^session:\s*(\S+)$` match. *It does inherit the fail-closed rule by accident: a flag with no `session:` line fails `session not in armed` and writes nothing.* This is [proposal 57](../.claude/skill-proposals.md)'s own subject, a detector re-implemented beside a working one, in the commit that sits three commits from the rescoring of that table.

### 3.3 The branch sits before the dispatch, and gates the turn end rather than the next action

**The placement claim is true.** The new block sits after the block counter, after the `stop-declared` check, and before `_skill_dirty` and the audit and verification branches, so it is reached in all three modes. The comment's *"It runs before the branch dispatch"* is the neighbouring comment about the skill-dirty guard, and it is right about both.

**The stronger claim in the commit body is not.** *"loop_stop.py refuses to hand back a plan item while that marker exists, blocking the resumption rather than the stop, because the reread is owed before the next action rather than after."* A Stop hook fires when the model tries to end a turn, and `sys.exit(2)` refuses that ending and injects the stderr. **It cannot gate an action taken inside a turn.** The marker is written at SessionStart, and a compacted loop typically resumes the cycle without ending a turn first, so the first post-compaction cycle action is gated by nothing except `skill_reread.py`'s own stdout, which is context, which is the exact channel the commit body says has *"been in context and skipped"*. The hard gate arrives at the next turn end, which may be several actions later. **The mechanism is a delayed gate, not a pre-action one**, and it is stronger than what was there before by exactly the amount that a blocked turn end is stronger than a printed reminder.

### 3.4 It cannot wedge the loop

*The path I looked for: marker written, model never deletes it, every Stop blocked forever.* It closes. `_bump` increments both counters, and the guard above exits `0` once `blocks >= MAX_BLOCKS` or `total >= MAX_BLOCKS_HARD`, so a session that will not clear the marker is stood down rather than held. `blocks` resets when `HEAD` moves, so a committing loop could re-block repeatedly, but `total` does not reset within a turn and `MAX_BLOCKS_HARD` bounds it, and a fresh user message resets both. **No wedge.** The residual cost of a stale marker is §3.2's cross-session block, which is a wrong instruction rather than a stuck loop.

## 4. Target D, the scoring rule

### 4.1 The occurrence test is right in direction and under-specified in one phrase

The mechanism it names is real and is demonstrated in the file it was written into: [proposal 81](../.claude/skill-proposals.md)'s three instances are a true consequence no declaration states, a vacuous declaration, and a false credit, and the second and third have fixes that point elsewhere, one to the exhibit-one-object check and one in the opposite direction entirely. **Widening a description until unrelated defects fit is a real way to reach a product bar, and the fix-beside-occurrence discipline catches it.**

*The attack the author asked for: does it block legitimate recurrences whose repairs differ only in detail?* **As written, it invites that reading, in one phrase.** *"Ask of each: does the rule as written stop this one, and is the repair the same repair?"* Read strictly, no two repairs are ever the same repair, because they touch different files and different lines. The sentence that follows is looser and is the one that carries the intent: *"Where two incidents need different repairs they are two proposals."* **"Need different repairs" is a test on the KIND of corrective act and is right. "Is the repair the same repair" is a test that no recurrence passes.** The table depends on the loose reading surviving: [63](../.claude/skill-proposals.md) at four occurrences, [9](../.claude/skill-proposals.md) at four, [32](../.claude/skill-proposals.md) at four and [55](../.claude/skill-proposals.md) at six are all recurrences of one failure in different places, and the strict reading takes every one of them to a single occurrence and to impact `3`. **The repair is one word: the same KIND of repair, or the same corrective act, applied in a new place.**

### 4.2 The `!` narrowing collides with the mark's own first disjunct

**The first two disjuncts are counterfactuals about consequence and are indifferent to who caught the defect.** A premise that reads `dim V ≤ dim V + 2` would have falsified a recorded rank bound whether or not two reviewers found it, which is what makes it worth a mark at all. On this record almost every incident surfaces through Gate 2 or a verification round, because that is how incidents reach the record, so the new sentence read as a blanket qualifier strips the mark from nearly everything and leaves *"a critical single occurrence scores 7"* with almost nothing to score. **The narrowing is right about the third disjunct and wrong as a blanket, and one clause fixes it: say which disjunct it qualifies.**
