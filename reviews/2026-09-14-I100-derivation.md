## Batch 1, the constraint inventory at the maximising frame

**Three exact consequences of the block structure, each one line from [L120](../findings/second-order.md).** Write $\Gamma$ for the sign group and $n_b$ for $N$'s component in block $b$.

1. **$T(N,X,X)=0$ for every $X$ in a single character block.** $\Gamma$ acts by algebra automorphisms, so $g\cdot(X\circ X)=\chi(g)^2X\circ X=X\circ X$: the square lies in the $\Gamma$-fixed part, which is $\mathcal D_n$, and $N\perp\mathcal D_n$. In particular $P(\nu)=0$ whenever $\nu$ lies in one block.
2. **H3 on the diagonal locus: $L_d\le\frac m{2\sqrt t}$ on $\mathcal D_n\cap d^\perp$.** For $D'\in\mathcal D_n$, $D'\perp D$ is $D'\perp W^\ast$, and $T(W^\ast,D',D')=\sqrt t\langle L_dD',D'\rangle+\langle N,D'\circ D'\rangle$ with the second term zero by item 1. *New against the record: it couples the diagonal part's second variation to $m$ and $t$.*
3. **Criticality per block.** Pairing $Q(W^\ast)=mW^\ast$ with $n_b$: $m\lVert n_b\rVert^2=2\langle L_Dn_b,n_b\rangle+\langle N\circ N,n_b\rangle$, the refinement of [L214](../findings/second-order.md)'s second relation to one block. The last term collects $n_\chi\circ n_{\chi'}$ over $\chi\chi'=\chi_b$: pair blocks sharing an index feed a pair block, disjoint pair blocks feed a four-set block, and a four-set block squared feeds $\mathcal D_n$ only.

**Frame optimality in block coordinates.** Rotating in the $(k,l)$ plane moves $D$ only inside the pair block $V_{kl}$, with coordinates $x^{(kl)}_i=K_{il}-K_{ik}$ for $i\notin\{k,l\}$ (row sums give $\sum_ix^{(kl)}_i=0$). So the first-order condition of the $t$-maximising frame is **$\langle n_{kl},x^{(kl)}\rangle=0$ in every pair block**, $\binom n2$ scalar conditions, and it says nothing about the four-set components. The second-order condition, along $\xi$: $\lVert\pi_F\,\mathrm{ad}_\xi N\rVert^2\le\lVert\mathrm{ad}_\xi D\rVert^2+\langle\mathrm{ad}_\xi D,\mathrm{ad}_\xi N\rangle$. *Both derived from $t(\varphi)=\lVert\pi_F(e^{-\varphi\,\mathrm{ad}_\xi}W^\ast)\rVert^2$, and neither is used below.*

**The elimination identity.** L214's two relations, $m\sqrt t=tp+(1-t)a'$ and $m=2\sqrt t\,a'+\sqrt{1-t}\,P(\nu)$, eliminate $m$ to
$$a'(3t-1)=t\,p-\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,P(\nu).$$
*Check at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$: $t=\tfrac12$, $a'=p=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, $P(\nu)=0$, both sides $\tfrac{\sqrt3}4$.*

## Batch 2, why the scalar system is weak at small $t$, and where it has teeth

**The plane system alone is satisfiable at every $t<1$ with $m>\Theta_n$** (the sweep's screen, redone with [L218](../findings/second-order.md)'s exact $\Lambda_n=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$): at $n=12$, $t=\tfrac12$, $m=1.24$, $a'=0.7$ gives $p=1.054\le\Theta_{12}$ and $P(\nu)=0.354\le m$, every plane constraint met. *The reason is structural: both relations scale with $t$, so at small $t$ they constrain small quantities, and no lower bound on $t=\max_Ft_F$ at a maximiser is on the record.* **Item 2 above has the same defect, its constant $\frac m{2\sqrt t}$ growing as $t\to0$.**

**The teeth appear once $P(\nu)$ is exact rather than bounded by $m$.** With $\nu$ in one four-set block, item 1 gives $P(\nu)=0$, and the elimination identity becomes $a'=\frac{tp}{3t-1}$, so $t>\tfrac13$ and $m=2\sqrt t\,a'=\frac{2t^{3/2}p}{3t-1}$.

- **At $d=$ padded $S^2\times S^2$ with $\nu$ its four-set top eigenvector** ($p=a'=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$ by [L57](../findings/problem.md) and [L121](../findings/second-order.md)), the system pins $t=\tfrac12$ and $m=\sqrt{3/2}$: **it reconstructs $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$**, which is excluded by H1 at $n\ge12$ since $\sqrt{3/2}<\Theta_n$. *The record's "at padded $\mathbb{CP}^2$, $t=\tfrac12$ and both equal $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$" is this configuration.*
- **In general**, write $e=e(d)=\tfrac12\sqrt{E_{\max}(d)}$ for the largest four-set eigenvalue of $L_d$ ([L121](../findings/second-order.md)), so that $a'\le e$. The constraints $m\ge\Theta_n$ and $a'\le e$ read $p\ge\Theta_n\frac{3t-1}{2t^{3/2}}$ and $p\le e\,\frac{3t-1}t$, an interval that is nonempty only when $e\ge\frac{\Theta_n}{2\sqrt t}>\frac{\Theta_n}2$. Both endpoints increase in $t$, so the union over admissible $t\in[\Theta_n^2/4e^2,1)$ is $[\,3e-4e^3/\Theta_n^2,\ 2e\,)$.

**So the single-four-set case of the far region is one inequality on the diagonal locus:**
$$\textbf{(J)}\qquad P(d)\ <\ 3e(d)-\frac{4e(d)^3}{\Theta_n^2}\quad\text{for every unit }d\in\mathcal D_n\text{ with }e(d)>\tfrac{\Theta_n}2 .$$
*If (J) holds, no global maximiser has its off-diagonal part inside a single four-set block, at every $n\ge12$. It uses H1, H4 through L57 only, criticality, the block structure, and nothing about the frame.*

**Where (J) stands.** At $d=$ padded $S^2\times S^2$, the right side is $\tfrac{3\sqrt3}2(1-\Theta_n^{-2})$: $0.898$ at $n=12$ against $P(d)=0.866$, a margin of $3.5\%$, rising to $1.299$ as $n\to\infty$. At the balanced product $e=\frac{\sqrt{2k-1}}{k\sqrt{k-1}}$ ($n=2k$, which is $0.247$ at $n=12$, against $\Theta_{12}/2=0.618$, from the split four-set with $E=4(\alpha-\gamma)^2$), so it is outside the class (J) quantifies over, consistent with [L27](../findings/nondegeneracy.md). *A $d$ with $e>\Theta_n/2$ carries at least $\Theta_n^2/3$ ($0.51$ at $n=12$) of its mass on one four-set, so (J) needs a bound on $P(d)$ in terms of the four-set mass, with the cross terms and the row-sum coupling handled; that derivation is not done here.* **The margin is thin at $n=12$ and at the extreme point, so (J) is plausible rather than obvious, and a screen over $d$ concentrated on one four-set is the first thing to run before deriving it.**

## Batch 3, the mixed case, and the verdict on the item

**With $\nu$ spread over several blocks, $P(\nu)$ is a sum over triangles of pair blocks and over disjoint pairs feeding a four-set, and the orbit directions $x^{(kl)}$ sit inside the pair blocks.** The frame condition removes exactly one direction per pair block, so the pair-block analogue of $e$ is the top of $\tfrac12[\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(d)-\kappa+S]$ on $\mathbf1^\perp\cap(x^{(kl)})^\perp$, which at the balanced product is $\Theta_n(\tfrac12-\gamma)$, $\gamma=\frac1{n-2}$, by [L216](../findings/second-order.md)'s corollary. *That is where the near-region gap re-enters, and it is the only place the frame optimality has been seen to bite.*

**Verdict.** The frame split at the $t$-maximising frame does not close the far region by scalar constraints: the identities are weak at small $t$, and no lower bound on $t$ at a maximiser exists on the record or emerged here. What the item produced is (i) three exact block facts and the frame conditions in coordinates, (ii) the elimination identity, and (iii) the reduction of the single-four-set case to (J), a finite inequality on the diagonal locus with a $3.5\%$ margin at its extreme point at $n=12$. **Recommended next step, on Opus: screen (J) on the family of diagonal $d$ with mass $M\in[0.51,1]$ on one four-set (seconds of arithmetic), then, if it survives, derive it from L57's chain with the four-set mass as a parameter and type it.** The mixed case waits on a pair-block analogue of $e$ under the frame condition.

## Opus, I101 restated: (J) is the diagonal theorem on a fixed-point subalgebra

**(J) is equivalent to $\sup P=\Theta_n$ on the linear subspace $\mathcal S_Q=\mathcal D_n\oplus V_Q$**, $Q=\{i,j,k,l\}$. For unit $d$ and unit $\nu\in V_Q$, $P(cd+s\nu)=c^3P(d)+3cs^2\langle L_d\nu,\nu\rangle$, since $T(d,d,\nu)=0$ and $P(\nu)=0$ by batch 1's item 1, and $\langle L_d\nu,\nu\rangle\le e(d)$ with equality on the top eigenvector. Maximising $f(c)=(p-3e)c^3+3ec$ over $c\in[0,1]$: for $p<2e$ the maximum is $2e\sqrt{e/(3e-p)}$, which is below $\Theta_n$ exactly when $p<3e-4e^3/\Theta_n^2$, and for $p\ge2e$ it is $p\le\Theta_n$. For $e\le\Theta_n/2$ the inequality is automatic, since $p<2e\le3e-4e^3/\Theta_n^2$ there. **So the maximiser machinery of batch 2 was not needed: (J) is Conjecture A on $\mathcal S_Q$.**

**$\mathcal S_Q$ is a subalgebra, the fixed space of $H=\ker\chi_Q$, $\chi_Q=\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k\varepsilon_l$.** The characters trivial on $H$ are the trivial one and $\chi_Q$ alone: a pair character $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$ is $-1$ on the flip $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$, which lies in $H$, taking $c\in Q\setminus\{a,b\}$ when $a\in Q$ and $c\notin Q\cup\{a,b\}$ when $a,b\notin Q$, and a four-set character $\chi_{Q'}$, $Q'\ne Q$, is $-1$ on $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$ with $a\in Q'\setminus Q$ and $c\notin Q\cup Q'$, which exists at $n\ge9$. A fixed space of a group of automorphisms is a subalgebra, so $Q(\mathcal S_Q)\subseteq\mathcal S_Q$, and by symmetric criticality **the maximum of $P$ on $\mathcal S_Q$ is attained at a genuine critical point of $P$ on $\mathcal W_n$**. Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ lies in $\mathcal S_Q$, with $t=\tfrac12$.

**The critical equations on $\mathcal S_Q$, $W=D+\nu$:**
$$D\circ D+\nu\circ\nu=\theta D,\qquad L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\,\nu .$$
*$\nu\circ\nu$ is diagonal and supported on $Q$*: in $\nu^\#_{\alpha\alpha}=-\tfrac12\sum c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}c_{\alpha\beta'\gamma'}\nu_{\beta\beta'}\nu_{\gamma\gamma'}$ a pair $\alpha$ leaving $Q$ forces one of $\gamma,\gamma'$ to leave $Q$, where $\nu$ vanishes. With zero row sums it is the pattern $K_{ij}=K_{kl}=x$, $K_{ik}=K_{jl}=y$, $K_{il}=K_{jk}=z$, $x+y+z=0$. **So I101 is the diagonal criticality equation $S^{\circ2}+S^2=\lambda S+D$ of [L57](../findings/problem.md) with a two-parameter forcing on $Q$, coupled to a $2\times2$ eigenvalue condition from [L121](../findings/second-order.md)**, and the natural attack is L57's own chain (the Dirichlet step at the top eigenvector, the projection $Z(v)$, the fourth moment) carried through the forcing term.

## Opus, first attempt at I101: L57's chain through the forcing term, and where it breaks

**The forcing, in closed form** (derived from $(A\#B)_{\alpha\alpha}$, whose only surviving terms pair complementary edges of $Q$, and screened at $n=8$ with residual $0$). In signed coordinates $\nu=a\,e_{ij}\odot e_{kl}+b\,e_{ik}\odot e_{jl}+c\,e_{il}\odot e_{jk}$ with Bianchi $a+b+c=0$ and $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)=:2\rho^2$,
$$\nu\circ\nu=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\big(K_{ij}=K_{kl}=2a^2-b^2-c^2,\ K_{ik}=K_{jl}=2b^2-a^2-c^2,\ K_{il}=K_{jk}=2c^2-a^2-b^2\big),$$
so its entries are $\rho^2\cos2\varphi$, $\rho^2\cos(2\varphi\mp\tfrac{4\pi}3)$, the $4\times4$ matrix $F$ on $Q$ has spectrum $\{0,2x,2y,2z\}$ and $\lambda_{\max}(F)\le2\rho^2=1-t$, and $P(\nu)=0$ as batch 1 said.

**The chain.** The off-diagonal part of $S^{\circ2}+S^2$ already has zero row sums, so on $\mathcal S_Q$ criticality is exactly $S^{\circ2}+S^2=\theta S-F+\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(r)$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S^2=2t$. Pairing with $v\otimes v$, $v$ the top unit eigenvector of $S$, as in [L57](../findings/problem.md) step 1:
$$\theta\mu=\mu^2-\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}^2(v_i-v_j)^2+v^{\mathsf T}Fv\ \le\ \tfrac{n-1}n\mu^2+(1-t)\sigma,\qquad\sigma=\sum_{a\in Q}v_a^2 .$$
Pairing with $S$ gives $\langle F,S\rangle=\theta(1-t)$ and $P(D)=\tfrac\theta2(3t-1)$, L214's relations again. With step 2 scaled, $\tfrac{n-1}n\mu\le\sqrt t\,\Theta_n$, and $S_{ij}\le(1-\tfrac2n)\mu$ gives $\mu\ge\frac{n\theta(3t-1)}{4(n-1)t}$.

**It closes near the balanced product and fails at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, which lies in $\mathcal S_Q$ at $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$, within $0.9\%$ of $\Theta_{12}$.** Near $t=1$ with $v$ two-valued, $\sigma=4/n$ and the forcing costs $(1-t)\frac{4(n-1)}{n^2\Theta_n}$ against a gain $\tfrac12(1-t)\Theta_n$, which wins at $n=12$ ($0.61<1.53$ after clearing). **At padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$** ($t=\tfrac12$, $v=\tfrac12(1,1,-1,-1,0,\dots)$, $\mu=\sqrt{2/3}$, $\sigma=1$) the forcing bound is exact, $v^{\mathsf T}Fv=\tfrac12=(1-t)\sigma$, **but the Dirichlet Cauchy--Schwarz is not**: it gives $\sum s^2(v_i-v_j)^2\ge\mu^2/n=0.056$ against the true $\tfrac16$, a factor $3$, because $v$ lives on four of the $n$ coordinates. The resulting bound, $\max_\mu\big[\tfrac{n-1}n\mu+(1-t)\sigma/\mu\big]$, is $1.40$ at $t=\tfrac12$, $\sigma=1$, and still $1.37$ after sharpening step 2 with $\sum v^4\ge\sigma^2/4$, against $\Theta_{12}=1.236$.

**Verdict.** L57's chain is exact on sphere products and loses a factor $3$ in its Dirichlet step on a vector supported on $Q$, which is exactly where $\mathcal S_Q$'s competitor sits. *So I101 needs a Dirichlet step that sees the support of $v$*, for instance Cauchy--Schwarz over the pairs meeting $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}v$ only, where $\sum(v_i-v_j)^2$ is $|{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}v}|\cdot(n-|{\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}v}|)$-weighted rather than $n$, *and a margin of $0.9\%$ at $n=12$ means every other step must be exact at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ too.*

## Opus, second attempt at I101: the frame split one level down, inside $\mathcal D_n$

**The $Q$-patterns $\Pi_Q=\{K_{ij}=K_{kl}=x,\ K_{ik}=K_{jl}=y,\ K_{il}=K_{jk}=z,\ x+y+z=0\}$ are a two-dimensional subalgebra of $\mathcal D_n$**: a matrix supported on $Q$ with zero row sums has $S^{\circ2}+S^2$ supported on $Q$ off the diagonal, with zero row sums. So for $d=q+r$, $q\in\Pi_Q$, $r\in\mathcal D_n\ominus\Pi_Q$, **$\langle q\circ q,r\rangle=0$ and**
$$P(d)=P(q)+3\langle L_qr,r\rangle+P(r),\qquad e(d)=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q\rVert .$$
*The second because $E$ depends on $K$ only through $A-B$ and $B-C$, which vanish on $\Pi_Q^\perp$ (orthogonality to every pattern is $A=B=C$), and $E=3\lVert q\rVert^2$ on $\Pi_Q$.* On $\Pi_Q$, $P(q)=18xyz\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q\rVert^3$ with equality at the $S^2\times S^2$ shape. Both identities screened at $n=8,12,16$ to $10^{-14}$.

**The coupling constant is $\lambda_R=\sup\langle L_qr,r\rangle=1/\sqrt3$ at every $n$ screened** ($n=8,12,16$, attained at the $S^2\times S^2$ shape), *a screen of the top eigenvalue, not yet derived.*

**Decoupled, it fails, and F67 says why.** With $m=\lVert q\rVert^2$, (J) needs $P(d)<\tfrac{3\sqrt3}2\sqrt m\,(1-m/\Theta_n^2)$ for $m>\Theta_n^2/3$. The decoupled bound $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2m^{3/2}+\sqrt3\sqrt m(1-m)+\Theta_n(1-m)^{3/2}$ is exact at $m=1$ (margin $0.0315$ at $n=12$) and **$1.347$ against $1.236$ at $m=\Theta_{12}^2/3$**: it would need $\lambda_R<0.472$ there. *The two suprema, $\langle L_qr,r\rangle$ and $P(r)$, are taken at different $r$, which is post F67's shape at one level down.* **So I101 needs the coupled supremum of $\sqrt3\sqrt m(1-m)\langle L_q\hat r,\hat r\rangle+(1-m)^{3/2}P(\hat r)$ over unit $\hat r$**, a cubic on the sphere of $\Pi_Q^\perp$, and the question for the next cycle is what $P$ is on the top eigenspace of $L_q$ there.

**Screened at $n=12$ with $q$ at the $S^2\times S^2$ shape, the coupling has the structure the decoupled bound threw away.** $L_q$ on $\Pi_Q^\perp$ has top eigenvalue $1/\sqrt3$ with multiplicity $8$ ($=n-4$, to be confirmed at other $n$), **next eigenvalue $0$**, and **$P\equiv0$ on the top eigenspace** (2000 samples, $\lvert P\rvert<10^{-4}$). *So a direction that gains from the coupling gains nothing from $P(r)$, and one with $P(r)$ large gets nothing from the coupling.* The coupled supremum then stays inside (J): the gap to (J)'s right side is $-0.144$ at $m=\Theta_{12}^2/3$, at most $-0.078$ for $m\le0.94$, and $-0.0315$ at $m=1$, where the bound is exact. *Near $m=1$ even the decoupled bound works, its slope in $1-m$ being $+0.43$ against (J)'s $+1.25$.* **These gaps replace a first run whose coupling weight was $\sqrt3\sqrt m(1-m)$ instead of $3\sqrt m(1-m)$, which had reported $-0.339$.** **So the analytic route for I101 is three facts: the spectrum of $L_q$ on $\Pi_Q^\perp$ in closed form, $P=0$ on its top eigenspace, and a bound on the mixed terms $T(u,u,w)$, $T(u,w,w)$ between that eigenspace and its complement.** *The shape $q\ne S^2\times S^2$ (the angle $\varphi$ in $\Pi_Q$) is not screened here, and (J)'s own screen over all $d$ covers it.*

**The spectrum of $L_q$ on $\Pi_Q^\perp$, screened at $n=8,10,12,14$ and uniform in $n$:** $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ with multiplicity $n-4$, $-\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$ with multiplicity $2(n-4)$, and $0$ on the $\binom{n-4}2$ outside pairs. *It is $\tfrac12$ of $q$'s spectrum on $\mathbf 1^\perp\subset\mathbb R^Q$, $\{\tfrac2{\sqrt3},-\tfrac1{\sqrt3},-\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\}$, once per outside index $m$, acting on the column $(r_{am})_{a\in Q}$.* **Derived by hand for $n-5$ of the $n-4$ top directions**: $r_{am}=s_m w_a$ with $w=\tfrac12(1,1,-1,-1)$, $\sum_ms_m=0$ and nothing else nonzero lies in $\mathcal D_n\ominus\Pi_Q$, has $\langle L_qr,r\rangle=\tfrac12\,w^{\mathsf T}qw\,\lVert r\rVert^2=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\lVert r\rVert^2$, and **$P(r)=0$ exactly**: its cubes sum to $\sum_ms_m^3\sum_aw_a^3=0$ and its support graph is bipartite, so it has no triangles. The last direction ($\sum_ms_m\ne0$) carries a $Q$-internal correction $u_{ij}=-u_{kl}=-\tfrac\sigma2$, mass $\tfrac1{n-2}$ of the eigenspace in the screen.

**The coupling bound, derived by hand at every shape: $\langle L_{\hat q}r,r\rangle\le\max(x,y,z)\,\lVert r\rVert^2\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\lVert r\rVert^2$ on $\Pi_Q^\perp$**, for unit $\hat q$ with pattern $(x,y,z)$, $x+y+z=0$, $2(x^2+y^2+z^2)=1$.

*Proof.* Split $r$ into its $Q$-internal entries $u$ (with $u_{01}=p$, $u_{23}=p'$, $u_{02}=a$, $u_{13}=b$, $u_{03}=c$, $u_{12}=d$), its $Q$-to-outside columns $c_m\in\mathbb R^Q$, and its outside entries. Then
$$2\langle L_{\hat q}r,r\rangle=\sum_{i\ne j}\hat q_{ij}r_{ij}^2+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat qr^2)=\underbrace{2\big[x(p^2+p'^2)+y(a^2+b^2)+z(c^2+d^2)\big]+2\Sigma}_{\text{internal}}+\sum_mc_m^{\mathsf T}\hat q\,c_m ,$$
the outside entries not appearing, where expanding $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat qu^2)$ over the four choices of the third index gives
$$\Sigma=z(p+p')(a+b)+y(p+p')(c+d)+x(a+b)(c+d).$$
Orthogonality to $\Pi_Q$ is $p+p'=a+b=c+d=:s$, so $\Sigma=s^2(x+y+z)=0$. The internal part is then at most $2\max(x,y,z)X_{\mathrm{int}}$, and since $\hat q$ has spectrum $\{0,2x,2y,2z\}$ on $\mathbb R^Q$ the columns give at most $2\max(x,y,z)X_{\mathrm{cross}}$. With $\max(x,y,z)\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ under the two constraints, $\langle L_{\hat q}r,r\rangle\le\max(x,y,z)(X_{\mathrm{int}}+X_{\mathrm{cross}})$. $\square$ **Equality needs no outside mass, $s=0$, internal mass on the pairing of the largest of $x,y,z$ only, and columns along its eigenvector**, which is the screened top eigenspace, with the $p'=-p$ correction. *So the $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ screened above is a theorem, and it depends on the shape through $\max(x,y,z)$, while $P(\hat q)=18xyz$ and $e=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\sqrt m$ do not see which of $x,y,z$ is largest.*

**What remains for I101** is the coupled term: a bound on $3\sqrt m(1-m)\langle L_{\hat q}\hat r,\hat r\rangle+(1-m)^{3/2}P(\hat r)$ jointly, using that the coupling lives on $X_{\mathrm{int}}+X_{\mathrm{cross}}$ while the outside entries, where the coupling vanishes, carry what $P(\hat r)$ can reach.

**A cleaner reduction, and the statistic it wants is not the row mass.** Since $\nu\circ\nu\in\Pi_Q$ and $r\perp\Pi_Q$, $\langle L_r\nu,\nu\rangle=\langle\nu\circ\nu,r\rangle=0$, so for $W=q+\nu+r\in\mathcal S_Q$
$$P(W)=P(q+\nu)+3\langle L_qr,r\rangle+P(r),\qquad q+\nu\in\Pi_Q\oplus V_Q\subset\text{padded }\mathcal W_4,$$
and $P(q+\nu)\le\sqrt{3/2}\,A^3$, $A=\lVert q+\nu\rVert$, by $M(4)=\sqrt{3/2}$. With the coupling bound, I101 would follow from $\sqrt{3/2}A^3+\sqrt3A(1-A^2)X+(1-A^2)^{3/2}\varphi(X)\le\Theta_n$, where $X$ is $\hat r$'s mass on pairs meeting $Q$ and $\varphi(X)=\sup\{P(\hat r):X(\hat r)=X\}$. **Screened at $n=12$, it fails**: $\varphi$ stays near $\Theta_{12}$ across the range ($1.146$ at $X=0$, which is $\theta_0(8)$ on the outside block, $1.236$ at $X=0.6$, $0.943$ at $X=1$), and the bound exceeds $\Theta_{12}$ by up to $0.28$ for $X\ge0.3$. *The vectors maximising $P(\hat r)$ at a given row mass spread that mass like a balanced product, while the coupling wants it on $w=\tfrac12(1,1,-1,-1)$ columns, so the row mass cannot separate them.* **This is F67's shape a third time**: the coupled supremum clears (J) (the earlier screen), and every scalar statistic tried so far that separates the two terms loses it. **The statistic that would work has to see the direction of each column $c_m$ against $w$, not only its length.**

**Fourth statistic, the coupling value itself, with the diagonal share of the $\mathcal W_4$ piece kept: it passes by screen, thinly.** Write $q+\nu$ with $\lVert q\rVert=A\cos\beta$, $\lVert\nu\rVert=A\sin\beta$. Then $P(q+\nu)\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2A^3(\cos^3\beta+3\cos\beta\sin^2\beta)$, its maximum over $\beta$ being $\sqrt{3/2}A^3$ at $\cos\beta=1/\sqrt2$ (padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$), and the coupling carries $\lVert q\rVert=A\cos\beta$ rather than $A$. With $\tilde\varphi(\lambda)=\sup\{P(\hat r):\langle L_{\hat q}\hat r,\hat r\rangle=\lambda\}$ screened at $n=12$ ($\hat q$ at the $S^2\times S^2$ shape, twelve values of $\lambda$ on $[-\tfrac14,\tfrac1{\sqrt3}]$, 25 constrained restarts each),
$$\sup_{A,\beta}\ \tfrac{\sqrt3}2A^3(c^3+3cs^2)+3A\,c\,(1-A^2)\lambda+(1-A^2)^{3/2}\tilde\varphi(\lambda)\ \le\ \Theta_{12}-0.0015$$
at every $\lambda$ on the grid, the tightest being $\lambda\approx0.28$ at $A=0.26$ and $\lambda\approx-0.02$ at $A=0$ (a near-balanced $\hat r$ with $P=1.2342$). *Dropping the diagonal share ($c=1$ in the coupling, $\sqrt{3/2}A^3$ for the rest) had failed by $+0.0126$.* **So the reduction that survives is I101 $\Leftarrow$ a bound $\tilde\varphi(\lambda)\le\Psi(\lambda)$ on one function, with margin $0.0015$ at $n=12$ on a coarse grid from a sampled floor of $\tilde\varphi$, which is too thin to trust and too thin for a lossy analytic step.** *The $\lambda$-constrained supremum of $P$ is L57's problem with one quadratic constraint, so I101 has been moved, not shrunk.*

## Opus, fifth attempt at I101: the critical equations close it, with equality at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$

**Claim.** *Let $n\ge5$ and $W=q+r+\nu$ be a unit critical point of $P$ lying in $\mathcal S_Q=\mathcal D_n\oplus V_Q$, with $q\in\Pi_Q$, $r\in\mathcal D_n\ominus\Pi_Q$, $\nu\in V_Q$, $\nu\ne0$ and $\theta>0$. Then $\theta\le\sqrt{3/2}$, with equality exactly when $r=0$, $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=\tfrac12$ and $q$ has the $S^2\times S^2$ shape.* **Hence $\sup P=\Theta_n$ on the unit sphere of $\mathcal S_Q$ at every $n\ge12$, attained only on $\mathcal D_n$, which is I101 and (J).**

**Proof.** The maximum of $P$ on $\mathcal S_Q$ is a critical point of $P$ on $\mathcal W_n$, $\mathcal S_Q$ being a subalgebra. If its $\nu$ vanishes it is diagonal and [L57](../findings/problem.md) gives $\theta\le\Theta_n$. Otherwise:

1. **Parity of the product.** $D=q+r\in\mathcal D_n$, so $D\circ D\in\mathcal D_n$ and $D\circ\nu\in V_Q$ ([L120](../findings/second-order.md)), and $\nu\circ\nu\in\Pi_Q$ (batch 1 of the forcing attempt above). So $Q(W)=\theta W$ splits into $D\circ D+\nu\circ\nu=\theta D$ and $L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$.
2. **$L_r\nu=0$.** $L_r\nu\in V_Q$, and for $\nu'\in V_Q$, $\langle L_r\nu,\nu'\rangle=\langle r,\nu\circ\nu'\rangle=0$ since $\nu\circ\nu'\in\Pi_Q\perp r$. So $L_q\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$.
3. **The eigenvalue fixes $\lVert q\rVert$.** $L_q$ on the two-dimensional $V_Q$ has spectrum $\pm\tfrac12\sqrt{E(q)}=\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q\rVert$ ([L121](../findings/second-order.md), with $E=3\lVert q\rVert^2$ on $\Pi_Q$). With $\theta>0$, $\lVert q\rVert^2=\theta^2/3$.
4. **Pair the $\mathcal D_n$ equation with $q$.** $\langle q\circ r,q\rangle=\langle r,q\circ q\rangle=0$ as $\Pi_Q$ is a subalgebra, $\langle r\circ r,q\rangle=\langle L_qr,r\rangle=:\kappa$ and $\langle\nu\circ\nu,q\rangle=\langle L_q\nu,\nu\rangle=\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, so
$$P(q)+\kappa+\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu\rVert^2=\theta\lVert q\rVert^2=\tfrac{\theta^3}3 .$$
5. **The two bounds on $\Pi_Q$**, both derived above: $P(q)\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q\rVert^3=\tfrac{\theta^3}6$, and $\kappa\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\lVert q\rVert\lVert r\rVert^2=\tfrac\theta3\lVert r\rVert^2$. Then step 4 gives $\tfrac{\theta^3}6-\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu\rVert^2\le\kappa\le\tfrac\theta3\lVert r\rVert^2$.
6. **Arithmetic.** With $\lVert r\rVert^2=1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, dividing by $\theta$: $\tfrac5{18}\theta^2\le\tfrac13+\tfrac16\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, and $\lVert\nu\rVert^2\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3$ gives $\tfrac65\theta^2\le\tfrac95$, so $\theta^2\le\tfrac32$. Equality forces $\lVert r\rVert=0$, $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=\tfrac12$ and $P(q)=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert q\rVert^3$. $\square$

**Why this closes where four attempts did not.** *Each earlier attempt bounded $P$ on the whole sphere of $\mathcal S_Q$ through statistics of an arbitrary point; this one uses the maximiser's own critical equations, and step 3 is the lever: the $\tfrac\theta2$-eigenvalue condition on $V_Q$ pins $\lVert q\rVert$ to $\theta/\sqrt3$, which makes every bound in step 5 a function of $\theta$ with its equality case at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ for both at once.* **No bound is decoupled from the other at a different point, which is what F67 forbids**: $P(q)$ and $\kappa$ are both maximised at the $S^2\times S^2$ shape.
