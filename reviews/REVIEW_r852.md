## 2. (B) The $\ell=2$ specialisation, step by step, and then without the paper

**Type I.** At $\ell=2$ the $f$'s number $\ell-1=1$, the independent set is $f_1,g_1,\dots,g_r$ of size $\ell+r-1=r+1$, and $E=\mathrm{span}\{f_1\wedge g_i\}=f_1\wedge\mathrm{span}\{g_1,\dots,g_r\}$. **A pencil, and $\dim V_E=\ell-1=1$ is the shared line.** Correct as transcribed.

**Type II at $r=3$.** The $u$'s number $\ell-r+1=0$, the independent set is $g_1,g_2,g_3$ of size $\ell+1=3$, and the spanning elements are $g_1\wedge\cdots\check g_i\cdots\wedge g_r$, that is $g_2\wedge g_3$, $g_1\wedge g_3$, $g_1\wedge g_2$. **So $E=\Lambda^2\mathrm{span}\{g_1,g_2,g_3\}$, and $\dim V^E=\ell+1=3$.** Correct as transcribed.

**Type II at $r=2$, which the brief asks about.** One $u$ survives, $u_1$, and the two spanning elements are $u_1\wedge g_2$ and $u_1\wedge g_1$, so $E=u_1\wedge\mathrm{span}\{g_1,g_2\}$. **That is a pencil**, which is the paper's own remark that the two notions are identical at $r=2$, and Lemma 5's dimensions agree from both sides: type I gives $(\dim V_E,\dim V^E)=(1,3)$ and type II gives $(\ell-r+1,\ell+1)=(1,3)$.

**Type II at $r=1$.** Two $u$'s survive and the single spanning element is $u_1\wedge u_2$, the deleted $\check g_1$ leaving the empty wedge. **So $E=\mathrm{span}\{u_1\wedge u_2\}$, a one-dimensional pencil**, and the type II clause additionally needs $g_1$ to exist independently, hence $\dim V\ge3$.

**So type II at $r=1,2$ gives nothing the two-item statement omits**, both cases being pencils, and *and nothing else* is licensed at $\ell=2$: every close subspace is a pencil, of any $r$ for which one exists, or $\Lambda^2U$ at $\dim U=3$. **The distinctness at $r=3$ is real**, $\Lambda^2U$ having no common line, its three basis planes meeting pairwise in three distinct lines.

**The record does not need to import the general-$\ell$ theorem for this, and that is worth recording.** The $\ell=2$ case follows from the paper's Lemma 3 alone, which is Bourbaki's Exercise 17 (c), in ten lines:

1. **$r=3$.** Let $E$ be all-decomposable with basis $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$ and planes $P_1,P_2,P_3$. Every $\omega_i+\omega_j$ is decomposable, so Lemma 3 gives $\dim P_i\cap P_j=1$ for each pair. If $P_3\cap P_1$ and $P_3\cap P_2$ are distinct lines then $P_3\subseteq P_1+P_2=:U$, a three-space, so all three planes lie in $U$ and $E\subseteq\Lambda^2U$, which is itself three-dimensional, whence $E=\Lambda^2U$. Otherwise $P_3\cap P_1=P_3\cap P_2\subseteq P_1\cap P_2$, so a single line lies in all three planes and $E$ is a pencil.
2. **$r\ge4$ contains no $\Lambda^2U$.** If $\mathrm{span}\{\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3\}=\Lambda^2U$ and $\omega_4\notin\Lambda^2U$, then $P_4\cap U$ has dimension at most one while $P_4$ must meet each of the three coordinate planes of $U$ in a line, forcing that one line into $P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3=0$.
3. **$r\ge4$ is a pencil.** Every triple is then a pencil by (1) and (2), the common line of $\{1,2,3\}$ and of $\{1,2,4\}$ both lie in $P_1\cap P_2$, which is a single line by Lemma 3, so all planes share one line $L$ and $E\subseteq L\wedge V$, whence $E=L\wedge U$ for $U=\{u:L\wedge u\in E\}$.

**A byproduct the record omits and should carry.** Corollary 7 of the paper reads at $\ell=2$, $m=n$ as $\mu=\max\{2,n-2\}+1$, with type I capped at $m-\ell+1=n-1$ and type II at $\ell+1=3$. **So an all-decomposable subspace of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ has dimension at most $n-1$ for $n\ge4$**, against $N=\binom n2$ available. That is the sharp form of *of any dimension* at [diagonal-half.md:1132](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1132), which I do not read as false, a classification statement quantifying over the $r$ that occur, and it is the number a consumer of sub-step (a) will want.

## 3. (C) The scope claim, the witness, and the term the chain is missing

**The witness is right, twice over.** For $\omega=\cos t\,e_1\wedge e_2+\sin t\,e_3\wedge e_4$ the Pfaffian of the coefficient array is $a_{12}a_{34}-a_{13}a_{24}+a_{14}a_{23}=\cos t\sin t=\tfrac12\sin2t$, and the self-wedge is $\omega\wedge\omega=2\cos t\sin t\,e_{1234}=\sin2t\,e_{1234}$, which is twice the Pfaffian as [SelfDual.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/SelfDual.lean) fixes. **So the span is not all-decomposable, and $\{e_1\wedge e_2,e_3\wedge e_4\}$ is a decomposable basis of it.** The two figures in the record agree with each other: [notes.md](../references/decomposable-subspaces-grassmann-codes/notes.md)'s $\cos t\sin t$ and [diagonal-half.md:1134](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1134)'s $\tfrac12\sin2t$ are one number. **Independently the span is not close**, being two-dimensional with $\dim P_1\cap P_2=0$ against Lemma 3's $\ell-1=1$.

**C1 is the right relation.** All-decomposable implies every basis decomposable, so the second condition is implied by the first, and the witness gives a subspace of the second kind that is not of the first. **Strictly weaker is correct**, and the operational consequence is the one the record draws: Theorem 6's hypothesis is unavailable at an arbitrary eigenspace, so the classification bounds the problem rather than solving it.

**C3 is where it goes wrong, and the fix strengthens the item.** The obligation at [diagonal-half.md:1114](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1114) is that *some decomposable orthonormal eigenbasis of $W$ lies in a common frame*. So the per-eigenspace condition is not (iii) *a decomposable basis exists* but (ii) *an orthonormal decomposable basis exists*, and the chain has three terms:

$$\text{(i) every element decomposable}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{(ii) an orthonormal decomposable basis}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{(iii) a decomposable basis}.$$

**Both inclusions are strict.** (i) to (ii) is strict by the record's own witness. **(ii) to (iii) is strict, and here is a closed-form witness for it**, which I believe is new to the record. In $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$ put

$$E=\mathrm{span}\{e_1\wedge e_2,\;e_3\wedge e_4,\;(e_1+e_3)\wedge(e_2+e_4)\}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{12},\,e_{34},\,e_{14}-e_{23}\},$$

three-dimensional with a decomposable basis by construction. **It has no orthonormal decomposable basis.** For $\mathrm{Pf}$ read as a quadratic form on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$, the sum $\sum_i\mathrm{Pf}(\omega_i)$ over an orthonormal basis of a subspace is the trace of that form restricted to the subspace, hence basis-independent, and it is exactly the record's $\mathfrak b(P_E)$: at paragraph (iv)'s $E$ the record computes $\mathrm{Pf}(\omega_1)+\mathrm{Pf}(\omega_2)=\tfrac12-\tfrac12=0=\mathfrak b(P_E)$ by the same rule. Here $\{e_{12},\,e_{34},\,(e_{14}-e_{23})/\sqrt2\}$ is orthonormal with Pfaffians $0$, $0$ and $a_{14}a_{23}=-\tfrac12$, so $\mathfrak b(P_E)=-\tfrac12$. In coordinates $a e_{12}+b e_{34}+c(e_{14}-e_{23})$ has $\mathrm{Pf}=ab-c^2$ and squared norm $a^2+b^2+2c^2$, so in the orthonormal coordinates $(x,y,z)=(a,b,\sqrt2c)$ the form is $xy-\tfrac12z^2$ of trace $-\tfrac12$, agreeing. **An orthonormal decomposable basis would make every diagonal entry zero and so force trace zero**, therefore none exists.

**Two consequences for sub-step (a).** First, *the subspaces of $\Lambda^2V$ carrying a decomposable basis* is not a classification worth asking for: $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ itself carries the basis $\{e_i\wedge e_j\}$, so the class contains the whole space and everything spanned by Grassmann points, and it imposes nothing. **The question with content is the orthonormal one.** Second, the $\Lambda^4$ test already cuts into the mixed kind, since (ii) forces $\mathfrak b(P_E)=0$ and my $E$ has $\mathfrak b(P_E)=-\tfrac12$: **the test is exactly the vanishing of the trace of $\mathrm{Pf}$ on each eigenspace, (ii) implies it, and I36's open half is its converse per eigenspace.** That reading is consistent with the given, the converse being true inside $\mathcal W_4$ and false among symmetric operators.

## 4. (D) Reselection inside the two types

**D1 is true, and the reason is that orthonormality does the work.** Let $E=f\wedge U$ with $f\notin U$, and replace $U$ by $U'=\pi_{f^\perp}U$, which changes nothing since $f\wedge U=f\wedge U'$. For $u,u'\in U'$ one has $\langle f\wedge u,f\wedge u'\rangle\propto\lVert f\rVert^2\langle u,u'\rangle$, so **an orthonormal basis of $E$ is exactly $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ for an orthonormal basis $\{u_i\}$ of $U'$** up to the fixed normalisation. Then $(\hat f,u_1,\dots,u_r)$ is an orthonormal set, extends to a frame of $\mathbb R^n$, and $f\wedge u_i=\lVert f\rVert\,\hat f\wedge u_i$ are coordinate $2$-planes of it. **So every orthonormal decomposable basis of a pencil lies in a common frame, which is stronger than free reselection.**

**The orthonormality is load-bearing and the brief's *any basis* reading is false.** In $e_1\wedge\langle e_2,e_3\rangle$ the basis $\{e_1\wedge e_2,\;e_1\wedge(e_2+e_3)\}$ is decomposable with planes $\langle e_1,e_2\rangle$ and $\langle e_1,e_2+e_3\rangle$, and no orthonormal frame contains both $e_2$ and $(e_2+e_3)/\sqrt2$. **The record's obligation quantifies over orthonormal eigenbases, so this costs it nothing**, and it is why the claim must be stated with orthonormality attached.

**D2 is false.** Let $\dim U=3$ and let $\star$ be the Hodge star inside $U$, an isometry $U\to\Lambda^2U$. Given any orthonormal basis $\{\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3\}$ of $\Lambda^2U$, put $h_i=\star^{-1}\omega_i$, an orthonormal basis of $U$, and then $\omega_1=\pm h_2\wedge h_3$, $\omega_2=\pm h_3\wedge h_1$, $\omega_3=\pm h_1\wedge h_2$. **So every orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2U$ is a coordinate basis of an orthonormal frame of $U$**, and reselection inside $\Lambda^2U$ is as free as inside a pencil, with the same $O(3)$ worth of choices at $r=3$. A worked instance, checked by hand: $\{(e_{23}+e_{31})/\sqrt2,\;(e_{23}-e_{31})/\sqrt2,\;e_{12}\}$ equals $\{h_3\wedge h_1,\;-h_3\wedge h_2,\;h_1\wedge h_2\}$ for $h_1=(e_1-e_2)/\sqrt2$, $h_2=(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2$, $h_3=e_3$.

**What is true in place of it.** The asymmetry between the types is **which frame vectors each consumes**, and not the freedom of reselection: a pencil of dimension $r$ pins one frame vector, the shared line $\hat f$, and leaves an $O(r)$ of choices inside $U'$, while $\Lambda^2U$ pins the three-space $U$ and leaves an $O(3)$ of choices inside it. **So the within-eigenspace half of sub-step (a) is settled for both of Theorem 6's types, and the whole difficulty is CROSS-eigenspace: that the frames chosen inside the several eigenspaces can be made one frame.** That is a sharpening of the item rather than a hole in it.

## 5. (E) The I73 withdrawal

**E1, from the definition rather than the README's word.** Three components must vanish for $Q(W)$ to lie in $\mathcal W_n$: the scalar one, the traceless-Ricci one and $\mathrm{Alt}$.

- **The scalar component is proved, on the record's own filed source.** $\langle A\#B,C\rangle$ is totally symmetric, so $\langle W^\#,I\rangle=\langle W\#W,I\rangle=\langle W\#I,W\rangle$, and [boehm-wilking notes](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/notes.md) records $W\#I=-W$ for every algebraic Weyl operator, quoting their own proof through $R\mapsto R+R\#I$. Hence $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q(W)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^2+\langle W^\#,I\rangle=\lVert W\rVert^2-\lVert W\rVert^2=0$. **The same identity is relation (B)**, which is the record's own note there.
- **The other two are asserted and not derived anywhere on the record.** [README.md:14](../README.md#L14) states the preservation flat, [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) tables it with [README.md](../README.md) as its authority, and no lemma proves it. The mathematics behind it is Hamilton's: $\mathrm{Alt}(R^2+R^\#)=0$ whenever $\mathrm{Alt}(R)=0$, and the Ricci contraction of $R^2+R^\#$ is a sum of terms each carrying a factor of $\mathrm{Ric}(R)$, which is why the Ricci-flow reaction term for $\mathrm{Ric}$ is $2R_{ipjq}\mathrm{Ric}_{pq}-2(\mathrm{Ric}^2)_{ij}$ and vanishes at $\mathrm{Ric}=0$. **That is a literature import with no citation on the record, and the whole I73 withdrawal rests on it**, so it should be filed against [boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/) with the clause named.
- **A closed-form confirmation at $n=4$, on the self-dual family.** Let $W=A\oplus0$ with $A=\mathrm{diag}(a,b,c)$ on $\Lambda^+$ and $a+b+c=0$. Since $[\Lambda^+,\Lambda^-]=0$, the identity $\langle R^\#x,y\rangle=-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{ad}_xR\,\mathrm{ad}_yR)$ kills every $x\in\Lambda^-$, so $W^\#$ is supported on $\Lambda^+$, and with $[\omega_i,\omega_j]=\gamma\varepsilon_{ijk}\omega_k$ the same identity gives $\langle W^\#\omega_1,\omega_1\rangle=\gamma^2bc$. The record's anchor $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$ fixes $\gamma^2=2$ at $n=4$, which is [L178](../findings/INDEX-results.md)'s own constant. So $Q(W)=\mathrm{diag}(a^2+2bc,\,b^2+2ac,\,c^2+2ab)$ on $\Lambda^+$ and $0$ on $\Lambda^-$, whose trace is $(a+b+c)^2=0$, whose mixed $\Lambda^+\otimes\Lambda^-$ block is zero, and which therefore satisfies $\mathrm{Alt}=0$ and $\mathrm{Ric}=0$. **$Q(W)\in\mathcal W_4$ on the whole family, and $\langle W^\#,I\rangle=2(ab+bc+ca)=-(a^2+b^2+c^2)=-\lVert W\rVert^2$ exactly, agreeing with $W\#I=-W$.**

**E2 follows in three lines and is right.** $P(W)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3+\langle W,W^\#\rangle$ is a cubic form whose ambient gradient is $3(W^2+W^\#)=3Q(W)$, and criticality on the unit sphere of the linear space $\mathcal W_n$ is $\Pi_{\mathcal W_n}Q(W)=\theta W$. **With $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$ the projection is the identity, so there is no second multiplier and no $\rho$**, exactly as the withdrawal says, and $\theta=\langle W,Q(W)\rangle=P(W)$. Then $W^\#=\theta W-W^2$ and $[W,W^\#]=\theta[W,W]-[W,W^2]=0$ **at every critical point, with no hypothesis**. And the diagonal is relation (C): in an orthonormal eigenbasis of $W$, $(W^\#)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_{cd}c_{kcd}^2\lambda_c\lambda_d=\tfrac12\sum_{\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma T_{k\beta\gamma}$, so $W^\#=\theta W-W^2$ read at $kk$ is $\sum_{\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma T_{k\beta\gamma}=2\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)$. **That is (C) with its constant**, and [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) already says *"(C) is literally the diagonal of $Q(W)=\theta W$"*.

**E4, the withdrawal, is correct and loses nothing.** The mechanism was to ask whether $[W,W^\#]$ vanishes at a maximiser. It vanishes at every critical point, so it separates no critical point from any other, and there is nothing for global maximality to buy. **The stronger surviving fact is on the record already**: criticality says $W^\#$ is not merely commuting with $W$ but diagonal in every eigenbasis of $W$, acting as the scalar $\lambda(\theta-\lambda)$ on the $\lambda$-eigenspace, which is [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md)'s *"$3\binom N2$ further relations with right side zero"*. So the burden the brief puts on the withdrawal is discharged: **what is withdrawn is strictly weaker than what the record keeps.**

**E3, the disanalogy, is right in substance and wrong as written.** The $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$ half is right: on $\{\lVert W\rVert=1,\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0\}$ the gradient condition is $3W^2=2\theta'W+\beta\,\mathrm{Id}$, so $W^2=aW+b\,\mathrm{Id}$, every eigenvalue is a root of one quadratic, and at most two distinct eigenvalues follow. **The stated reason for the failure of the analogy is false at exactly the points under discussion**: at every critical point $W^\#=\theta W-W^2$, manifestly a polynomial in $W$. Two accurate replacements, either of which carries the argument:

- **the map** $W\mapsto W^\#$ is not $W\mapsto p(W)$ for a fixed polynomial $p$, so $W^2+W^\#=\theta W$ is not an a priori polynomial identity in $W$ and no root count applies to it;
- sharper, **the constraint set does the opposite of what it does in the cubic case**. There, the linear constraint $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$ contributes the multiplier $b\,\mathrm{Id}$ and the criticality condition lands inside $\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{Id},W,W^2\}$. Here, $\mathcal W_n$ being a linear subspace with $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$, **no extra multiplier appears at all**, and the content of criticality is precisely that $W^\#$, which has no reason to lie in $\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{Id},W\}$, is forced to lie in $\mathrm{span}\{W,W^2\}$. The information is a constraint on $\#$ rather than an equation on the spectrum.

## 6. (F) The surviving byproduct

**F1 is true and understates itself.** $T$ is hollow, so $T_{k\beta\gamma}=0$ whenever $\beta$ or $\gamma$ equals $k$, and therefore **row $k$ of (C) has a left side that does not involve $\lambda_k$ at all**. Row $k$ alone reads $F_k(\lambda_{\ne k})=2\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)$, whose solution set in $\lambda_k$ at fixed $\theta$ and fixed other eigenvalues is the two-element fibre $\{\alpha,\theta-\alpha\}$. **That is the sharp form of the symmetry**, and it is stronger than the recorded sentence.

**F2 is true of one row read alone, and it comes with no rival critical point.** The reflected spectrum $\lambda'=(\lambda_1,\dots,\theta-\lambda_k,\dots)$ is not a candidate: $\sum\lambda'=\theta-2\lambda_k$ against the tracelessness $\sum\lambda=0$ that $\mathcal W_n$ forces, and $\lVert\lambda'\rVert^2=1+\theta(\theta-2\lambda_k)$ against unit norm. **Both fail unless $\lambda_k=\theta/2$, the fixed point of the reflection**, and rows $k'\ne k$ change as well, their left sides containing $\lambda_k$ through $T_{k'\beta\gamma}$ with $\beta$ or $\gamma$ equal to $k$. So the honest reading is **a limit on one inference pattern**: from the right sides alone one recovers each $\lambda_k$ only up to reflection, so a spectrum $\{\alpha,\theta-\alpha,\dots\}$ of three or more values is not excluded by the shape of the right side. It is not an ambiguity between two rival critical points.

**And the same symmetry cuts the other way, which the record does not say.** If the right side were constant across all $k$, the spectrum would already have at most two values, $\alpha$ and $\theta-\alpha$, since a quadratic has two roots. **So the two-element fibre is the thing that would deliver I73's conclusion**, and the obstruction is not the fibre but the fact that the left sides $F_k$ differ across rows. Stating the symmetry as the obstacle points at the wrong half of the row.

**F3 overreaches and should be narrowed.** *Any route to spectral simplicity must spend the second-order condition* does not follow from a remark about the diagonal of the criticality equation. Criticality carries more than (C): the off-diagonal relations, $3\binom N2$ of them with right side zero, are recorded as unused at [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md), and they say that $W^\#$ is scalar on every eigenspace of $W$. A maximiser carries more than criticality and more than the Hessian: it carries global comparison, $P(W)\ge P(V)$ for every unit $V\in\mathcal W_n$, which is what [L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) trades against. **The defensible sentence**: no route can reach spectral simplicity from the reflection-invariant content of (C)'s diagonal rows alone, so it must spend something else, and [L24](../findings/second-order.md) is one candidate among the off-diagonal relations, first Bianchi, Ricci-flatness and global maximality. **Recorded as a *must*, it would send a successor to the one instrument whose own scope is [L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s owed middle branch at $\theta=0$.**

## 10. What would break the route, and what I did to look for it

**The route under review is sub-step (a) of [I36](../plan.md): use the structure of the eigenspaces of a $W$ passing the $\Lambda^4$ test to force a common frame at $n\ge5$.** Four things would break it, and here is what I did about each.

1. **An eigenspace with an orthonormal decomposable basis whose planes lie in no common frame.** That is [diagonal-half.md:1112](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1112)'s refutation of the universal crux, already on the record with the six-plane witness at $n=4$, so the route survives only in the existential form, over all decomposable orthonormal eigenbases at once. **I did not look for a new one**, the recorded one being enough to fix the form of the claim.
2. **A $W$ inside $\mathcal W_n$, $n\ge5$, passing the test with an eigenspace of the mixed kind admitting no orthonormal decomposable basis.** This is the live falsifier. **A search for it is computation and I did not open one**, per the analysis-only rule. What I did instead was bound the mixed kind analytically: (ii) forces $\mathfrak b(P_E)=0$, so the test itself excludes every mixed-kind eigenspace with nonzero $\mathrm{Pf}$-trace, and my $E$ at section 3 is such a subspace, which is why it cannot appear as an eigenspace of a test-passing $W$.
3. **A failure of $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$.** It would break criticality's form, relation (C), [L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s $L_e$ and the I73 withdrawal at once. **I attacked it at the component where a failure would be cheapest to see, the scalar one, and it holds identically**, then confirmed all three components in closed form on the $n=4$ self-dual family. **What remains is a citation debt rather than a doubt.**
4. **The $\ell=2$ dichotomy being false or misquoted.** **I re-derived it from Lemma 3 alone**, at section 2, so the record's use of it no longer depends on a general-$\ell$ theorem read in extraction.
