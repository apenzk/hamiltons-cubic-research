**One-line summary: CONFIRMED WITH GAP.** Every theorem is correct and faithful at the presentation level, but the cited chain $c_2(H)=\delta^2(H)-\delta^2(u)\le\tfrac14-\delta^2(u)$ joins a presentation of $H$ supported off $S$ to a full eigenframe of $H$, which cannot coincide, through a frame-independence of `defectSq` that the layer does not have.

## A. Statement fidelity

**Support hypotheses.** `hS : ∀ γ a b, a ∉ S → om γ a b = 0` kills every row of $\omega_\gamma$ outside $S$. Skewness gives $\omega_{ba}=-\omega_{ab}$, so every column outside $S$ is zero too, and $\omega_\gamma$ is supported on $S\times S$. `hSc` symmetrically puts $\eta_\delta$ on $S^c\times S^c$. That is exactly "disjoint index support" for skew bivectors. The proof only uses the row half (`comp_specOp_zero` reads the first index), which is weaker and suffices.

**The appended presentation.** `specOp_arc` proves `specOp (append (cos t · lam) (sin t · mu)) (append om eta) X = cos t • specOp lam om X + sin t • specOp mu eta X` for every $X$. So it presents the arc point whenever `specOp lam om` is $u$ and `specOp mu eta` is $H$ on bivectors.

**Orthonormality.** `defectSq` is `altNormSq (negPartOp lam om)`, a function of the presentation rather than of the operator. It equals the record's $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)\rVert^2$ when the presentation is an orthonormal eigen-presentation, since then `negPartOp` is the functional calculus. It can differ otherwise. Example: $\omega_1=\omega_2=\omega$ with $\lambda=(1,-1)$ presents the zero operator, whose $\delta^2$ is $0$, while `negPartOp` is $\omega\otimes\omega$. `defectSq_append` assumes no orthonormality and so is stronger than the record's rule. For faithfulness, orthonormality of the appended presentation follows from the two parts' orthonormality: $\langle\omega_\gamma,\eta_\delta\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{ab}\omega_{ab}\eta_{ab}=0$ because the supports are disjoint. Lean does not state this, but it costs nothing, and the eigenvalues $\cos t\,\lambda$, $\sin t\,\mu$ on the union are the arc point's spectrum. On its own this is a note.

**G1: the two presentations of $H$ cannot coincide.** `c2_padded_le` ([DefectBlock.lean:164](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/DefectBlock.lean#L164)) takes `hf : EigenFrame W eta mu` ([L41Statement.lean:97](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L41Statement.lean#L97)), whose `complete` field spans every skew matrix of $\mathbb R^n$ by the `eta`. `defect_arc` ([DefectBlock.lean:131](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/DefectBlock.lean#L131)) takes `hSc`, zero rows on $S$. For $i\in S$ and $j\ne i$, `complete` at $\varphi_{ij}$ reads, at entry $(i,j)$, $-1=\sum_\gamma\langle\varphi_{ij},\eta_\gamma\rangle(\eta_\gamma)_{ij}=0$. So the two hypotheses are contradictory whenever $S$ is nonempty and $n\ge2$, and on the padded flat $S$ is $u$'s four indices.

- *Screen, not evidence:* the scratch file proves `EigenFrame W eta mu → (∀ δ a b, a ∈ S → eta δ a b = 0) → i ∈ S → i ≠ j → False` by `hf.complete (phi i j)` at entry $(i,j)$ and `simp`. It compiles.
- *Consequence:* the `defectSq mu eta` in `defect_arc`'s right side (a presentation of $H$ on $S^c$) and the `defectSq mu eta` that `c2_padded_le`'s second conjunct bounds (a full frame) are never the same term in any instantiation. The reader who composes them must supply "`defectSq` agrees on two orthonormal eigen-presentations of the same operator, one padded by zero-eigenvalue vectors". r1461 recorded that frame-independence as owed in AltPart.lean, and a grep finds no such lemma.
- *Arguing the opposite.* The missing lemma is true: `negPartScalar 0 = 0`, so zero-eigenvalue vectors add nothing, and any two orthonormal eigenframes of a symmetric operator give the same spectral projections. A careful reader could also bound $\delta^2(H)$ at the $S^c$ presentation by rerunning DefectQuarter's argument on $\mathbb R^{S^c}$. Both routes are extra work that the row and the citation present as done. The candidate survives the three tests: located at DefectBlock.lean:164 against :131, exhibited by the compiled screen, and the opposite argument names the lemma that would close it rather than showing it is in hand.

## B. $c_2$

The record defines $c_2(H)=\tfrac12\tfrac{d^2}{dt^2}\big|_{t=0^+}\delta^2(\cos t\,u+\sin t\,H)$ ([route-a.md:182](../findings/route-a.md#L182)). `c2_padded_le`'s first conjunct is `(1/2) * deriv (deriv f) 0 = b - a` with $f(t)=\cos^2t\,a+\sin^2t\,b$, and it holds for any reals $a,b$: the presentations enter only as the two constants. `defect_arc` gives $g(t)=f(t)$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, where $g$ is `defectSq` of the appended arc presentation.

**Position: agreement on $[0,\pi/2]$ with an analytic $f$ is enough for the value, and no `derivWithin` statement is needed for correctness.** One-sided derivatives at $0^+$ read only values on $[0,\varepsilon)$. So $g'$ on $[0,\pi/2)$ taken within `Ici 0` equals $f'$, and the one-sided second derivative is $f''(0)=2(\delta^2(H)-\delta^2(u))$. The two-sided reading is right as well: for $t<0$, $\sin t<0$, and $\delta^2(\sin t\,H)=\sin^2t\,\delta^2(-H)=\sin^2t\,\delta^2(H)$ because $\mathrm{Alt}(W_+)=\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)$ for Bianchi $W$ (ATTEMPTS.md Result 7, quoted in DefectQuarter.lean). So the surrogate is not hiding a kink at $0$.

**G2: the composition is informal and the docstring names the wrong function.** [DefectBlock.lean:162](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/DefectBlock.lean#L162) says "half the second derivative at `0` of the defect along the great circle", and line 19 of the module docstring writes it as $\tfrac12(d^2/dt^2)|_0$. The theorem's function is the surrogate. Dropping every hypothesis on `lam om mu eta` leaves the first conjunct true, so it cannot carry the block-sum content. *Arguing the opposite:* `defect_arc` sits one declaration above and the locality of one-sided derivatives is standard. That is why this is a minor gap in wording and composition. It is not a defect in the value.
