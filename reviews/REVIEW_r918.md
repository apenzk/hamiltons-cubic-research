**One line.** Every step of L210 is true and the lemma's own statement stands, three of the four steps are established by arguments other than the ones written, and the corollary sentence that L47 and L48 now cover every compact Einstein symmetric space is not established, because nothing on the record excludes a noncompact-type factor.

## 1. Proposition 3.1.2, its sign and its hypothesis

**The hypothesis fits exactly.** The source states 3.1.2 for a locally symmetric $M$ (line 2213 region of the stored text), its proof runs through Lemma 3.1.1 and the Jacobi-field property of a Killing field along one geodesic, and nothing in it asks for completeness, simple connectivity or compactness. The block needs no more than that, so its claim to be free of topology at this input is correct.

**The sign is not load-bearing in L210.** Gorodski fixes $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ at line 2122 of the stored text, immediately above 3.1.2. Every conclusion L210 draws from 3.1.2 is a vanishing: step 1 concludes $R(X,Y)Z=0$, step 2 concludes $R(X,Y)Z=0$, step 3 concludes $R=0$ on $\mathfrak p_0$ and then $\operatorname{Ric}=0$ there, and step 4 restricts an identity. **A global sign on $R$, and likewise any positive factor between the record's $\operatorname{Ric}$ and the classical one, changes none of those statements.** So the usual convention hazard does not reach this lemma, and the block's display $R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]$ enters and leaves with the same sign it is quoted in.

**One absence, recorded rather than charged against the lemma.** The brief says the record's curvature sign convention is at [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md). It is not there. That file pins the operator-level conventions that matter for this programme: $\operatorname{scal}=2\operatorname{tr}$ on $\Lambda^2$, the half-trace pairing, Li's $iklj$ order for $\mathring R$, the sphere's $R_{ijkl}=g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk}$, and $\mathrm{ric}(I)=\tfrac{n-1}2\mathrm{id}$. It states no $(1,3)$ convention, so the comparison the brief asks for cannot be made against the record, only against the record's operator anchors, which are consistent with Gorodski's convention up to the index order of the $(0,4)$ tensor. Since the lemma is sign-robust, this is a hole in [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) and not a defect in L210.

## 2. The imported-hypothesis check, and where the transcription is too weak

**What $B$ is taken to be, and whether it is available.** For a locally symmetric $M$ the source builds the triple directly, in section 1.6: $K$ is the group of linear isometries of $T_xM$ preserving the curvature tensor, $\mathfrak k$ is its Lie algebra realised faithfully as vector fields vanishing at $x$, $\mathfrak p$ is the space of infinitesimal transvections at $x$ with $Y\mapsto Y_x$ a linear isomorphism onto $T_xM$, Proposition 1.6.1 gives $[\mathfrak k,\mathfrak k]\subseteq\mathfrak k$, $[\mathfrak k,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak p$, $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak k$ and the involution $s$ induced by the geodesic symmetry, and **$B$ is the inner product of $T_xM$ lifted to $\mathfrak p$** (line 775 of the stored text). So $B$ is the Riemannian metric at the point. It is positive definite because the metric is, and $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariant because $\mathfrak k$ acts on $T_xM$ by skew-symmetric maps, $K$ being a group of linear isometries. **Nondegeneracy is automatic and never assumed, on every space the Theorem ranges over.**

**The record's definition of the object is weaker than the source's, and the weaker version does not support the proposition.** The source's abstract definition (line 621) asks for four things: $\mathfrak g$ real finite-dimensional, $s$ involutive, **the fixed set $\mathfrak k$ containing no nontrivial ideal of $\mathfrak g$**, and **$B$ an $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak p$**. The record's [notes.md](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md) says instead "$B$ an $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$-invariant nondegenerate form" and omits the ideal clause entirely. Both omissions bite inside the proof the record cites:

- 2.1.3's proof uses Lemma 2.1.2(c), whose proof uses 2.1.2(b), $\beta|_{\mathfrak k}$ negative definite. **That proof reads "Since $\mathrm{ad}_X$ leaves $B$ invariant and $B$ is positive-definite"**, and closes with "the multiples of $X$ form an ideal of $\mathfrak g$ contained in $\mathfrak k$, hence $X=0$", which is the ideal clause. Under the record's transcription of the definition, neither line is available and 2.1.3 does not follow.
- The same ideal clause is what turns the lemma's $\mathfrak p_0=0$ into its stated "no Euclidean factor", see section 6.

**And the bridge from a locally symmetric space to the algebra is cited nowhere.** The L210 block opens "A symmetric space carries an orthogonal involutive Lie algebra $(\mathfrak g,s,B)$" with no reference, and [notes.md](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md) transcribes 2.1.3, 3.1.2, 2.1.5, 2.1.6 and one paragraph of the introduction, none of which is the construction. The construction is in the source at section 1.6 and is untranscribed. It is also the one step where "locally symmetric" is doing real work, since the algebra is one of local vector fields on a normal neighbourhood rather than the isometry algebra of a homogeneous space.

**What I checked myself rather than granting.** That the section 1.6 triple satisfies the ideal clause: if $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak k$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak g$ then $[\mathfrak a,\mathfrak p]\subseteq\mathfrak a\cap\mathfrak p=0$, so $\mathfrak a$ acts trivially on $\mathfrak p$, so $\mathfrak a=0$ by the faithfulness the section 1.6 construction supplies. **So the imported hypotheses are genuinely met and the mathematics is safe.** The GAP is that a reader working from the record's own files gets a definition too weak for the cited proposition and no statement that a locally symmetric space supplies the triple. Both are one-line repairs: quote the source's definition verbatim, and transcribe Proposition 1.6.1 with the paragraph that follows it.

## 3. Is Gorodski's irreducible the notion L47 uses

**Yes, and the reason is two statements the transcription leaves out.** Gorodski's irreducible is "not Euclidean, and $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$ irreducible on $\mathfrak p$", where $\mathfrak k$ is the whole local isotropy algebra and so can a priori exceed the holonomy algebra $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]$. That would matter, since L47's census is indexed by the classification, whose irreducibility is holonomy irreducibility. It does not matter here: an irreducible OIL-algebra has no Euclidean factor, so $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple by Corollary 2.1.5, and **for semisimple $\mathfrak g$ Lemma 2.1.1 gives $\mathfrak k=[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]$** (its proof shows $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]+\mathfrak p$ is an ideal and then that its complement sits inside $\mathfrak k$, hence is zero). So on an irreducible algebra the two representations coincide and the notions agree exactly.

**The "not Euclidean" clause is what makes this work, and it is doing real work rather than tidying.** On a flat space $R=0$, so the local isotropy group is all of $O(T_xM)$ and $\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak k}$ is irreducible on $\mathfrak p$, while $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]=0$ and the space is not irreducible in any geometric sense. The definition excludes it by hand.

**The index sets match on the compact side.** Proposition 2.3.2 sorts irreducible OIL-algebras into four classes: 1, $\mathfrak g$ compact simple with an involution; 2, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak h$ with $\mathfrak h$ compact simple and $s$ the swap; 3 and 4, the noncompact duals. **Classes 1 and 2 are exactly L47's type I and type II**, which is the census L47 runs, so a compact-type factor of L210's decomposition is an object L47 covers. The mismatch that remains is not about irreducibility, it is that classes 3 and 4 are irreducible too, and L210's statement quantifies over them. That is section 9.

**No finding against the $r=1$ step.** With the two notions identified, $r=1$ hands L47 an irreducible algebra of the kind its census is indexed by, once the type is settled.

## 4. Step 1 and the commuting claim

**Distinct ideals of an internal direct sum commute, and here is the proof.** Let $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak g_i$ as vector spaces with each $\mathfrak g_i$ an ideal, and $i\ne j$. Then $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]\subseteq\mathfrak g_i$ because $\mathfrak g_i$ is an ideal, and $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]\subseteq\mathfrak g_j$ for the same reason, so $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]\subseteq\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak g_j=0$ by directness. The claim is true as stated and needs no semisimplicity.

**Step 1 then follows.** For $X\in\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak g_i$ and $Y\in\mathfrak p_j\subseteq\mathfrak g_j$ the bracket $[X,Y]$ lies in $[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$, and 3.1.2 turns $[X,Y]=0$ into $R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z]=0$ for every $Z\in\mathfrak p$, with no case distinction on $Z$. **Gorodski's own proof of 2.1.3 reaches the same conclusion one level lower**, at (2.1.4): $[\mathfrak p_i,\mathfrak p_j]=0$ for $i\ne j$ both positive, and $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p]=0$. So the record's route and the source's agree, and the source's is the stronger statement.

## 5. Step 2, and whether it is L48's block-diagonality

**Step 2's ingredient checks.** $\mathfrak g_i$ is an ideal, hence a subalgebra, so $X,Y\in\mathfrak p_i\subseteq\mathfrak g_i$ gives $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak g_i$, and in fact $[X,Y]\in\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak k$. Then for $Z\in\mathfrak p_j$ with $j\ne i$, $[[X,Y],Z]\in[\mathfrak g_i,\mathfrak g_j]=0$. And for $Z\in\mathfrak p_i$, $[[X,Y],Z]\in\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_i$, so $R(X,Y)$ preserves $\mathfrak p_i$ and annihilates every other block.

**Block-diagonality as an operator on $\Lambda^2$ is the same statement, given the pair symmetry.** Write $\Lambda^2\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_i\Lambda^2\mathfrak p_i\oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}\mathfrak p_i\wedge\mathfrak p_j$. Step 1 gives $R(X,Y)Z=0$ for every $Z$ when $X\wedge Y$ is mixed, hence every component $\langle R(X\wedge Y),\cdot\rangle$ vanishes and the mixed summand lies in the kernel of the operator. Step 2 plus the preceding paragraph give $R(\Lambda^2\mathfrak p_i)\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathfrak p_i$. Together that is precisely "$R$ is block-diagonal on $\Lambda^2$ and zero on the mixed terms", which is L48's phrase, and the only structural fact used in the translation is $R_{ijkl}=R_{klij}$, which a Riemannian curvature tensor has. **So the answer to the brief's question is yes, the triple statement and the operator statement are the same statement here.**

**One hypothesis of L48 comes free and the block does not notice.** L48 assumes $n_a\ge2$ for every factor, and v192 section 7a flags $n_a=1$ as a pole of $f$. If $\dim\mathfrak p_i=1$ then $[\mathfrak p_i,\mathfrak p_i]=0$ by antisymmetry, so that factor is Euclidean and cannot be one of the irreducible $\mathfrak g_i$. **Irreducibility supplies $n_a\ge2$ outright**, so the pole is unreachable, and L210 could say so in a clause.

**A scope remark, in L210's favour.** L210 delivers a pointwise algebraic splitting rather than a Riemannian product decomposition, and every quantity L48 computes from it ($\lVert W\rVert^2$, the factorwise $\lVert W_a\rVert^2$, the scalar $B$, and the implication $W_a=0\Rightarrow$ constant curvature) is pointwise algebra in the curvature operator. So the weaker conclusion is enough for its consumer, which is the right trade and is worth stating in the block, since L48's own opening sentence says "let the space be a product".

## 6. Step 3, the load-bearing one

**The conclusion is right and the written reason does not reach it.** For $X\in\mathfrak p_0$, $\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)$ is a trace over a full basis of $\mathfrak p$, so it is $\sum_a\langle R(e_a,X)X,e_a\rangle$ up to the record's positive normalisation, and the summands split in two families. For $e_a\in\mathfrak p_0$ the term dies by $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$, which is what the block quotes. **For $e_a\in\mathfrak p_j$ with $j\ge1$ the term dies by step 1, which the block does not quote here.** As written, step 3 says "$[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$ gives $R=0$ there by 3.1.2, hence $\operatorname{Ric}$ vanishes on $\mathfrak p_0$", and that inference is the one the brief warns about: vanishing of the $\mathfrak p_0$-internal curvature does not by itself kill a trace over $\mathfrak p$. The repair is one clause, and the ingredient is already in the same block. **Gorodski supplies an even shorter repair**: his (2.1.4) is $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p]=0$, not merely $[\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_0]=0$, so $R(\cdot,X)\cdot=0$ identically for $X\in\mathfrak p_0$ and the trace is zero term by term. Either way the conclusion $\operatorname{Ric}|_{\mathfrak p_0}=0$ holds.

**The contradiction then closes.** If $X\in\mathfrak p_0$ is nonzero, $\lambda g(X,X)=\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)=0$ with $g(X,X)>0$ forces $\lambda=0$, against the hypothesis. So $\mathfrak p_0=0$. This survives any positive rescaling of the record's $\operatorname{Ric}$ relative to the classical one, which is what makes the missing convention of section 1 harmless.

**Corollary 2.1.5 is corroboration, and the block labels it correctly.** The argument above never uses it. What 2.1.5 does add is the passage from the geometric conclusion $\mathfrak p_0=0$ to the algebraic phrase "no Euclidean factor": $\mathfrak p_0=0$ makes $\mathfrak g_0$ an ideal of $\mathfrak g$ inside $\mathfrak k$, so **the ideal clause of the OIL definition gives $\mathfrak g_0=0$**, and then 2.1.5 reads $\mathfrak g$ semisimple. That is the second place the clause section 2 found missing from the transcription is load-bearing, and it is why the lemma's headline phrase "no Euclidean factor" is a statement about the algebra while its proof reaches a statement about $\mathfrak p$.

## 7. Step 4, the shared constant

**The conclusion is right, and the reason given is the wrong one.** What L48 needs is that each factor is Einstein with constant $\lambda$ in its own right, that is $\operatorname{Ric}_i=\lambda g|_{\mathfrak p_i}$ where $\operatorname{Ric}_i$ is the Ricci contraction of the factor's own curvature over $\mathfrak p_i$ alone. The block derives it from "$\operatorname{Ric}$ is block-diagonal", and block-diagonality is not that property: a block-diagonal $\operatorname{Ric}$ says the mixed components vanish, and says nothing about whether the ambient trace over $\mathfrak p$ agrees with the intrinsic trace over $\mathfrak p_i$. **The property that delivers it is again the mixed vanishing of step 1**: for $X,Y\in\mathfrak p_i$ and $e_a\in\mathfrak p_l$ with $l\ne i$, $[e_a,X]=0$, so the term drops and $\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\sum_{e_a\in\mathfrak p_i}\langle R(e_a,X)Y,e_a\rangle=\operatorname{Ric}_i(X,Y)$. With $\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g$ globally, that is $\operatorname{Ric}_i=\lambda g|_{\mathfrak p_i}$ with one and the same $\lambda$ for every $i$. So the step is a GAP in the sense the brief defines, true but not established by what is written, and the repair is again one clause.

**It is the hypothesis v192 section 7a names.** Section 7a's point 1 is that $B=\sum_af(n_a)-f(n)$ is computed at one shared constant, so the identity needs the same $\lambda=\tfrac12$ on every factor at once and a per-factor normalisation will not do. Step 4 delivers exactly that, from the global Einstein condition, and the mixed vanishing above is the algebraic form of section 7b's "for a Riemannian product the Ricci tensor is the direct sum". **L210 therefore closes section 7a's requirement without assuming the space is a product**, which is the whole point of the lemma, and its own justification understates what it has.

## 8. The decomposition of $\mathfrak p$

**Checked in both directions rather than granted.** Each $\mathfrak g_i$ is $s$-invariant, so $s|_{\mathfrak g_i}$ is an involution of $\mathfrak g_i$ and $\mathfrak g_i=(\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak k)\oplus(\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p)$ by the projections $x\mapsto\tfrac12(x\pm sx)$, both of which stay in $\mathfrak g_i$. For the converse inclusion take $X\in\mathfrak p$ and write $X=\sum_ix_i$ with $x_i\in\mathfrak g_i$. Then $\sum_isx_i=sX=-X=\sum_i(-x_i)$ with $sx_i\in\mathfrak g_i$, so uniqueness of the decomposition gives $sx_i=-x_i$, that is $x_i\in\mathfrak g_i\cap\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_i$. Hence $\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak p_i$, and the sum is direct because the $\mathfrak g_i$ sum is. **$s$-invariance is exactly the hypothesis needed and 2.1.3(a) supplies it.** No finding.

## 9. The reach, against what the Theorem quantifies over

**Two things stand between L210 and the Theorem's quantifier, and only one of them is on the record.**

*Is $\lambda\ne0$ available?* Yes, through [L46](../findings/problem.md#L556)'s parenthetical: at $\lambda=0$ a locally symmetric Ricci-flat space is flat, so $W=0$, which the Theorem's $W\ne0$ excludes. Two observations about it. **First, that parenthetical routes through "Cartan--Ambrose--Hicks with de Rham"**, so the Theorem still leans on a geometric de Rham step somewhere, and L210's claim to have removed the topology from the reduction is true of L210 and not of the chain. **Second, L210's own apparatus can replace it.** In 2.1.3's proof $\beta|_{\mathfrak q_i}=\lambda_iB|_{\mathfrak q_i}$ with $\lambda_i\ne0$ for $i\ge1$, and Corollary 3.1.4 gives $K(X,Y)=\lambda_i\beta([X,Y],[X,Y])$ on a factor, while 2.1.2(b) makes $\beta$ negative definite on $\mathfrak k$. So on an irreducible factor $\operatorname{Ric}$ is definite, of sign $-\operatorname{sign}(\lambda_i)$, and in particular nonzero. Hence $\operatorname{Ric}=0$ forces $r=0$, hence $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_0$, hence $R=0$: **the $\lambda=0$ case is algebraic too, at the price of transcribing 2.1.2(b) and 3.1.4.**

*Does anything else need to be said?* Yes, and this is the finding. **Gorodski's irreducible includes classes 3 and 4, the noncompact type**, so L210's conclusion is "block-diagonal over irreducible factors" with no statement of type, while L47's census is over compact irreducible symmetric spaces. The block disposes of this in a parenthesis, "compactness excluding noncompact-type factors", and no argument for it exists on the record. The same sentence appears in [VERIFY_v192.md](../verification/VERIFY_v192.md)'s hypothesis audit, also unargued. What is actually true, by the definiteness computed above: a factor of compact type ($\lambda_i<0$, since $B$ is positive definite and $\beta|_{\mathfrak p}$ negative definite there) has $\operatorname{Ric}>0$ on its block, a factor of noncompact type has $\operatorname{Ric}<0$, and the global Einstein condition then forces **every** factor to have the sign of $\lambda$. So the type is decided by the sign of $\lambda$ and not by compactness directly, and what the chain owes is $\lambda>0$. For a compact symmetric space that holds because such a space is homogeneous, hence has a transitive isometry group, while Bochner's theorem gives a compact manifold with $\operatorname{Ric}<0$ a finite isometry group. **Neither Bochner nor the homogeneity step is anywhere on the record**, so the honest statement of the reach today is: L210 plus L46's parenthetical exhausts the Theorem's quantifier as far as the Euclidean factor and the shared constant, and leaves the type of the surviving factors unsettled. The repair I would recommend is the sign argument above, which is two sentences and stays inside the cited source, plus one sentence naming $\lambda>0$ as what compactness is used for.

**A third, smaller item in the same place.** The $r=1$ branch hands L47 an irreducible OIL-algebra, and L47's census is over spaces. The bridge is that a class 1 or class 2 algebra is the algebra of a compact simply connected irreducible symmetric space, whose pointwise curvature invariants are the ones L47 tabulates, so the invariants transfer even when the space at hand is a quotient or is incomplete. That is true and unwritten.

## 10. The claimed advantage over the geometric route

**The conclusion is fair and the stated reason is partly a straw man.** Three separate claims are made in the block and in the Lean docstring: that the geometric de Rham theorem needs simple connectivity, that reaching a non-simply-connected space therefore costs a universal cover, and that one then owes that cover's compactness, "which wants Myers".

- The first is correct for the **global** theorem, the one Gorodski states as introduction prose at line 45 of the stored text, "every simply-connected symmetric space is a Riemannian product of irreducible symmetric spaces". The record's [notes.md](../references/gorodski-symmetric-spaces-intro/notes.md) correctly observes it carries no theorem number.
- **The LOCAL de Rham decomposition theorem needs neither simple connectivity nor completeness**: a Riemannian manifold whose tangent bundle splits into parallel distributions is locally isometric to a product, which is the standard local statement. Since everything L48 computes is pointwise, a local splitting at the point where $\theta$ is evaluated would serve. So the geometric route is not obliged to pass through the universal cover at all, and the block's account of it is the account of one particular route through it.
- **Myers is owed by neither route.** Even taking the universal cover, the factors of a compact-type algebra are compact automatically (classes 1 and 2 have compact $\tilde G$), so what is needed is the type, exactly as in section 9, and a diameter bound never enters. "Which wants Myers" is an overstatement, and it appears three times on the record, in the block, in [INDEX-results.md](../findings/INDEX-results.md) row 210 and in the Lean docstring.

**What the advantage really is, and it is real.** Both inputs of the algebraic route are numbered propositions in a source that is fetched, stored, transcribed and indexed, and both are free of topological hypotheses, whereas the record has no statement of local de Rham on file and the geometric statement it does have is unnumbered prose. That is a genuine reason to prefer the algebraic route, and it is a citation reason rather than a mathematical one. **The $\mathbb{RP}^{n/2}$ point is correct**: $\mathbb{RP}^{n/2}\times\mathbb{RP}^{n/2}$ has fundamental group $\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/2$, it is named in L48's equality clause, and it meets every hypothesis of the Theorem, so non-simply-connected spaces are inside the quantifier and a reduction resting on simple connectivity would not reach them.

## 11. The Lean file

**`curv` matches 3.1.2.** [L210DeRhamSplit.lean:44](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L210DeRhamSplit.lean#L44) defines `curv x y z = -⁅⁅x, y⁆, z⁆`, which is the right-hand side of (3.1.3) verbatim, and the docstring names the sign convention it is fixed against.

**The three theorems state the mixed-term vanishing without assuming it, and they are non-vacuous.** `curv_eq_zero_of_bracket_eq_zero` takes $⁅x,y⁆=0$ and concludes `curv x y z = 0` for every `z`. `curv_eq_zero_of_mem_of_mem` takes two sets whose cross-brackets vanish, and `curv_eq_zero_of_abelian_part` is the same with one set. The hypothesis in each case is the bracket condition, never the conclusion, and each is satisfiable (any abelian algebra, or the zero ideal), so nothing is vacuous. **I re-checked the file live**: `lake env lean` on it prints the three `#print axioms` lines as `[propext]`, so the claim of three declarations sorry-free on `propext` alone is accurate today.

**What they do not carry.** The substance of step 1 is two implications, that distinct ideals of an internal direct sum commute and that commuting elements have zero curvature. **Lean has the second and assumes the first.** `I` and `J` are arbitrary `Set L` with no ideal structure, so the statement is a rewriting of "bracket zero gives expression zero", and the three declarations are one lemma applied three ways, each closed by `simp [curv, h]`.

**The abstention from `statement: typed` in [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) is the honest call**, and for a stronger reason than the row gives: steps 3 and 4 are absent, and the part of step 1 that is not a triviality is a hypothesis rather than a theorem.

**Two claims in the surrounding prose do not hold up.**

- The row says the ideal hypothesis is stated over subsets "because `LieIdeal` is not in the vendored Mathlib under that name". **It is**, at `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Ideal.lean:46`, as `abbrev LieIdeal`. And the fact the block turns on is there too: `LieSubmodule.lie_le_inf : ⁅I, J⁆ ≤ I ⊓ J` at `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/IdealOperations.lean:142`, which with disjointness of the summands proves the commuting the file assumes. So step 1 is formalisable as it stands in the prose, and the row's excuse should go.
- The commit trailer `Lean: blocked -- no lieRic` names a genuine absence in the sense that the layer has no Ricci contraction of a Lie-algebra curvature and no Riemannian layer at all. ## 12. What would break it, and what I did to look

**The object is the signature of $B$.** Every step of L210 is downstream of Proposition 2.1.3, and 2.1.3 is downstream of Lemma 2.1.2(b), whose proof uses positive definiteness of $B$ and the effectiveness clause. **Replace the inner product by an indefinite nondegenerate form, which is exactly what the record's transcription of the definition licenses, and the decomposition fails**: in the pseudo-Riemannian setting there are indecomposable non-Euclidean symmetric spaces with degenerate holonomy invariants, the Cahen--Wallach family among them, so no splitting into irreducible and Euclidean ideals is available and the mixed blocks of $R$ need not vanish. The Riemannian hypothesis is what closes this, since $B$ is then the metric at a point. **So the falsifier is not in the geometry the Theorem ranges over, it is in the record's statement of the definition**, which is why section 2 is a GAP rather than a remark.

**Where else I looked.** A Riemannian falsifier would have to be a locally symmetric space with $\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g$, $\lambda\ne0$, and a nonzero flat factor: the Ricci tensor of such a space is block-diagonal with a zero block, so it is $\lambda g$ only at $\lambda=0$, and the candidates of that shape ($S^2\times T^2$, $\mathbb{CP}^2\times T^{n-4}$, the latter already on the record in v192 section 7b) fail the Einstein condition rather than the lemma. A falsifier of step 1 would need two ideals of an internal direct sum with a nonzero cross-bracket, which the two-line argument of section 4 forbids in any Lie algebra. A falsifier of the reach does exist in the sense of section 9: an irreducible factor of noncompact type satisfies every hypothesis L210 states and is outside L47's census, so the corollary sentence rather than the lemma is where the record is exposed.
