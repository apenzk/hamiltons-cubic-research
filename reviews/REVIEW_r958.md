## Item 1, batch 18's constant. CONFIRMED, the factor is two

**The factor derived from scratch.** The $V$ gradient of $\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)$ in direction $Y$ is $\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat U(YV+VY))=\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\{\hat U,V\}Y)$ and the $V$ gradient of $T$ is $V$, so the $V$ equation is $\beta\{\hat U,V\}+C=\mu V$, which is batch 10's $C\equiv\mu V-\beta\{\hat U,V\}$. With $\{\hat U,V\}=2\mathcal LV$ that is $2\beta\mathcal LV+C=\mu V$, exactly as batch 18 writes it.

**Two independent routes to the bound, both giving two.** Write the $V$ objective as $f(V)=\beta\langle V,\mathcal LV\rangle+\langle V,C\rangle=\tfrac12\langle V,HV\rangle+\langle C,V\rangle$ with $H=2\beta\mathcal L$, self-adjoint for the Frobenius product. In the standard trust-region normalisation the stationarity is $HV+C=\lambda V$, so $\lambda=\mu$, and the necessary condition at a **global** maximiser over the sphere is $\lambda I\succeq H$, that is $\mu\ge2\beta\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)$. Independently, along a curve $\gamma$ on the sphere with $\gamma(0)=V$ and $\gamma'(0)=Y$, differentiating $\langle\gamma,\gamma\rangle=r^2$ twice gives $\langle V,\gamma''\rangle=-\lvert Y\rvert^2$, so $\tfrac{d^2}{ds^2}f(\gamma)=2\beta\langle Y,\mathcal LY\rangle-\mu\lvert Y\rvert^2\le0$. Both give the factor two. It is not one and not four.

**The gap.** The curve computation yields only $\mu\ge2\beta\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L\vert_{V^\perp})$, over directions orthogonal to $V$, and on a four-dimensional slice of the five-dimensional space that bounds $\lambda_2(\mathcal L)$ rather than $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)$. Reaching the full $\lambda_{\max}$ needs the trust-region theorem in its global form, that the multiplier of a globally optimal solution dominates the quadratic form on the **whole** space. That theorem is available here, since at a global maximum of $\Phi$ the partner $V$ is a global maximiser of $f$ on its sphere, but batch 18 invokes it only by the phrase "its necessary condition is that the trust-region multiplier dominates the form" and never says that globality is what supplies it. *The conclusion stands and the written justification does not establish it without that sentence.*

## Item 2, the Jordan spectrum. CONFIRMED, except for the equality case

**The spectrum is right.** On $E_{ij}+E_{ji}$ the eigenvalue is $\tfrac{u_i+u_j}2=-\tfrac{u_k}2$. For the diagonal block, let $M=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(u)$ act on $\mathbb R^3$ and $P$ project off $e=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3$. Then $\langle e,Me\rangle=\tfrac13\sum u_i=0$ and $\lVert Me\rVert^2=\tfrac13\lvert\hat U\rvert^2$, so $\lVert PMP\rVert_F^2=\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-0-2\cdot\tfrac13\lvert\hat U\rvert^2=\tfrac13\lvert\hat U\rvert^2$, and a traceless symmetric $2\times2$ operator of that squared norm has eigenvalues $\pm\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$. Batch 18's two assertions about the compression are therefore both correct, and the identification of the diagonal matrices with $\mathbb R^3$ is an isometry, so no normalisation is lost. The domination identity $2\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-3u_k^2=(u_i-u_j)^2$ checks by hand. Hence $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)=\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$ and $\mu\ge2\beta\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$.

**The equality case is wrong as stated, and batch 19 inherits it.** The identity is about squares, so it gives equality in $3u_k^2\le2\lvert\hat U\rvert^2$ exactly when $u_i=u_j$. The inequality actually used is $-\tfrac{u_k}2\le\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$, and **that** is an equality only when $u_i=u_j$ **and** $u_k\le0$. At $u\sim(t,t,-2t)$ with $t<0$, for instance $(-1,-1,2)$, the off-diagonal directions give $\max_k(-u_k/2)=\lvert t\rvert/2$ against $\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6=\lvert t\rvert$, so the bound is **not** tight there. Batch 18's "with equality exactly when the other two eigenvalues agree, that is at $u\sim(t,t,-2t)$" is therefore correct only on the ray $t\ge0$, and batch 19's opening, "batch 18's bound is tight exactly at $\hat U\sim(t,t,-2t)$, so that is the spectrum on which the second-order condition says least", carries the error into the motivation for the whole slice at $t<0$.

**The arithmetic of batch 19 survives.** It uses only $\mu\ge2\beta\lvert t\rvert$, which holds at both signs, and both floors check by hand. At $t>0$, $Q(2\beta t)=\tfrac{2673}{16}t^2-\tfrac{81}{32}\lvert V\rvert^2$ and $Q\le0$ asks $\lvert V\rvert^2\ge66t^2$. At $t<0$ the vertex $\mu^\ast=-3t=3\lvert t\rvert$ does fall below $4.5\lvert t\rvert$ and $Q(2\beta\lvert t\rvert)=\tfrac{81}{16}t^2-\tfrac{81}{32}\lvert V\rvert^2$, giving $\lvert V\rvert^2\ge2t^2$. What fails is only the claim that $t<0$ is a case where the second-order condition says least.

**One more stale constant in the same sentence.** Batch 19's opening still writes the bound as $\mu\ge\beta\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$, the halved form batch 18 withdrew, and repairs it three paragraphs later. The superseded constant is left standing in the sentence a reader meets first.

## Item 4, batch 21's second variation. CONFIRMED, with two unnamed hypotheses

**The coefficients.** $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\hat U+sX)^3)$ has $s^2$ coefficient $3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX^2)$ by cyclicity, and the $s^2$ part of $\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\hat U+sX)(V+sY)^2)$ is $\beta(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UY^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XYV))$. The two cross traces are equal **because $X$, $Y$ and $V$ are symmetric**: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XYV)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((XYV)^{\mathsf T})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VYX)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)$. So the coefficient is $3\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX^2)+\beta(2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UY^2))$ as displayed, and the norm's two coefficients are as displayed. Doubling the $s^2$ coefficients to get the second derivative reproduces the stated condition $6\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX^2)+4\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)+2\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UY^2)\le\mu(3\lvert X\rvert^2+\lvert Y\rvert^2)$ exactly. *The symmetry step is named in the Lean docstring and not in the prose.*

**The acceleration argument is stated correctly.** $D(\Phi-\mu T)=0$ at a critical point is precisely the Lagrange condition, so along a constraint curve $\tfrac{d^2}{ds^2}(\Phi\circ\gamma)=D^2(\Phi-\mu T)[\gamma',\gamma']+D(\Phi-\mu T)[\gamma'']$ and the second term vanishes for every curve at once. **It does need the constraint to be a manifold near the point**, both to produce a curve with a prescribed tangent and to know the tangent space is the kernel of the tangency form. The regularity holds, since $\nabla T=(3\hat U,V)$ vanishes only at $\hat U=V=0$, that is only at $T=0$, but batch 21 never states it. Second unnamed hypothesis.

**The specialisation is right and its comparison is misleading.** $Y=0$ with $X=E_{ij}+E_{ji}$ is tangent because $\hat U$ is diagonal, $X^2=E_{ii}+E_{jj}$ and $\lvert X\rvert^2=2$ give $6\alpha(-u_k)\le6\mu$, so $\mu\ge-\alpha u_k$ at every $k$ and $\mu\ge\alpha\lvert u_{\min}\rvert$. But the closing phrase, "a bound with $\alpha=\tfrac{15}4$ in place of batch 18's $\beta$", invites the reading that this is batch 18's bound improved, and it is not: this one is $2\alpha$ times the top Rayleigh ratio of the **off-diagonal block only**, while batch 18's is $2\beta$ times the top ratio over the whole space. Neither dominates. At $u=(1,1,-2)$ they are $7.5$ and $4.5$, at $u=(2,-1,-1)$ they are $3.75$ and $4.5$.

## Item 8, batch 26's parity. CONFIRMED, with the stated reason incomplete

**The group action is identified correctly.** Conjugating by the transposition of $e_1$ and $e_2$ sends $V_{12}\mapsto V_{21}$, so $w_3$ is fixed, and sends $V_{13}\mapsto V_{23}$, so $w_1$ and $w_2$ swap. Flipping the sign of $e_1$ sends $V_{12}\mapsto-V_{12}$ and $V_{13}\mapsto-V_{13}$ and leaves $V_{23}$, so it flips $w_3$ and $w_2$ and fixes $w_1$: **exactly two**, as claimed. The sign patterns generated are the Klein four-group of even flips, so flipping all three $w_k$ is **not** in the group. *Screened, as a control: the displayed polynomial is neither fixed nor negated by flipping all three, which is what one expects of a substitution outside the group, and this is the same trap as batch 26's second false alarm.*

**The parity checks.** *Screened on the explicit displayed polynomial, with simultaneous substitution*: the transposition of indices 1 and 2 negates it, the three-cycle with the $w$ indices cycled the same way as the axes fixes it, and the sign flip of $e_1$ fixes it. The $\alpha$ side is $3\alpha w_1w_2w_3D_1D_2D_3$ with $w_1w_2w_3$ invariant and $D_1D_2D_3$ the Vandermonde, so the sign character is forced there, exactly as batch 26 argues. Two repairs are available and either is enough: conjugate $A$ and $C$ too, which moves the frame $F$ and is a symmetry of the whole problem, or observe that the eliminated equation involves only the coordinates of one pair in one eigenframe of $\hat U$, so relabelling is a change of representation of the same pair and invariance is automatic. **The second is the stronger statement and it makes the check a consistency test rather than a discovery.**

## Item 9, the two negative results. Batch 24 correctly scoped, batch 23's reason refuted

**Batch 23's spectrum claim is right and its structural claim is not.** The five eigenvalues of $\mathcal L_V$ on traceless symmetric matrices are $\pm c$ with $c=\lvert V\rvert/\sqrt6$, together with $-v_1/2,-v_2/2,-v_3/2$, and by the same domination as item 2 every $-v_k/2$ lies in $[-c,c]$. So the sorted list is $c\ge p\ge q\ge r\ge-c$ and **$\lambda_3$ is exactly the median of $\{-v_k/2\}$**. That median is not pinned near zero. Maximising it subject to $\sum(-v_k/2)=0$ and $\sum(v_k/2)^2=\tfrac32c^2$ gives $p=q$, $r=-2q$ and $q=c/2$, so $\lambda_3\le\lambda_{\max}/2$ with equality at a degenerate spectrum of $V$. **The correct structural statement is that interlacing can certify at most half the unrestricted maximum**, and that is not "a quantity that cannot be large". Nor does it close the route by itself: the cap is roughly $\sqrt3\mu/(2\beta)$, which the record's own bound puts at $\ge\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt2$, while $\lambda_3\le\lvert V\rvert/(2\sqrt6)$, and $\lvert V\rvert$ is not controlled against $\lvert\hat U\rvert$ at fixed total norm, so $\lambda_3$ exceeding the cap is not excluded a priori.

**The conclusion survives on a different reason.** $\lambda_3(\mathcal L_V)$ is a function of $V$'s spectrum alone, so an interlacing bound cannot see $[\hat U,V]$ whatever its size, and even a $\lambda_3$ above the cap would constrain $\lvert V\rvert$ against $\mu$ rather than deliver commutativity. That is batch 24's reason, stated in batch 24, and it is the one that carries. **Batch 23's verdict PLAUSIBLE, its stated reason REFUTED.** The observation that Mathlib carries no interlacing or Courant-Fischer statement is a cost claim about typing, not part of the mathematics.

**Batch 24 is correctly scoped, and it says so itself.** The sample is of generic pairs, and the conclusion drawn is that **no inequality holding for every pair** can bound the restricted maximum below by the commutator, which is exactly what a generic counterexample supports. The batch then writes, in its own words, that this does not rule out a proof consuming the criticality equations and does not show the inequality useless. **That is the right scope and it is not an overreach.** The one sentence to watch is "which is the only thing the second-order condition by itself can supply", which is a claim about a method rather than a theorem, and the surrounding text treats it as such. I cannot re-run the $159273$-pair sample on this footing, so the number itself is taken as reported and the reasoning around it is what I have checked.

## Item 10, batch 27's block. CONFIRMED, with one overstatement

**The saturation is right.** Adjoining $t$ with $tw_1w_2w_3-1$ makes the variety of the enlarged ideal the set of solutions with $w_1w_2w_3\ne0$, and a Groebner basis $\{1\}$ says $1$ is in the ideal, so by the Nullstellensatz there is no solution over $\mathbb C$, hence none over $\mathbb R$. **So the direction batch 27 needs is sound**, and it holds for the fixed spectrum $(3,1,-4)$ only, which the batch states twice and closes on.
