## The statement

In [L271](../findings/second-order.md)'s coordinates, $P=U+V$ and $Q=U-V$ are symmetric $m\times m$ with zero diagonal, $d_i=\tfrac12\sum_l(P_{il}^2+Q_{il}^2)$, $b^2=\sum_id_i$, $T=\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)$ and $g^2=\lVert A_\mu\rVert^2=\sum_id_i^2+\sum_{i\ne j}P_{ij}^2Q_{ij}^2$. With $\lambda_m=\sqrt{2m}\,(m-2)/(m-1)^{3/2}$,

$$\textbf{(J1)}\qquad T\le\lambda_m(1-g^2)\quad\text{whenever }b=1,\ m\ge5.$$

## Forms of the same statement

- Since $\sum_id_i=1$, $1-g^2=\sum_{i\ne j}\big(d_id_j-P_{ij}^2Q_{ij}^2\big)$, and every summand is non-negative because $d_i\ge\tfrac12(P_{ij}^2+Q_{ij}^2)\ge\lvert P_{ij}Q_{ij}\rvert$. So J1 is $T\le\lambda_m\sum_{i\ne j}(d_id_j-P_{ij}^2Q_{ij}^2)$, and $g\le1$ on the whole sphere with equality exactly when $d_id_j=P_{ij}^2Q_{ij}^2$ for every pair.
- In $U,V$: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3=12\sum_{\text{triangles }abc}\big(U_{ab}U_{bc}U_{ca}+U_{ab}V_{bc}V_{ca}+V_{ab}U_{bc}V_{ca}+V_{ab}V_{bc}U_{ca}\big)$, the terms with an even number of $V$'s, and $P_{ij}Q_{ij}=U_{ij}^2-V_{ij}^2$. The right side depends on squares only, so J1 reduces to non-negative $\lvert U_e\rvert$, $\lvert V_e\rvert$.

## Equality cases on file

- The uniform point: $U=V=u(J-I)$, $u^2=1/(2m(m-1))$, $Q=0$, $g^2=\tfrac1m$, $T=\sqrt2(m-2)/\sqrt{m(m-1)}=\lambda_m(1-\tfrac1m)$.
- The $\Lambda^2_+$ edge: $P_{12}=Q_{12}=1/\sqrt2$ and every other entry zero, $T=0$, $g^2=1$.

- Second order at the uniform point (this note, by hand): with $P=\alpha K+R$, $K=(J-I)/\sqrt{m(m-1)}$, the deficit is $\tfrac{3\alpha}{2\sqrt{m(m-1)}}\big(\tfrac m2\lVert R\rVert^2-\lVert R\mathbf 1\rVert^2\big)$ against a concentration of $\alpha^2\lVert R\mathbf 1\rVert^2/(m(m-1))$. On the worst direction $R_{ij}=a_i+a_j$ their ratio is $2(m-2)^2/(3(m-1)^2)<1$, so the uniform point has slack in every $Q=0$ direction at second order; the binding configurations are global.

## Batch 1 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): J1 reduces to a pair-sum inequality J1*, screened exact

*The cocktail-party form.* $M=\begin{pmatrix}U&V\\V&U\end{pmatrix}$ is a symmetric $2m\times2m$ matrix vanishing on the diagonal and on the matching $\{i,i'\}$, the root graph of $D_m$: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3=2T$, $\lVert M\rVert^2=\lVert P\rVert^2+\lVert Q\rVert^2$, and $(M^2)_{aa}=d_i$ at $a\in\{i,i'\}$. At the uniform point $M=u(J-I-\Pi)$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3/\lVert M\rVert^3=c_m=(m-2)/\sqrt{m(m-1)}$, the same constant as the $m\times m$ traceless bound. Also $A_\mu=\sum_{i<j}\big(U_{ij}^2(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^{\mathsf T}+V_{ij}^2(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^{\mathsf T}\big)$ is positive semidefinite of trace $1$, so $1-g^2=(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_\mu)^2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_\mu^2$.

*The row bound.* $(M^2)_{ab}=\sum_cM_{ac}M_{cb}$ over $c\notin\{a,b,a',b'\}$, since $M$ vanishes on the matching. Cauchy–Schwarz over that range, with $y_{ij}=U_{ij}^2$, $z_{ij}=V_{ij}^2$, $w_{ij}=y_{ij}+z_{ij}$, $D_i=\sum_jw_{ij}$, gives $(M^2)_{ij},(M^2)_{ij'}\le\sqrt{(D_i-w_{ij})(D_j-w_{ij})}$, so
$$T\le\sum_{i\ne j}s_{ij}\sqrt{(D_i-w_{ij})(D_j-w_{ij})},\qquad s_{ij}=\lvert U_{ij}\rvert+\lvert V_{ij}\rvert,$$
tight at the uniform point and at the $\Lambda^2_+$ edge (where $D_i=w_{ij}$). With $1-g^2=\sum_{i\ne j}\big(D_iD_j-w_{ij}^2+(s_{ij}^2-w_{ij})^2\big)$ at $S=\sum_iD_i=1$ (using $(y-z)^2=w^2-(s^2-w)^2$), J1 follows from
$$\textbf{(J1*)}\qquad\sqrt S\sum_{i\ne j}s_{ij}\sqrt{(D_i-w_{ij})(D_j-w_{ij})}\le\lambda_m\sum_{i\ne j}\big(D_iD_j-w_{ij}^2+(s_{ij}^2-w_{ij})^2\big),\qquad\sqrt{w_{ij}}\le s_{ij}\le\sqrt{2w_{ij}}.$$
*Screen (250 L-BFGS starts per $m$, [j1_gmfull2.py] in the session scratchpad):* J1* holds at $m=4,5,6,7,8$ with maximum exactly $0$. The first version, with $c$ ranging over $a'$, $b'$ too, fails by exactly $1/\sqrt{2m(m-1)}$ at the uniform point, which is the mismatch of supports it ignores. At $Q=0$ the same bound is the $m\times m$ row bound kept as a geometric mean, and holds at $m=5$ to $8$; [batch 52](2026-09-26-I73-class-ceiling.md)'s failure came from the arithmetic-mean step after it.

*What J1* is.* Both sides are sums over pairs, coupled only through the vertex sums $D_i$ and through $\sqrt S$. The pairwise summand of the difference is not non-negative (a pair of low-degree vertices in a heavy graph breaks it), so the proof must be global in the degrees.

## Batch 2 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): J1 at $Q=0$ proved, a degree-refined traceless cubic bound

**Theorem (J1₀).** *Let $W$ be a real symmetric $m\times m$ matrix with zero diagonal, $m\ge3$, with row sums of squares $D_i=\sum_jW_{ij}^2$ and $S=\sum_iD_i=\lVert W\rVert^2$. Then*
$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3\le K_m\,\frac{S^2-\sum_iD_i^2}{\sqrt S},\qquad K_m=\frac{\sqrt m\,(m-2)}{(m-1)^{3/2}},$$
*with equality at $W=J-I$.* Since $\sum_iD_i^2\ge S^2/m$ it contains the traceless cubic bound $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3\le\tfrac{m-2}{\sqrt{m(m-1)}}S^{3/2}$, and at $Q=0$ ($P=W$, $\lVert W\rVert^2=2$, $d_i=D_i/2$) it is J1 exactly, because $\sqrt2K_m=\lambda_m$.

**Proof.** Put $x_{ij}=W_{ij}^2$, $n=m-1$, and, for $D_i>0$, the reversible walk $p_{ij}=x_{ij}/D_i$ with stationary law $\pi_i=D_i/S$.

1. *Rows.* $(W^2)_{ij}=\sum_{k\ne i,j}W_{ik}W_{kj}$, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3=\sum_{i\ne j}W_{ij}(W^2)_{ij}\le\sum_{i\ne j}\sqrt{x_{ij}(D_i-x_{ij})(D_j-x_{ij})}$.
2. *Pairing.* Writing each term as $\sqrt{x_{ij}(1-p_{ij})(1-p_{ji})}\cdot\sqrt{D_iD_j}$ and applying Cauchy–Schwarz over ordered pairs, with $\sum_{i\ne j}D_iD_j=S^2-\sum D_i^2$: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3\le\sqrt{S\,\mathbb E\big[(1-p_{ij})(1-p_{ji})\big]}\,\sqrt{S^2-\sum_iD_i^2}$, the expectation over the edge law $x_{ij}/S$.
3. *Symmetrising.* $(1-p_{ij})(1-p_{ji})\le\tfrac12(1-p_{ij})^2+\tfrac12(1-p_{ji})^2$ and the edge law is symmetric, so $\mathbb E[\cdot]\le\sum_i\pi_ih(p_{i\cdot})$ with $h(p)=\sum_kp_k(1-p_k)^2$.
4. *Lemma A.* For a probability vector $p$ on $n\ge2$ points, $h(p)\le h_{\max}-\tfrac{n-1}n\big(\sum_kp_k^2-\tfrac1n\big)$, $h_{\max}=\big(\tfrac{n-1}n\big)^2$. It is equivalent to $\sum_kp_k(1-p_k)(np_k-1)\ge0$, and since $\sum_k(p_k-\tfrac1n)=0$ and $f(p)=p(1-p)$ has $f(p)-f(\tfrac1n)=(p-\tfrac1n)(1-\tfrac1n-p)$, the left side is $n\sum_k(p_k-\tfrac1n)^2(1-\tfrac1n-p_k)$. Every term is non-negative unless some $p_1=a>1-\tfrac1n$, and there is at most one such coordinate; then every other $p_k\le1-a$ has $1-\tfrac1n-p_k\ge a-\tfrac1n$, and Cauchy–Schwarz on $\sum_{k\ge2}(p_k-\tfrac1n)=\tfrac1n-a$ gives a total of at least $(a-\tfrac1n)^2\big[(1-\tfrac1n-a)+\tfrac{a-1/n}{n-1}\big]=(a-\tfrac1n)^2\tfrac{n-2}{n-1}(1-a)\ge0$.
5. *(★).* $\sum_i\pi_i\sum_jp_{ij}^2\ge\big(1+(n^2-1)\sum_i\pi_i^2\big)/n^2$. With $S=1$: $\sum_i\pi_i\sum_jp_{ij}^2=\sum_j\sum_ix_{ij}^2/D_i\ge\sum_jD_j^2/(1-D_j)$ by Cauchy–Schwarz over $i$ ($\sum_{i\ne j}D_i=1-D_j$), and $\psi(t)=t^2/(1-t)-\tfrac{n^2-1}{n^2}t^2$ has $\psi''=2/(1-t)^3-2\tfrac{n^2-1}{n^2}>0$ on $[0,1)$, so Jensen over the $m=n+1$ vertices gives $\sum_j\psi(D_j)\ge(n+1)\psi(\tfrac1{n+1})=\tfrac1{n^2}$.
6. *Assembly.* Steps 3–5 give $\mathbb E[\cdot]\le h_{\max}+\tfrac{n-1}{n^2}-\tfrac{n-1}n\cdot\tfrac{1+(n^2-1)\sum\pi_i^2}{n^2}=K_m^2\big(1-\sum_i\pi_i^2\big)$, $K_m^2=\tfrac{(n+1)(n-1)^2}{n^3}$, and step 2 closes. $\square$

*Screens behind each step (L-BFGS, 200 to 300 starts):* steps 1–2 at $m=4$ to $10$, step 3 at $m=4$ to $10$, Lemma A at $n=3$ to $9$ and (★) at $m=4$ to $10$, every maximum of the defect exactly $0$; they were run before the proofs and are now redundant.

*Where the edge is paid.* At the $\Lambda^2_+$ edge every step but Jensen in (★) is an equality; the whole slack of step 6 there, $K_m^2/2$, comes from the concentration of $\pi$ on two vertices.

## Batch 3 (research level): toward $Q\ne0$

The cocktail-party row bound and a Cauchy–Schwarz pairing with $R_{ij}=d_id_j-\delta_{ij}s_{ij}^2$, $\delta_{ij}=(\lvert U_{ij}\rvert-\lvert V_{ij}\rvert)^2$, reduce J1 to
$$\textbf{(J1*b)}\qquad\sum_{i\ne j}\frac{s_{ij}^2(d_i-w_{ij})(d_j-w_{ij})}{R_{ij}}\le\lambda_m^2\sum_{i\ne j}R_{ij}\qquad(\textstyle\sum_id_i=1),$$
screened with maximum exactly $0$ at $m=4$ to $8$; $\sum_{i\ne j}R_{ij}=1-g^2$. Pairing with $\sqrt{d_id_j}$ instead fails by about $0.05$ at every $m$ tried: the $\sum(y-z)^2$ term of $1-g^2$ must sit inside the pairing. In the block walk ($x_{ij}=w_{ij}$, $q_{ij}=w_{ij}/d_i$, $\gamma_{ij}=2\sqrt{y_{ij}z_{ij}}/w_{ij}$), J1*b is $\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}(1+\gamma)A/(1-B(1-\gamma^2))\le2K_m^2\sum_{i\ne j}\pi_i\pi_j(1-B(1-\gamma^2))$ with $A=(1-q_{ij})(1-q_{ji})$, $B=q_{ij}q_{ji}$; at $\gamma\equiv1$ it is step 2's inequality of batch 2, which is proved.

*The separate bound.* $T=\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Q^3)\le\tfrac12(\Gamma(P)+\Gamma(Q))$ with $\Gamma(W)=\sum_{i\ne j}\sqrt{x_{ij}(D_i-x_{ij})(D_j-x_{ij})}$ step (1) of batch 2 for each matrix, and
$$\textbf{(SEP)}\qquad\tfrac12\big(\Gamma(P)+\Gamma(Q)\big)\le\lambda_m\sum_{i\ne j}\big(d_id_j-P_{ij}^2Q_{ij}^2\big),\qquad d=\tfrac12(D^P+D^Q),\ \textstyle\sum_id_i=1,$$
screens exact at $m=4$ to $8$. In it the right side splits over ordered pairs as $\tfrac14D^P_iD^P_j+\tfrac14D^Q_iD^Q_j+\tfrac14(D^P_iD^Q_j+D^Q_iD^P_j)-x^P_{ij}x^Q_{ij}$, zero on the edge pair. Bounding each $\Gamma$ by L274 alone fails at the edge (L274's bound on a single edge is positive, $\Gamma$ is $0$), and so does a fixed-weight AM-GM after step (2): whatever combines the halves must keep step (2)'s factor $\mathbb E[(1-p_{ij})(1-p_{ji})]$, which vanishes there.

*J1*b as slack accounting.* For fixed pair weights the $\gamma_{ij}$ are free and independent, so J1*b is $\sum_{i\ne j}\max_{\gamma}\big(\varphi_{ij}(1)-\varphi_{ij}(\gamma)\big)\le2\,\mathrm{Slack}_0$, where $\varphi_{ij}(\gamma)=2K_m^2\pi_i\pi_j(1-c)-x_{ij}(1+\gamma)A/(1-c)$, $c=B(1-\gamma^2)$, and $\mathrm{Slack}_0=K_m^2(1-\sum\pi_i^2)-\sum x_{ij}A_{ij}\ge0$ is L274's slack, the sum of the slacks of its steps (3), (4) and (5). Per pair, $\varphi(\gamma)-\varphi(1)=\pi_i\pi_j\big[-2K_m^2B(1-\gamma^2)+\omega A(1-\gamma)(1-2B(1+\gamma))/(1-c)\big]$, $\omega=x_{ij}/(\pi_i\pi_j)$: a pair can only be in deficit when $B=q_{ij}q_{ji}$ is large, and pairs with $B\ge\tfrac14$ need $q_{ij},q_{ji}$ large, so the pairs in deficit form nearly a matching. At the $\Lambda^2_+$ edge the deficit is exactly $K_m^2/2$ per ordered pair direction, paid by the Jensen slack of (5); at an isolated heavy pair in a large graph it is paid by the Cauchy–Schwarz slack of (5) at its two vertices. A proof along these lines bounds each heavy pair's deficit by the slack its two vertices carry.

## Batch 4 (research level): J1 at $Q\ne0$ in polar form

Write $(P_{ij},Q_{ij})=r_{ij}(\cos\theta_{ij},\sin\theta_{ij})$ and $\hat W=(r_{ij})$, a non-negative zero-diagonal matrix with $\hat W_{ij}^2=P_{ij}^2+Q_{ij}^2=2w_{ij}$, degrees $2d_i$ and $\lVert\hat W\rVert^2=2$. Hölder on each triangle, $\lvert P_1P_2P_3+Q_1Q_2Q_3\rvert\le r_1r_2r_3$, gives $T\le\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat W^3$, and L274 at $\hat W$ gives exactly $\lambda_m(1-\sum_id_i^2)$: J1 without its cross term. So J1 at fixed $\hat W$ is
$$\max_\theta\Big[\tfrac12\sum_{\text{ordered triangles}}r_1r_2r_3(c_1c_2c_3+s_1s_2s_3)+\tfrac{\lambda_m}4\sum_{i\ne j}r_{ij}^4\sin^22\theta_{ij}\Big]\le\lambda_m\Big(1-\sum_id_i^2\Big),$$
true at $\theta\equiv0$ by L274. The cross term has to be paid by the cubic's loss away from $\theta\equiv0,\tfrac\pi2$ or by L274's slack at $\hat W$; at the edge it is paid exactly by the slack ($\lambda_m/2$ each).

## Batch 5 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): J1 on the face $P=Q$ proved for $m\ge4$

At $V=0$ ($P=Q=U$, the face through the $\Lambda^2_+$ edge) J1 reads, homogeneously, for a real symmetric zero-diagonal $U$ with $x_{ij}=U_{ij}^2$, $D_i=\sum_jx_{ij}$, $S=\sum_iD_i$:
$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3\sqrt S\le\lambda_m\sum_{i\ne j}\big(D_iD_j-x_{ij}^2\big).$$
*Screened exact at $m=4$ to $8$ (maximum $0$, at the edge).* **Proof** ($m\ge4$, $\kappa=2K_m^2=\lambda_m^2$, walk $p_{ij}=x_{ij}/D_i$, $\pi_i=D_i/S$, $\rho_i=\sum_jp_{ij}^2$):
1. Rows, as in L274: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3\le\sum_{i\ne j}\sqrt{x_{ij}e_{ij}e_{ji}}$, $e_{ij}=D_i-x_{ij}$.
2. Pairing with $R_{ij}=D_iD_j-x_{ij}^2\ge e_{ij}e_{ji}$: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3\le\sqrt{\sum x_{ij}e_{ij}e_{ji}/R_{ij}}\sqrt{\sum R_{ij}}$.
3. $x\,ee'/R=x\,f(p_{ij},p_{ji})$ with $f(p,q)=\tfrac{(1-p)(1-q)}{1-pq}$. In $a=\tfrac{1-p}{1+p}$, $b=\tfrac{1-q}{1+q}$ one has $f=\tfrac{2ab}{a+b}\le\tfrac{a+b}2\le\tfrac12\big((1-p)+(1-q)\big)$, so $\sum x\,ee'/R\le\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}(1-p_{ij})=S\sum_i\pi_i(1-\rho_i)$.
4. **(V0-b)** $\sum_i\pi_i(1-\rho_i)\le\kappa\big(1-\sum_i\pi_i^2-\sum_i\pi_i^2\rho_i\big)$, equivalently $\sum_i\pi_i(1-\kappa\pi_i)\rho_i\ge1-\kappa+\kappa s$, $s=\sum\pi_i^2$. At $S=1$ the left side is $\sum_j\sum_ie_{ij}^2(1/\pi_i-\kappa)$ ($e_{ij}=x_{ij}$), and $1/\pi_i-\kappa>0$ since $\pi_i\le\tfrac12$ and $\kappa<2$; Cauchy–Schwarz over $i$ gives at least $\sum_j\pi_j^2/(B-\beta_j)$ with $\beta_i=\pi_i/(1-\kappa\pi_i)$, $B=\sum\beta_i$.
5. **$H(\pi)=\sum_j\pi_j^2/(B-\beta_j)-\kappa s-(1-\kappa)\ge0$ on the simplex with $\pi_j\le\tfrac12$.** If $s\le1-1/\kappa$ the subtracted part is $\le0$. Otherwise: the chord of the convex $1/(1-\kappa t)$ on $[0,\tfrac12]$ gives $\beta_i\le\pi_i(1+\gamma\pi_i)$, $\gamma=2\kappa/(2-\kappa)$, so $B-\beta_j\le1-\pi_j+\gamma(s-\pi_j^2)$; Titu's form of Cauchy–Schwarz and $\sum\pi^3\ge s^2$, $\sum\pi^4\ge s^3$ give $\sum_j\pi_j^2/(B-\beta_j)\ge s/\big((1-s)(1+\gamma s)\big)$. Multiplying by $(1-s)(2-\kappa+2\kappa s)>0$, the needed $s/((1-s)(1+\gamma s))\ge\kappa s+1-\kappa$ is $(1-2s)\big[(\kappa-1)-(\kappa(1-s)-1)^2\big]\ge0$ (symbolic factorisation); with $\kappa(1-s)\in[\tfrac\kappa2,1]$ the bracket is at least $-\tfrac14(\kappa^2-8\kappa+8)>0$ for $\kappa>4-2\sqrt2$, so this holds for $m\ge4$. (A first version argued by convexity of $u(s)=s/((1-s)(1+\gamma s))-\kappa s-(1-\kappa)$ on $[\tfrac14,\tfrac12]$ and needed $m\ge5$; the factorisation replaces it.)
6. Closing: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3\le\sqrt{\kappa\sum R/S}\sqrt{\sum R}=\lambda_m\sum R/\sqrt S$. $\square$

*Screens behind the steps:* step 3's inequality on $2\times10^5$ random pairs, (V0-b) at $m=4$ to $14$, $H\ge0$ at $m=4$ to $20$ (400 Nelder–Mead starts), every minimum exactly $0$ (at $\pi=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0,\dots)$).

With L274 this settles J1 on both faces, $Q=0$ and $P=Q$; the mixed interior is open.

## Batch 6 (research level): the mixed interior

With both faces proved, J1*b remains at mixed $\gamma_{ij}\in(0,1)$. Per pair its summand is $\varphi(t)=x(1+\sqrt{1-t})A/(1-Bt)-\kappa(\pi_i\pi_j-x^2t)$ in $t=1-\gamma^2$, the right side being linear in $t$. $\varphi$ is not convex: at $B\approx0$ the first term is concave, and for $A>0$ its derivative tends to $-\infty$ as $t\to1$, so the maximum over $t$ can sit strictly inside $(0,1)$ (at the edge, $A=0$, it sits at $t=1$). So the mixed case does not reduce to configurations whose pairs are each of pure type ($\gamma\in\{0,1\}$), and it needs an argument that interpolates the two face proofs: L274's AM-GM bound $A\le\tfrac12((1-p)^2+(1-q)^2)$ and L275's harmonic bound $A/(1-B)\le\tfrac12((1-p)+(1-q))$ both have to enter with $\gamma$-dependent weights.

*A second attempt on the interior, ending at an obstacle.* In (SEP), bound each $\Gamma(W)$, $W\in\{P,Q\}$, by the smaller of its two proved single-matrix bounds, L274's $K_mE_W/\sqrt{S_W}$ and L275's $\lambda_m(E_W-X_W)/\sqrt{S_W}$ ($E_W=S_W^2-\sum D^W_i{}^2$, $X_W=\sum x^W_{ij}{}^2$). The resulting inequality among the moments $S_W,E_W,X_W,E_{PQ},X_{PQ}$ fails by about $0.04$ at every $m=4$ to $8$ (screen): the coupling of $P$ and $Q$ through shared vertices is not carried by these moments.

*Where the interior stands.* Four approaches have ended at named obstacles: reduction to pure-type pairs (interior maxima in $\gamma$), the $\sqrt{d_id_j}$ pairing (fails by $0.05$), a fixed-weight AM-GM decoupling of (SEP) (fails at the edge), and the per-matrix moment bounds (fails by $0.04$). The two face proofs share their first two steps and differ in the pairing ($D_iD_j$ against $D_iD_j-x_{ij}^2$) and in the walk bound (the symmetrised square against the harmonic mean); an interior proof has to interpolate both with $\gamma$-dependent weights at the level of the walk, not of the moments.

## Batch 7 (research level): J1 reduced to a per-pair interpolation and a mixed walk inequality

The two face proofs interpolate. With $p=q_{ij}$, $q=q_{ji}$, $A=(1-p)(1-q)$, $B=pq$ and $\gamma=\gamma_{ij}$:
$$\textbf{(PI)}\qquad\frac{(1+\gamma)A}{1-B(1-\gamma^2)}\le\gamma\big((1-p)^2+(1-q)^2\big)+(1-\gamma)\tfrac{(1-p)+(1-q)}2\qquad\text{on }[0,1]^3,$$
L274's symmetrisation at $\gamma=1$ and L275's harmonic bound at $\gamma=0$. Summed over ordered pairs against $w_{ij}=\pi_iq_{ij}$ ($S=1$) and symmetrised, J1*b follows from
$$\textbf{(MW)}\qquad\sum_i\pi_i\sum_jq_{ij}(1-q_{ij})\big(1+\gamma_{ij}(1-2q_{ij})\big)\le\kappa\Big(1-\sum_i\pi_i^2-\sum_{i\ne j}w_{ij}^2(1-\gamma_{ij}^2)\Big),$$
which at $\gamma\equiv1$ is L274's walk inequality and at $\gamma\equiv0$ is L275's (V0-b). *Screens:* PI on $2\times10^6$ random points of the cube, maximum $-7\times10^{-7}$ (tight only as $\gamma\to1$, $p=q$); MW at $m=5$ to $8$ (200 L-BFGS starts), maximum $0$; MW fails at $m=4$ by $0.023$. So **J1 ⟸ J1*b ⟸ PI + MW**, and it remains to prove PI and MW.

*MW at constant $\gamma$.* With $F^1_{ij}=2w_{ij}(1-q_{ij})^2$, $F^0_{ij}=w_{ij}(1-q_{ij})$, the face slacks $\mathrm{Sl}_1=\kappa(1-s)-\sum F^1$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_0=\kappa(1-s-\sum w^2)-\sum F^0$ (both $\ge0$, proved) give MW at constant $\gamma$ as soon as $(1-\gamma)\mathrm{Sl}_0+\gamma\,\mathrm{Sl}_1\ge\kappa\gamma(1-\gamma)\sum w_{ij}^2$, and minimising $\mathrm{Sl}_0/\gamma+\mathrm{Sl}_1/(1-\gamma)$ over $\gamma$ shows this holds for every $\gamma$ iff $(\sqrt{\mathrm{Sl}_0}+\sqrt{\mathrm{Sl}_1})^2\ge\kappa\sum_{i\ne j}w_{ij}^2$. That slack inequality screens exact at $m=5$ to $10$ (maximum $0$, at the edge, where $\mathrm{Sl}_0=0$ and $\mathrm{Sl}_1=\kappa\sum w^2=\kappa/2$).

*MW at varying $\gamma$.* Its defect is separable and concave in the $\gamma_{ij}$ (linear on the left, $+\kappa\sum w^2\gamma^2$ on the right), so its maximum over the cube is at $\gamma_{ij}^*=\mathrm{clip}\big(a_{ij}/(2b_{ij})\big)$ with $a_{ij}=w_{ij}\big[(1-q_{ij})(1-2q_{ij})+(1-q_{ji})(1-2q_{ji})\big]$, $b_{ij}=2\kappa w_{ij}^2$, generally not constant; the constant-$\gamma$ argument does not reach it.

## Batch 8 (research level): PI proved; MW is a heavy-pair cost against L274's slack

**PI is proved** (kernel-checked as `L275_interp` in [L275.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L275.lean)). In $a=1-p$, $b=1-q$ and with $g=\sqrt{ab}$, AM-GM gives $t(a^2+b^2)+(1-t)\tfrac{a+b}2\ge2tg^2+(1-t)g$ and $(1-a)(1-b)\le(1-g)^2$, so PI reduces to its symmetric case, which is $g(1-g)(1-t)Q(g,t)\ge0$ with $Q=2t(t+1)g^2-(3t-1)(t+1)g+t^2$. For $t\le\tfrac13$ every term of $Q$ is non-negative; for $t\ge\tfrac13$, $8tQ=(t+1)(4tg-3t+1)^2+(1-t)(t^2+4t-1)$ and $t^2+4t-1>0$.

**MW as a heavy-pair cost.** Per unordered pair put $c=2\kappa w^2$ and $\delta=w\big[(1-q)(1-2q)+(1-q')(1-2q')\big]$. MW's defect is separable and concave in the $\gamma$'s, and maximising pair by pair turns MW into
$$\sum_{\text{pairs}}\Psi_1\le\mathrm{Sl}_1,\qquad\Psi_1=\max_{u\in[0,1]}\big(u(2c-\delta)-cu^2\big),$$
with $\mathrm{Sl}_1=\kappa(1-s)-2\sum_i\pi_ih(q_{i\cdot})\ge0$ L274's slack (twice the slack of its steps (4) and (5)). $\Psi_1=0$ on every pair with $\delta\ge2c$ (the light pairs), so only heavy pairs are charged; at the $\Lambda^2_+$ edge $\Psi_1=c=\kappa/2=\mathrm{Sl}_1$. *Screens:* the reformulation reproduces MW exactly (maximum $0$ at $m=5$ to $8$); the simpler per-pair cost $c+\max(0,-\delta)$ on pairs with $\delta<2c$ also holds at $m=6,7,8$ but fails at $m=5$ by $0.007$, so at $m=5$ the exact $\Psi_1$ is needed. The remaining step is to bound $\sum\Psi_1$ by the three pieces of $\mathrm{Sl}_1$ (Lemma A's slack, the Cauchy–Schwarz slack and the Jensen slack of L274 (5)), heavy pair by heavy pair.

## Batch 9 (research level): MW decoupled

Relaxing the symmetric $\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ji}$ of MW to an independent $\gamma$ per ordered pair can only raise its left side, and it decouples the vertices: MW follows from
$$\textbf{(MW')}\qquad\sum_i\pi_i\sum_jF(q_{ij},\pi_i)\le\kappa\Big(1-\sum_i\pi_i^2\Big),\qquad F(q,\pi)=q(1-q)+\kappa\pi q^2+\max_{\gamma\in[0,1]}\big(q(1-q)(1-2q)\gamma-\kappa\pi q^2\gamma^2\big).$$
*Screen:* MW' holds at $m=5$ to $8$ (250 L-BFGS starts), maximum $0$. $F=2q(1-q)^2+E(q,\pi)$, L274's face term plus an excess $E=\max_{u\in[0,1]}\big(u(2\kappa\pi q^2-a)-\kappa\pi q^2u^2\big)$, $a=q(1-q)(1-2q)$, which vanishes unless $(1-q)(1-2q)<2\kappa\pi q$ (heavy entries of heavy vertices) and equals $\kappa\pi$ at $q=1$. So MW' is L274's walk inequality with an excess $\sum_i\pi_i\sum_jE(q_{ij},\pi_i)$ to be paid by L274's slack. A vertex-by-vertex payment fails at hub vertices with spread rows, where the (★) part of the slack is negative locally; the payment has to go through (★)'s column Cauchy–Schwarz and Jensen, as the edge configuration shows ($E=\kappa\pi_i$ at both endpoints, paid by the Jensen slack).

## Batch 10 (research level): three closed approaches on MW'

1. *A per-entry parabola.* Lemma A is not an entrywise bound: no $C/n+\beta'(q-\tfrac1n)-Dq^2$ with L274's constants dominates $2q(1-q)^2$ at $q=1$ (it is negative there, $-2(n-1)^2/n^3$); Lemma A's proof needs its single-large-coordinate case, and MW' will too.
2. *Paying the excess vertex by vertex.* Writing L274's slack as $\sum_i\pi_i\big[2\,\mathrm{LA}_i+2\beta(\rho_i-\tfrac1{n^2}-\tfrac{n^2-1}{n^2}\pi_i)\big]$ and asking each vertex to pay its own excess $\sum_jE(q_{ij},\pi_i)$ fails at hub vertices with spread rows, where the (★) term is negative; the excess has to be paid through the column Cauchy–Schwarz, globally.
3. *The face envelope.* Bounding each row by the larger of the two faces' row bounds, $\max\big(2h_{\max}+\tfrac{2\beta}n-2\beta\rho,\;1-(1-\kappa\pi)\rho\big)$, fails by about $0.2$ at every $n=4$ to $9$ (screen): a row mixing one moderately heavy entry with spread light ones beats both, so the choice of $\gamma$ must stay per entry.

4. *A split excess.* Bounding $E\le\kappa\pi q^2\,[a<2\kappa\pi q^2]+q(1-q)(2q-1)^+$ and charging the total to L274's slack holds at $m=6,7,8$ (screen) but not at $m=5$, where the $\kappa\pi q^2$ part alone already exceeds it by about $0.002$ (Nelder–Mead, indicative): the crude split loses exactly at the threshold dimension of H.

MW' is the open statement. Its two equality configurations are the uniform point (all entries light, $E=0$) and the edge (two point-mass rows, $E=\kappa\pi$ each, paid by the Jensen slack of (★)).

## Batch 11 (research level, `$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`): MW' proved for $m\ge7$, so J1 holds on the whole torus slice for $m\ge7$

*Numbered [L276](../findings/second-order.md) and proved in Lean as `MWprime` ([MWPrime.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/MWPrime.lean)). Gate 2 by [r1671](REVIEW_r1671.md) and [r1672](REVIEW_r1672.md), independently: CONFIRMED, every step; the gaps they named (all presentational) are closed below.* Put $n=m-1$, $\kappa=\lambda_m^2=2(n+1)(n-1)^2/n^3$, and at $S=1$ the walk $q_{ij}=w_{ij}/\pi_i$ with $\pi_i\le\tfrac12$ (since $\pi_i=\sum_jw_{ij}\le\sum_{j\ne i}\pi_j$), $\rho_i=\sum_jq_{ij}^2$, $s=\sum_i\pi_i^2$. The proof has L274's shape with a slope that depends on the vertex: a row chord, a weighted column Cauchy–Schwarz, and a $\pi$-only inequality.

**Step 1, the row chord (R).** *For $n\ge6$, $\pi\in[0,\tfrac12]$ and a probability vector $q$ on $n$ points,*
$$\sum_jF(q_j,\pi)\le\kappa\pi+\beta(\pi)(1-\rho),\qquad\beta(\pi)=\frac{2(n-1)}n-\frac{n\kappa\pi}{n-1},$$
*with equality at the uniform row and at a point mass, for every $\pi$.* ($\beta$ is fixed by those two rows: the uniform entry $\tfrac1n$ is light for every $\pi\le\tfrac12$, so $nF(\tfrac1n,\pi)=2(n-1)^2/n^2$, and $F(1,\pi)=\kappa\pi$.)

- *(a) Reduction to two values of $\pi$.* Unfolding the maximum in $F$, (R) says that for every $\gamma\in[0,1]^n$, $\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)\big[\gamma_j(1-2q_j)+A(\pi)\big]\le\kappa\pi\sum_j\gamma_j^2q_j^2$ with $A(\pi)=\kappa\pi/(n-1)-(n-2)/n$. At fixed $(q,\gamma)$ both sides are affine in $\pi$, so (R) holds on $[0,\tfrac12]$ as soon as it holds at $\pi=0$ and at $\pi=\tfrac12$.
- *(b) R0, at $\pi=0$.* The best $\gamma_j$ is $1$ for $q_j\le\tfrac12$ and $0$ above, so R0 is $\sum_{q_j\le1/2}2q_j(1-q_j)(c-q_j)-\tfrac{n-2}n\sum_{q_j>1/2}q_j(1-q_j)\le0$, $c=\tfrac1n$. With $2q(1-q)(c-q)=-2(q-c)^2(1-c-q)-2c(1-c)(q-c)$ and $\sum_j(q_j-c)=0$ this is Lemma A when no $q_j$ exceeds $\tfrac12$. Otherwise exactly one does, $a$, the rest are at most $1-a$, so each has weight $1-c-q_k\ge a-c$, and Lemma A's Cauchy–Schwarz on $\sum_{k\ne1}(q_k-c)=c-a$ bounds the sum by $-D(a)$, $D(a)=\frac{(a-1)\,(2n^2a^2-n(n^2-5n+8)a-2(n-2))}{n^2(n-1)}$. The quadratic factor is convex, equal to $-(n-2)^3/2$ at $a=\tfrac12$ and to $-n^3+7n^2-10n+4<0$ at $a=1$ for $n\ge6$, so $D\ge0$ on $[\tfrac12,1]$.
- *(c) R½, at $\pi=\tfrac12$.* Put $\tilde K=\kappa/2$ and $\psi(x)=\max_{\gamma\in[0,1]}\big[x(1-x)(\gamma(1-2x)+A)-\tilde K\gamma^2x^2\big]$, $A=A(\tfrac12)=-(n^3-3n^2+1)/n^3$. R½ is $\sum_j\psi(q_j)\le0=n\psi(c)$.
  - *Concave on $[0,c]$.* Entries $x\le\tfrac7{32}$ are light: $(1-x)(1-2x)$ is decreasing on $[0,\tfrac12]$ and equals $\tfrac{225}{512}$ at $\tfrac7{32}$, while $2\tilde Kx<2x\le\tfrac{224}{512}$ because $\tilde K<1$, so there $\psi=\psi_L$, the $\gamma=1$ cubic, and $\psi_L(x)=\lambda(x-c)+2(x-c)^2(x-r)$ with $\lambda=-(n-1)(3n+1)/n^3$ and $r=(3n+1)(n-1)/(2n^2)>1$. So $\psi_L''=12x-8c-4r<0$ on $[0,c]$.
  - *The left-concave reduction.* If $f$ is concave on $[0,c]$ and $f(x)+(n-1)f\big(\tfrac{1-x}{n-1}\big)\le nf(c)$ for every $x\in[c,1]$, then $\sum_jf(q_j)\le nf(c)$ on the simplex. *Proof.* Let $J=\{j:q_j>c\}$, $k=\lvert J\rvert$, $I$ the rest, and $z$ the mean over $I$. For $j\in J$ put $y_j=(1-q_j)/(n-1)$. Then $(n-1)y_j\ge(n-k)z+(k-1)c\ge(n-1)z$, so $z\le y_j<c$, and concavity writes $f(y_j)\ge\theta_jf(z)+(1-\theta_j)f(c)$ with $y_j=\theta_jz+(1-\theta_j)c$. Summing the hypothesis over $J$ and matching the two linear constraints (count $k(n-1)$ and total $k-1+\sum_Iq_i$) gives $(n-1)\sum_J\theta_j=n-k$, and what is left is exactly Jensen's $\sum_If(q_i)\le(n-k)f(z)$. The edge cases: $k=0$ forces every $q_j=c$; $k\ge1$ forces $I\ne\emptyset$ (else $\sum q>kc=1$) and $z<c$ (since $\sum_Iq_i=1-\sum_Jq_j<(n-k)c$). $\square$ (It is Cirtoaje's right-convex-function theorem applied to $-f(\tfrac2n-x)$, with the $n-1$ equal values on the concave side.)
  - *The one-variable inequality.* With $y=\tfrac{1-x}{n-1}\le c$ light, $(n-1)\psi(y)=-\lambda(x-c)+\tfrac{2}{n-1}(x-c)^2(y-r)$, so the condition is $\chi(x)=\psi(x)-\lambda(x-c)\le\tfrac2{n-1}(x-c)^2(r-y)$ on $[c,1]$. It holds in three ranges:
    - on $[c,\tfrac7{32}]$, $\chi=2(x-c)^2(x-r)\le0$;
    - on $[\tfrac7{32},\tfrac12]$, $\psi$ is at most its unconstrained maximum $x(1-x)A+(1-x)^2(1-2x)^2/(4\tilde K)$, and the difference is $(1-x)L_2(x,n)/(4n^3(n-1)^2(n+1))$ with $L_2$ a cubic in $x$ whose Bernstein coefficients on $[\tfrac7{32},\tfrac12]$ are polynomials in $n-6$ with positive coefficients;
    - on $[\tfrac12,1]$, $\gamma=0$ is optimal and $\psi=x(1-x)A$, and the difference is $(x-1)L_1(x)/(n^3(n-1)^2)$ with $L_1=2n^3x^2+(-n^5+8n^4-12n^3-n^2+3n-1)x-3n^3+5n^2+n-1$ convex, equal to $(-n^5+8n^4-17n^3+9n^2+5n-3)/2<0$ at $\tfrac12$ and to $-n^5+8n^4-13n^3+4n^2+4n-2<0$ at $1$ for $n\ge6$.

**Step 2, the weighted column Cauchy–Schwarz.** $\beta(\pi)\ge\beta(\tfrac12)=(n-1)^2/n^2>0$, and $\sum_i\beta_i\pi_i\rho_i=\sum_j\sum_i(\beta_i/\pi_i)w_{ij}^2\ge\sum_j\pi_j^2/T_j$ with $T_j=\sum_{i\ne j}\pi_i/\beta_i$, by Cauchy–Schwarz over the $i$ with $w_{ij}>0$, because $\sum_iw_{ij}=\pi_j$ and $w_{jj}=0$. A row with $\pi_i=0$ has $w_{i\cdot}=0$ and contributes nothing on either side, and $T_j>0$ whenever $\pi_j>0$, since then some $i\ne j$ has $\pi_i\ge w_{ij}>0$. So by (R), $\mathrm{MW'}_{\rm LHS}\le\kappa s+\sum_i\pi_i\beta_i-\sum_j\pi_j^2/T_j$, and MW' follows from $\sum_i\pi_i\beta_i-\sum_j\pi_j^2/T_j\le\kappa(1-2s)$.

**Step 3, the $\pi$-only inequality $H_c$.** With $\beta=b_0(1-c'\pi)$, $b_0=2(n-1)/n$ and $c'=(n+1)/n$, $g_i=\pi_i/(1-c'\pi_i)$ and $B=\sum g_i$, the needed inequality becomes, after $b_0-\kappa=2(n-1)/n^3$ and $2\kappa-b_0c'=2(n+1)(n-1)(n-2)/n^3$,
$$\textbf{(}H_c\textbf{)}\qquad\sum_j\frac{\pi_j^2}{B-g_j}\ge\frac{1+(n+1)(n-2)s}{n^2}.$$
*Proof, for every $n\ge3$.* Titu's form gives $\sum_j\pi_j^2/(B-g_j)\ge s^2/\sum_j\pi_j^2(B-g_j)$, and $\sum_j\pi_j^2(B-g_j)=\sum_i\pi_i\phi(\pi_i)$ with $\phi(\pi)=(s-\pi^2)/(1-c'\pi)$. In $t=1-c'\pi$, $\phi=\big((c'^2s-1)/t+2-t\big)/c'^2$, and $c'^2s\le c'^2/2<1$ (as $s\le\max_i\pi_i\le\tfrac12$ and $(n+1)^2<2n^2$), so $\phi$ is concave on $[0,\tfrac12]$. Its tangent at $\pi=s$ then gives $\sum_i\pi_i\phi(\pi_i)\le\phi(s)=s(1-s)/(1-c's)$, because $\sum_i\pi_i(\pi_i-s)=0$. What remains, $n^2s(1-c's)\ge(1+(n+1)(n-2)s)(1-s)$, is
$$(1-2s)\big((n+1)s-1\big)\ge0,$$
true since $\tfrac1{n+1}\le s\le\tfrac12$. $\square$ Equality holds at the uniform point and at the edge, MW''s two equality configurations.

**Assembly.** J1 ⟸ J1*b (the cocktail-party row bound and the pairing with $R_{ij}$) ⟸ PI (`L275_interp`) + MW ⟸ MW' ⟸ Steps 1 to 3, all for $m\ge7$. So J1 holds on the whole torus slice at $m\ge7$, and by [L273](../findings/second-order.md) (3), whose proof uses J1 only at the point itself, H at every torus-invariant point for $m\ge7$.

## Batch 13 (research level): MW' at $m=6$, with one step a machine certificate
