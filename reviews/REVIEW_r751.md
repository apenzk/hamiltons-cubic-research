## 0. Conventions, fixed once

$V=\mathcal W_n$ with its Euclidean structure, $S=\{W\in V:\lVert W\rVert=1\}$, and $P(W)=\langle W,W^2+W^\#\rangle$, a homogeneous cubic on $V$. Write $T$ for the symmetric trilinear polarisation of $P$, so $P(W)=T(W,W,W)$, and $X\circ Y$ for the associated product $\langle X\circ Y,Z\rangle=T(X,Y,Z)$, $Q(X)=X\circ X$. Then $\nabla P=3Q$ and $L_X=X\circ(\cdot)$ is **self-adjoint** for every $X$, by full symmetry of $T$. At a unit critical point of $P\vert_S$, $\nabla P(W)\parallel W$ gives $Q(W)=\theta W$ with $\theta=P(W)$; $e=W/\theta$ needs $\theta\ne0$, and $L_e=L_W/\theta$ by linearity of $X\mapsto L_X$.

$\mathcal O=SO(n)\cdot W$; $T_W\mathcal O=\mathfrak{so}(n)\cdot W$. Blocks are [L120](../findings/second-order.md)'s: $\mathcal D_n$ on the trivial sign character, $V_{ijkl}$ on $\chi_{\{i,j,k,l\}}$, $V_{jk}$ on $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$. A **pure** $W$ is frame-diagonal, so $W\in\mathcal D_n$ in that frame, with sectional matrix $M_{ab}=W_{abab}$. All arithmetic below is rational or exact-radical.

One convention worth pinning because two steps rest on it: $E(\tfrac12)$ is the $L_e$-eigenspace **in the ambient $\mathcal W_n$**, not in $W^\perp$, otherwise "the intersection is the whole eigenspace" says nothing. And $L_e$ acts on $\mathcal W_n$: if $Q$ needed a Weyl projection $\Pi$, then $L_e=\Pi M_e\vert_{\mathcal W_n}$ is still self-adjoint on $\mathcal W_n$, since $\langle\Pi M_eX,Y\rangle=\langle M_eX,Y\rangle=\langle X,\Pi M_eY\rangle$ for $X,Y\in\mathcal W_n$. So §3 survives either reading of Böhm–Wilking's eq. (6).

## 1. The Hessian identity, and what the constant $6\theta$ is for

For $f$ smooth on $V$ and $W\in S$ a critical point of $f\vert_S$ with $\nabla f(W)=\lambda W$, the Riemannian Hessian on $T_WS=W^\perp$ is $\mathrm{Hess}(f\vert_S)_W[H,H]=D^2f_W[H,H]-\lambda\lVert H\rVert^2$, the second term being the sphere's second fundamental form contracted against the normal component of $\nabla f$. For the cubic $P$, $D^2P_W[H,H]=6\,T(W,H,H)=6\langle L_WH,H\rangle$, and $\lambda=3\theta$. Hence, as an operator on $W^\perp$,

$$\mathrm{Hess}(P\vert_S)_W=6L_W-3\theta\,\mathrm{Id}=6\theta\Big(L_e-\tfrac12\mathrm{Id}\Big).$$

No projection is lost in restricting to $W^\perp$: $L_W$ is self-adjoint with $L_WW=\theta W$, so it preserves $\langle W\rangle$ and therefore $W^\perp$. The identity is **CONFIRMED**, and the constant is forced rather than chosen, the brief's phrase "normalised so that $W$ is a $1$-eigenvector and the orbit tangent sits at $\tfrac12$" describes two theorems ($L_eW=W$ from criticality, $L_eo=\tfrac12o$ from equivariance, §3) rather than a normalisation with a free parameter.

**What the proof uses of it.** Only that $6\theta\ne0$. For any $c\ne0$, $\ker\big(c\,(L_e-\tfrac12\mathrm{Id})\big)=\ker(L_e-\tfrac12\mathrm{Id})$; neither the factor $6$ nor the sign of $\theta$ enters. $\theta\ne0$ suffices for the displayed equality. $\theta>0$ is spent in exactly two places, both outside the equality: the direction of the inequality $\mathrm{Hess}\preceq0\Rightarrow L_e\preceq\tfrac12$ (at $\theta<0$ it flips to $L_e\succeq\tfrac12$), which is claim (b)'s and [L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s; and the existence of $e=W/\theta$.

## 2. Second-order optimality, and whether the passage is complete

$S$ is the unit sphere of the **linear subspace** $\mathcal W_n$, so $T_WS=W^\perp$ taken inside $\mathcal W_n$. That is the right tangent space, and it is the one the record means throughout: a "local maximum" here is a local maximum of $P$ restricted to unit-norm Weyl operators, not among all curvature operators. **CONFIRMED**.

A local maximum of a $C^2$ function on a manifold gives $\mathrm{Hess}\preceq0$ on the tangent space, the standard second-order necessary condition, applied along geodesics. With §1 and $\theta>0$ that is $L_e\preceq\tfrac12$ on $W^\perp$, which is [L24](../findings/second-order.md) verbatim.

**The passage to the kernel is complete, and it does not go through the inequality.** The written proof reads "*$\theta>0$, so a local maximum has $L_e\preceq\tfrac12$ there and $\ker\mathrm{Hess}=E(\tfrac12)\cap W^\perp$*", one sentence, two conjuncts, with "so" suggesting the second follows from the first. It does not: the second conjunct is $\ker\big(6\theta(L_e-\tfrac12)\big)=\ker(L_e-\tfrac12)$ on $W^\perp$, true for every $\theta\ne0$ and at every critical point, maximum or not. **This is a presentational defect with a real consequence for the lemma's scope**, and the same conflation is repeated twice more: in the Lean file's docstring (§11) and in [plan.md](../plan.md)'s I59 row.

**Does the argument need $\tfrac12$ to be the top eigenvalue on $W^\perp$?** No, see the paragraph above. **Does maximality deliver top-ness anyway?** Yes, and the argument is worth recording because it also closes one of the brief's edges. At a maximum $\lambda_{\max}(L_e\vert_{W^\perp})\le\tfrac12$; and $T_W\mathcal O\subseteq E(\tfrac12)$ with $T_W\mathcal O\ne0$, because $T_W\mathcal O=0$ would make $W$ a fixed point of the connected group $SO(n)$, and $\mathcal W_n^{SO(n)}=0$, for $n\ne4$ because $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ is irreducible, so the invariant symmetric forms are $\mathbb R\cdot\mathrm{Id}$, which is constant curvature and fails Ricci-flatness; at $n=4$ the invariants are spanned by $\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^2_\pm}$, whose difference is the Hodge star, killed by first Bianchi, and whose sum is again $\mathrm{Id}$. So $\tfrac12$ is attained on $W^\perp$ at **every** unit critical point, and is the top of the spectrum there at every maximum. The consequence: $\lambda_{\max}(L_e\vert_{W^\perp})=\tfrac12$ exactly at a maximum, and $E(\tfrac12)\ne0$ always.

## 3. $L_eW=W$, derived, and why it turns the intersection into the eigenspace

Criticality of $P\vert_S$ at unit norm means $\nabla P(W)=3Q(W)$ is parallel to $W$; pairing with $W$ gives the factor, $\langle Q(W),W\rangle=T(W,W,W)=P(W)=\theta$, so $Q(W)=\theta W$. Since $L_WW=W\circ W=Q(W)=\theta W$ and $L_e=L_W/\theta$, we get $L_eW=W$. **CONFIRMED**, one line, and it is criticality alone, no maximality, no purity, no dimension.

$L_e$ is self-adjoint (§0), so eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal; $1\ne\tfrac12$, so $E(\tfrac12)\perp E(1)\ni W$, i.e. $E(\tfrac12)\subseteq W^\perp$ and $E(\tfrac12)\cap W^\perp=E(\tfrac12)$. **The step is correct and the page's one line is enough**, with the caveat that self-adjointness is the hypothesis doing the work and is never named on the page.

Combining §1–§3: for every critical $W$ of $P\vert_S$ at unit norm with $\theta\ne0$,

$$\ker\mathrm{Hess}(P\vert_S)_W=E(\tfrac12).$$

**So the displayed equality is a theorem about critical points, and the hypothesis "local maximum" is idle in it.** This is not a defect in the mathematics, it is a defect in the statement's scope, and it matters because the record already carries the wider fact: [L128](../findings/second-order.md)'s *Proof of (ii)* opens with "*[L24](../findings/second-order.md) gives $\mathrm{Hess}(P\vert_S)_W=6\theta(L_e-\tfrac12\mathrm{Id})$ on $T_WS=W^\perp$, so $\ker\mathrm{Hess}=E_{L_e}(\tfrac12)\cap W^\perp$*", at an arbitrary pure critical point. L170's novelty is therefore the *reading* (that L13's clause is a nondegeneracy hypothesis) and not the identity, which is on the record and was Gate-2 confirmed with L128.

## 4. The definitional step

The general definition: $f$ is **Morse–Bott** when $\mathrm{Crit}(f)$ is a submanifold and $\ker\mathrm{Hess}_x=T_x\mathrm{Crit}(f)$ at each critical $x$. The record's "$W$ is Morse–Bott nondegenerate" means $\ker\mathrm{Hess}_W=T_W\mathcal O$, nondegeneracy **relative to the orbit**, a pointwise condition. The two differ in principle: if $\mathrm{Crit}(P\vert_S)$ near $W$ were a manifold *strictly larger* than $\mathcal O$ whose tangent space were the whole kernel, $P$ would be Morse–Bott near $W$ in the general sense while the record would call $W$ degenerate. So "**by definition**" is true of the record's convention and would be false of the textbook one; the equivalence in the lemma is a restatement, and its content lies entirely in the displayed equality above it.

**The surplus in the general definition is delivered rather than assumed**, which is worth saying because it is what makes the record's convention harmless. Two of the three extras are free: $\mathcal O$ is a compact-group orbit, hence an embedded submanifold, and $\mathcal O\subseteq\mathrm{Crit}(P\vert_S)$ by $O(n)$-invariance of $P$ and of $S$. The third (that the critical set is *locally exactly* $\mathcal O$, so no larger critical manifold exists), is [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(iii), whose proof (self-adjoint $dF_W$, $\ker dF_W=T_W\mathcal O$ in the ambient space, implicit function theorem on $\Pi_{V'}F=0$) uses **neither purity nor maximality** and so applies here verbatim. I checked its arithmetic: with $F(X)=3(Q(X)-P(X)X)$, $dF_W[H]=6L_WH-9\theta\langle W,H\rangle W-3\theta H$, giving $dF_WW=-6\theta W$ and $dF_W\vert_{W^\perp}=6\theta(L_e-\tfrac12)$, so $\ker dF_W=\ker\mathrm{Hess}$ whenever $\theta\ne0$. **Verdict: the equivalence is not weaker than claimed, but "by definition" should read "in the sense used throughout this file, and the difference from the general notion is closed by L128(iii)".**

## 5. Claim (a), the two witnesses

**$S^3\times S^3$ at $n=6$, degenerate: CONFIRMED, exactly.** [L121](../findings/second-order.md)'s four-set block has $L_e$-spectrum $\pm\sqrt\Sigma/(2\theta)$ with $\Sigma=A^2+B^2+C^2-AB-BC-CA$ on the opposite-pair sums. I re-derived the sphere-product value independently: with $B=C=2\gamma$ on a $(2,2)$-split, $\Sigma=(A-2\gamma)^2$ with $A=\alpha+\beta$; Ricci-flatness $(p-1)\alpha=-q\gamma$, $(q-1)\beta=-p\gamma$ and criticality $\theta=-(n-1)\gamma$ give
$$\frac{\sqrt\Sigma}{2\theta}=\frac{\lvert\gamma\rvert\big[\tfrac q{p-1}+\tfrac p{q-1}+2\big]}{2(n-1)\lvert\gamma\rvert}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)}=\frac{n-2}{2(p-1)(q-1)}=\mu_{p,q},$$
reproducing [L26](../findings/nondegeneracy.md)/[L126](../findings/second-order.md) identically in $p,q$. At $(3,3)$, $\mu=\tfrac{4}{8}=\tfrac12$ on each of the nine $(2,2)$-splits, which are orbit-free by [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(i), so $\ker\mathrm{Hess}\supseteq T_W\mathcal O\oplus\mathbb R^9$ and the point is degenerate. Exact.

**$S^3\times S^3$, not a local maximum: SUPPORTED NUMERICALLY ONLY.** Note first that [L24](../findings/second-order.md) does **not** exclude it: $\mu_{3,3}=\tfrac12$ sits *on* the bound, not above it. So the exclusion must come from third order, and here is the exact criterion it has to meet. For unit $v\in E(\tfrac12)$ take the great circle $\gamma(t)=\cos t\,W+\sin t\,v$; then $T(W,W,v)=\theta\langle W,v\rangle=0$ and $\langle L_Wv,v\rangle=\tfrac\theta2$, so
$$P(\gamma(t))=\theta\cos^3t+\tfrac{3\theta}2\cos t\sin^2t+T(v,v,v)\sin^3t=\theta+T(v,v,v)\,t^3+O(t^4),$$
the second-order terms cancelling exactly. **Hence: if the cubic form $v\mapsto T(v,v,v)$ is not identically zero on $E(\tfrac12)$, $W$ is not a local maximum.** Two structural facts follow at a pure point, and they say precisely where the record's computation must be looking. On a *single* four-set direction the cubic vanishes: $X\in V_{ijkl}$ lies in one nontrivial sign character $\chi$, so $X\circ X$ lies in $\chi^2=\mathbf 1$ and $\langle X\circ X,X\rangle=0$ by character orthogonality, [L123](../findings/second-order.md)'s argument. The surviving terms are the cross terms $T(X_{S_1},X_{S_2},X_{S_3})$ over triples with $S_1\triangle S_2\triangle S_3=\varnothing$, which at $S^3\times S^3$ exist among the nine $(2,2)$-splits ($\{1,2,4,5\}$, $\{2,3,5,6\}$, $\{1,3,4,6\}$ is one), and I checked that at least one matrix-product term of such a triple is nonzero in the $e_{ab}$ basis. **What I could not do without computation is exclude cancellation against the $\#$ terms**, which is what decides the sign. The record's support is [nondegeneracy.md](../findings/nondegeneracy.md), "*$S^3\times S^3$ is a degenerate saddle, not a local maximum: zero positive Hessian directions, but $\theta$ takes both signs at cubic order on the $9$ null directions transverse to the orbit*", together with the cubic tensor $\langle F_i,F_j\circ F_k\rangle$ measured at $6.6\times10^{-2}$ on $E(\tfrac12)$ there. **That is a numerical discharge of an exactly stated criterion, and no closed form is on file.** It is robust in magnitude and I do not doubt it; but claim (a) inherits its status, and L170 states it flatly.

**$S^2\times S^3\times S^3$ at $n=8$, not a local maximum: CONFIRMED, and I give the closed form the record lacks.** Take $F_1=\{1,2\}$, $F_2=\{3,4,5\}$, $F_3=\{6,7,8\}$, sectional matrix $\alpha_1$ within $F_1$, $\beta$ within $F_2$ and $F_3$, $g$ on $F_1$-crosses, $h$ on the $F_2F_3$ cross. Ricci-flatness gives $\alpha_1+6g=0$ and $2\beta+2g+3h=0$. Criticality is $M_{ij}^2+\sum_{k\ne i,j}M_{ik}M_{jk}=\theta M_{ij}$ per pair; the $F_1F_2$ pair gives $\theta=-7g$, and the $F_2F_3$ pair gives $2g^2+3gh-5h^2=0$, i.e. $(2g+5h)(g-h)=0$. The root $h=-\tfrac25g$ collapses to $S^2\times S^6$ ($\beta=h$); the root $h=g$ is the three-factor point. Setting $g=-1$: $\alpha_1=6$, $\beta=\tfrac52$, all crosses $-1$, $\theta=7$, and the two remaining criticality equations check exactly ($42=42$ and $\tfrac{35}2=\tfrac{35}2$). Normalising, $\theta=\sqrt{42}/9$. Now take the four-set $\{1,2,3,4\}$, two indices from $F_1$ and two from $F_2$:
$$A=M_{12}+M_{34}=6+\tfrac52=\tfrac{17}2,\qquad B=M_{13}+M_{24}=-2,\qquad C=M_{14}+M_{23}=-2,$$
$$\Sigma=(A-B)^2=\Big(\tfrac{21}2\Big)^2=\tfrac{441}4,\qquad \frac{\sqrt\Sigma}{2\theta}=\frac{21/2}{14}=\frac34 .$$
The ratio is scale-invariant, so $\lambda_{\max}(L_e\vert_{W^\perp})\ge\tfrac34>\tfrac12$ at the normalised point, the eigenvector lying in a four-set block and hence in $W^\perp$ (§7). By [L24](../findings/second-order.md) the point is **not a local maximum**, and $\tfrac34$ is exact. The orbit dimension checks: $\dim SO(8)-\dim(\mathfrak{so}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3))=28-7=21$, the record's figure, so this is the same witness. *This closes claim (a)'s second half analytically, where the record had only the eigenspace dimensions.*

**"Would contradict nothing on the record", SUPPORTED, and correctly a claim about the record.** It is checkable in the weak sense that one can look, and I did: [L24](../findings/second-order.md) is compatible with $\tfrac12$ being attained off the orbit (equality is admitted); [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(ii)–(iii) merely say what fails when it is; [L11](../findings/nondegeneracy.md) and [L27](../findings/nondegeneracy.md) exclude specific families, not the phenomenon; and the one recorded point where $\tfrac12$ is attained off the orbit at a *pure* point is $S^3\times S^3$, whose exclusion is §5's numerical third-order argument. **No result on the record forbids a degenerate local maximum.** What the sentence cannot be is evidence that none exists, and the lemma does not claim otherwise.

## 6. Claim (a)'s strong form: "no proof of it is available"

As written this is a claim that a proof does not exist, which no argument can establish. **The defensible statement is that no proof can come from the structural features the setting supplies**, and that one is provable. Here is the proof.

Take $V=\mathbb R^3$ with coordinates $(x,y,z)$, the cubic $P(x,y,z)=x^3+\tfrac32x(y^2+z^2)$, and $G=SO(2)$ rotating $(y,z)$, acting orthogonally with $P$ invariant. At $W=e_1$: $\nabla P=(3x^2+\tfrac32(y^2+z^2),3xy,3xz)$ equals $3e_1$, so $W$ is critical on $S^2$ with $\theta=P(W)=1>0$. The third-derivative tensor gives $T(W,e_i,e_i)=\tfrac12$ for $i=2,3$ and $T(W,W,W)=1$, so $L_e$ has eigenvalue $1$ on $W$ and $\tfrac12$ on all of $W^\perp$: $\mathrm{Hess}(P\vert_S)_W=6(L_e-\tfrac12)=0$, and $E(\tfrac12)=W^\perp$ is two-dimensional while $T_W\mathcal O=0$. And $W$ is a maximum, with $s=y^2+z^2$ and $x=\sqrt{1-s}$,
$$P\vert_S=\sqrt{1-s}\,\Big(1+\tfrac s2\Big),\qquad \big(P\vert_S\big)^2=(1-s)\Big(1+\tfrac s2\Big)^2=1-\tfrac34s^2-\tfrac14s^3,$$
strictly less than $1$ for every $s\in(0,1]$, while $P\le0$ on the hemisphere $x\le0$. So $e_1$ is the **strict global maximum** of a real-analytic, $G$-invariant, homogeneous cubic on a compact sphere, and it is totally degenerate: $\ker\mathrm{Hess}=W^\perp\ne T_W\mathcal O=0$.

Every ingredient the brief names is present in that example, real-analyticity (it is a polynomial, so Łojasiewicz applies with the best possible exponents), compactness of $S$, an orthogonal group action leaving $P$ invariant, cubic homogeneity, criticality with $\theta>0$, and even globality of the maximum. **Therefore no argument built from those ingredients alone can force nondegeneracy at a maximum**, and Łojasiewicz in particular is the wrong instrument: it controls the gradient's decay and the convergence of flow lines, and is insensitive to the dimension of the Hessian kernel. Any proof of L13's clause must consume something specific to $Q$ on $\mathcal W_n$. That is the sentence the box should carry; "no proof is available" as printed asserts more than any argument on the page or elsewhere supports.

## 7. Claim (b), the simplification

**Four-set blocks lie in $W^\perp$, CONFIRMED, exactly and not generically.** $W$ pure means $W\in\mathcal D_n$ in the fixed frame, the trivial character space of the full sign group $\Gamma$ ([L120](../findings/second-order.md)). Each $V_{ijkl}$ sits in the character $\chi_{\{i,j,k,l\}}\ne\mathbf 1$. $\Gamma$ acts orthogonally and the inner product is $\Gamma$-invariant, so distinct isotypic components are orthogonal: $\langle W,X\rangle=0$ for every $X\in V_{ijkl}$, with no genericity and no dimension restriction. This is exactly [L120](../findings/second-order.md)'s boxed sentence "*$W^\perp$ cuts $\mathcal D_n$ and nothing else*", read in the direction claim (b) needs. **Purity is what buys it**, at a non-pure critical point there is no such frame and the statement has no content, which is §8.

**The exclusion becomes strict, CONFIRMED, and empty of content.** At a maximum, §2 gives $L_e\preceq\tfrac12$ on $W^\perp$, hence on every four-set block, whose spectrum is the symmetric pair $\pm\mu$ with $\mu=\sqrt\Sigma/(2\theta)\ge0$. So "$\tfrac12$ is not an eigenvalue of the block" and "$\mu<\tfrac12$" are the same condition, and $\mu<\tfrac12\iff\Sigma<\theta^2$. **But this buys nothing for a prover**: the inequality $\Sigma\le\theta^2$ is *already free* at a maximum, it is [L121](../findings/second-order.md)'s own consequence of [L24](../findings/second-order.md), so what must still be proved at a maximum is exactly the non-equality $\Sigma\ne\theta^2$. Calling the weakened obligation "unnecessary at maxima" reads as though the strict form were the cheaper target; the two are equivalent there and the residual burden is identical. **Recommend rewording to: at a maximum the two forms coincide, so the repair costs nothing and gains nothing.**

**"Which I18 can consume", REFUTED, a scope error.** [I18](../plan.md) reads "*at $n\ge12$, no pure **critical point** with $\theta\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ carries the $L_e$-eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ in an L121 block, an off-orbit L122 block or a direction of $\mathcal D_n\cap W^\perp$*". Its quantifier is over critical points, and the entire reason [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(ii) was repaired from "$<\tfrac12$" to "$\ne\tfrac12$" is that a critical point may sit above $\tfrac12$, $S^2\times S^4$ at $\mu_{2,4}=\tfrac23$ being the record's own witness. Nothing on the record shows that a pure critical point with $\theta\ge\sqrt{3/2}$ at $n\ge12$ is a local maximum, and establishing it would be most of the conjecture. So I18 **cannot** consume the simplification, and [plan.md](../plan.md)'s I59 row ("*so I18's obligation there is $\Sigma<\theta^2$ rather than the weakened $\Sigma\ne\theta^2$*") states the same error in the plan. The finding's own body sentence ("*right for critical points and unnecessary at maxima*"), is correct; it is the box heading and the plan row that overreach.

**Nothing in the pair-block half is disturbed, CONFIRMED, and the box understates the simplification.** Pair blocks $V_{jk}$ carry the character $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k\ne\mathbf 1$, so they too lie in $W^\perp$, as does $\mathcal D_n\cap W^\perp$ by construction. Hence at a maximum: on an orbit-free pair block ($o^{jk}=0$, e.g. every within-factor block of a sphere product) the clause is again the strict $\mu_{jk}<\tfrac12$; on a block carrying $o^{jk}\ne0$ the value $\tfrac12$ is attained and the clause is that the **top** eigenvalue $\tfrac12$ is simple; and on $\mathcal D_n\cap W^\perp$ the exclusion is likewise strict. The box names four-set blocks only, so it is right as far as it goes and misses two of the three places where the same reasoning applies.

## 8. Scope: where purity is silently assumed, and where it is not

**Not assumed**: §1, §2, §3, the Hessian identity, the kernel identity, $L_eW=W$, $E(\tfrac12)\subseteq W^\perp$, the equivalence with the record's Morse–Bott condition, and [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(iii)'s local-exactness upgrade. All of these hold at an arbitrary critical point of $P\vert_S$ with $\theta\ne0$, pure or not, at every $n$.

**Assumed, and correctly declared**: the final clause "at a pure $W$ it is decided by the block criterion", which is [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(ii) and inherits *all* of that lemma's scope, pure in a **fixed eigenframe**, Ricci-flatness (through [L122](../findings/second-order.md)'s identification of the Ricci cut with $\mathbf 1^\perp$), $\theta>0$, and the full sign group rather than its determinant-one subgroup at $n=4,6,8$.

**Assumed and NOT declared**: the whole of claim (b). Its every step (four-set blocks, their location in $W^\perp$, the value $\mu$, the pair-block clause), presupposes the [L120](../findings/second-order.md) decomposition, hence purity in a fixed frame. The box never says so. At a non-pure critical point there are no blocks and claim (b) is not false but undefined.

## 9. Hypotheses that go unused

Tabulated above. The two that matter: **"local maximum" is idle in the displayed equality and in the equivalence** (it is used only in claim (b)) and **$\theta>0$ is stronger than the equality needs**, $\theta\ne0$ sufficing. Ricci-flatness, first Bianchi, $n$'s value and parity, and any condition on $\ker W$ are all idle in L170 proper; they re-enter only through the lemmas the final clause cites.

## 10. The edges

- **$\theta=0$.** $e=W/\theta$ does not exist, $L_e$ is undefined, and the statement is not false but empty. What survives is $\mathrm{Hess}(P\vert_S)_W=6L_W$ on $W^\perp$ (the $-3\theta$ term vanishing) with $\ker\mathrm{Hess}=\ker L_W\cap W^\perp$; the record's $\tfrac12$-normalisation has no analogue there, since $T_W\mathcal O\subseteq\ker L_W$ instead. **The hypothesis is needed and is stated.**
- **$\theta<0$**, not on the brief's list but adjacent: the equality still holds; only the inequality of §2 flips. Nothing downstream is affected because $P(-W)=-P(W)$ makes the case redundant.
- **$\ker W\ne0$.** Nowhere used. Padding a critical point preserves criticality and $\theta$ ([nondegeneracy.md](../findings/nondegeneracy.md)), so the lemma applies to padded points verbatim, and it is worth noting that padding does *not* preserve nondegeneracy, so a padded local maximum is exactly the shape of object claim (a) says cannot be excluded.
- **A local maximum that is not global.** Nothing in §1–§4 uses globality, and §6's witness is in fact a *global* maximum that is degenerate, so globality would not help either.
- **$E(\tfrac12)=T_W\mathcal O$ trivially because both are zero.** Impossible: §2 shows $T_W\mathcal O\ne0$ at every unit $W\in\mathcal W_n$, since $\mathcal W_n^{SO(n)}=0$ at every $n$ including $n=4$. So the equivalence is never vacuous, and $\tfrac12$ is always an $L_e$-eigenvalue on $W^\perp$.
- **Odd $n$.** No step distinguishes parity. Parity enters the record only through the balanced product's existence, which L170 does not touch.

## 11. What would break it, and what I did to look

The displayed equality itself cannot be broken: given self-adjointness of $L_e$ and $\theta\ne0$ it is two lines, and a falsifier would have to be a failure of the Hessian identity or of $Q(W)=\theta W$, both of which I re-derived (§1, §3).

**The object that would falsify the clause L170 is about is a degenerate local maximum**: a unit critical $W\in\mathcal W_n$ with $\theta>0$, $P\vert_S$ having a local maximum at $W$, and $\tfrac12$ attained by $L_e$ on $W^\perp$ off $T_W\mathcal O$. At a pure such point that is, by §7, a four-set with $\Sigma=\theta^2$ exactly, or an off-orbit pair-block or $\mathcal D_n$ direction at $\tfrac12$.

**What I did to look: analysis only, no search, no sweep, no census, the brief forbids them and I opened none.** What analysis gives is the location of the nearest candidate and the reason it does not count. $S^3\times S^3$ at $n=6$ *is* such a point in every respect except maximality: nine orbit-free four-set blocks at exactly $\Sigma=\theta^2$, sitting on [L24](../findings/second-order.md)'s bound rather than above it. The only thing separating it from being a counterexample to L13's clause is the third-order computation of §5, which is numerical. **So the clause's safety at $n=6$ rests on one number, and L170's box states the conclusion without that qualification.** The second witness I removed from the candidate list analytically (§5): $S^2\times S^3\times S^3$ fails at second order, $\tfrac34>\tfrac12$.

**And §6's witness names what a falsifier need not have**: no soft feature of the problem obstructs one, so the search space is not narrowed by anything except $\mathcal W_n$'s own structure.

## Overall verdict

**PROVEN, hypotheses corrected.**

The statement is true, its proof is sound, and both of its lines survive re-derivation. Four repairs are owed:

1. **The hypothesis "if $W$ is a local maximum" is idle** in the displayed equality and in the equivalence: both hold at every critical point with $\theta\ne0$, and the record already carries the fact at that generality inside [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(ii)'s proof. The proof's own sentence should stop implying the kernel identity follows from $L_e\preceq\tfrac12$.
2. **"Morse–Bott nondegeneracy is $\ker\mathrm{Hess}=T_W\mathcal O$ by definition"** holds for the record's convention; the general definition carries more, and the surplus is [L128](../findings/second-order.md)(iii) rather than a definition.
3. **"No proof of it is available" is not provable as printed.** §6 proves the statement that should stand in its place, with an exact witness: a real-analytic, group-invariant, homogeneous cubic whose strict *global* maximum on a sphere is totally degenerate.
4. **"Which [I18](../plan.md) can consume" is a scope error** and should be struck, in the finding and in [plan.md](../plan.md)'s I59 row: I18 quantifies over pure critical points, where a block above $\tfrac12$ is admitted by L128's own witness. Two smaller edits go with it, at a maximum the strict and the non-equality forms are equivalent, so the simplification changes nothing that must be proved; and the same simplification applies to $\mathcal D_n\cap W^\perp$ and to orbit-free pair blocks, which the box omits.

One item is not a repair but a status correction: **claim (a)'s "$S^3\times S^3$ is not a local maximum" is numerical**, resting on the third-order computation in [nondegeneracy.md](../findings/nondegeneracy.md), and the exact criterion it must meet is in §5. Its companion, $S^2\times S^3\times S^3$, is now closed analytically by the $\tfrac34$ of §5.

*A note on [L170.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L170.lean), read after the verdicts were fixed and treated as no authority.* Its three theorems are correct and its disclaimer is honest about what the base layer cannot type. **Its docstring on `kernel_is_half_eigenspace` repeats the proof's confusion**, "*maximality enters only as $\lambda\le\tfrac12$, which makes $\tfrac12$ the top eigenvalue on $W^\perp$ and so makes the kernel the whole eigenspace rather than a part of it*". What makes the kernel the whole eigenspace is $L_eW=W$ and self-adjointness (§3), not top-ness; and the formalised statement itself uses only $\theta\ne0$, its own `hθ : 0 < θ` being stronger than the proof requires.
