## Claim 1, the fourth-order expansion

- **Coefficients.** $e^{\varphi\xi}\cdot W=\sum_k\varphi^k\xi^k\cdot W/k!$, so $f(\varphi)=\sum_{k,l}\varphi^{k+l}\langle D_k,D_l\rangle/(k!\,l!)$. Order 2: $\lVert D_1\rVert^2+2\cdot\tfrac12\langle D_0,D_2\rangle$. Order 3: $2\cdot\tfrac16\langle D_0,D_3\rangle+2\cdot\tfrac12\langle D_1,D_2\rangle$. Order 4: $2\cdot\tfrac1{24}\langle D_0,D_4\rangle+2\cdot\tfrac16\langle D_1,D_3\rangle+\tfrac14\lVert D_2\rVert^2$. All three match the claim. The linear term is $2\langle D_0,D_1\rangle$.
- **$D_1=0$ at pair-free $W$.** $\xi\cdot W=[\hat\xi,W]$ with $\hat\xi(e_a\wedge e_b)=\xi e_a\wedge e_b+e_a\wedge\xi e_b$, which joins $e_{ab}$ only to $e_{cb}$ and $e_{ac}$. The diagonal entry is $([\hat\xi,W])_{ab,ab}=2\sum_u\hat\xi_{ab,u}W_{u,ab}$ (antisymmetric $\hat\xi$, symmetric $W$), and every $u$ shares one index with $ab$, so only pair entries enter. CONFIRMED, for every $\xi$, one-plane or not.
- **Orders of magnitude.** $|c_2|=|\langle A,D_2\rangle|\le t\lVert D_2\rVert$ with $\lVert D_2\rVert\le4\lVert\hat\xi\rVert_{op}^2$, and $\lVert\hat\xi\rVert_{op}\le2$ for a unit-angle matching generator, so $c_2=O(t)$ with an absolute constant. $c_3=\tfrac13\langle D_0,D_3\rangle=O(t)$ as well, and $c_4=\tfrac14\lVert D_2\rVert^2+O(t)$. The two-index diagonal of $\hat\xi W\hat\xi$ reaches four-set entries $W(e_{cb},e_{ad})$, so $D_2$ carries the four-set part. CONFIRMED.
- **The inference "raises the diagonal mass at a small angle".** $c_2\varphi^2+c_4\varphi^4>0$ needs $\varphi^2>-c_2/c_4$, so the angle is of order $\sqrt t$, and at that angle $c_2\varphi^2$ and $c_4\varphi^4$ are both of order $t^2$ while $c_3\varphi^3$ and the fifth-order term are $O(t^{5/2})$. The inference therefore holds only with constants: it needs the four-set share of $\tfrac14\lVert D_2\rVert^2$ bounded below by an absolute constant, which is exactly the visibility hypothesis. PLAUSIBLE as a heuristic.
- **The witness as the extreme case.** In the witness's rotated frame, one block of the product tensor has diagonal $(\alpha,\beta,\beta,\gamma)$. Along the matching generator, with $u=c^2-\tfrac12$, the block's diagonal mass is $4(S/4)^2+2(\alpha-\gamma)^2u^2+O(u^3)$ with $S=\alpha+2\beta+\gamma$, and the expansion is symmetric under $u\to-u$. At $p=q$, $\alpha=\gamma$, so $c_2=c_3=0$ there. CONFIRMED. This frame has $t>0$ ($S\ne0$) and is pair-free, but it is far from maximal: its maximal frame is the product frame, where $B_f=0$.
- **Side statements.** The average $\mathbb E_ME(M)=\tfrac32\lVert B_f\rVert^2/((n-1)(n-3))$ is CONFIRMED: on a four-set with $X=W_{ab,cd}$, $Z=W_{ad,bc}$, $Y=X+Z$ by Bianchi, the three splits give $(X+2Z)^2+(X-Z)^2+(2X+Z)^2=6(X^2+XZ+Z^2)$ against the four-set's share $4(X^2+XZ+Z^2)$ of $\lVert B_f\rVert^2$ (both orders counted), and a fixed pair of disjoint edges lies in a uniform perfect matching with probability $1/((n-1)(n-3))$. The same computation gives $(X+2Z)^2\le4(X^2+XZ+Z^2)$, so $E(M)\le\lVert B_f\rVert^2$ for every $M$, which is why the witness's $1.0000$ is the ceiling. "The $A$-side is $O(t^2)$ on average over $M$" understates: claim 2 shows it for every $M$.

## Claim 2, the matching inequality

- **The identity.** The $\tfrac\pi4$ rotation in every plane of $M$ fixes each $e_i\wedge e_j$ with $\{i,j\}\in M$ and leaves each four-dimensional block $\{e_{ik},e_{il},e_{jk},e_{jl}\}$ invariant. The rotated columns are $\tfrac12(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)$ Hadamard vectors, the ones `block_exchange` uses, so the change of diagonal mass is $\sum_{e<f}\Delta_{ef}$. Reversing the orientation of one plane permutes the same four Hadamard vectors up to sign, so the combination $W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk}$ is independent of orientations. The identification $m_{01}=x^{kl}_i$, $m_{23}=x^{kl}_j$, $m_{02}=x^{ij}_k$, $m_{13}=x^{ij}_l$, $\tfrac14(m_{00}-m_{11}+m_{22}-m_{33})^2=(\delta^{kl}_i+\delta^{kl}_j)^2$ and $\tfrac14(m_{00}+m_{11}-m_{22}-m_{33})^2=(\delta^{ij}_k+\delta^{ij}_l)^2$ checks. Global maximality gives $\sum\Delta_{ef}\le0$. CONFIRMED.
- **The bounds as written.** $\tfrac14\square_{ef}^2\le$ the four cross squares (Cauchy–Schwarz), and each cross pair $\{i,k\}$ with $i,k$ in different edges lies in exactly one block, so $\sum\tfrac14\square^2\le t^2$. Each $k\notin e$ lies in exactly one $f$, so the $\delta$ side is $\le2\sum_e\lVert\delta^e\rVert^2\le\sum_e(r_i+r_j)=\sum_ir_i=2t^2$. The total $3t^2$ is right and uses nothing beyond the givens. The row sums $\sum_ka_{ik}=0$ are never used.
- **Correction: the constant is $1$.** The three negative terms of one block are the squares of three Hadamard contrasts of its diagonal $m=(a_{ik},a_{il},a_{jk},a_{jl})$ divided by four, and the four Hadamard contrasts together carry $4\lVert m\rVert^2$. So the whole negative side of block $ef$ is exactly
$$\tfrac14\square_{ef}^2+(\delta^f_i+\delta^f_j)^2+(\delta^e_k+\delta^e_l)^2=\lVert m_{ef}\rVert^2-\tfrac14(a_{ik}+a_{il}+a_{jk}+a_{jl})^2 .$$
Summing over blocks, which partition the cross entries,
$$E(M)+\sum_{e\ne f\in M}(x^e_k+x^e_l)^2+\tfrac14\sum_{e<f}(a_{ik}+a_{il}+a_{jk}+a_{jl})^2\ \le\ t^2-\sum_{e\in M}a_e^2\ \le\ t^2 .$$
The Hadamard step was checked on random reals (seconds). The bound $2t^2$ for the $\delta$ side is loose because it double-counts the cross entries that the $\square$ bound already spent.

## Claim 3, the visibility route

- **What the implication gives.** At unit $W$, $1=t^2+\lVert\text{pair}\rVert^2+\lVert B_f\rVert^2$. With the pair mass at most $(n-1)t^2$, $\lVert B_f\rVert^2\ge1-nt^2$, and $c\lVert B_f\rVert^2\le3t^2$ gives $t^2\ge c/(3+cn)$, or $t^2\ge c/(1+cn)$ with the corrected constant. At $n=12$, $c=0.5$ this is $t\ge0.24$ ($0.27$ corrected), against Schur's floor $0.18$ quoted in the sweep. As $n$ grows the bound is of order $n^{-1/2}$, which does not leave the window $n^{-1}\lesssim t\lesssim n^{-1/2}$ that the sweep itself names as carrying pair mass. The inequality is $n$-free, the conclusion is $n$-dependent, and the text's "an $n$-free inequality to feed" hides that. The "pair mass small" branch is precisely what the record does not have in that window.
- **The frame.** The inequality holds only where the $\tfrac\pi4$ matching rotation cannot raise the diagonal mass, so visibility must be asserted in a globally maximal frame (a local maximum does not suffice for a finite angle). Every high-value tensor the record holds has large $t$ in its maximal frame (the witness has $t=1$ and $B_f=0$ there), so the hypothesis is untestable on known examples and concerns only hypothetical small-$t$ high-value tensors, which the conjecture says do not exist.
- **"The conjecture's core in combinatorial form".** Overstated. Visibility in a maximal frame is a sufficient condition that the route would need, stated with a constant for which no evidence exists in the right frame (claim 4). Nothing shows the conjecture implies it or that it is equivalent to the node.
- **Correction.** State the route as: in a globally maximal frame, $c\lVert B_f\rVert^2\le t^2-\sum_{e\in M}a_e^2$, hence $t^2\ge c/(1+cn)$ with the pair bound $(n-1)t^2$. An absolute floor on $t$ needs, in addition, a pair-mass bound $\lVert\text{pair}\rVert^2\le Kt^2$ with $K$ independent of $n$, or visibility of the pair part as well.

## Claim 4, the screen

- **The combination is right.** `visible` sums `(ent2(W,i,k,j,l)+ent2(W,i,l,j,k))**2` over pairs of edges, which is $W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk}$, and it is symmetric under $i\leftrightarrow j$ and $k\leftrightarrow l$. `four` sums both orders of each four-set entry, matching $\lVert B_f\rVert^2$. CONFIRMED.
- **The frame is wrong for the route.** The random and ascent tensors are built in the span `Fops`, cut out by zeroing every diagonal and pair entry, so they have $t=0$ in the frame screened. There the matching inequality would force $E(M)\le0$, yet the screen reports shares $0.04$ to $0.54$. So those frames are not maximal, and the reported shares are visibility in the construction frame, not in the frame the inequality needs. The witness is screened in its rotated frame, whose maximal frame (the product frame) has $B_f=0$.
- **Use of the numbers.** The sweep's screen bullet reads them as a screen ("high value goes with high visibility"), which is fair for a screen of three random tensors, one ascent and the witness. The route bullet then calls $c\approx0.5$ at $P\approx0.95$ "a screened constant ... to feed" the inequality, which the screen cannot supply. REFUTED in that use. A screen that would bear on the route would need the maximal frame of each tensor, which is an optimisation over $O(12)$ of unknown cost and so falls under the analysis-only rule's approval requirement.
