## A. L162

**The record** (findings/second-order.md, Lemma L162 statement): *"$\lambda=\frac2{\sqrt{9(\theta_0-\sqrt{3/2})}},\qquad C(n)=\frac{2(2n-3)}{n-1}$, put $\beta=\lambda/(2C)$"*, and the paragraph *"**$\beta$ falls.** $C(n)=\frac{2(2n-3)}{n-1}=4-\frac2{n-1}$ **increases**, and $\lambda=2/\sqrt{9(\theta_0-\sqrt{3/2})}$ **decreases** because $\theta_0(n)$ increases; so $\beta=\lambda/(2C)$ strictly decreases."* $\theta_0(n)=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$ is findings/problem.md:21.

- **`lam`.** The definition is `2 / √(9 * (√(2(n−1)(n−2)/n²) − √(3/2)))`. For $n>0$, $\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)/n^2}=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/\lvert n\rvert=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$. Faithful. `Real.sqrt` truncates negative arguments to $0$, and every use sits at $n\ge12$, where the inner argument is positive, so the truncation never acts.
- **`theta0Sq_gt`.** $\tfrac32<2(n-1)(n-2)/n^2\iff 3n^2<4(n^2-3n+2)\iff n^2-12n+8>0$, which is $n(n-12)+8>0$ and holds with margin $8$ at every $n\ge12$. At $n=12$ the margin in $\theta_0^2$ is $\tfrac{55}{36}-\tfrac32=\tfrac1{36}$, which matches L182's recorded value. The roots $6\pm2\sqrt7\approx0.71,11.29$ are below $12$. Correct.
- **`beta_strictAnti`.** The expression is `lam n / (2 * (2 * (2n − 3) / (n − 1)))`, which is $\lambda/(2C)$ with $C$ the record's closed form. `C_closed` (earlier commit) ties that form to $(4+2D)/D$. Faithful.
- **Strictness chains.** `lam_strictAnti`: $\theta_0(n)^2<\theta_0(m)^2$ (`theta0Sq_strictMono`, valid at $n\ge2$) and $\theta_0(n)^2>\tfrac32$ give $\sqrt{3/2}<\theta_0(n)<\theta_0(m)$. So $0<9(\theta_0(n)-\sqrt{3/2})<9(\theta_0(m)-\sqrt{3/2})$, the square root is strictly monotone on nonnegatives, and $2/\cdot$ is strictly antitone on positives. `beta_strictAnti`: first step $\lambda(m)/(2C(m))<\lambda(m)/(2C(n))$ from $\lambda(m)>0$ and $0<C(n)<C(m)$. Second step $\lambda(m)/(2C(n))<\lambda(n)/(2C(n))$ from $\lambda(m)<\lambda(n)$ and $C(n)>0$. Both strict. The Lean statement holds at every real $n\ge12$, which contains the record's even $n\ge12$.
- *Argued against:* the record's "because $\theta_0$ increases" omits $\theta_0>\sqrt{3/2}$, which Lean needs for positivity. The record states $\lambda$ only where it is defined, so the omission is a domain condition, and Lean supplies it through `theta0Sq_gt`. Nothing withdrawn, no gap.

## B. L124Det

**The record** (findings/second-order.md:1469, L124(iii)): *"On the half-flat family supported at one four-set, $\theta=9\det V_+$; the sharp bound $\lvert\det X\rvert\le\lVert X\rVert^3/(3\sqrt6)$ on traceless symmetric $3\times3$ (machine-checked as `det_sq_le` in L124Det.lean) then gives $\theta^2\le\tfrac32\rho^2$, with equality exactly at $\pm W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$"*. The proof of (ii) gives *"$\rho^2=\lVert V_+\rVert^2=\sum A^2+2\sum_{a<b}(V_+)_{ab}^2$"*, and withdrawn reading (c) gives *"on this family ... $\rho^2\equiv1$"*.

- **`trace_cube_eq_three_det`.** By Newton's identities $\operatorname{tr}X^3=e_1^3-3e_1e_2+3e_3$, and $e_1=\operatorname{tr}X=0$ leaves $3e_3=3\det X$. This holds for every traceless $3\times3$, symmetric or not, which is why the theorem needs no `IsHermitian`. The `ring` after substituting $X_{22}=-X_{00}-X_{11}$ closes a polynomial identity that is true. Correct.
- **`trace_cube_sq_le`.** `matrix_cube_le_unnormalised` gives $\operatorname{tr}M^3\le\frac{k-2}c(\operatorname{tr}M^2)^{3/2}$ with $c^2=k(k-1)$. At $k=3$, $c=\sqrt6$ satisfies $c^2=6$ and the coefficient is $1/\sqrt6$. At $-X$ (Hermitian and traceless) it gives $-\operatorname{tr}X^3\le(\operatorname{tr}X^2)^{3/2}/\sqrt6$, since $(-X)^3=-X^3$ and $(-X)^2=X^2$. So $\lvert\operatorname{tr}X^3\rvert\le b$ with $b=r^3/\sqrt6$, $r^2=\operatorname{tr}X^2\ge0$, and `sq_le_sq'` gives $(\operatorname{tr}X^3)^2\le b^2=r^6/6=(\operatorname{tr}X^2)^3/6$. Sharp at $\operatorname{diag}(2,-1,-1)$: $36=216/6$. Correct.
- **`det_sq_le`.** $9(\det X)^2\le t^3/6$ gives $(\det X)^2\le t^3/54$. With $t=\lVert X\rVert_F^2$ for symmetric $X$ ($\operatorname{tr}X^2=\sum_{ij}X_{ij}^2$), $\lvert\det X\rvert\le\lVert X\rVert^3/\sqrt{54}=\lVert X\rVert^3/(3\sqrt6)$. The record's norm on $V_+$ is Frobenius by its own proof of (ii). Faithful.
- **`theta_sq_le` and the normalisation.** $\theta^2=81(\det X)^2\le\tfrac{81}{54}t^3=\tfrac32t^3$. The record writes $\tfrac32\rho^2$, which is not homogeneous in $V_+$ ($\theta$ has degree $3$, $\rho^2$ degree $2$), so it is the $\rho=1$ reading, and the family carries $\rho^2\equiv1$ by the record's reading (c). At $t=\rho^2=1$, $\tfrac32t^3=\tfrac32\rho^2$. The Lean theorem is the homogeneous statement and is the right one. Faithful, with the identification owed (see G1).
- **$\rho^2=\operatorname{tr}V_+^2$.** Half-flatness makes $W|_{\Lambda^2\langle S\rangle}=V_+\oplus0$, so $\rho^2=\lVert W|_{\Lambda^2\langle S\rangle}\rVert^2=\lVert V_+\rVert_F^2=\operatorname{tr}V_+^2$, which is the record's own display. This is geometric, needs $\Lambda^2_+$, and is untyped. The row does not owe it by name (G1).
