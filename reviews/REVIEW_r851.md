## Conventions, quoted from the brief

- **$P(W)=\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$ with $Q(W)=W^2+W^\#$**, restricted to $\lVert W\rVert=1$ in $\mathcal W_n$, the space of algebraic Weyl curvature operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$. [README.md](../README.md) states that $Q$ sends $\mathcal W_n$ to itself.
- **Criticality**, from [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md): "'Critical point' is $Q(W)=\theta W$, the first-order condition on $\{\lVert W\rVert=1\}$. The bracket form $[W,Q(W)]=0$ is strictly weaker and is satisfied by every diagonal Weyl tensor."
- **Relation (C)**, from [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md): $\sum_{\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma T_{k\beta\gamma}=2\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)$ at every index $k$ at a critical point of unit norm, with $T_{abc}=\langle[v_a,v_b],v_c\rangle^2$ totally symmetric and hollow.
- **[L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)**: every two-eigenvalue critical point has $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$.
- **The sharp product**, from [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md): $(A\#B)_{ab}=\tfrac12\sum c_{acd}c_{bef}A_{ce}B_{df}$, normalised so $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$, and $\langle R^\#x,y\rangle=-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{ad}_xR\,\mathrm{ad}_yR)$.
- **A $2$-form is decomposable** iff $\omega\wedge\omega=0$ iff $\omega=u\wedge v$, and its support is the plane $\langle u,v\rangle$. **The record's wedge is TWICE the Pfaffian**, fixed at `wedgeSelf` in [SelfDual.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/SelfDual.lean).
- **"Diagonal" means diagonal in the coordinate $2$-planes $e_i\wedge e_j$** of an orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$, not diagonal as an operator on $\Lambda^2$.
- **The $\Lambda^4$ test**: a coordinate-diagonal $W$ has $\mathrm{Alt}(W^k)=0$ for every $k\ge1$, and by Vandermonde that family is $\mathfrak b(P_\mu)=0$ at every eigenprojector with $\mu\ne0$. Its converse is false among symmetric operators and true inside $\mathcal W_4$.

## 2. Claim B, the $\ell=2$ specialisation

**Type I.** At $\ell=2$ the definition asks for $\ell+r-1=r+1$ independent vectors $f_1,g_1,\dots,g_r$ with $E=\mathrm{span}\{f_1\wedge g_i\}$, which is $f_1\wedge U$ for $U=\mathrm{span}\{g_1,\dots,g_r\}$ and $f_1\notin U$. Lemma 5 gives $\dim V_E=\ell-1=1$, the shared line, as recorded. **B4 is the defect**: independence of $r+1$ vectors in an $n$-space, which is Corollary 7 read at $\ell=2$, caps $r$ at $n-1$. The block's "of any dimension" is therefore wrong, and the cap matters here because the object is an eigenspace inside $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ and its dimension is exactly what the route wants to bound.

**Type II.** At $\ell=2$ the definition asks for $\ell+1=3$ independent vectors $u_1,\dots,u_{3-r},g_1,\dots,g_r$, and each generator $u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{3-r}\wedge g_1\wedge\cdots\check g_i\cdots\wedge g_r$ is a wedge of $(3-r)+(r-1)=2$ vectors, as it must be. The count $3-r\ge0$ forces $r\le3$, matching Corollary 7's $r\le\ell+1$. At $r=3$ there are no $u$'s and the three generators are $g_2\wedge g_3$, $g_1\wedge g_3$ and $g_1\wedge g_2$, so $E=\Lambda^2\mathrm{span}\{g_1,g_2,g_3\}$ with $\dim V^E=3$. Everything the record states about type II at $r=3$ is right.

**B2, the gap in the enumeration.** The record's derivation goes straight from "$3-r$ vectors $u_i$, forcing $r\le3$" to "at $r=3$ none", and never says what type II gives at $r=1$ and $r=2$, which is where the paper itself flags that the two notions coincide or are both available. Worked out: at $r=2$ the data is $u_1,g_1,g_2$ and the generators are $u_1\wedge g_2$ and $u_1\wedge g_1$, so $E=u_1\wedge\mathrm{span}\{g_1,g_2\}$, a two-dimensional pencil, which is exactly the paper's own remark that the two types are identical in dimension two. At $r=1$ the data is $u_1,u_2,g_1$ and the single generator is $u_1\wedge u_2$ with $g_1$ deleted, so $E=\mathrm{span}\{u_1\wedge u_2\}=u_1\wedge\mathrm{span}\{u_2\}$, a one-dimensional pencil. **Both are already pencils, so the pencil-or-$\Lambda^2U$ statement omits nothing and the conclusion is untouched.** What is missing is the sentence saying so, and its absence is the difference between a complete case split and one that stops at the case it wanted.

**B3.** "And nothing else" is licensed. Theorem 6 is an equivalence, close means type I or type II by the definition at line 227, and the two specialisations above exhaust both types at $\ell=2$. The only hypothesis the record does not carry is $\ell<m$, met at $n\ge3$.

## 3. Claim C, the scope separation

**The witness is right, in both places it appears.** For $\omega=\cos t\,e_1\wedge e_2+\sin t\,e_3\wedge e_4$ the Pfaffian of the underlying skew matrix is $a_{12}a_{34}-a_{13}a_{24}+a_{14}a_{23}=\cos t\sin t$, and $\omega\wedge\omega=2\cos t\sin t\,e_{1234}$, twice it, as the record's `wedgeSelf` convention requires. So [diagonal-half.md:1134](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1134)'s $\tfrac12\sin2t$ and [notes.md](../references/decomposable-subspaces-grassmann-codes/notes.md)'s $\cos t\sin t$ are the same number and both are correct, which is worth saying because the two files state it in different forms.

**"Strictly weaker" is the correct relation.** Write (S) for "every nonzero element is decomposable" and (W) for "some basis is decomposable". (S) implies (W) because under (S) every basis is decomposable. (W) fails to imply (S) at the witness. So (W) is strictly weaker, and the block's placement of $\mathrm{span}\{e_1\wedge e_2,e_3\wedge e_4\}$ as "a subspace of the second kind that is not of the first" is exact.

**C2, where "only" is wrong.** Coordinate-diagonality of $W$ means there is one orthonormal frame $\{e_i\}$ of $\mathbb R^n$ in which every eigenspace is spanned by coordinate $2$-forms $e_i\wedge e_j$. Per eigenspace that is a third condition, call it (F): **there is an orthonormal basis of decomposable forms whose supports are coordinate planes of a common frame.** (F) implies (W) and is implied by neither the converse nor by any weakening of it, and (F) is what the route has to produce. So the true chain is (S) implies (F) implies (W), with both implications strict, and the block's "needs only a decomposable BASIS" names the weakest of the three where the obligation is the middle one. The record's own next sentence, which is claim D, is about (F) and not about (W), so the two sentences describe different conditions one clause apart.

**That (S) implies (F) is not free either**, and it is proved in section 5 below rather than assumed. It is the useful half of the classification and the record does not state it.

## 4. Claim D, reselection inside a pencil

**Confirmed, and here is the proof the record does not give.** Let $E=f\wedge U$ be a pencil of dimension $r$. Replace $f$ by a unit vector and replace $U$ by $U'$, its orthogonal projection onto $f^\perp$, using $f\wedge u=f\wedge(u-\langle u,f\rangle f)$, so that $E=f\wedge U'$ with $U'\subseteq f^\perp$ and $\dim U'=r$. Pick an orthonormal basis $u_1,\dots,u_r$ of $U'$ and set $\omega_i=f\wedge u_i$. Then
$$\langle f\wedge u_i,\,f\wedge u_j\rangle=\langle f,f\rangle\langle u_i,u_j\rangle-\langle f,u_j\rangle\langle u_i,f\rangle=\delta_{ij},$$
so $\{\omega_i\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $E$. Extend $\{f,u_1,\dots,u_r\}$ to an orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$. Every $\omega_i$ is a coordinate $2$-form of that frame. **So a pencil satisfies (F), and the choice is free over the whole of $O(r)$ acting on $U'$.**

## 5. Claim D again, and why "constrained inside $\Lambda^2U$" is false

**Refuted.** Let $E=\Lambda^2U$ with $\dim U=3$, so $\dim E=3$ and every element is decomposable because $\Lambda^4U=0$. Take **any** orthonormal basis $u_1,u_2,u_3$ of $U$. Then $\{u_1\wedge u_2,\,u_1\wedge u_3,\,u_2\wedge u_3\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $E$ whose three supports are coordinate planes of any frame extending $u_1,u_2,u_3$. So $\Lambda^2U$ satisfies (F) too, and the freedom is a full $O(3)$ of frames of $U$ rather than a restricted set.

**Comparing the two, the asymmetry the record asserts is not there.** At equal dimension $r=3$ a pencil pins the line $\langle f\rangle$, which is $V_E$, and pins the $3$-space $U'$, leaving an $O(3)$ of bases inside $U'$. And $\Lambda^2U$ pins the $3$-space $U$, which is $V^E$, leaving an $O(3)$ of frames of $U$. Both leave a three-parameter family. The one structural difference is which of Lemma 5's two invariants is pinned, the intersection $V_E$ for type I and the sum $V^E$ for type II, and that is duality rather than constraint, which is the paper's own section 3.

**The opposite case, argued.** "Constrained" could be meant as the dimension cap: type II exists only at $r\le3$, so an all-decomposable eigenspace of dimension four or more must be a pencil. That is true and is Corollary 7. It is also not what the sentence says, whose subject is reselection, and reselection inside $\Lambda^2U$ is unconstrained. **The verdict stays REFUTED AS STATED with the charitable reading named.**

**What survives, and it is the one thing the classification actually gives the route.** Combining sections 4 and 5: **every all-decomposable eigenspace satisfies (F) on its own.** So Theorem 6 does not merely bound the problem, it settles the per-eigenspace question on the all-decomposable branch, and the whole residue is the joint problem, one frame serving every eigenspace at once. That positive statement is stronger and cleaner than the block's "reselection is free inside a pencil and constrained inside $\Lambda^2U$", and it should replace it.

## 6. Claim E, first half: $Q$ maps $\mathcal W_n$ to itself

**Verified from the definition on two families, and the general statement identified.** Take $W$ coordinate-diagonal with weights $w_{ij}$. The structure constants of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ in the basis $e_i\wedge e_j$ satisfy $c_{acd}^2=1$ exactly when $\{a,c,d\}$ is a triangle of $2$-planes sharing three vertices, the constant being pinned by $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$ through $\tfrac12\kappa\cdot2(n-2)=n-2$. Hence
$$(W^\#)_{\{ij\}\{ij\}}=\tfrac12\sum_{c,d}c_{acd}^2w_cw_d=\sum_{k\ne i,j}w_{ik}w_{jk},$$
and the off-diagonal entries vanish, because two distinct $2$-planes have no common pair $\{c,d\}$ completing both to a triangle. So $Q(W)$ is diagonal with $q_{ij}=w_{ij}^2+\sum_{k\ne i,j}w_{ik}w_{jk}$, which is the same formula `test_diagonal_formula` in [curvature_operator.py](../scripts/lib/curvature_operator.py) validates against.

**Now spend Ricci-flatness.** For a diagonal Weyl tensor, Ricci-flatness is $r_i:=\sum_{k\ne i}w_{ik}=0$ for every $i$, and off-diagonal Ricci vanishes automatically. Then
$$\mathrm{Ric}(Q(W))_{ii}=\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}^2+\sum_{j\ne i}\sum_{k\ne i,j}w_{ik}w_{jk}=\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}^2+\sum_{k\ne i}w_{ik}(r_k-w_{ik})=0,$$
and $Q(W)$ is diagonal, so its off-diagonal Ricci vanishes too and first Bianchi is automatic on a coordinate-diagonal operator. **So $Q(W)\in\mathcal W_n$ at every diagonal Ricci-flat $W$ and every $n$.** The same computation applied to $\sum_kr_k^2=0$ gives $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^\#)=-\lVert W\rVert^2$, which cross-checks against relation (C) summed over $k$: $\sum_k2\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)=2\theta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W-2\lVert W\rVert^2=-2$ using $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$, and the left side is $2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^\#)$. Two independent routes to the same identity, one of them free of criticality.

**And on all of $\mathcal W_4$, without diagonality.** At $n=4$, $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ splits into two three-dimensional ideals, on each of which $\#$ is twice the adjugate, which is the record's [F2c](../findings/fence-posts.md) with $\gamma^2=2$ derived at [diagonal-half.md](../findings/diagonal-half.md). Writing $W=(A_+,A_-)$ with each $A_\pm$ a trace-free symmetric $3\times3$, and using $\mathrm{adj}(A)=A^2-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^2)I$ for trace-free $3\times3$,
$$Q(W)\big|_{\Lambda^\pm}=A_\pm^2+2\,\mathrm{adj}(A_\pm)=3A_\pm^2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_\pm^2)\,I.$$
Each block is trace-free, since $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(3A^2)-3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^2)=0$, so $Q(W)$ has both blocks trace-free and no cross block, which is exactly $\mathcal W_4$. **$Q$ maps $\mathcal W_4$ into $\mathcal W_4$, computed and not quoted.**

**The general statement.** The clean form is $\mathrm{Ric}(Q(R))_{ij}=\sum_{k,l}R_{ikjl}\mathrm{Ric}(R)_{kl}$, whose coefficient is pinned by $R=\mathrm{id}$, where $Q(\mathrm{id})=(n-1)\mathrm{id}$ and both sides read $(n-1)^2\delta_{ij}$. Ricci-flatness of $R$ then kills the right side, and preservation of first Bianchi is Hamilton's lemma, which the record holds through [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/). **r851 verified the consequence directly on the two families above and takes the identity's general form from that standard source, and says so rather than resting on [README.md](../README.md)'s word.** Note also that representation theory does not settle it on its own: $S^2(\mathcal W_n)$ carries a copy of $S^2_0(\mathbb R^n)$ through the contraction $W_{iabc}W_{jabc}$, so an equivariant obstruction is available and has to be computed away, which is what the two families do.

**E2 follows.** $P$ is a cubic with $\nabla P=3Q(W)$, using that $\langle A,B\#C\rangle$ is totally symmetric. On $\{\lVert W\rVert=1\}$ inside $\mathcal W_n$ the gradient is $3\Pi_{\mathcal W}Q(W)$, and $Q(W)\in\mathcal W_n$ makes the projector the identity, so criticality is $Q(W)=\theta W$ with $\theta=\langle Q(W),W\rangle=P(W)$ and no complement multiplier. Then $W^\#=\theta W-W^2$ and $[W,W^\#]=\theta[W,W]-[W,W^2]=0$. **There is no $\rho$, and the mechanism the plan opened I73 on is empty.**

**E3, a precision defect that strengthens the withdrawal.** The block reads "$W^\#=\theta W-W^2$ and $[W,W^\#]=0$ at every critical point, and its diagonal is relation (C)". The nearest antecedent of "its" is the commutator, and the commutator's diagonal is identically zero in any eigenbasis of $W$, since $[W,W^\#]_{kk}=\lambda_k(W^\#)_{kk}-(W^\#)_{kk}\lambda_k$. The identity whose diagonal is (C) is $W^\#=\theta W-W^2$: reading it at index $k$ gives $\tfrac12\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{k\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma=(W^\#)_{kk}=\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)$, which is (C) after doubling. **So the commutator is emptier than the block says**, carrying nothing at all rather than carrying (C), and the correction makes the withdrawal stronger. Argued the other way: a reader who takes "its" to point at the displayed identity gets a true sentence, so this is a defect of statement rather than of mathematics, and it is recorded as such.

## 7. Claim E, second half: the $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$ disanalogy

**The $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3$ half is right.** For $f(W)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$ on symmetric $W$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$ and $\lVert W\rVert=1$, the multipliers give $3W^2=2\lambda W+\mu\,\mathrm{Id}$, so $W^2=aW+b\,\mathrm{Id}$, a genuine quadratic identity, and a symmetric operator annihilated by a quadratic has at most two distinct eigenvalues.

**"$W^\#$ is not a polynomial in $W$" is literally false and its intent is right.** Any diagonal operator with simple spectrum is a polynomial in itself through Lagrange interpolation, so the claim cannot be about the existence of some polynomial. What the argument needs is that no polynomial of $W$-independent degree and $W$-independent coefficients represents $\#$, and the sharp form of that is: **$W^\#$ is not a function of $W$ at all**, because it takes different values on a repeated eigenvalue of $W$.

**A closed-form witness at $n=5$.** Take the coordinate-diagonal Ricci-flat weights
$$w_{12}=1,\ w_{13}=1,\ w_{14}=-1,\ w_{15}=-1,\ w_{23}=0,\ w_{24}=0,\ w_{25}=-1,\ w_{34}=-1,\ w_{35}=0,\ w_{45}=2,$$
whose five row sums are each $0$, so $W$ is a Weyl tensor. Using $(W^\#)_{\{ij\}}=\sum_{k\ne i,j}w_{ik}w_{jk}$,
$$(W^\#)_{23}=w_{12}w_{13}+w_{24}w_{34}+w_{25}w_{35}=1+0+0=1,\qquad (W^\#)_{24}=w_{12}w_{14}+w_{23}w_{34}+w_{25}w_{45}=-1+0-2=-3.$$
**But $w_{23}=w_{24}=0$.** So $W^\#$ is diagonal in $W$'s eigenbasis and assigns two different values to one eigenvalue of $W$, which no polynomial in $W$ can do. **The disanalogy holds at $n=5$, in closed form, and the same construction runs at every larger $n$ by padding with zeros.**

**And it fails at $n=4$, against the record's own fence post.** By section 6's formula, criticality at $n=4$ is $3A_\pm^2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_\pm^2)I=\theta A_\pm$ on each chirality, a quadratic identity in $A_\pm$ with $W$-independent shape. Equivalently, on the $n=4$ diagonal Ricci-flat locus, where the weights are $w_{12}=w_{34}=s$, $w_{13}=w_{24}=t$, $w_{14}=w_{23}=u$ with $s+t+u=0$, the formula gives $(W^\#)_{12}=2tu=2s^2+2e_2=2s^2-\tfrac12\lVert W\rVert^2$, so
$$W^\#=2W^2-\tfrac12\lVert W\rVert^2\,\mathrm{Id}\qquad\text{on the }n=4\text{ locus.}$$
**So at $n=4$ the root count applies exactly and everything collapses**, and [F2c](../findings/fence-posts.md) already says the reason: $\#$ is twice the adjugate on each three-dimensional ideal, which is why $P$ is a constant multiple of $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)$ there. The withdrawal states the disanalogy universally and does not cite F2c, which is a **GAP** with a site.

**How far the $n=4$ collapse goes, offered as a byproduct and derived here.** Solving $3x^2-\theta x-s_\pm=0$ for the eigenvalues of a trace-free $3\times3$ $A_\pm$ with $s_\pm=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_\pm^2)$ forces each block to be either $0$ or of spectrum $(2\theta/3,-\theta/3,-\theta/3)$ with $s_\pm=2\theta^2/3$. Unit norm $s_++s_-=1$ then admits exactly two solutions: one block active, giving $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$ and padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, and both blocks active, giving $4\theta^2/3=1$, so $\theta=\sqrt3/2=\theta_0(4)$, with $\Lambda^2$-spectrum $\{\sqrt3/3$ twice, $-\sqrt3/6$ four times$\}$, which is $u/w=2=n/(n-2)$ at $r=2=n(n-2)/4$, exactly L105's equality case and so $S^2\times S^2$. **Every critical point of $P$ at $n=4$ therefore has at most two distinct nonzero eigenvalues, and there are exactly two critical values up to sign.** That is I73's target proved at $n=4$ from criticality alone, in five lines from F2c, and it is the sharpest available statement of why the withdrawal's "so nothing collapses" needs a dimension attached. It is derived by r851, unaudited, and it rescues nothing at $n\ge5$, where section 7's witness stands.

**E5, the withdrawal.** Confirmed. The proposed mechanism asked whether $[W,W^\#]$ vanishes at a maximiser, and it vanishes at every critical point with no hypothesis, so the question has no content. [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md)'s line that the bracket form is strictly weaker than criticality is the whole of it.

## 8. Claim E, third half: I73's bridge to L105 is broken

**This is the one refutation with consequences for the route.** [plan.md:93](../plan.md#L93) states I73 as "a global maximiser of $P$ has exactly two distinct nonzero eigenvalues" and says that with L105 "this IS Conjecture A with no further step". **L105's hypothesis is different.** Its statement reads: "Let $W$ be a $\theta$-critical point of unit norm whose spectrum takes exactly two values, $u>0$ with multiplicity $r$ and $-w<0$ with multiplicity $N-r$." No zero eigenvalue is permitted, and L105's own closing line says so: padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ "has a kernel and three distinct eigenvalues, so it lies outside $d=2$", and "a two-eigenvalue traceless $W$ has trivial kernel automatically".

**And padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is an exact critical point at every $n$, with exactly two distinct nonzero eigenvalues.** Let $U=\mathbb R^4\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ and let $W$ be supported on $\Lambda^2U$ with $A_+=c\,\mathrm{diag}(2,-1,-1)$ and $A_-=0$. Section 6's formula gives $Q(W)|_{\Lambda^+}=3c^2\mathrm{diag}(4,1,1)-6c^2I=3c\,A_+$, so $Q(W)=3c\,W$ on $\Lambda^2U$, and $Q(W)$ vanishes off $\Lambda^2U$ because $c_{acd}\ne0$ forces $a$ into the triangle of $c$ and $d$, and a triangle with two planes inside $U$ has its third plane inside $U$ as well. Unit norm is $6c^2=1$, so $\theta=3/\sqrt6=\sqrt{3/2}$. **Its spectrum is $\{2c,-c,-c\}$ on $\Lambda^2U$ and $0$ elsewhere: exactly two distinct nonzero eigenvalues, and a kernel of dimension $\binom n2-6$.** So the hypothesis I73 aims at is satisfied at a point L105 explicitly excludes, and the inference "with no further step" fails.

**The repair.** I73's target must read "the $\Lambda^2$-spectrum takes exactly two values", equivalently "exactly two distinct nonzero eigenvalues **and** trivial kernel". That adds a genuine sub-obligation, since nothing in the record says a global maximiser has trivial kernel, and [plan.md:88](../plan.md#L88)'s I68 entry records that the kernel side is where F43's kernel-free witness sits. **The $n=4$ arithmetic makes the same point without any numerics**: there every critical point has at most two distinct nonzero eigenvalues, and $\theta$ still reaches $\sqrt{3/2}>\theta_0(4)=\sqrt3/2$, so "two distinct nonzero eigenvalues" is provably not enough to give $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$.

**Argued the other way.** One could say $n=4$ is outside Conjecture A's range and that padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ sits below $\theta_0(n)$ for $n\ge12$, so the bridge is harmless in the range that matters. That defends the conclusion and not the inference: the plan's sentence claims a theorem follows from a lemma, and it does not, and the missing piece is a statement about kernels that nobody has. **The refutation stands.**

## 9. Claim F, the surviving byproduct

**F1 is trivially true.** $(\theta-\lambda)\big(\theta-(\theta-\lambda)\big)=\lambda(\theta-\lambda)$.

**F2 does not follow.** For the invariance of the right side to say anything about spectra, the involution would have to act on the solution set of relation (C), and relation (C)'s left side $\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{k\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma$ is not invariant under replacing any subset of the $\lambda$'s by their reflections, $T$ being a fixed nonnegative hollow symmetric array with no such symmetry. What the remark really identifies is that **relation (C) read at one index, with its left side frozen, is a quadratic in $\lambda_k$ with two roots**. Freezing the left side is illegitimate, because $\lambda_k$ appears in it. So no critical point is produced from another, and the remark supports no statement about what criticality can distinguish.

**Now the brief's question: can such a spectrum satisfy the other constraints.** Suppose $\lambda$ and $\theta-\lambda$ are both eigenvalues at a unit-norm critical point, so both lie in $[-1,1]$. That needs $\lambda\ge\theta-1$ and $\lambda\le\theta+1$, and $\lambda^2+(\theta-\lambda)^2\ge\theta^2/2$ forces $\theta\le\sqrt2$. Since $\theta_0(n)=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n<\sqrt2$ at every $n$, the pair is **not excluded**, and both members are strictly positive whenever $\theta>1$, because each is at least $\theta-1$. **What is excluded is reflection-closure.** Tracelessness forces a negative eigenvalue $\mu$, and $\theta-\mu>\theta>1$ cannot itself be an eigenvalue of a unit-norm operator. **So the involution $\lambda\mapsto\theta-\lambda$ never maps a critical spectrum to itself**, and there is no symmetry to exploit or to defeat. The pair, when it occurs, also costs: it consumes $\theta^2/2$ of the norm budget and $\theta$ of the trace budget, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives at least $\theta^2/(1-\theta^2/2)$ other eigenvalues, six at $\theta=\sqrt{3/2}$.

**F3, the conclusion, is right for another reason.** Criticality alone genuinely cannot deliver two eigenvalues, because the record holds a critical point with three or more distinct nonzero eigenvalues: [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md)'s non-diagonal $n=8$ witness at $\theta=1.088948$, reported with trivial kernel and two simple eigenvalues. A two-value spectrum at $n=8$ with a simple eigenvalue has multiplicities $(1,27)$ or $(27,1)$, and L105's own two branches then cap $\theta$ at $0.2546$ in the first case and below zero in the second, neither of which is $1.088948$. So that point has at least three distinct eigenvalues and no zero among them, and any argument using only criticality would have to hold there. **The obstruction is real and the reflection remark is not what establishes it**, so F3 is a GAP with a site rather than a new fact. Two caveats, disclosed: the $n=8$ witness is a numerical solution on the record and not a closed form, and I73 concerns a maximiser, so the argument is that no criticality-only proof can exist rather than that criticality fails at the maximiser.

**Argued the other way.** One could read the remark as an internal note about which quantity (C) pins, namely a product rather than a value, which is true. **The verdict stays REFUTED AS STATED**, because the recorded sentence draws a conclusion about distinguishing spectra and that step is where it breaks.

## 12. What would break the route, and what r851 did to look for it

**The route is the block's closing obligation: classify the subspaces of $\Lambda^2V$ carrying a decomposable basis, "one classification and not a search". A dimension count says there is no such classification.** The Grassmann cone in $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ has dimension $2n-3$. At $n=4$ and $r=2$ the subspaces spanned by two points of the cone form a family of dimension $2(2n-3)-2=8$, and $\dim\mathrm{Gr}(2,\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4)=8$ as well, so **the decomposable-basis $2$-planes are a full-dimensional family**. Directly: $\mathrm{Pf}$ is a quadratic form of signature $(3,3)$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$, its restriction to a $2$-plane is a binary quadratic form, and that form has two independent real roots exactly when it is indefinite, which is an open condition holding on a positive-measure set of planes. So condition (W) is semialgebraic and open, not a list of types, and the block's framing of the residue as a classification with "known members" is the wrong shape for the object. **By contrast the all-decomposable $2$-planes at $n=4$ are the pencils $f\wedge U'$, a family of dimension $3+2=5$ inside the same $8$-dimensional Grassmannian, so measure zero.** That quantifies "it bounds the problem rather than solving it" sharply: Theorem 6 settles a measure-zero sliver of the candidate eigenspaces.

**What else would break it, and what was checked.** A pencil or a $\Lambda^2U$ eigenspace failing (F) would break section 5's positive statement, and sections 4 and 5 prove no such failure exists. A field obstruction would break the transcription's usability, and section 1 checked the proofs are field-free. An algebraically-closed hypothesis hiding in Lemma 1 or Lemma 3 would do the same, and both proofs were read. For the I73 side, a fixed universal polynomial representing $\#$ at some $n\ge5$ would revive the root count, and section 7's $n=5$ witness rules it out at every $n\ge5$ by padding. A critical point with three distinct nonzero eigenvalues would settle F3's conclusion, and section 9 located one on the record and eliminated the two-value multiplicity patterns that could have explained it away.
