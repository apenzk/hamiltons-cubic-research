## Claim 1 -- the boundedness theorem, link by link

Script: [c1_blockwise_recession.py](../scripts/reviewers/r245/c1_blockwise_recession.py). It runs the whole recession argument exactly, in the shape the theorem's objects actually have -- vector-valued covariants (block A: dimension 2, multiplicity 2; block B: dimension 1, multiplicity 1), a block-diagonal Gram, the represented form P_D(W) = sum tr(D_lambda G_lambda(W)) -- in both directions, with the plant q2 = 2 q1 as the below-stable-range regression.

### (i) The augmented system changes nothing, and the one real content of this link sits elsewhere

The augmented system's homogeneous solutions satisfy A d = 0 AND (D x I)v = 0. The proof uses only the first conjunct, which is legitimate: it proves the stronger statement that even the un-augmented psd kernel is zero, and the kernel rows can only shrink the recession cone. Verified both ways in the script: in the independent case the rows cut the kernel 1 -> 0; in the plant they are satisfied AUTOMATICALLY by the annihilator a relation manufactures -- a functional relation vanishes at W_* in particular -- so the augmentation can never rescue boundedness below the stable range, exactly as the file's failure paragraph says.

What the link does silently assume: A d = 0 means the 89 functionals mu(D) vanish, and that equals "P_D is the zero sextic" only where the 89 constraint functionals separate the 89-dimensional invariant space -- independence of the chosen invariant subset at every real n >= 12. That is A2's owed item (ii) (the det Gram_S whose one named factor is 869n-2767, root ~3.18; the remaining roots on [12,inf) unexamined), and [plan.md](../plan.md) grades A2 PARTLY DONE. The precondition should be stated in the theorem; it is the same kind of Sturm-type obligation as claim 2's, not a new mechanism.

### (ii) The psd conversion is sound, and the file's sentence is notation rather than mathematics

There is no scalar vector p(W) in R^184 with P_D = p(W)^T D p(W): matching outer products forces det G_lambda(W) == 0, and the script's det G_A = (a^2-b^2)^2 is a witness that independent covariants refuse it. The statement that is true, and that the proof needs, is blockwise: D psd <=> every D_lambda psd; each tr(D_lambda G_lambda(W)) >= 0 pointwise since both factors are psd; nonnegative terms summing to the zero polynomial vanish separately; and the identity tr(LL^T G_lambda) = \|L^T E_lambda\|_F^2 (verified symbolically) turns each vanishing trace into D_lambda E_lambda(W) = 0, with E_lambda the m x d matrix of evaluations. So "D p(W) = 0" is (D_lambda x I_d) acting on the concatenated evaluations -- the conclusion is per-block annihilation of coordinate slices, and it does follow.

Two observations the file should carry. First, the conversion needs the pairing behind G_lambda to be an honest positive-definite inner product on real evaluations; the record's own n=5 history (reviewer 204's real-structure refutation in [sym3-blocks.md](../findings/sym3-blocks.md)) shows the construction can silently sit in a Q(i) basis where that has to be made explicit -- an obligation on the eventual n=12 build, not a flaw in the argument. Second, the file's screen tests a different regime than the theorem occupies: its honest branch imposes the annihilation conditions D p = 0 directly -- the POST-psd conclusion -- and gets a zero linear kernel, so psd-ness never does any work there. In the real object the linear kernel is the span of the K_i, of dimension k >= 1 by construction (it is the t-space), and the theorem's entire content is that the psd part of that nonzero kernel is 0. The script's miniature has the honest shape: linear kernel of dimension 1 (D_A = diag(x, -x)), cut to 0 by psd-ness alone.

### (iii) Spanning of which space, and what the 184-count does and does not warrant

The right statement: D_lambda E_lambda(W) = 0 for all W kills D_lambda iff the d-dimensional coordinate slices of the evaluations span R^{m_lambda}, which holds iff the m_lambda covariants in that block are linearly independent as functions; blockwise independence equals independence of all 184 in Sym^3, by isotypic projection. So the file's equivalence is correct once "the coordinate space" is read per block. The audited count enters only as the converse's fuel: below n=12 the dimension drops, so relations are forced and the recession cone genuinely opens -- the failure paragraph is right.

**What the warrant does not cover.** The theorem needs the covariant family the constraint data is built on -- one family, rational in n -- to be independent at EVERY n >= 12; equivalently, the 184x184 covariant Gram over Q(n) has root-free determinant on [12,inf). The audited object ([sym3-stability.md](../findings/sym3-stability.md), reviewers 224/225) is the multiplicity count, and its own scope section says so: it fixes the shape and "says nothing about their values". Basis-ness of a constructed set is a different fact, and the record's own state of it is: the n=12 construction is incomplete (7 completed blocks from the stopped tail run, [plan.md](../plan.md) section 2 item 1); the upward transfer of independence is marked UNAUDITED in plan.md's standing state; and the diagrammatic carrier of that transfer is OWED by [run_n24_via_diagrams.py](../scripts/runs/run_n24_via_diagrams.py)'s own note ("the construction emits VECTORS in a weight space, not diagrams"). The sentence "which is the audited count at n >= 12: the 184 are a basis, no relation survives" attaches the audit to the count and lets "the 184 are a basis" inherit it -- the exact subordinate-clause pattern the brief's claim 5 names, here inside the theorem's proof. The theorem stands as a conditional; the condition is real, unaudited, and should be listed beside the two Sturm-type obligations rather than cited as settled.

## Claim 2 -- the Helly step, and where "fixed R^k" actually lives

Script: [c2_nullity_helly.py](../scripts/reviewers/r245/c2_nullity_helly.py).

**The brief's premise, refined.** The ambient R^k needs no constancy statement: k is the nullity of the augmented system over the field Q(n), one number computed once, and every S_n sits in that same R^k wherever the rational entries are defined. What nullity-constancy protects is two other things, and both are governed by one hypothesis. If the chosen pivot minor xi(n) has a root at some n* >= 12, then either entries of B_p, K_i are undefined at n*, or the true kernel at n* is larger than the span of the specialised K_i -- the script's witness family has generic nullity 1 jumping to 2 exactly at the root n = 20, with a homogeneous solution e2 invisible to the family. At such an n* a Farkas refutation proves "no constant t IN THIS FAMILY", a strictly weaker statement than "no constant certificate". Conversely xi root-free on [12,inf) pins the rank by semicontinuity, the nullity is constant, and the Cramer-form K_i -- unit vector on each free coordinate, so never dependent under specialisation -- stay a basis throughout. **Constant nullity is equivalent to the root-freeness obligation.**

**Is it established anywhere?** No. It exists in the record only as r244's conditional rank-semicontinuity argument, whose hypothesis r244 itself lists as owed ("state the hypothesis as root-freeness of the chosen xi(n)") and prices honestly: xi is a determinant of order ~89 with entries of degree <= 6, so its degree is of order several hundred, and the check "is one item, not a cheap one". The verification r244 reports ("rank and nullity anomaly-free at every even n in [12, 60]") is of the 2x4 miniature in [c3_pivot_minor.py](../scripts/reviewers/r244/c3_pivot_minor.py), not of the real system -- which has never been assembled, since A2 still owes the coordinate-picking steps and the diagram Gram ([plan.md](../plan.md) section 2, items 1-2), and the value of k is unknown. [run_a2pp_constant_t.py](../scripts/runs/run_a2pp_constant_t.py) and plan.md's A2'' both write "a fixed R^k" with no condition attached. So: the mechanism is in the record, the discharge is not, and A2'' inherits the obligation silently. It belongs on A5's Sturm list now, as the item everything in A2'/A2'' routes through.

**Two findings in the record's favour.** First, the Helly step needs LESS than the file claims to have proved: with all S_n closed (free, they are spectrahedra), ONE compact member suffices -- intersect every S_n with S_12, the finite-intersection-property argument runs on compact sets, and finite Helly needs convexity only; the count "some k+1 dimensions" then holds verbatim, with n=12 allowed among them. So the Helly application survives even if per-n covariant independence (claim 1(iii)) fails at some n above 12: compactness of S_12 alone is a finite exact computation once the system exists. Second, the phrase "an exact Farkas certificate" is sound for a reason the record never states: semidefinite Farkas alternatives need a regularity condition, and weak infeasibility carries no certificate -- but a trivial recession cone excludes weak infeasibility outright (a sequence approaching the cone with unbounded t would yield, after normalising, a nonzero psd element of the kernel span), so joint infeasibility over k+1 dimensions is strong infeasibility, the separating hyperplane has a gap, and the alternative Y exists. The boundedness theorem is exactly what buys the exactness of its own refutation certificate. One residue stays asserted rather than argued: that the witness Y can be taken rational; the witness set is convex with rational data, and a rational point should be extractable, but nothing in the record says how.

**One scope note.** "Constant t" is relative to the parametrisation (B_p, K_i): a t constant in one rational parametrisation is a Q(n)-varying t in another, and a Farkas refutation does not exclude a different rational base point admitting a constant t'. The fallback chain is unaffected -- t in Q(n) is parametrisation-invariant and subsumes every such reparametrisation -- but a recorded refutation should say "no constant t over this (B_p, K_i)", which is what it will have proved.
