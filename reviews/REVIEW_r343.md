# Review r343, Gate 2 on [DERIVE-sp3.md](DERIVE-sp3.md), claims D1–D5

**D1 (the obstruction), PLAUSIBLE as stated, CONFIRMED and strengthened in its within-row half, and its cross-row count is wrong.** Within a single row the failure is universal, not generic: I prove below that at $d=3$ no quadratic multiplier isolates any type in any row, at every spectrum with three distinct values. Across rows the stated count ("$9$ homogeneous conditions on $9$ parameters generically forces zero"), is false as an argument: [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s two dependencies always solve those conditions, so the zero multiplier is never the unique solution. The correct statement is that the achievable coefficient vectors span a space of dimension at most $3d-2=7$ inside $\mathbb R^{10}$, so isolating a type is $3$ conditions on the spectrum; and the real reason $d=2$ works is $3d-2=\binom{d+2}3$ there ($4=4$) and fails at $d=3$ ($7<10$).

**D2 (the vertex theorem), CONFIRMED in its core, REFUTED in the sentence that turns it into an enumeration.** The feasible set is a nonempty-or-empty compact polyhedron of affine dimension $4$ containing no line, $\theta$ is affine on it, and every vertex has at least four vanishing $t_{ijk}$; "at least" is right and "exactly" would be wrong. But "$\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is the maximum of at most $\binom{10}4=210$ linear solves" is false without a feasibility filter (the unfiltered maximum is an upper bound for $\theta^\ast$ and can exceed it) and the theorem's clause "equivalently, at a configuration in which at least four of the ten bracket types vanish" presupposes realisability and contradicts the note's own scope paragraph.

**D3 (consistency at $d=2$), CONFIRMED in substance, one clause REFUTED against the record.** The balanced sphere product does have $t_{112}=t_{222}=0$, and that is the equality case. "The segment's two endpoints meet" is false: [bounds-relaxations.md](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) records the $S^3\times S^3$ segment as $\theta\in[\sqrt{10}/18,\sqrt{10}/3]$, a factor of six long, with $\sqrt{10}/3=\theta_0(6)$ its upper endpoint. The right word is a degenerate vertex, two active constraints where one suffices.

**D4 (the cuts), REFUTED as a cut, true as a statement.** It is vacuous twice over: breaking a row needs six zeros and only four are asserted, and (A) is an equality already imposed in every one of the $210$ solves, so it can never contradict a named four-subset. All $210$ survive (A)-positivity. The heading "Three types can never vanish, by (A) and positivity" is unsupported, (A) forces no named type positive. A cut that does bite comes from (B), and is derived at the end of this report.

**D5 (scope), CONFIRMED and incomplete.** The realisability caveat is stated correctly and is the right caveat. Two things it omits: $\Pi(\lambda)$ drops the pair cap that [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) itself flags as available at no cost, and restoring it destroys the "at least four zeros" count; and at equality $\theta=\theta^\ast$ the critical point's own $T$ lies in the optimal *face*, not at the vertex, so even a realisable vertex does not force the critical point's vanishing pattern. Nothing Gate-2-passed on the record settles which four-subsets are realisable; [DERIVE-sp3b.md](DERIVE-sp3b.md) is the candidate answer and is itself unreviewed.

## What I derived before reading the note

**The row structure, re-derived.** Write $\mu^{(i)}_j=m_j-\delta_{ij}$ and, for a class $i$ and an unordered pair $\{j,k\}$ drawn from the $d$ classes, $\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}=2\mu_j\mu_k$ when $j<k$ and $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$ when $j=k$, which is exactly the number of *ordered* pairs $(b,c)$, $b\ne c$, both distinct from a fixed $a$ in class $i$, with $b$ in class $j$ and $c$ in class $k$. Reading [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s reduced system in that notation, the coefficient of $t_{i,\{j,k\}}$ is $\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}\cdot 1$ in (A$_i$), $\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}\cdot\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2$ in (B$_i$) and $\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}\cdot\nu_j\nu_k$ in (C$_i$). So a row multiplier $\alpha_iA_i+\beta_iB_i+\gamma_iC_i$ acts on that coefficient as $\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}\,\Psi_i(\nu_j,\nu_k)$, where $\Psi_i$ is the **polar form** of the quadratic $\varphi_i(x)=\alpha_i+\beta_ix+\gamma_ix^2$. That is the mechanism D1 names, and it checks out; a row consumes a quadratic, and it sees the spectrum only through the $\binom{d+1}2$ pairs.

**Within a row, isolation fails at every $d=3$ spectrum, "generically" is doing no work here.** Suppose $\varphi$ isolates the type of pair $\{j_0,k_0\}$ in row $i$, so $\Psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)=0$ for the other five pairs. If $\{j_0,k_0\}$ is a mixed pair, the three diagonal conditions read $\varphi(\nu_1)=\varphi(\nu_2)=\varphi(\nu_3)=0$, three distinct roots of a quadratic, hence $\varphi\equiv0$. If $\{j_0,k_0\}$ is a diagonal pair, say $\{1,1\}$, then $\varphi(\nu_2)=\varphi(\nu_3)=0$ fixes $\varphi(x)=c(x-\nu_2)(x-\nu_3)$, whose polar form at the remaining mixed pair $(\nu_2,\nu_3)$ is
$$\Psi(\nu_2,\nu_3)=\nu_2\nu_3-\tfrac{\nu_2+\nu_3}2(\nu_2+\nu_3)+\nu_2\nu_3=-\tfrac12(\nu_2-\nu_3)^2\ne0$$
whenever $\nu_2\ne\nu_3$, which is the hypothesis. So the within-row half of D1 is a theorem, not a genericity statement, and the note should claim it as one.

**A smaller correction to D1's $d=2$ side.** "Five at $d=3$, which $3$ parameters cannot; two at $d=2$, which $3$ parameters do with one to spare", there is no spare. The multiplier is homogeneous, so $3$ coefficients are $2$ essential parameters, matched exactly by the $2$ annihilations; that is why [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s two branch polynomials are *determined* rather than chosen, which the record already says elsewhere.

**Across rows, the stated count is wrong and the corrected one is sharper.** Let $V_t\subseteq\mathbb R^{10}$ be the span of the coefficient vectors of the nine equations, read on the ten type variables. Isolating type $\tau$ means $e_\tau\in V_t$. D1 counts this as nine homogeneous conditions on nine parameters and concludes the zero multiplier generically. That inference does not stand: [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) proves two dependencies among the nine rows, $\sum_im_i\nu_i(\mathrm A_i)=\sum_im_i(\mathrm B_i)$ and $\sum_im_i(\mathrm C_i)=\sum_im_i\nu_i(\mathrm B_i)$, and both lie in the kernel of the parameters-to-coefficients map. So the nine conditions always have at least a two-dimensional solution space (the trivial one) and "generically forces zero" is a claim the count does not support. What the same dependencies *do* give is $\dim V_t\le 3d-2=7$, so
$$\text{isolating }\tau\iff e_\tau\in V_t,\qquad\text{which is }10-\dim V_t\ge3\text{ conditions on the spectrum.}$$
**And this identifies the real reason $d=2$ worked.** At $d=2$ the rank is $3d-2=4$ and the number of types is $\binom{d+2}3=4$, so $V_t=\mathbb R^4$ and *every* type is isolable at *every* spectrum. I checked this against [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) by isolating the two types it does not name: $t_{111}$ needs $\Psi(u,-w)=\varphi(-w)=0$, which forces $\varphi(x)=(x+w)^2$ and leaves $\varphi(u)=(u+w)^2\ne0$; symmetrically $t_{122}$ needs $\varphi(x)=(x-u)^2$. All four constraints are isolable, which is exactly the statement $V_t=\mathbb R^4$, and it is what makes L105's proof a facet-by-facet reading rather than an estimate. At $d=3$, $7<10$; at $d=4$, $10<20$. **The coincidence $3d-2=\binom{d+2}3$ holds only at $d\le2$, and that single line is the whole obstruction.** I recommend the note carry it in place of the parameter count.

**What survives of L105's method, which D1 gives up too early.** The note concludes that "the route has to be built rather than reused". That is too pessimistic, and the record's own completeness statement says so: dual certificates are complete by strong duality ([Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)). At an optimal vertex, complementary slackness says the optimal multiplier annihilates the coefficients of the **six positive** variables and leaves coefficients $\le0$ on the four vanishing ones. That is six annihilations against an effectively seven-dimensional space of functionals, so a certificate exists (necessarily, by duality) and is unique up to scale and the two dependencies. **So the correct generalisation of L105's device is "annihilate the support", not "isolate a facet"**, and it is constructive: naming a four-subset $Z$ names its complementary support, six linear conditions produce the multiplier $(\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i)$, and the certificate reads off $\theta\le\big[\sum_i(2(n-2)\alpha_i-2\nu_i\beta_i-2\nu_i^2\gamma_i)\big]\big/\big[2\sum_i\nu_i\gamma_i\big]$. That is the same closed form the note owes as its item (i), obtained without solving for $t$ at all.

## D2, checked step by step

**Compactness and boundedness, CONFIRMED, and the note's citation of it is thinner than the fact.** With every $m_i\ge3$ we have $\mu^{(i)}_j\ge2$, so every $\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}>0$: each of the ten types appears in row (A$_i$) for each class $i$ in its support with a strictly positive coefficient, and all coefficients in that row are positive with right-hand side $2(n-2)>0$. Hence $0\le t_\tau\le 2(n-2)/\varepsilon\le n-2$ for every type. The feasible set is therefore bounded, contains no line, and if nonempty is a compact polytope with at least one vertex. The attack "could it be unbounded or contain a line" resolves in the note's favour, but only because $m_i\ge3$ makes every $\varepsilon$ positive, at a multiplicity $2$ the diagonal $\varepsilon$ vanishes and the argument would have to be redone, which is another reason the $m_i\ge3$ hypothesis is load-bearing.

**Dimension and the projection, CONFIRMED.** The affine solution set lives in $(t,\theta)\in\mathbb R^{11}$ and has dimension $\binom{d+2}3+1-(3d-2)=4$. A traceless nonzero spectrum has some $\nu_i\ne0$, and (C$_i$) at that row expresses $\theta$ as an affine function of $t$; so the projection to $t$-space is injective and the polytope is genuinely $4$-dimensional in the ten nonnegativity constraints. A point is a vertex exactly when the active constraints' normals span the $4$-dimensional direction space, which needs at least four active constraints, hence **at least four vanishing coordinates**, and this holds even if the polytope's own dimension has dropped below $4$. So "at least four" is correct and "exactly four" would be wrong; the $d=2$ balanced product is the standing witness that the surplus is not pathological.

**Maximum at a vertex, CONFIRMED.** A linear functional on a nonempty compact polytope attains its maximum on a face, and a face of a polytope with vertices has a vertex, which is a vertex of the whole. The theorem should nevertheless carry the hypothesis $\Pi(\lambda)\ne\varnothing$ explicitly: [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) admits $\theta^\ast=-\infty$, and there is then no vertex to speak of.

**"The maximum of at most 210 linear solves", REFUTED as written.** Two things are missing. First, **the feasibility filter**: a named four-subset $Z$ gives a solve whose output must be tested for $t\ge0$ in the other six coordinates, and the maximum of the *unfiltered* $210$ outputs is only an upper bound for $\theta^\ast$, which can be strictly larger. Since the programme's use of this is to prove $\theta^\ast\le\theta_0(n)$, an unfiltered maximum exceeding $\theta_0$ would be reported as a fence post and would mean nothing. Second, **rank**: a four-subset whose four coordinate functionals are dependent on the affine space gives a singular system with no solution or a positive-dimensional one; those cases must be discarded, and the enumeration stays complete because a vertex with an active set of rank $4$ contains four independent active constraints and so is the unique output of at least one nonsingular four-subset. The corrected sentence is: *$\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is the largest $\theta$ among the nonsingular four-subset solves that are componentwise nonnegative, and there are at most $210$ of them.*

**The bracket equivalence, REFUTED inside the theorem, CONFIRMED as a conditional.** For a $T$ that comes from an orthonormal frame, $t_{ijk}$ is the average of nonnegative $T_{abc}=c_{abc}^2$ over the class-orbit, so $t_{ijk}=0$ if and only if every $c_{abc}$ in the block vanishes, and since $c$ is trilinear that is exactly the basis-free statement $\langle[E_i,E_j],E_k\rangle=0$, independent of the choice of adapted basis inside each eigenspace. Note this works for the critical point's own $T$ even though that $T$ need not be $G$-invariant, so the note's justification "orbit-constancy of $t$" can be weakened to "an average of nonnegatives vanishes only if each does". **But a general point of $\Pi(\lambda)$ has no $E_i$ at all.** $\Pi(\lambda)$ is a system of linear equations on a nonnegative array; nothing in it says the array is a square of structure constants. So the theorem's clause "equivalently, at a configuration in which at least four of the ten bracket types vanish" is not a theorem, and it contradicts the note's own scope paragraph five paragraphs later. The fix is to state the equivalence as a conditional, *if the optimal vertex is realised by a frame, then its four vanishing types are four vanishing bracket types*, and to change the note's title, which currently asserts "the $d=3$ optimum is a partial grading". [plan.md](../plan.md) has already copied the unconditional form into its I13 entry; that copy needs the same fix.

## D3, checked

**Both types vanish at the balanced product, CONFIRMED, from structure and not from the crossing.** For $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ the eigenspace decomposition of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is $\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak m$ with $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{so}(n/2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(n/2)$ of dimension $2\binom{n/2}2=n(n-2)/4$ and $\mathfrak m$ of dimension $n^2/4$, matching [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s crossing values $r=n(n-2)/4$ and $N-r=n^2/4$ exactly. It is a symmetric pair: $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]\subseteq\mathfrak h$ gives $\langle[E_1,E_1],E_2\rangle=0$, so $t_{112}=0$; $[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak h$ gives $\langle[E_2,E_2],E_2\rangle=0$, so $t_{222}=0$; and $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak m$ with $\mathfrak h$ nonabelian for $n\ge6$ keeps $t_{122}$ and $t_{111}$ positive. So the vertex is degenerate on exactly the two upper facets and on neither lower one. This is independent of, and agrees with, the algebraic crossing $\rho=n/(n-2)$.

**"Where the segment's two endpoints meet", REFUTED, against the record.** That phrase says the feasible segment collapses to a point. It does not: [bounds-relaxations.md](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) records the $S^3\times S^3$ segment as $\theta\in[\sqrt{10}/18,\sqrt{10}/3]$ and notes that a factor of six refutes a session claim that small $d$ makes the feasible set small, and $S^3\times S^3$ *is* the balanced product at $n=6$, with $\sqrt{10}/3=\theta_0(6)$ its upper endpoint. What is degenerate is the **upper vertex**: two of the four nonnegativity constraints are active where one suffices in a one-dimensional polytope. That is the correct $d=2$ shadow of D2's "at least four", and it is what makes the consistency check meaningful; the phrase as written would break it.

**One further imprecision.** "Which is exactly Lemma L105's two upper facets $t_{112}=0$ and $t_{222}=0$", at a general $d=2$ spectrum only one of the two is the upper endpoint, the other being slack; they bind together only at $r=n(n-2)/4$. The vertex condition asks for one, and the balanced product supplies two. [DERIVE-sp3b.md](DERIVE-sp3b.md) states this correctly, so the two notes disagree and this one is the wrong side of it.

## D4, checked

**True, and inert.** With every $m_i\ge3$ all coefficients of (A$_i$) are strictly positive and the right-hand side is $2(n-2)>0$, so not all six types of a row can vanish. At $d=3$ each row carries exactly the six types containing its class index, so killing a row needs six zeros while D2 asserts four. **No four-subset is excluded: all $210$ survive (A)-positivity.** The cut is in fact empty for a stronger reason, (A) is one of the equalities that every one of the $210$ solves imposes, so it is satisfied by construction and can never eliminate a candidate; the only way it could bite is by making a subsystem inconsistent, which needs the six zeros. The note half-concedes this ("four zeros always leave every row loaded") while its heading claims "three types can never vanish, by (A) and positivity", which is unsupported: (A) names no type. I would delete the heading and keep the observation as a sanity check.

## D5, checked, and the two omissions

**The stated caveat is correct.** $\Pi(\lambda)$ is a relaxation whose points need not be squares of structure constants of any frame; [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) already records that "the pinned system knows nothing of realizability", and the note's reading of the [LIT-spectra.md](LIT-spectra.md) nilsoliton analogue is the right one. The direction of the inference is safe: a non-realisable optimal vertex leaves $\theta\le\theta^\ast$ true and merely unsharp.

**Omission one, and it is serious for the enumeration's structural payoff.** $\Pi(\lambda)$ **drops the pair cap** $\sum_cT_{abc}=\lVert[v_a,v_b]\rVert^2\le2$, which [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) holds as a standing fact and which [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) says is available in reduced variables at no cost as $\sum_k\mu^{(ij)}_kt_{ijk}\le2$, inert at $d=2$. At $d=3$ that is six further inequalities, one per unordered class pair. Adding them, a vertex of the tightened polytope needs four active constraints out of sixteen rather than out of ten, and **could have as few as zero vanishing $t$**. So "the optimum is a partial grading" is a property of $\Pi(\lambda)$ as defined and does not survive the tightening the record recommends. Two consequences worth stating in the note: a positive outcome of the $210$-case enumeration ($\theta^\ast\le\theta_0$) still closes $d=3$, because the tightened optimum is no larger; a negative outcome decides nothing about the tightened relaxation, so the fence post it would produce is a fence post against $\Pi(\lambda)$ and must be labelled as such.

**Omission two.** At equality $\theta=\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ the critical point's own $T$ is an optimal point of the linear programme, hence lies in the **optimal face**, which need not be a vertex. So even a realisable optimal vertex does not force the critical point's own vanishing pattern; what it forces is that the critical point's $T$ is a convex combination of optimal vertices, and a type vanishes at $W$ only if it vanishes at every optimal vertex. The $d=2$ equality case survives this because the optimal face there is the single degenerate endpoint, and that is an extra fact about $d=2$, not a general one.

**Nothing on the record settles which four-subsets are realisable.** The nearest results are the $d=2$ ones, [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s reading of $t_{112}=0$ as "$E_{\max}$ is a subalgebra" and the correction at [post F40](../findings/fence-posts.md), and [DERIVE-sp3b.md](DERIVE-sp3b.md), which names two families and is itself Gate 2 owed. So D5's implicit "nothing settles it" is right as of this review.

## The owed step, part two: which four-subsets survive, and the cut that actually bites

**(A) cuts nothing: all $210$ survive**, for the reasons under D4.

**(B) cuts, and by a lot in two of the three sign regimes.** Row $i$ of (B) reads $\sum_\tau\varepsilon^{(i)}_{jk}(\nu_j+\nu_k)\,t_\tau=-4\nu_i$. With $t\ge0$ and $\varepsilon>0$, if $\nu_i>0$ then some type of row $i$ whose pair-sum is negative must be strictly positive, and if $\nu_i<0$ then some type whose pair-sum is positive must be. Write $N_i$ for that set of types; **no admissible four-subset may contain all of $N_i$**, and when $N_i$ is a singleton its member is forced positive.

Order $\nu_1>\nu_2>\nu_3$, so tracelessness gives $\nu_1>0>\nu_3$. In row $1$ the pair-sums are $2\nu_1,\ \nu_1+\nu_2,\ \nu_1+\nu_3,\ 2\nu_2,\ \nu_2+\nu_3,\ 2\nu_3$ on $111,112,113,122,123,133$, and $2\nu_3<0$ always, so $133\in N_1$ at every spectrum. In row $3$ the pair-sums on $113,123,133,223,233,333$ are $2\nu_1,\ \nu_1+\nu_2,\ \nu_1+\nu_3,\ 2\nu_2,\ \nu_2+\nu_3,\ 2\nu_3$, and $2\nu_1>0$ always, so $113\in N_3$ at every spectrum. Hence:

- **If $\nu_2>0$ and $\nu_2+\nu_3>0$** then $N_1=\{133\}$, so $t_{133}>0$ and every four-subset containing $133$ dies: $\binom93=84$ of the $210$ removed, leaving $126$.
- **If $\nu_2<0$ and $\nu_1+\nu_2<0$** then $N_3=\{113\}$, so $t_{113}>0$ and the $84$ four-subsets containing $113$ die, leaving $126$.
- **Otherwise** both $N_1$ and $N_3$ have at least two members and the cut is weaker: a four-subset must omit a member of each.

**A second cut, from (C), once $\theta>0$ is assumed, which the programme may do**, since only vertices with $\theta>\theta_0(n)>0$ are of interest when the goal is $\theta^\ast\le\theta_0(n)$. Row $3$ of (C) has right-hand side $2\nu_3(\theta-\nu_3)<0$, so some type of row $3$ with $\nu_j\nu_k<0$ is positive; those are $133$ always, together with $233$ when $\nu_2>0$ and $123$ when $\nu_2<0$. So in *every* regime at least one of $\{133,233\}$ or of $\{133,123\}$ is positive, which removes the $\binom82=28$ four-subsets containing both members of the applicable pair. Symmetrically row $1$ of (C) with $\nu_1>0$ and $\theta-\nu_1$ of unknown sign gives nothing without a bound on $\nu_1$, and I do not assert one.

**Cross-check against the two named grading vertices.** [DERIVE-sp3b.md](DERIVE-sp3b.md)'s vanishing sets are $\{112,113,222,333\}$ for a $\mathbb Z_3$-grading and $\{113,123,223,333\}$ for a symmetric pair with a split even part, both written in the *grading* labelling rather than the eigenvalue order, so the cut applies only after the classes are matched up. Read literally in the order $\nu_1>\nu_2>\nu_3$, both sets contain $113$ and neither contains $133$: both are killed outright in the regime $\nu_2<0,\ \nu_1+\nu_2<0$, and both survive the (B) cut in the regime $\nu_2>0,\ \nu_2+\nu_3>0$. **That is a constraint on the eigenvalue ordering a $d=3$ grading vertex can carry**, and it is the kind of thing the next step should pin down before evaluating the six candidate multiplicity triples.
