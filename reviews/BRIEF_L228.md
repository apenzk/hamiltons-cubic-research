#### Lemma L228, the $\lvert J\rvert=3$ step of R27 on two strata at every $n$: one block against a common frame, or one pair's difference against it

*Gate 2 owed. Derived 2026-09-15 for [I106](../plan.md) ([derivation](../reviews/2026-09-15-I106-m3-derivation.md), batches 1 to 3), on Opus at the user's instruction in place of the Fable switch the loop requests for a route-deciding derivation. Contains [L227](second-order.md) as its case of two outer indices. The polynomial cores and the alignment to a frame are kernel-checked in [L228Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L228Statement.lean).*

**Setting.** $n\ge4$, $\lvert J\rvert=3$, outer set $A$ with $m=\lvert A\rvert=n-3$, and $W\in\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$ with blocks $M_a=t_aI+X_a$, $\operatorname{tr}X_a=0$, outer diagonal $K$ and $S=N-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}N)I$ as in [L226](second-order.md). Put $X=\sum_aX_a$, $\mu=\sum_at_a$ and $\beta_{ab}=\tfrac32\big(K_{ab}-t_a-t_b-\tfrac12\mu\big)$.

**Statement.**
1. *$P(W)=2\operatorname{tr}X^3+3\sum_a\operatorname{tr}(XX_a^2)+\sum_a\operatorname{tr}X_a^3+\sum_{a\ne b}\beta_{ab}\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_b)+P_0(t,K)$ and $\lVert W\rVert^2=\lvert X\rvert^2+\sum_a\lvert X_a\rvert^2+N_0(t,K)$, with $P_0,N_0$ depending on the scalars only.*
2. *For each $a$, with $R_a=\sum_{c\ne a}X_c$, $T_a=\sum_{c\ne a}X_c^2$ and $Y_a=X_a+\tfrac12R_a$: $P(W)=6\operatorname{tr}Y_a^3+\operatorname{tr}(Y_a\ell'_a)+c_a$ and $\lVert W\rVert^2=2\lvert Y_a\rvert^2+n_a$, where $\ell'_a=\tfrac32R_a^2+3T_a+2\sum_{c\ne a}\beta_{ac}X_c$ and $c_a,n_a$ do not depend on $X_a$.*
3. *If the blocks $X_c$, $c\ne a$, are simultaneously diagonal in one orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^J$, then $P(W)\le\Theta_n\lVert W\rVert^3$.*
4. *If the blocks $X_c$, $c\notin\{a,b\}$, and $X_a+X_b$ are simultaneously diagonal in one orthonormal frame, then $P(W)\le\Theta_n\lVert W\rVert^3$.*

*In items 3 and 4 the value $\Theta_n$ is attained in $\mathcal D_n\subseteq\mathcal S_J$. At $m\le2$ item 3 covers every $W$.*

**Proof.**
1. **Item 1.** With $c=\tfrac12\mu$, $S=X-cI$ since $\operatorname{tr}N=3\mu$. For traceless $3\times3$ $Z$, Cayley–Hamilton gives $\det(Z-cI)=\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}Z^3+\tfrac12c\lvert Z\rvert^2-c^3$ and $\operatorname{adj}(tI+Z)=Z^2-tZ+\big(t^2-\tfrac12\lvert Z\rvert^2\big)I$, so in L226's item 3: $\operatorname{tr}S^3+3\det S=2\operatorname{tr}X^3-\tfrac34\mu\lvert X\rvert^2-\tfrac34\mu^3$, $\operatorname{tr}M_a^3=3t_a^3+3t_a\lvert X_a\rvert^2+\operatorname{tr}X_a^3$, $3\operatorname{tr}(S\operatorname{adj}M_a)=3\operatorname{tr}(XX_a^2)-3t_a\operatorname{tr}(XX_a)+\tfrac34\mu\lvert X_a\rvert^2-\tfrac92\mu t_a^2$, and $3K_{ab}\operatorname{tr}(M_aM_b)=9K_{ab}t_at_b+3K_{ab}\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_b)$. With $\lvert X\rvert^2=\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_b)$ and $\operatorname{tr}(XX_a)=\sum_b\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_b)$, the coefficient of $\lvert X_a\rvert^2$ is $-\tfrac34\mu+3t_a-3t_a+\tfrac34\mu=0$ and that of $\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_b)$ over ordered pairs $a\ne b$ is $-\tfrac34\mu-\tfrac32(t_a+t_b)+\tfrac32K_{ab}=\beta_{ab}$. The norm is L226's $\lvert S\rvert^2+\sum\lvert M_a\rvert^2+\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^2$ with $\lvert S\rvert^2=\lvert X\rvert^2+\tfrac34\mu^2$ and $\lvert M_a\rvert^2=3t_a^2+\lvert X_a\rvert^2$.
2. **Item 2.** Fix everything but $X_a$. Expanding item 1 in $X_a$, $2\operatorname{tr}(X_a+R_a)^3$ contributes $2\operatorname{tr}X_a^3+6\operatorname{tr}(X_a^2R_a)+6\operatorname{tr}(X_aR_a^2)$, $3\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(XX_c^2)$ contributes $3\operatorname{tr}X_a^3+3\operatorname{tr}(X_a^2R_a)+3\operatorname{tr}(X_aT_a)$, $\sum_c\operatorname{tr}X_c^3$ contributes $\operatorname{tr}X_a^3$, and the Gram part $2\sum_{c\ne a}\beta_{ac}\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_c)$, each up to terms free of $X_a$. So $P=6\operatorname{tr}X_a^3+9\operatorname{tr}(X_a^2R_a)+\operatorname{tr}(X_a\ell_a)+\text{const}$ with $\ell_a=6R_a^2+3T_a+2\sum_{c\ne a}\beta_{ac}X_c$, and $\lVert W\rVert^2=2\lvert X_a\rvert^2+2\operatorname{tr}(X_aR_a)+\text{const}=2\lvert Y_a\rvert^2-\tfrac12\lvert R_a\rvert^2+\text{const}$. Substituting $X_a=Y_a-\tfrac12R_a$, cyclicity gives $6\operatorname{tr}(Y_a-\tfrac12R_a)^3+9\operatorname{tr}\big((Y_a-\tfrac12R_a)^2R_a\big)=6\operatorname{tr}Y_a^3-\tfrac92\operatorname{tr}(Y_aR_a^2)+\tfrac32\operatorname{tr}R_a^3$, **the two $\operatorname{tr}(Y_a^2R_a)$ terms cancelling**, and $\operatorname{tr}(X_a\ell_a)=\operatorname{tr}(Y_a\ell_a)-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(R_a\ell_a)$. Collecting, $\ell'_a=\ell_a-\tfrac92R_a^2$.
3. **Item 3.** Let $F$ be the common frame. $R_a$, $T_a$ and each $X_c$ with $c\ne a$ are diagonal in $F$, so $\ell'_a=F\operatorname{diag}(l)F^{\mathsf T}$. Write $Y_a=Q\operatorname{diag}(y)Q^{\mathsf T}$. Then $\operatorname{tr}(Y_a\ell'_a)=\sum_{i,k}(Q^{\mathsf T}F)_{ik}^2\,y_il_k$, the matrix of squared entries of the orthogonal $Q^{\mathsf T}F$ is doubly stochastic, and by Birkhoff's theorem the linear function is at most its value at a permutation matrix: $\operatorname{tr}(Y_a\ell'_a)\le\sum_iy_il_{\sigma(i)}$ for some permutation $\sigma$. Put $Y'=F\operatorname{diag}(y\circ\sigma^{-1})F^{\mathsf T}$. It has $Y_a$'s spectrum, so $\operatorname{tr}Y'=0$, $\lvert Y'\rvert=\lvert Y_a\rvert$ and $\operatorname{tr}Y'^3=\operatorname{tr}Y_a^3$, and $\operatorname{tr}(Y'\ell'_a)=\sum_ky_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}l_k\ge\operatorname{tr}(Y_a\ell'_a)$. Replace $X_a$ by $X_a'=Y'-\tfrac12R_a$, traceless and diagonal in $F$, keeping every other block and the scalars. The result $W'$ lies in $\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$, since the traceless blocks are free once the scalars satisfy L226's item 2, and by item 2 it has $\lVert W'\rVert=\lVert W\rVert$ and $P(W')\ge P(W)$. Every block of $W'$ and $S'=X'-\tfrac12\mu I$ are diagonal in $F$, the rotation of $\mathbb R^J$ by $F^{\mathsf T}$ conjugates them all (any orthogonal matrix does, $\Lambda^2F$ acting on the frame $\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31},\varphi_{12}$ by $(\det F)F$), and it carries $W'$ to a coordinate-diagonal Weyl tensor of the same norm and value, since $P$, the norm and $\mathcal W_n$ are $O(n)$-invariant. [L57](problem.md) gives $P(W)\le P(W')\le\Theta_n\lVert W\rVert^3$.
4. **Item 4.** Fix everything but $X_a,X_b$ and put $U=X_a+X_b$, $V=X_a-X_b$ with $U$ and $\lvert V\rvert$ fixed. Then $X$ is fixed and $\lvert X_a\rvert^2+\lvert X_b\rvert^2=\tfrac12(\lvert U\rvert^2+\lvert V\rvert^2)$, so the norm is fixed. In item 1, $3\operatorname{tr}\big(X(X_a^2+X_b^2)\big)=\tfrac32\operatorname{tr}\big(X(U^2+V^2)\big)$, $\operatorname{tr}X_a^3+\operatorname{tr}X_b^3=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}U^3+\tfrac34\operatorname{tr}(UV^2)$ by cyclicity, $\operatorname{tr}(X_aX_b)=\tfrac14(\lvert U\rvert^2-\lvert V\rvert^2)$, and the Gram terms with $c\notin\{a,b\}$ give $\sum_{c\ne a,b}\big((\beta_{ac}+\beta_{bc})\operatorname{tr}(UX_c)+(\beta_{ac}-\beta_{bc})\operatorname{tr}(VX_c)\big)$. So the value depends on the frame of $V$ only through $\operatorname{tr}(V^2Z)+\operatorname{tr}(VL)$ with $Z=\tfrac32X+\tfrac34U$ and $L=\sum_{c\ne a,b}(\beta_{ac}-\beta_{bc})X_c$, both diagonal in the common frame $F$. With $V=Q\operatorname{diag}(\nu)Q^{\mathsf T}$ this is $\sum_{i,k}(F^{\mathsf T}Q)_{ik}^2\big(z_i\nu_k^2+l_i\nu_k\big)$, linear in one doubly stochastic matrix, so a permutation does at least as well, and $V'=F\operatorname{diag}(\nu\circ\sigma^{-1})F^{\mathsf T}$ makes $X_a',X_b'$ diagonal in $F$ at the same norm and no smaller value. Item 3's last three sentences finish. $\square$

#### Lemma L226, the cubic on the block subalgebra $\mathcal S_J$: every coupling carries the coefficient $3$, and at $\lvert J\rvert=3$ Ricci-flatness fixes $R_J$ as $N-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}N)I$

*Gate 2 passed: [r948](../reviews/REVIEW_r948.md) and [r949](../reviews/REVIEW_r949.md) on identical briefs, every step CONFIRMED by both, with the definition of $\mathcal S_J$, three links of the chain and the scope of Gate 1 and of the Lean file repaired on their reports. Derived 2026-09-15 for [I106](../plan.md), the coefficients the [2026-09-14c sweep](../reviews/2026-09-14c-LIT-step8-sweep.md) wrote unverified, in the coordinates of the [block note](../reviews/2026-09-14-I104-block-J.md). The operator and the bridge to $P$ are typed in [L226Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L226Statement.lean).*

**Setting.** Fix $J\subseteq[n]$ with $\lvert J\rvert=k\ge2$, outer set $A=[n]\setminus J$ and $m=n-k$. Put $V_a=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{pa}:p\in J\}$, identified with $\mathbb R^J$ by $x\mapsto\sum_px_p\varphi_{pa}$, and for $x,y\in\mathbb R^J$ put $x\wedge y=\sum_{p,q}x_py_q\varphi_{pq}\in\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$. Here $\mathcal S_J$ is the fixed space of $H_J$ in the symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$. The block note's character argument uses no Weyl condition and gives the same block form on it, and the block note's $\mathcal S_J$ is $\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$ in this notation. By the block note, $W\in\mathcal S_J$ is block-diagonal on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n=\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A}V_a\oplus\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$: a symmetric $R_J$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, a symmetric $M_a$ on $V_a$ with $(M_a)_{pq}=W_{pa,qa}$, and $W\varphi_{ab}=K_{ab}\varphi_{ab}$. So $\lVert W\rVert^2=\lvert R_J\rvert^2+\sum_a\lvert M_a\rvert^2+\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^2$. $\Lambda^2M$ is the operator $x\wedge y\mapsto Mx\wedge My$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$.

**Statement.**
1. *For every $W\in\mathcal S_J$,* $$P(W)=P(R_J)+\sum_{a\in A}\Big(\operatorname{tr}M_a^3+3\operatorname{tr}\big(R_J\,\Lambda^2M_a\big)\Big)+\sum_{a<b}\Big(K_{ab}^3+3K_{ab}\operatorname{tr}(M_aM_b)\Big)+3\sum_{a<b<c}K_{ab}K_{ac}K_{bc},$$ *where $P(R_J)=\operatorname{tr}R_J^3+\langle R_J,R_J^\#\rangle$ is the cubic of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J=\mathfrak{so}(k)$.*
2. *$W\in\mathcal S_J$ lies in $\mathcal W_n$ exactly when $R_J$ satisfies the first Bianchi identity on $\mathbb R^J$, $\rho(R_J)+\sum_aM_a=0$ with $\rho(R_J)_{pq}=\sum_{r\in J}\langle R_J\varphi_{pr},\varphi_{qr}\rangle$, and $\operatorname{tr}M_a+\sum_{b\ne a}K_{ab}=0$ at every $a\in A$.*
3. *At $k=3$, write $R_J$ in the orthonormal frame $\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31},\varphi_{12}$ as a symmetric $S$. Then $P(R_J)=\operatorname{tr}S^3+3\det S$, $\operatorname{tr}(R_J\Lambda^2M)=\operatorname{tr}(S\operatorname{adj}M)$, $\rho(R_J)=(\operatorname{tr}S)I-S$, and Bianchi holds automatically. So on $\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$, with $N=\sum_aM_a$, $S=N-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}N)I$ and* $$P(W)=\operatorname{tr}S^3+3\det S+\sum_a\Big(\operatorname{tr}M_a^3+3\operatorname{tr}(S\operatorname{adj}M_a)\Big)+\sum_{a<b}\Big(K_{ab}^3+3K_{ab}\operatorname{tr}(M_aM_b)\Big)+3\sum_{a<b<c}K_{ab}K_{ac}K_{bc}.$$

**Proof.**
1. **$P$ in an eigenbasis.** Let $v_\alpha$ be an orthonormal eigenbasis of $W$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_\alpha$, and $c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle[v_\alpha,v_\beta],v_\gamma\rangle$, totally antisymmetric. The index formula of [conventions.md](conventions.md), $(W^\#)_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12\sum c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}c_{\beta\epsilon\zeta}W_{\gamma\epsilon}W_{\delta\zeta}$, gives $\langle W,W^\#\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^2$ over ordered triples, and $\operatorname{tr}W^3=\sum\lambda_\alpha^3$. *At $W=\mathrm{id}$ the first is $\tfrac12\cdot2(n-2)\dim\Lambda^2$, which is $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$.* $W\in\mathcal S_J$ is block-diagonal, so the eigenbasis is chosen block by block, taking $\varphi_{ab}$ itself as the eigenvector on each line $\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$ of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$.
2. **Brackets between blocks.** From $[\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{cd}]=\delta_{ac}\varphi_{bd}+\delta_{bd}\varphi_{ac}-\delta_{ad}\varphi_{bc}-\delta_{bc}\varphi_{ad}$, for distinct $p,q\in J$ and distinct $a,b,c\in A$: $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{qa}]=\varphi_{pq}$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{pb}]=\varphi_{ab}$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{qb}]=0$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{ab}]=-\varphi_{pb}$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{bc}]=0$, $[\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{ac}]=\varphi_{bc}$, $[\varphi_{pq},\varphi_{ra}]=\delta_{pr}\varphi_{qa}-\delta_{qr}\varphi_{pa}$, $[\varphi_{pq},\varphi_{rs}]\in\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, and $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ commutes with $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$. So $[V_a,V_a]\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ with $[x,y]=x\wedge y$, $[V_a,V_b]=\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$ with $[x,y]=\langle x,y\rangle\varphi_{ab}$, $[\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J,V_a]\subseteq V_a$, $[V_a,\varphi_{ab}]\subseteq V_b$, and $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ and $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$ are subalgebras. Hence $c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\ne0$ only when the three blocks are, up to order, three copies of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, one of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ with two of one $V_a$, the three blocks $V_a,V_b,\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$, or three pairs of $A$ forming a triangle.
3. **The four sums.** *Three copies of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$*: the structure constants are those of $\mathfrak{so}(k)$, so these triples give $\langle R_J,R_J^\#\rangle$ and, with $\operatorname{tr}R_J^3$, $P(R_J)$. *$\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ with two of $V_a$*: the one $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ vector takes $3$ positions in an ordered triple. For orthonormal eigenvectors $v_\beta,v_\gamma$ of $M_a$, $v_\beta\wedge v_\gamma$ is an eigenvector of $\Lambda^2M_a$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma$, and since $\langle x\wedge y,u\wedge v\rangle=\langle x,u\rangle\langle y,v\rangle-\langle x,v\rangle\langle y,u\rangle$ these form an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ over $\beta<\gamma$, so for a unit eigenvector $\omega$ of $R_J$ with eigenvalue $\mu$, $\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma\langle v_\beta\wedge v_\gamma,\omega\rangle^2=2\langle\Lambda^2M_a\,\omega,\omega\rangle$. Summing, $\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot2\sum_\omega\mu\langle\Lambda^2M_a\omega,\omega\rangle=3\operatorname{tr}(R_J\Lambda^2M_a)$. *$V_a,V_b,\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$*: $6$ orders, and $\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\lambda_\beta(M_a)\lambda_\gamma(M_b)\langle v_\beta,v_\gamma\rangle^2=\operatorname{tr}(M_aM_b)$, giving $\tfrac12\cdot6\,K_{ab}\operatorname{tr}(M_aM_b)$. *Triangles in $A$*: $6$ orders and $c^2=1$, giving $3K_{ab}K_{ac}K_{bc}$. With $\operatorname{tr}W^3=\operatorname{tr}R_J^3+\sum_a\operatorname{tr}M_a^3+\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^3$ this is item 1.
4. **The Weyl conditions.** $W$ is symmetric and preserves $\Lambda^2$ by construction. An entry $W_{ij,kl}$ can be nonzero only when its index multiset lies in $J$, is $\{p,q,a,a\}$, or is $\{a,b,a,b\}$. A Bianchi sum with a repeated index follows from the pair symmetries, and one over four distinct indices not all in $J$ meets only entries of none of these shapes, so Bianchi reduces to $R_J$'s. For Ricci, $\operatorname{Ric}_{pq}=\rho(R_J)_{pq}+\sum_a(M_a)_{pq}$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_{aa}=\operatorname{tr}M_a+\sum_{b\ne a}K_{ab}$, while $\operatorname{Ric}_{pa}$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}$ pair vectors of different blocks and vanish. This is item 2.
5. **$k=3$.** The frame $\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31},\varphi_{12}\mapsto e_1,e_2,e_3$ carries $x\wedge y$ to $x\times y$, and it carries the bracket of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ to the cross product, since the bracket formula gives $[\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31}]=\varphi_{12}$ and its cyclic images. So every distinct orthonormal triple has $c^2=1$, and $\langle R_J,R_J^\#\rangle=\tfrac12\cdot6\det S$. The cofactor identity $Mx\times My=\operatorname{adj}(M)(x\times y)$ for symmetric $M$ makes $\Lambda^2M$ the adjugate. Entry by entry, $\rho_{11}=S_{22}+S_{33}$ and $\rho_{12}=-S_{12}$, so $\rho(S)=(\operatorname{tr}S)I-S$. Every symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^3$ satisfies Bianchi, since four indices from a set of three repeat one and step 4's repeated-index case applies. Taking traces in $(\operatorname{tr}S)I-S=-N$ gives $\operatorname{tr}S=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}N$, hence $S=N-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}N)I$. $\square$

Let $\mathcal D_n=\{S\ \text{symmetric, hollow},\ S\mathbf1=0\}$ be the vertex matrices of coordinate-diagonal Weyl operators. Then
\[
\langle Q(W),W\rangle\;\le\;\Theta_n\,\|W\|^3\qquad\text{for every diagonal }W\text{ at every }n\ge4,
\]
\[
\Theta_n^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-2)}{n^2}\ \ (n\text{ even}),\qquad
\Theta_n^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-3)}{(n+1)(n-2)}\ \ (n\text{ odd}),
\]
with equality exactly at $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$ for even $n$ and at $S^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\times S^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$ for odd $n$, at the *geometric* Weyl tensor of that product, which is $-V/\lVert V\rVert$ under the record's $V$ convention. At even $n$, $\Theta_n=\theta_0(n)$.
