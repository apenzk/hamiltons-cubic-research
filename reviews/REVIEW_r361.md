## Method, and the two screens

I derived the multiplier calculus from [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s reduced rows before opening [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md), then used that note only as an independent check on the algebra. Two screens were run, both seconds of symbolic or set arithmetic on objects built for the purpose, both stated with their falsifier first. The first recomputed all eighteen right-hand sides and all eighteen survivor sets from $(\mathrm A_i),(\mathrm B_i),(\mathrm C_i)$ in sympy, falsifier: any mismatch against my closed forms would have withdrawn S1 and S2 outright; it returned zero mismatches, and it would have caught any coefficient slip, since the check is against the row expressions and not against my formulas. The second enumerated the $\binom{10}4=210$ four-subsets and applied forbidden sets, falsifier: any figure differing from my hand count would have meant the hand count was wrong; every figure below is reproduced by both.

## S1, the two right-hand sides, derived from the rows

**One line covers both families, and it is worth writing down because the classification then reads off it.** Group the reduced rows by unordered pair: with $a$ in class $i$ and $\mu_j=m_j-\delta_{ij}$, set $c_{jk}=2\mu_j\mu_k t_{ijk}$ for $j<k$ and $c_{jj}=\mu_j(\mu_j-1)t_{ijj}$. Then $(\mathrm A_i)$ reads $\sum_{j\le k}c_{jk}=2(n-2)$, $(\mathrm B_i)$ reads $\sum_{j\le k}c_{jk}\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2=-2\nu_i$, the $(\nu_j+\nu_k)$ against the $2$ is exactly the two orders of a mixed pair, so the halving is right, and $(\mathrm C_i)$ reads $\sum_{j\le k}c_{jk}\nu_j\nu_k=2\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)$. For $\varphi(x)=\alpha x^2+\beta x+\gamma$ with polarisation $\psi(x,y)=\alpha xy+\beta\tfrac{x+y}2+\gamma$, the combination $\gamma(\mathrm A_i)+\beta(\mathrm B_i)+\alpha(\mathrm C_i)$ is

$$\sum_{j\le k}c_{jk}\,\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)\;=\;2(n-2)\gamma-2\beta\nu_i+2\alpha\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i).$$

**The double-root form is immediate and matches.** $\varphi(x)=(x-\nu_r)^2$ gives $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(1,-2\nu_r,\nu_r^2)$, so the right-hand side is $2(n-2)\nu_r^2+4\nu_r\nu_i+2\nu_i\theta-2\nu_i^2$, i.e. $\mathrm{rhs}/2=(n-2)\nu_r^2-\nu_i^2+2\nu_i\nu_r+\nu_i\theta$ as claimed, and $\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)=(\nu_j-\nu_r)(\nu_k-\nu_r)$ vanishes iff $j=r$ or $k=r$, leaving the three types $\{i,j,k\}$ with $j,k\ne r$. CONFIRMED.

**The four-type form is right only when the row index is one of the two roots, and that scope is missing from the reconciliation table.** $\varphi(x)=(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$ gives $\mathrm{rhs}=2(n-2)\nu_a\nu_b+2(\nu_a+\nu_b)\nu_i+2\nu_i\theta-2\nu_i^2$; setting $i=a$ and $j=b$ collapses this to $2\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_j+\theta]$, while for $i\notin\{a,b\}$ it stays the genuine quadratic $2[(n-2)\nu_a\nu_b-\nu_i^2+\nu_i(\nu_a+\nu_b+\theta)]$. So the family has $3\times3=9$ members: six clean and three quadratic. [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) lists all nine and my forms agree with every row of its table, including the three quadratics; [bounds-relaxations.md](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s reconciliation table says "four-type, six" and silently drops the other three. They change no count below (each of the three involves $\nu_2$ inseparably) but the table should say it is classifying a sub-family rather than the family.

## S2, the classification, recounted from the definitions

**The criterion is nonvanishing, and this is where the table goes wrong.** The identity is $\sum c_{jk}\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)=\mathrm{rhs}$ over the surviving types; if all of them vanish the left side is $0$, so a *nonzero* right-hand side forbids that type-set. [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) states this explicitly ("a nonzero right-hand side forbids that four-subset outright, whatever the coefficient signs") and states the corollary too: $\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_j+\theta]=0$ needs either $\nu_i=0$, excluded by kernel-freeness, or $\theta=-(n-1)\nu_j$, which needs $\nu_j<0$. The reconciliation table nonetheless classifies by *sign*, which is a strictly stronger demand, and that is what put the wrong identities in the $\nu_2$ column.

**The four-type six, recounted.** Writing the identity as $(i,j)$ with $\varphi=(x-\nu_i)(x-\nu_j)$ at row $i$:

| $(i,j)$ | right-hand side $/2$ | forbidden four-subset | what it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(2,1)$ | $\nu_2[(n-1)\nu_1+\theta]$ | $\{122,123,223,233\}$ | **nothing**, $\nu_2\ne0$ by kernel-freeness, bracket $>0$ since $\nu_1>0<\theta$ |
| $(3,1)$ | $\nu_3[(n-1)\nu_1+\theta]$ | $\{123,133,223,233\}$ | **nothing**, the record's one free member |
| $(1,3)$ | $\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_3+\theta]$ | $\{112,113,122,123\}$ | $\Pi$ strict |
| $(2,3)$ | $\nu_2[(n-1)\nu_3+\theta]$ | $\{112,122,123,223\}$ | $\Pi$ strict |
| $(1,2)$ | $\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]$ | $\{112,113,123,133\}$ | $\nu_2>0$, or second-$\Pi$-strictness |
| $(3,2)$ | $\nu_3[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]$ | $\{113,123,133,233\}$ | $\nu_2>0$, or second-$\Pi$-strictness |

**So the split is $2/2/2$ and not $1/1/4$, and the pattern is legible: the bracket $(n-1)\nu_j+\theta$ knows only about $j$.** At $j=1$ it is a sum of positives, so both rows are free; at $j=3$ it vanishes exactly at $\Pi$-saturation, so both rows need $\Pi$ strict; at $j=2$ it vanishes exactly on the locus $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$ with $\nu_2<0$, so both rows need the sign or the non-degeneracy. The factor $\nu_i$ is never the obstruction, because kernel-freeness kills every case of it. **The record's demotion of $(2,1)$ to the $\nu_2$ column is the error, and $(2,3)$ is mis-filed with it: it is a $\Pi$-strictness identity that happens to carry a $\nu_2$ factor.**

**The double-root nine: the partition is right, the column label is not.** The right-hand side is $D(i,r)=(n-2)\nu_r^2-\nu_i^2+2\nu_i\nu_r+\nu_i\theta$, and on the diagonal it collapses to $\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta]$, the same shape with $j=i$, so $(1,1)$ is free, $(2,2)$ needs $\nu_2$, $(3,3)$ needs $\Pi$ strict, exactly as recorded. Off the diagonal, only $(1,3)$ and $(3,1)$ avoid $\nu_2$ altogether, and both of the record's arguments for them check out: $D(1,3)\ge[(n-2)-\rho_1^2-2\rho_1]\lvert\nu_3\rvert^2$ after dropping $\nu_1\theta\ge0$, positive iff $(\rho_1+1)^2<n-1$; and $D(3,1)\ge(\rho_1+1)[(n-2)\rho_1-n]\lvert\nu_3\rvert^2$ after substituting $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert$, the substitution is in the right direction, since $\nu_3<0$ makes $D(3,1)$ decreasing in $\theta$, positive iff $\rho_1>n/(n-2)$. The dichotomy at $n\ge7$ is also right: $\sqrt{n-1}-1$ increases, $n/(n-2)$ decreases, and they cross between $n=6$ ($1.2361<1.5$, a genuine gap) and $n=7$ ($1.4495>1.4$). So $3$ free-listed, $1$ needing $\Pi$, $5$ left. CONFIRMED as a partition.

**But the label on that column of five is false for three of them.** $\nu_2>0$ makes $D(2,2)$ and $D(2,1)=(n-2)\nu_1^2+\nu_2(2\nu_1-\nu_2+\theta)$ strictly positive, and nothing more. Each of $D(1,2)$, $D(2,3)$, $D(3,2)$ has a vanishing locus with $\nu_2>0$ strictly inside the $\Pi$-bound: at $\lvert\nu_3\rvert=1$, $D(1,2)=0$ at $n=12,\nu_1=1,\nu_2=0.1,\theta=0.7$; $D(2,3)=0$ at $n=7,\nu_1=3,\nu_2=2.5,\theta=2.5$ against $\Pi=6$; $D(3,2)=0$ at $n=12,\nu_2=0.5,\theta=0.5$. *These are points of the $(\nu,\theta)$ parameter space, not exhibited Weyl tensors, but the classification lives in that same space, so the comparison is the fair one.* **The honest column heading is "not shown nonzero", and only two of the five are actually bought by the sign of $\nu_2$.** That is what breaks the $173$ figure below.

**The pair cut is not free, CONFIRMED, and for a reason stronger than the table gives.** [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md)'s $28$-subset cut is a *signed* argument: the surviving coefficients of the $(1,2)$ identity have mixed signs, and moving the two negative terms across needs $\mathrm{rhs}>0$, hence $\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]>0$, hence $\nu_2>0$. Nonvanishing is not enough for it, so second-$\Pi$-strictness recovers the four-subset cut and not the pair cut. The record's statement that the pair cut "is $\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]$ and needs $\nu_2$" is right, and understates the gap.

## S3, the chain, recounted

**Hypothesis-free set, from the two tables above:** the four-type $(2,1)$ and $(3,1)$, each forbidding one four-subset; the double-root $(1,1)$, forbidding the three-subset $\{122,123,133\}$; and at $n\ge7$ exactly one of the double-root $(3,1)$, forbidding $\{223,233,333\}$, and $(1,3)$, forbidding $\{111,112,122\}$. A forbidden three-subset kills the $10-3=7$ four-subsets containing it; a forbidden four-subset kills itself.

**The overlaps are all empty, and the arithmetic is $210-1-1-7-7=194$.** The two four-subsets differ in one element and neither contains any of the three-subsets; and any four-subset containing two of the three-subsets would have to contain their union, which has five or six elements in every pair that arises. Both $\rho_1$ branches give $194$.

$$210\ \xrightarrow{\ (3,1)_{4\mathrm t}\ }\ 209\ \xrightarrow{\ (2,1)_{4\mathrm t}\ }\ 208\ \xrightarrow{\ (1,1)_{\mathrm{dr}}\ }\ 201\ \xrightarrow{\ (3,1)_{\mathrm{dr}}\ \text{or}\ (1,3)_{\mathrm{dr}}\ }\ \mathbf{194}$$

**The recorded $195$ is reproduced exactly by the recorded free set**, which is the same chain with the $(2,1)$ four-type identity omitted, so the discrepancy is isolated to that one identity and to nothing else. **This is the third figure at this table**, $192$ counted $\nu_2$-dependent identities as free, $201$ used a wrong free set, $195$ under-counts the free set by one, and all three failures are the same failure, classifying by sign where the mechanism only asks for nonvanishing.

**The conditional figures do not survive either, and they fail in opposite directions.** Under $\nu_2>0$ the free set gains the double-root $(2,2)$ and $(2,1)$ and the four-type $(1,2)$ and $(3,2)$ (four, not the nine the table's columns promise) and the enumeration returns $\mathbf{179}$ at $\rho_1>n/(n-2)$ and $178$ at $\rho_1<\sqrt{n-1}-1$, so the guaranteed figure is $179$, not $173$. Taking the record's own claimed $\nu_2>0$ set at face value (its eight double roots and five four-types), returns $152$, so $173$ is reproduced from neither its own classification nor the corrected one, and I cannot reconstruct where it came from. **And $130$ is unreachable: taking all eighteen identities of both families as nonzero, quadratics included, floors the count at $141$.** So the $130$ row must be drawing on something outside the identity family (the Jacobi filter's $121$, or the $28$-subset pair cuts) and the chain's presentation says otherwise. *That the pair cuts are omitted from every figure is worth noting on its own: under $\nu_2>0$ the $(2,1)$ four-type identity supports a second pair cut by the same signed argument, "$t_{223}$ and $t_{233}$ do not both vanish", and adding both pair cuts to the corrected $\nu_2>0$ set brings $179$ down to $150$.*

## S4, the branch

**The sign is genuinely undetermined, CONFIRMED.** Tracelessness $\sum_im_i\nu_i=0$ with $\nu_1>\nu_2>\nu_3$ forces $\nu_1>0>\nu_3$ and says nothing about $\nu_2$; [Lemma L35](../findings/stratum-two-negative.md) applies only at two or fewer negative eigenvalues, and $\nu_2<0$ with $m_2,m_3\ge3$ leaves at least six, so it does not reach. I checked the two other candidate obstructions and neither bites: the row measure of $(\mathrm A_i),(\mathrm B_i)$ at a class-$3$ row asks only $\lvert\nu_3\rvert\le(n-2)\nu_1$, and $\lambda$ being a $-\tfrac1{n-2}$-eigenvector of a symmetric hollow doubly stochastic matrix ([Lemma L112](../findings/bounds.md)) constrains nothing about a middle sign. **Whether the $\nu_2<0$ stratum is LP-feasible at $d=3$ is a computation, and the analysis-only rule blocks it here.** *What it would decide:* infeasibility would remove the branch and restore the $\nu_2>0$ chain unconditionally; feasibility would make the branch permanent and the repair below load-bearing. *It cannot be inferred from anything the record holds.*

**"Exactly one identity" is true only inside the diagonal-only scope the section sets, and false for the classification as a whole.** Comparing the two branches over all eighteen identities, the negative branch loses four: the double-root $(2,2)$ and $(2,1)$, and the four-type $(1,2)$ and $(3,2)$. The loss of the double-root $(2,1)$ is the one the section misses, and it is the expensive one, a three-subset, $\{222,223,233\}$, worth seven four-subsets against the four-type pair's one apiece.

**And something else flips that the section does not mention: the direction of the pair cut.** On the positive branch $\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]>0$ gives "$t_{123}$ and $t_{133}$ do not both vanish". On the negative branch that quantity is $\nu_1[\theta-(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert]$, whose sign is unknown even under second-$\Pi$-strictness; if $\theta<(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$ the inequality reverses and the cut becomes "$t_{112}$ and $t_{113}$ do not both vanish" instead. So the branch-independent content of that identity is only its four-subset, which is what the $194$ count already uses.

## S5, the repair

**Second-$\Pi$-strictness does not make the branches agree, because it does not touch the double-root $(2,1)$.** Its right-hand side on the negative branch is $(n-2)\nu_1^2-\nu_2^2-2\nu_1\lvert\nu_2\rvert-\lvert\nu_2\rvert\theta$, which has a vanishing locus nowhere near $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$, it is a genuinely different hypersurface, and no non-degeneracy condition stated at the $(2,2)$ locus can exclude it. With the repair in force the positive branch carries eight free identities and the negative branch seven, so the branches agree on the diagonal three and disagree overall. **The repair is cheaper than advertised because it buys less than advertised.**

**"Lemma L71 characterises $\Pi$-saturation" is false as stated.** [Lemma L71](../findings/bounds.md) characterises saturation of [Lemma L70](../findings/bounds.md), which is the scalar equation $\theta=\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}$, a different condition from $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$. [Reviewer 323](REVIEW_r323.md)'s sharpening, as [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) records it, is that L70-saturation is equivalent to $\Pi$-saturation **together with** the ratio $\lambda_{\max}/\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert=n/(n-2)$, so $\Pi$-saturation alone is strictly weaker than what L71 classifies, and L71 cannot decide it.

**And nothing transfers to the middle eigenvalue, because there is no second $\Pi$-bound to be strict about.** [Lemma L107](../findings/bounds.md) is the $\lambda_{\max}$ row read against the pointwise inequality $(X-\lambda_{\min})(\lambda_{\max}-X)\ge0$, with $X=\tfrac{\lambda_b+\lambda_c}2$ ranging over the row's support. Replacing $\lambda_{\min}$ by $\nu_2$ needs $X\ge\nu_2$ on the support, which fails on any pair inside class $3$, where $X=\nu_3<\nu_2$. So $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$ is not a theorem and is not even the right shape of statement; $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$ is a locus where one identity degenerates and carries no saturation theory at all. **The name "second-$\Pi$-strictness" imports a legitimacy the object does not have, and should be dropped for something like "the $\nu_2$ degeneracy locus".**

**On legitimacy: PLAUSIBLE as a hypothesis, useless as a step in a bound.** It does not secretly exclude the cases of interest, with $\nu_2<0$ one has $\lvert\nu_2\rvert<\lvert\nu_3\rvert$, so the locus sits strictly inside the $\Pi$-bound and is a genuine hypersurface in the interior rather than a boundary in disguise, and the target maximiser has $d=2$ and is outside this class entirely. But a conclusion proved off a hypersurface of the $(\nu,\theta)$ space is not a bound on $\theta$, since the supremum may sit on it, and unlike $\Pi$-saturation, where [Lemma L71](../findings/bounds.md) and [Lemma L117](../findings/bounds.md) tell the record exactly which tensors saturate, nothing here identifies what lives on the $\nu_2$ locus. **The repair converts an unproved sign assumption into an unproved non-degeneracy assumption, which is progress in honesty and not in content.**

## What I would change in the record

**Three edits, in order of cost.** *(i)* Correct the reconciliation table's four-type row to $2$ free / $2$ needing $\Pi$ strict / $2$ needing $\nu_2$, name $(2,1)$ alongside $(3,1)$ as free, and correct the chain to $194$. *(ii)* Relabel the double-root table's middle column "not shown nonzero", since only two of its five are bought by $\nu_2>0$, and withdraw $173$ in favour of $179$ and $130$ as unreconciled, $141$ is the family's floor. *(iii)* Withdraw the claim that second-$\Pi$-strictness makes the branches agree, and the claim that [Lemma L71](../findings/bounds.md) characterises $\Pi$-saturation. **The classification should be stated by the bracket rather than by the identity**: for the nine identities of the form $\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_j+\theta]$ (the six clean four-type ones and the three diagonal double roots), the hypothesis is a function of $j$ alone, and $\nu_i$ never obstructs because kernel-freeness has already handled it. Stated that way the table cannot be miscounted a fourth time.
