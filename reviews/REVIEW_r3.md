**1. Petersen–Wink formulas, all CONFIRMED, word-for-word/formula-for-formula.** The curvature-operator formula $R_{\mathbb{HP}^m}(X\wedge Y)=X\wedge Y+IX\wedge IY+JX\wedge JY+KX\wedge KY+2g(X\wedge Y,\omega_I)\omega_I+2g(X\wedge Y,\omega_J)\omega_J+2g(X\wedge Y,\omega_K)\omega_K$, the stated eigenvalues $4m$ (on the 3-dimensional $\mathfrak{sp}(1)$-eigenspace $\{\omega_I,\omega_J,\omega_K\}$) and $4$ (on the $m(2m+1)$-dimensional $\mathfrak{sp}(m)$-eigenspace), $|R_{\mathbb{HP}^m}|^2=16m(5m+1)$, and $\mathrm{scal}(R_{\mathbb{HP}^m})=16m(m+2)$, all appear verbatim in Remark 4.1 of the PDF, exactly as FINDINGS.md quotes them. Corollary 4.5's norm relation $|R^{\mathfrak{sp}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(1)}|^2=\tfrac43(3m+4)|R^0|^2$ also appears verbatim. **CONFIRMED.**

**2. Ishihara/Besse formula, PLAUSIBLE-BUT-UNVERIFIED against the primary source, but internally self-consistent.** Ishihara (1974) and Besse Ch. 14 are not freely accessible; a web search surfaced only a secondary paraphrase of Ishihara's operator-form curvature formula, $R_{XY}Z=\tfrac{c}{4}[\langle Y,Z\rangle X-\langle X,Z\rangle Y+\sum_A(\langle AY,Z\rangle AX-\langle AX,Z\rangle AY)+2\langle X,AY\rangle AZ]$. Converting this to the 4-tensor index convention used by [scripts/run_hpm_qk.py](scripts/run_hpm_qk.py) gives a "$2A_{ij}A_{kl}$"-type cross term with the *opposite sign* from what FINDINGS.md's $T(i,j,k,l)$ formula states. This is almost certainly an artifact of a differing overall curvature-tensor sign/index-order convention between sources (extremely common in this literature, e.g. $R(X,Y,Z,W):=\langle R_{XY}Z,W\rangle$ vs. $\langle R_{XY}W,Z\rangle$), not a substantive error: the formula as implemented in the codebase passes every internal consistency check available (Bianchi identity exactly zero, Einstein constant exactly $4(m+2)$, sectional-curvature pinching ratio exactly $4.0$, see point 3) and independently matches, to machine precision, a Weyl tensor built via the entirely different Kulkarni–Nomizu route and cross-validated against the confirmed-correct Petersen–Wink operator formula (FINDINGS.md's $\cos(\text{angle})=1.0000000000$ claim, not independently re-checked by this reviewer but consistent with everything re-derived here). Flagged as unverified against the primary source rather than confirmed, since only a secondary paraphrase was checked.

**3. Pinching-ratio calibration ($c_1{:}c_2$), CONFIRMED both analytically and numerically, and shown to be irrelevant to the final numerical claim.** Hand-derived $T(0,1,1,0)=-(c_1+3c_2)$ (quaternionic line, $e_1=Ie_0$) and $T(0,4,4,0)=-c_1$ (totally real plane, different $\mathbb H$-block) directly from the stated formula: $K_{\text{quat}}/K_{\text{real}}=(c_1+3c_2)/c_1=4\iff c_1=c_2$, exactly matching the ratio required by the standard $[c/4,c]$ quaternionic pinching definition. This independent hand derivation (not just a numerical re-check) confirms the calibration is correct, with no sign or factor-of-2 error. **More importantly, a new finding not noted in FINDINGS.md**: scanning $c_1\in[0.5,2.0]$ at fixed $c_2=1$ (script: `adversarial_check4_qk.py`, run interactively) shows $|W|$ and $P/\|W\|^3$ are **exactly, bit-for-bit unchanged** across the whole range (six agreeing digits at every sampled point), because the $c_1(g\otimes g)$ term is itself Weyl-flat (round sphere) and Weyl decomposition is linear, so $\mathrm{Weyl}(T)=\mathrm{Weyl}(c_2\Omega)$ is exactly independent of $c_1$. Scanning $c_2\in\{0.5,1,1.5,2,-1\}$ at fixed $c_1=1$ shows $|W|$ scales linearly with $|c_2|$ while $P/\|W\|^3$ stays exactly $\pm1.115547$ (sign flips only for $c_2<0$). **Consequence: the entire $c_1{:}c_2$ calibration debate in FINDINGS.md's "direct construction" section (including the documented dead-end and the pinching-ratio fix), is provably immaterial to the headline number.** The QK Weyl tensor's shape (hence $P/\|W\|^3$) is determined entirely by the $I,J,K$ structure itself, not by the ratio used to build the ambient Einstein tensor around it. This makes the numeric conclusion considerably more robust than the calibration discussion suggests, even a wrong calibration could not have produced a different $P/\|W\|^3$, only a formally non-QK (but still Weyl-equivalent) Einstein tensor.

**6. Qualitative/structural sanity check, PLAUSIBLE-BUT-UNVERIFIED, not independently resolved.** Attempted to use Corollary 4.5's norm relation ($|R^{\mathfrak{sp}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(1)}|^2=\tfrac43(3m+4)|R^0|^2$) together with the Fox idempotent-norm framework ($P=1/|x|$ for an idempotent, so larger norm $\Rightarrow$ smaller $P$) to get an independent, non-numerical read on why QK should lose to the sphere product.
