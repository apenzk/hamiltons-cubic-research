## Batch 1, one block against the rest: the quadratic term cancels

Fix every block but $X_a$ and the scalars, and put $R=\sum_{c\ne a}X_c$, $T=\sum_{c\ne a}X_c^2$. Expanding in $X_a$:
- $2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_a+R)^3=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_a^3+6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_a^2R)+6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aR^2)+\text{const}$;
- $3\sum_c\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XX_c^2)=3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_a^3+3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_a^2R)+3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aT)+\text{const}$, the $c=a$ term giving the first two;
- $\sum_c\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_c^3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_a^3+\text{const}$, and the Gram part is $2\sum_{c\ne a}\beta_{ac}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aX_c)+\text{const}$.

So $P=6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_a^3+9\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_a^2R)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_a\ell)+\text{const}$ with $\ell=6R^2+3T+2\sum_{c\ne a}\beta_{ac}X_c$, and $\lVert W\rVert^2=2\lvert X_a\rvert^2+2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aR)+\text{const}$. **Complete the square with $Y=X_a+\tfrac12R$**: $\lVert W\rVert^2=2\lvert Y\rvert^2+\text{const}$, and substituting $X_a=Y-\tfrac12R$,
$$6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(Y-\tfrac R2\big)^3+9\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Big(\big(Y-\tfrac R2\big)^2R\Big)=6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Y^3+(-9+9)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y^2R)+\big(\tfrac92-9\big)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(YR^2)+\text{const},$$
so $$P=6\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Y^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y\ell')+c_a,\qquad\ell'=\tfrac32R^2+3T+2\sum_{c\ne a}\beta_{ac}X_c,\qquad\lVert W\rVert^2=2\lvert Y\rvert^2+n_a .$$
**The quadratic term in the moving block vanishes identically.** Along one block the value is L225's per-block cubic plus a linear pairing with a matrix built from the other blocks alone, against a norm that sees only $\lvert Y\rvert$.

## Batch 2, the stratum where all blocks but one share a frame

**Claim.** *If $X_c$, $c\ne a$, are simultaneously diagonal in an orthonormal frame $F$ of $\mathbb R^J$, then $P(W)\le\Theta_n\lVert W\rVert^3$, and the bound is attained at commuting blocks.*

**Proof.** $R$, $T$ and every $X_c$ ($c\ne a$) are diagonal in $F$, so $\ell'$ is. Let $\ell'=F\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(l)F^{\mathsf T}$ and $Y=Q\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(y)Q^{\mathsf T}$. Then $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y\ell')=\sum_{i,k}(F^{\mathsf T}Q)_{ik}^2\,l_iy_k$, the matrix $\big((F^{\mathsf T}Q)_{ik}^2\big)$ is doubly stochastic, and by Birkhoff's theorem (the step `le_perm_pairing` of [L227Alignment.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L227Alignment.lean)) there is a permutation $\sigma$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y\ell')\le\sum_il_iy_{\sigma(i)}$. Put $Y'=F\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(y\circ\sigma)F^{\mathsf T}$: same spectrum, so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Y'^3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Y^3$ and $\lvert Y'\rvert=\lvert Y\rvert$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y'\ell')=\sum_il_iy_{\sigma(i)}\ge\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y\ell')$. With the scalars and the other blocks unchanged, $X_a'=Y'-\tfrac12R$ is traceless, diagonal in $F$, and defines $W'\in\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$ with $\lVert W'\rVert=\lVert W\rVert$ and $P(W')\ge P(W)$. Every block of $W'$ and $S'=X'-\tfrac12\mu I$ are diagonal in $F$, so the rotation of $\mathbb R^J$ by $F^{\mathsf T}$ carries $W'$ to a coordinate-diagonal Weyl tensor of the same norm and value, and [L57](../findings/problem.md) gives $P(W)\le P(W')\le\Theta_n\lVert W\rVert^3$. Attainment is L57's maximiser, which lies in $\mathcal D_n\subseteq\mathcal S_J$. $\square$

**At $m=2$ every configuration is in this stratum**, one block being trivially simultaneously diagonal, so batch 2 is a second proof of [L227](../findings/second-order.md), and a shorter one: the change of variables $U=X_1\pm X_2$ is replaced by the shift $Y=X_1+\tfrac12X_2$, and $\ell'=\tfrac92X_2^2+2\beta_{12}X_2$ is a polynomial in $X_2$. *At $m=2$ the two proofs are the same move seen from two variables.*

## Batch 3, the stratum where the rest and one pair's sum share a frame

**Claim.** *If $X_c$ for $c\notin\{a,b\}$ and $U=X_a+X_b$ are simultaneously diagonal in $F$, the same conclusion holds.*

**Proof.** With $V=X_a-X_b$ and everything else fixed, $X$ is fixed and $\lvert X_a\rvert^2+\lvert X_b\rvert^2=\tfrac12(\lvert U\rvert^2+\lvert V\rvert^2)$, so the norm depends on $V$ only through $\lvert V\rvert$. The value depends on the frame of $V$ only through $$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(V^2Z)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VL),\qquad Z=\tfrac32X+\tfrac34U,\qquad L=\sum_{c\ne a,b}(\beta_{ac}-\beta_{bc})X_c,$$ from $3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(X(X_a^2+X_b^2)\big)=\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(X(U^2+V^2)\big)$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_a^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_b^3=\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac34\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aX_b)=\tfrac14(\lvert U\rvert^2-\lvert V\rvert^2)$, and the Gram terms $2\sum_{c\ne a,b}\big(\beta_{ac}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aX_c)+\beta_{bc}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_bX_c)\big)$. Both $Z$ and $L$ are diagonal in $F$, so with $V=Q\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu)Q^{\mathsf T}$ and $s_{ik}=(F^{\mathsf T}Q)_{ik}^2$ the frame-dependent part is $\sum_{i,k}s_{ik}\big(z_i\nu_k^2+l_i\nu_k\big)$, linear in one doubly stochastic matrix, and Birkhoff gives a permutation that does at least as well. $V'=F\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu\circ\sigma)F^{\mathsf T}$ makes $X_a',X_b'$ diagonal in $F$ with the norm unchanged, and L57 finishes as in batch 2. $\square$

## Batch 4, what the two strata leave, stated sharply

A move of one block is controlled when the other blocks share a frame, and a move of one pair's difference when the rest and the pair's sum share a frame. **In both, the matrices the moving variable pairs against must commute with each other, and in general they do not**: for one block, $\ell'$ mixes $R^2$, $T$ and the $X_c$ of non-commuting blocks, so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(Y\ell')$ over rotations of $Y$ is maximised at $Y$ aligned with $\ell'$, which is diagonal in no frame the other blocks share. *So batch 1 turns the node into a question about reaching a stratum*: from a general $W$, is there a sequence of moves, each non-decreasing in $P$ at fixed norm, ending in the stratum of batch 2? Coordinate ascent over single blocks aligns each $Y_a$ with its own $\ell'_a$ and stops at a configuration where every block is aligned with its own pairing matrix, which is the criticality system and not commutation.

**At a maximiser** these moves give necessary conditions beyond criticality: every $Y_a$ is aligned with $\ell'_a$ in eigenvalue order, and every pair difference $V_{ab}$ maximises $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(V^2Z_{ab})+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VL_{ab})$ over its whole sphere, a trust-region condition. Whether these force a common eigenframe is the open question at $m\ge3$.

**Relation to F67.** Both strata arguments are coupled: the comparison tensor keeps every cross term and moves one variable against the exact linear or quadratic pairing it enters, so neither is a bound of separately maximised pieces.

## Batch 5, two free blocks against a common frame reduce to L227 in two diagonal fields

Let every block but $X_a,X_b$ be diagonal in $F$, with $R=\sum_{c\ne a,b}X_c$ and $T_R=\sum_{c\ne a,b}X_c^2$. Put $U=X_a+X_b$, $V=X_a-X_b$ and shift $\hat U=U+\tfrac23R$.

- **The norm.** $\lvert X\rvert^2+\lvert X_a\rvert^2+\lvert X_b\rvert^2=\lvert U+R\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2=\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2+\text{const}$.
- **The cubic part in $U,V$.** From item 1 of L228, $2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(U+R)^3+\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((U+R)(U^2+V^2)\big)+3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((U+R)T_R\big)+\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac34\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)+\text{const}$, so $\tfrac{15}4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac{15}2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(U^2R)+\tfrac94\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)+\tfrac32\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(V^2R)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(U(6R^2+3T_R)\big)+\text{const}$.
- **The shift.** Substituting $U=\hat U-\tfrac23R$: the $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat U^2R)$ coefficient is $\tfrac{15}4\cdot3\cdot(-\tfrac23)+\tfrac{15}2=0$, and the $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(V^2R)$ coefficient is $\tfrac94\cdot(-\tfrac23)+\tfrac32=0$. **Both quadratic couplings to the frame cancel.** The linear coefficient of $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UR^2)$ is $\tfrac{15}4\cdot3\cdot\tfrac49-\tfrac{15}2\cdot\tfrac43+6=1$.
- **The Gram part.** $2\beta_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_aX_b)=\tfrac12\beta_{ab}(\lvert U\rvert^2-\lvert V\rvert^2)$, where $\tfrac12\beta_{ab}\lvert U\rvert^2=\tfrac12\beta_{ab}\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-\tfrac23\beta_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UR)+\text{const}$ puts $-\tfrac23\beta_{ab}R$ into $A$ ([r954](REVIEW_r954.md)), and the cross terms with $c\ne a,b$ are $\sum_c\big((\beta_{ac}+\beta_{bc})\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UX_c)+(\beta_{ac}-\beta_{bc})\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VX_c)\big)$.

So, up to terms fixed by the frame data and the norms, $$\Phi(\hat U,V)=\tfrac{15}4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat U^3+\tfrac94\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UA)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC),\qquad A=R^2+3T_R+\sum_c(\beta_{ac}+\beta_{bc})X_c-\tfrac23\beta_{ab}R,\quad C=\sum_c(\beta_{ac}-\beta_{bc})X_c,$$ with $A$ and $C$ diagonal in $F$ and the norm $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2$. **The internal part is exactly [L227](../findings/second-order.md)'s value at two outer indices, with $U$ shifted, and the rest of the blocks acts as two diagonal external fields.**

**Where the alignments agree and where they may not.** For strong fields each of $\hat U$, $V$ aligns with its own field, both diagonal in $F$. For weak fields the internal part is maximised at commuting $\hat U,V$ (L227), and one common rotation, Birkhoff-linear in a single doubly stochastic matrix because $\hat U$ and $V$ share a frame, aligns the pair with both fields. In between, three orderings are involved, $\hat U$ against $A$, $V$ against $C$ and $\hat U$ against $V^2$, and they can disagree.

**Screen** ([screen_I106_pair_field.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_pair_field.py)): over 60 random diagonal fields and norm ratios, the general maximum of $\Phi$ was attained at diagonal $\hat U,V$ every time (off-diagonal entries at most $2\cdot10^{-5}$, commutator at most $5\cdot10^{-6}$), and it matched an exhaustive grid over diagonal configurations to within the grid's resolution. *Random fields rarely conflict: batch 8's conflicting fields refute the claim at fixed separate norms, and batch 9 finds no gap at fixed total norm.*

## Batch 6, the node as the fieldless case of a family, and the recommendation

**Define $G(k)$**: *for $k$ free traceless blocks, fixed scalars, and external blocks all diagonal in one frame $F$, the maximum of $P$ at fixed norm over the free blocks is attained with the free blocks diagonal in $F$.* Then:
- $G(1)$ is L228's item 3, proved.
- $G(2)$ is batch 5's question: **false in its fixed-norms form** (batch 8, [post F68](../findings/fence-posts.md)), and **at fixed total norm it survives a conflict screen** (batch 9).
- **The $\lvert J\rvert=3$ node is $G(m)$ with no external blocks**, since then the conclusion is that a maximiser has commuting blocks.

*$G$ is a strengthening of the node rather than a reduction of it*, and batch 8 shows its fixed-norms form fails at $k=2$.

**Recommendation**, revised by batch 9: prove $G(2)$ at fixed total norm, where the freedom to trade norm between $\hat U$ and $V$ is what the screen says removes the conflict.

## Batch 7, $G(2)$ with one field vanishing is proved, and the general case is two majorisations that disagree

Keep batch 5's $\Phi(\hat U,V)=\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat U^3+\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UA)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$ at $\lvert\hat U\rvert=p$, $\lvert V\rvert=q$, with $\alpha,\beta\ge0$ and $A,C$ diagonal in $F$. For a symmetric $M$ put $\psi(M)=\sup\{\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UM):U\text{ traceless symmetric},\lvert U\rvert=p\}$. It is a supremum of affine functions of $M$, hence convex, and $O(3)$-invariant, hence $\psi(M)=g(\lambda(M))$ with $g$ symmetric and convex on $\mathbb R^3$, hence Schur-convex. For fixed $V$, $\sup_{\hat U}\Phi=\psi(\beta V^2+A)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$, and the supremum is attained at $\hat U$ commuting with $\beta V^2+A$, diagonal in any eigenframe of it.

**$C=0$.** By Fan's theorem ([Chayes, Theorem 2.4](../references/matrix-rearrangement-2020-arxiv/notes.md)), $\lambda(\beta V^2+A)\prec\lambda^\downarrow(\beta V^2)+\lambda^\downarrow(A)$, with equal sums. So $\psi(\beta V^2+A)\le g\big(\lambda^\downarrow(\beta V^2)+\lambda^\downarrow(A)\big)$. The right side is $\psi(\beta V'^2+A)$ for $V'=F\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu')F^{\mathsf T}$, where $\nu'$ is $V$'s spectrum arranged so that $\nu'^2$ is ordered like the diagonal of $A$: $V'$ is traceless with $\lvert V'\rvert=\lvert V\rvert$ and $\beta V'^2+A$ is diagonal with that sorted spectrum. Then the optimal $\hat U'$ is diagonal in $F$. **So $G(2)$ holds when $C=0$**, which at the block level is $\sum_{c\ne a,b}(\beta_{ac}-\beta_{bc})X_c=0$, for instance a pair with $K_{ac}-t_a=K_{bc}-t_b$ at every external $c$.

**$A=0$.** $\psi(\beta V^2)$ depends on $V$'s spectrum alone, so at fixed spectrum only $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$ depends on $V$'s frame, it is maximised at $V$ diagonal in $F$ in von Neumann's order, and $\hat U$ then aligns with the diagonal $\beta V^2$. **So $G(2)$ holds when $A=0$.**

**Both fields.** Fan bounds the $\hat U$-side value by the arrangement of $V$'s squares ordered like $A$, von Neumann bounds $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$ by the arrangement of $V$'s entries ordered like $C$, and a single diagonal $V'$ realises one arrangement. *An argument through the pinching $\Delta_F$ runs the wrong way*: for convex invariant $\psi$, $\psi(\beta V^2+A)\ge\psi\big(\Delta_F(\beta V^2)+A\big)$, a lower bound where an upper bound is needed. **The general $G(2)$ is therefore the question whether, at an optimum, the square-ordering Fan prefers and the entry-ordering von Neumann prefers can be taken equal**, and batch 8 answers it in the negative at fixed separate norms, while batch 9 finds no gap at fixed total norm.

## Batch 10, $G(2)$ at fixed total norm as one cubic on a sphere of $6\times6$ matrices, and the condition a non-diagonal critical point must meet

**The embedding.** Put $c=3\pm2\sqrt2$, the roots of $c^2-6c+1=0$, and $\lambda^2=c$, which is $(1+c^2)/6$ there. For traceless symmetric $U,V$ let $M=\begin{pmatrix}U&\lambda V\\\lambda V&cU\end{pmatrix}$. Then
$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3=(1+c^3)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+3\lambda^2(1+c)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2),\qquad\lvert M\rvert^2=(1+c^2)\lvert U\rvert^2+2\lambda^2\lvert V\rvert^2=4c\big(\tfrac32\lvert U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2\big),$$
the first by cyclicity (the terms odd in $V$ vanish), and the ratio of its coefficients is $\frac{1-c+c^2}{c}=5=\frac{15/4}{9/4}\cdot3$, so $\tfrac{15}4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac94\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)=\kappa\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3$ with $\kappa=\frac{\beta}{3\lambda^2(1+c)}=\frac3{4c(1+c)}$, which is $0.018845$ at $c=3+2\sqrt2$. The diagonal fields embed in the same shape: with $F=\begin{pmatrix}A'&C'\\C'&cA'\end{pmatrix}$, $A'=A/(1+c^2)$, $C'=C/(2\lambda)$, $\langle M,F\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UA)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$. **So $G(2)$ at fixed total norm is the maximisation of $\kappa\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3+\langle M,F\rangle$ on a sphere of the ten-dimensional space $\mathcal M$ of such $M$, with $F\in\mathcal M$ and its blocks diagonal**, and the norm trade that batch 9's screen found decisive is built into $\lvert M\rvert$. Screened to $10^{-13}$ ([screen_I106_G2_embed6.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_G2_embed6.py)).

**Why the obvious relaxation is loose.** Over all traceless symmetric $6\times6$ matrices of the same norm, the maximum of $\kappa\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M^3+\langle M,F\rangle$ is attained at $M$ diagonal in an eigenbasis of $F$, by L228's alignment argument, and $F$ splits into three $2\times2$ blocks, one per coordinate of $\mathbb R^3$. That maximiser has the block form of $F$, which would give diagonal $U,V$, but it is generally not in $\mathcal M$: the relaxation frees the lower-right block from $cU$ and the traces of $U$ and $V$ from vanishing separately. *So the relaxation bounds the value and does not locate the maximiser.*

**A necessary condition at a non-diagonal critical point.** At a critical point of $\Phi$ on $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2=T$ with multiplier $\mu$, the gradients give $A\equiv3\mu\hat U-3\alpha\hat U^2-\beta V^2$ and $C\equiv\mu V-\beta\{\hat U,V\}$ up to multiples of $I$ ($\alpha=\tfrac{15}4$, $\beta=\tfrac94$). The fields commute, and with Cayley–Hamilton ($[Z^3,Y]=\tfrac12\lvert Z\rvert^2[Z,Y]$ for traceless $3\times3$ $Z$) this reads
$$0=[A,C]=\Big(3\mu^2+\tfrac32\alpha\beta\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2\Big)[\hat U,V]-3\mu(\alpha+\beta)[\hat U^2,V]+3\alpha\beta\,\hat U[\hat U,V]\hat U-\beta^2\,V[\hat U,V]V,$$
screened to $10^{-13}$. Diagonal pairs satisfy it trivially. **$G(2)$ at fixed total norm follows if every non-diagonal solution of this equation, with the two Lagrange forms, is not a global maximum**, and batch 9's screen found none that is. *The next step is to classify its non-commuting solutions: it is one $3\times3$ skew equation in $\hat U,V$ and $\mu$, and in an eigenframe of $\hat U$ it is three scalar equations for the three off-diagonal entries of $V$.*

## Batch 11, the stratum where the two blocks share an eigenvector is a three-coupling phase problem

**The reduction.** Let $\hat U$ and $V$ share the eigenvector $e_2$ of the frame $F$ in which $A,C$ are diagonal, as the witness of [post F68](../findings/fence-posts.md) does. On $e_2^\perp=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(e_1,e_3)$ write $\hat U=-\tfrac{u_2}2I+pR(2\varphi)$ and $V=-\tfrac{v_2}2I+rR(2\chi)$, with $R(\vartheta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\vartheta&\sin\vartheta\\\sin\vartheta&-\cos\vartheta\end{pmatrix}$, $p,r\ge0$, and eigenvalues $u_2,v_2$ on $e_2$. With $d_A=\tfrac12(A_1-A_3)$ and $d_C=\tfrac12(C_1-C_3)$, since $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(R(\vartheta)R(\vartheta')\big)=2\cos(\vartheta-\vartheta')$ and the eigenvalues of $\hat U$ are $u_2,-\tfrac{u_2}2\pm p$,
$$\Phi=\alpha\big(\tfrac34u_2^3-3u_2p^2\big)+\beta\big(u_2v_2^2-u_2\big(\tfrac{v_2^2}4+r^2\big)\big)+A_2u_2-\tfrac{u_2}2(A_1+A_3)+C_2v_2-\tfrac{v_2}2(C_1+C_3)+g,$$
$$g=K_{12}\cos(2\varphi-2\chi)+K_1\cos2\varphi+K_2\cos2\chi,\qquad K_1=2p\,d_A,\quad K_2=2r\,d_C,\quad K_{12}=-2\beta p\,v_2\,r,$$
with norms $\lvert\hat U\rvert^2=\tfrac32u_2^2+2p^2$ and $\lvert V\rvert^2=\tfrac32v_2^2+2r^2$. Screened to $10^{-14}$ ([screen_I106_G2_commonaxis.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_G2_commonaxis.py)). **All of the frame dependence is the three-coupling phase sum $g$.**

**Unfrustrated signs give diagonal blocks at the same amplitudes.** $g\le\lvert K_1\rvert+\lvert K_2\rvert+\lvert K_{12}\rvert$, with equality at phases $2\varphi,2\chi\in\{0,\pi\}$ exactly when signs $s_1,s_2$ exist with $s_1K_1\ge0$, $s_2K_2\ge0$ and $s_1s_2K_{12}\ge0$, that is when $K_1K_2K_{12}\ge0$. At those phases $R(2\varphi),R(2\chi)=\pm\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(1,-1)$, so $\hat U$ and $V$ are diagonal in $F$. **So on this stratum a non-diagonal maximiser needs frustration, $K_1K_2K_{12}<0$, which is $d_A\,d_C\,v_2>0$**, and then the phase maximum can be interior. *This is the fixed-norms witness's regime: at fixed $\lvert\hat U\rvert,\lvert V\rvert$ the amplitudes cannot move to escape it.*

**What the total norm adds.** Flipping $v_2\mapsto-v_2$ removes the frustration and changes only the $v_2$-odd terms, $C_2v_2-\tfrac{v_2}2(C_1+C_3)$ and $K_{12}$, while the amplitudes $p,r$ and $u_2$ can be moved between the two blocks at fixed $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2$. **$G(2)$ on this stratum is therefore a comparison between the frustrated interior phase maximum and the best unfrustrated diagonal configuration over the norm ellipsoid**, a finite problem in $(u_2,p,v_2,r)$ and two phases. Batch 9's screen, which included this stratum, found the diagonal side always at least as large. *The next computation is the frustrated phase maximum in closed form, $\max g$ for $K_1,K_2>0>K_{12}$, and the comparison.*

## Batch 12, the frustrated phase maximum in closed form, and what it leaves on the common-eigenvector stratum

**The closed form.** For $a,b,c>0$ let $g=a\cos\vartheta_1+b\cos\vartheta_2-c\cos(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_2)$. Maximising over $\vartheta_1$ first gives $\lvert a-c\,e^{-i\vartheta_2}\rvert=\sqrt{a^2+c^2-2ac\,x}$ with $x=\cos\vartheta_2$, so $\max g=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}G(x)$ with $G(x)=bx+\sqrt{a^2+c^2-2acx}$, concave. $G'(x)=0$ at $x_*=\frac{a^2+c^2-a^2c^2/b^2}{2ac}$, which lies in $(-1,1)$ exactly when $\lvert a-c\rvert<ac/b<a+c$, that is when $1/a,1/b,1/c$ satisfy the triangle inequalities. There
$$\max g=bx_*+\frac{ac}b=\tfrac12\Big(\frac{ab}c+\frac{bc}a+\frac{ca}b\Big),$$
and otherwise the maximum is at $x=\pm1$, the best collinear value $a+b+c-2\min(a,b,c)$. **And in the triangle case $\max g\le a+b+c$**: with $x=1/a$, $y=1/b$, $z=1/c$, $(a+b+c)-\max g=\tfrac12abc\big(2(xy+yz+zx)-x^2-y^2-z^2\big)=\tfrac12abc\sum_{\text{cyc}}x(y+z-x)>0$. Screened on 400 triples against a $2001^2$ grid ([screen_I106_frustrated_phase.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_frustrated_phase.py)).

**What it gives on batch 11's stratum.** Put $\tilde c=C_2-\tfrac12(C_1+C_3)$. In batch 11's formula the only term odd in $v_2$ outside $g$ is $\tilde cv_2$, and $\lvert V\rvert^2$ depends on $v_2^2$. So flipping $v_2\mapsto-v_2$ keeps both norms and $p,r,u_2$, changes the value outside $g$ by $-2\tilde cv_2$, and flips the sign of $K_{12}$, making the phase problem unfrustrated with maximum $\lvert K_1\rvert+\lvert K_2\rvert+\lvert K_{12}\rvert$ at diagonal blocks (batch 11). With $a=\lvert K_1\rvert$, $b=\lvert K_2\rvert$, $c=\lvert K_{12}\rvert$, every frustrated configuration therefore satisfies
$$\Phi\ \le\ \Phi_{\text{diagonal}}(-v_2)+2\tilde c\,v_2 .$$
**So on the stratum where the two blocks share an eigenvector, a global maximiser off the diagonal needs all three of**: frustration, $d_A\,d_C\,v_2>0$; the triangle condition on $1/\lvert K_1\rvert,1/\lvert K_2\rvert,1/\lvert K_{12}\rvert$; and $\tilde c\,v_2>0$, so that flipping $v_2$ costs. *Together these fix $\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}v_2=\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}\tilde c=\mathop{\mathrm{sign}}(d_Ad_C)$.* In that last case the interior value is $S+\frac{\lvert d_Ad_C\rvert}{\beta\lvert v_2\rvert}+\beta\lvert v_2\rvert\Big(r^2\frac{\lvert d_C\rvert}{\lvert d_A\rvert}+p^2\frac{\lvert d_A\rvert}{\lvert d_C\rvert}\Big)$, $S$ being batch 11's phase-free part, and **the comparison with the diagonal configurations must move the amplitudes $p,r,u_2,v_2$ along the norm ellipsoid**, which is the remaining analysis on this stratum. *The stratum without a common eigenvector is a separate open case of $G(2)$.*

## Batch 13, the deficit in scale-free variables, and the costly-flip regime screened

**The deficit.** In batch 12's remaining case take $d_A,d_C,v_2>0$ by symmetry and put $s=\beta v_2$, $\rho_1=sr/d_A$ and $\rho_2=sp/d_C$. Then $ab/c=2d_Ad_C/s$, $bc/a=2(d_Ad_C/s)\rho_1^2$ and $ca/b=2(d_Ad_C/s)\rho_2^2$, so
$$\max g=\frac{d_Ad_C}{s}\big(1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2\big),\qquad a+b+c=\frac{2d_Ad_C}{s}\big(\rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_1\rho_2\big),$$
the triangle condition is $\rho_1+\rho_2>1$ with $\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert<1$, and the deficit is
$$D=(a+b+c)-\max g=\frac{d_Ad_C}{\beta v_2}\Big(2(\rho_1+\rho_2)-1-(\rho_1-\rho_2)^2\Big)>0 .$$
**By batch 12, a frustrated interior configuration can beat the diagonal configuration with $v_2$ flipped only where $2\tilde cv_2>D$.**

**The screen** ([screen_I106_G2_remaining.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_G2_remaining.py)): 54 field cases with $d_A,d_C\in\{0.3,1,3\}$ and $\tilde c\in\{1,4,12\}$, the last making the flip costly, at total norms $0.5$ and $2$. The phase-free maximum over $(u_2,p,v_2,r)$ and both phases equalled the maximum over the four collinear phase pairs, which is the maximum over diagonal pairs on this stratum, to $2\cdot10^{-15}$ in every case. **So on the common-eigenvector stratum $G(2)$ at fixed total norm survives the regime batch 12 could not exclude analytically.** *A screen, not a proof: the analytic step is that wherever $2\tilde cv_2>D$ the norm trade to a diagonal configuration gains at least $2\tilde cv_2-D$, a comparison in four amplitudes.*

## Batch 14, the phase maximum as one squared distance, and the common-eigenvector stratum closed

**The identity.** With $\Lambda=\frac{d_Ad_C}{\beta v_2}$, $\rho_1=\frac{\beta v_2r}{d_A}$ and $\rho_2=\frac{\beta v_2p}{d_C}$, batch 11's phase sum is $g=2\Lambda\rho_2\cos\vartheta_1+2\Lambda\rho_1\cos\vartheta_2-2\Lambda\rho_1\rho_2\cos(\vartheta_1-\vartheta_2)$, and expanding the squared modulus of $1-\rho_2e^{i\vartheta_1}-\rho_1e^{i\vartheta_2}$ gives
$$\Lambda\big(1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2\big)-g=\Lambda\big\lvert1-\rho_2e^{i\vartheta_1}-\rho_1e^{i\vartheta_2}\big\rvert^2 .$$
**So the phase sum is capped by $\Lambda(1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2)$ at every pair of phases, and its deficit is one squared distance in the plane.** Screened to $6\cdot10^{-14}$ over $200000$ draws ([screen_I106_annulus.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_annulus.py)).

**The closed form.** The set $\{\rho_2e^{i\vartheta_1}+\rho_1e^{i\vartheta_2}\}$ is the annulus $\mathcal A$ with radii $\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert$ and $\rho_1+\rho_2$, so
$$\max_{\vartheta}g=\Lambda\Big[1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2-\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(1,\mathcal A)^2\Big],\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(1,\mathcal A)=\max\big(0,\ 1-\rho_1-\rho_2,\ \lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert-1\big).$$
**Batch 12's triangle condition is exactly $1\in\mathcal A$**, and the three regimes it separated by cases are the three branches of this one distance. Screened against a $1441^2$ phase grid to $9\cdot10^{-5}$, the residual being that grid's own resolution.

**The diagonal values are the same formula on four points.** At $\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2\in\{0,\pi\}$ with signs $\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2$, $g=2\Lambda(\epsilon_1\rho_2+\epsilon_2\rho_1-\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\rho_1\rho_2)$ and $\Lambda(1+\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2)-g=\Lambda(1-\epsilon_1\rho_2-\epsilon_2\rho_1)^2$, so the best diagonal pair has deficit $\Lambda\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(1,\{\pm\rho_1\pm\rho_2\})^2$. **Those four points include both radii of the annulus, and since the annulus is invariant under rotation about $0$ while $1$ is real and positive, the free and the diagonal maxima agree exactly when $1\notin\mathop{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal A$**, which covers both the annulus boundary, $\rho_1+\rho_2=1$ or $\lvert\rho_1-\rho_2\rvert=1$, and everything outside the annulus.

**Affineness, and the closure.** Fix $u_2$ and $v_2>0$, and write $x=p^2$, $y=r^2$. Since $\Lambda\rho_2^2=\frac{\beta v_2d_A}{d_C}x$ and $\Lambda\rho_1^2=\frac{\beta v_2d_C}{d_A}y$, the value wherever $1\in\mathcal A$ is
$$\Phi=\Big(\tfrac34\alpha u_2^3+\tilde au_2+\tilde cv_2+\tfrac34\beta u_2v_2^2+\frac{d_Ad_C}{\beta v_2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\beta v_2d_A}{d_C}-3\alpha u_2\Big)x+\Big(\frac{\beta v_2d_C}{d_A}-\beta u_2\Big)y,$$
**affine in $(x,y)$**, while the norm constraint is the segment $3x+y=B$, $x,y\ge0$. An affine function is monotone along a segment, so its maximum over the closed part of the segment where $1\in\mathcal A$ is attained at an endpoint of that part, and every such endpoint is either a vertex of the segment, where one of $\rho_1,\rho_2$ vanishes and the annulus is a circle with empty interior, or a point with $1$ on the annulus boundary. **In both cases $1\notin\mathop{\mathrm{int}}\mathcal A$, and there the free maximum is already a diagonal value.** So every configuration of the stratum is matched, at the same total norm and the same $u_2$ and $v_2$, by one with both blocks diagonal in $F$.

**What this closes.** $G(2)$ at fixed total norm holds on the stratum where the two free blocks share an eigenvector of the field frame, the stratum carrying [post F68](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s witness. Over $400$ field cases the free maximum along the norm segment equalled the diagonal maximum to $0$, the argmax landing where the two formulas agree. **The four-amplitude comparison batch 13 left owed is not needed, and $\tilde c$ plays no part in the argument**, because the move never flips $v_2$.

**A re-derivation, classified.** Earlier in this batch the derivation reached the statement that a diagonal $\hat U$ in diagonal fields forces the best $V$ at fixed $\lvert V\rvert$ to be diagonal, through the Jordan operator $V\mapsto\tfrac12(\hat UV+V\hat U)$ carrying its top eigenvalue on the traceless diagonal directions. **[L228](../findings/second-order.md)'s item 4 already gives that, in the stronger form at fixed spectrum and by Birkhoff**, so it is recorded as a confirmation of the record rather than as a lemma.

## Batch 15, the criticality system in one eigenframe, and the quadratic that runs it

**The system.** At a critical point of $\Phi$ on $\tfrac32\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2=T$ with multiplier $\mu$, batch 10's Lagrange forms are $A\equiv3\mu\hat U-3\alpha\hat U^2-\beta V^2$ and $C\equiv\mu V-\beta\{\hat U,V\}$ up to multiples of $I$, and $A,C$ are diagonal in $F$, so $[A,C]=0$. Screened to $5\cdot10^{-13}$ against batch 10's displayed expansion ([screen_I106_criticality.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_criticality.py)).

**In an eigenframe of $\hat U$.** Write $\hat U=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(u_1,u_2,u_3)$ and $K=[\hat U,V]$, so $K_{ij}=(u_i-u_j)V_{ij}$. The first three terms of batch 10's expansion are all of the form (diagonal) times $K$ times (diagonal), and their $(i,j)$ entry is $(u_i-u_j)V_{ij}$ times
$$3\mu^2+\tfrac32\alpha\beta\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2-3\mu(\alpha+\beta)(u_i+u_j)+3\alpha\beta\,u_iu_j .$$
With $\hat U$ traceless, $u_i+u_j=-u_k$ and $u_iu_j=u_k^2-\tfrac12\lvert\hat U\rvert^2$ for the complementary index $k$, **so the two $\lvert\hat U\rvert^2$ terms cancel** and the bracket is $\phi_k=q(u_k)$ for the single quadratic
$$q(t)=3\alpha\beta\,t^2+3\mu(\alpha+\beta)\,t+3\mu^2-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2 .$$
**So the criticality system is the three scalar equations**
$$(u_i-u_j)V_{ij}\,q(u_k)=\beta^2\,(VKV)_{ij},\qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\},$$
the diagonal entries carrying nothing because $VKV$ is skew. Screened to $6\cdot10^{-13}$. **The whole frame dependence of the first-order conditions is one quadratic sampled at the three eigenvalues of $\hat U$**, and a commuting pair satisfies the system trivially, since $K=0$ kills both sides.

**What it leaves.** $G(2)$ on the stratum with no shared eigenvector follows if every solution with $K\ne0$ fails to be a global maximum. *The next step is that classification: $q$ has at most two roots, so at most two of $u_1,u_2,u_3$ can make the left side vanish through $q(u_k)=0$, and the remaining equations tie $V$'s off-diagonal entries to the cubic form $VKV$.* **This is a system in the three off-diagonal entries of $V$ alone once $\hat U$'s spectrum and $\mu$ are fixed.**

## Batch 16, what the criticality system says, and where it says anything new

**The remaining term, regrouped.** In $\hat U$'s eigenframe write the diagonal of $V$ as $(a_1,a_2,a_3)$ and its off-diagonal entries as $w_k$, the entry opposite index $k$, and put $D_3=u_1-u_2$, $D_2=u_1-u_3$, $D_1=u_2-u_3$. Then
$$(VKV)_{12}=D_3w_3\,a_1a_2+w_1w_2\big(a_1D_2-a_2D_1\big)+w_3\big(w_1^2D_1-w_2^2D_2-w_3^2D_3\big),$$
exact in sympy, and the other two entries are its images under the cyclic relabelling. **So every term of the equation indexed by $k$ carries either $w_k$ or the product $w_iw_j$ of the other two**, which is what makes the system degenerate on a whole stratum rather than at isolated points.

**Sharing an eigenvector is a condition on the $w$'s.** For $\hat U$ with distinct eigenvalues, $\hat U$ and $V$ share an eigenvector exactly when **at most one $w_k$ is nonzero**, since $Ve_k\parallel e_k$ says precisely that the two off-diagonal entries of row $k$ vanish. Screened over $20000$ draws with no mismatch.

**On that stratum the system collapses to one scalar condition.** Take $w_1=w_2=0$. The equations for the entries $(1,3)$ and $(2,3)$ then hold identically, every one of their terms carrying $w_2$ or $w_1w_3$, and the equation for $(1,2)$ becomes $D_3w_3\big[3\alpha\beta u_3^2+3\mu(\alpha+\beta)u_3+3\mu^2-\beta^2a_3^2\big]=0$, **the $w_3^2$ terms cancelling against the $-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2$ inside $q$ and the $a_1,a_2$ dependence collapsing to $a_3^2$ through $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}V=0$**. So a non-commuting critical point sharing the eigenvector $e_3$ satisfies
$$3\alpha\beta\,u_3^2+3\mu(\alpha+\beta)\,u_3+3\mu^2=\beta^2a_3^2,$$
a single equation in the two eigenvalues on the shared axis and the multiplier, **free of $w_3$ entirely**. Exact in sympy ([screen_I106_criticality.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_criticality.py)).

**What this locates.** [L229](../findings/second-order.md) closes the stratum where the shared eigenvector is also a frame vector of $F$, and the display above is the first-order shadow of that stratum. **The criticality system carries new information only where at least two of $w_1,w_2,w_3$ are nonzero**, which is exactly the case with no shared eigenvector at all. *There the three equations are genuinely coupled, since the $w_iw_j$ terms no longer drop, and classifying their solutions is the next step.*

## Batch 17, the criticality system paired with the commutator, and one frame-free scalar constraint

**The pairing.** The system is the matrix equation $[A,C]=0$, and pairing it with $K=[\hat U,V]$ itself in the skew inner product $\langle X,Y\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$ collapses it to one scalar. Batch 15's first three terms are $K$ conjugated by diagonals, with $(i,j)$ entry $K_{ij}q(u_k)$, so $\langle\text{those},K\rangle=2\sum_kD_k^2w_k^2\,q(u_k)$ with $D_k$ and $w_k$ as in batch 16, while $\langle-\beta^2VKV,K\rangle=\beta^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((VK)^2\big)$ because $VKV$ is skew. So every critical point satisfies
$$2\sum_{k}D_k^2w_k^2\,q(u_k)\ +\ \beta^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((VK)^2\big)\ =\ 0,\qquad q(t)=3\alpha\beta t^2+3\mu(\alpha+\beta)t+3\mu^2-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2 .$$
Exact in sympy ([screen_I106_criticality.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_criticality.py)). **The fields $A$ and $C$ have left the statement entirely**, so this is a constraint on $\hat U$, $V$ and the multiplier alone, and it holds on both strata at once.

**Tracelessness is load-bearing, and a first check missed it.** Batch 10's displayed expansion equals $[A,C]$ only after Cayley--Hamilton for traceless $3\times3$ matrices, so with $u_i$ and $a_i$ left free the identity above is false, and the first symbolic check reported exactly that. *Recorded because the displayed form reads like an algebraic rearrangement and is not one: it carries the traceless hypothesis inside it.*

**What it does not do.** $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big((VK)^2\big)$ takes both signs over traceless symmetric pairs, ranging over $[-50.1,\,8407.5]$ on the $200000$ draws the filed screen makes, so no sign argument follows from this identity by itself, and it cannot close the non-commuting case alone. **What it is good for is that it is frame-free and quadratic in the off-diagonal entries**, so a sign on $q$ at the three eigenvalues of $\hat U$ would force $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((VK)^2)$ to a definite sign and conversely. *The next step is therefore second-order: at a global maximum the partner $V$ maximises $\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$ over its own sphere at fixed $\lvert V\rvert$, which bounds $\mu$ below by the top eigenvalue of the Jordan operator $V\mapsto\beta\cdot\tfrac12(\hat UV+V\hat U)$ on traceless symmetric matrices, and that is a bound on $\mu$ in terms of $\hat U$'s spectrum alone.*

## Batch 18, the multiplier is bounded below by L225's constant, and batch 17's proposed lever is refuted

**The second-order bound.** At a global maximum, $V$ maximises $\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VC)$ over its own sphere at fixed $\lvert V\rvert$, everything else held. That is a trust-region problem for the quadratic form $V\mapsto\beta\langle V,\mathcal LV\rangle$ with $\mathcal L(V)=\tfrac12(\hat UV+V\hat U)$ compressed to traceless symmetric matrices, and its necessary condition is that the trust-region multiplier dominates the form. **The normalisation is where a factor of two lives, and the first version of this batch lost it.** Written out, the $V$ equation is $2\beta\mathcal LV+C=\mu V$, since $\{\hat U,V\}=2\mathcal LV$, so the multiplier of $\max\langle V,\beta\mathcal LV\rangle+\langle V,C\rangle$ at fixed $\lvert V\rvert$ is $\nu=\mu/2$ rather than $\mu$, and optimality reads $\nu\ge\beta\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)$, that is $\mu\ge2\beta\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)$.

**The spectrum of $\mathcal L$, and why the diagonal directions win.** In $\hat U$'s eigenframe the traceless symmetric space splits into the traceless diagonal plane and the three off-diagonal directions, both $\mathcal L$-invariant. On $E_{ij}+E_{ji}$ the eigenvalue is $\tfrac{u_i+u_j}2=-\tfrac{u_k}2$. On the traceless diagonal plane the compression of $\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(u)$ has trace $0$ and squared norm $\tfrac13\lvert\hat U\rvert^2$, so its eigenvalues are $\pm\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$. **And the diagonal block always dominates**, because for traceless $u$
$$2\lvert\hat U\rvert^2-3u_k^2=2(u_i^2+u_j^2)-(u_i+u_j)^2=(u_i-u_j)^2\ \ge\ 0,$$
so $-\tfrac{u_k}2\le\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$, **with equality exactly when the other two eigenvalues agree AND the repeated pair is the larger one**, that is at $u\sim(t,t,-2t)$ with $t>0$. *At $t<0$ the two still agree and the bound is not tight, the off-diagonal block giving $\lvert t\rvert/2$ against $\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6=\lvert t\rvert$, so the clause as first written was wrong ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md), [r958](REVIEW_r958.md), reproduced independently).* Hence $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)=\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$ and
$$\mu\ \ge\ \frac{2\beta\,\lvert\hat U\rvert}{\sqrt6}\qquad\text{at every global maximum.}$$
*The constant is [L225](../findings/second-order.md)'s at $k=3$, reappearing as the top eigenvalue of a Jordan operator rather than as a cubic bound.* Screened to $9\cdot10^{-16}$ on the spectrum, and at the same ten constrained maximisers the ratio $\mu/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$ runs over $[2.13,\,9.37]$, so the doubled bound holds with margin everywhere and the halved one was simply slack. **The weaker form is what the first screen tested, which is why the screen could not catch the lost factor**: a bound stated below the truth is never violated.

**And the lever batch 17 proposed does not exist.** That batch closed by suggesting this bound would sign $q$ at the three eigenvalues of $\hat U$, which with its identity $2\sum_kD_k^2w_k^2q(u_k)+\beta^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((VK)^2)=0$ would force $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((VK)^2)<0$ and constrain non-commuting critical points. **At five of the same ten maximisers some $q(u_k)$ is negative**, so $q$ is not positive at all three eigenvalues and no sign follows. *The bound on $\mu$ is real and the inference from it is not, so batch 17's identity stays unsigned and its closing sentence is withdrawn.*

**One corroboration.** All ten maximisers came out commuting to $10^{-6}$, which is $G(2)$ doing what batch 9's larger screen already found, and says nothing about the non-commuting critical points the system admits.

**What remains** is the classification of solutions with at least two of $w_1,w_2,w_3$ nonzero, now without a sign lever, so the next attempt is the three coupled equations of batch 16 directly.

## Batch 19, the degenerate spectrum, where a non-commuting critical point would have to hide

**Why this slice.** Batch 18's bound, in its corrected form $\mu\ge2\beta\lvert\hat U\rvert/\sqrt6$, is tight exactly at $\hat U\sim(t,t,-2t)$ **with $t>0$** (this sentence carried the withdrawn halved constant until [r957](REVIEW_r957.md) and [r958](REVIEW_r958.md) caught it, and the reason for studying $t<0$ therefore fails, though the arithmetic below survives at both signs), so that is the spectrum on which the second-order condition says least. Take $u_1=u_2=t$ and $u_3=-2t$, write the diagonal of $V$ as $(a_1,a_2,a_3)$ with $a_3=-a_1-a_2$, put $S=w_1^2+w_2^2$ and $Q=q(u_1)$.

**The system becomes three equations, one of which has lost its $q$.** Since $D_3=u_1-u_2=0$, the equation indexed by $3$ carries no $q$ term at all and is a pure constraint, while $D_1=D_2=3t$ scale the other two:
$$\text{(i)}\ \ w_1w_2(a_1-a_2)+w_3\big(w_1^2-w_2^2\big)=0,$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ \ w_1\big[Q-\beta^2(a_2a_3-S)\big]=\beta^2a_3w_2w_3,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \ w_2\big[Q-\beta^2(a_1a_3-S)\big]=\beta^2a_3w_1w_3 .$$
Exact in sympy against the general system of batch 16.

**Equation (i) is redundant off $a_3=0$.** Forming $w_2\cdot\text{(ii)}-w_1\cdot\text{(iii)}$ returns $\beta^2a_3$ times the left side of (i), so wherever $a_3\ne0$ the constraint carrying no multiplier is a consequence of the other two rather than an extra condition.

**Two of the equations give one equation, with no side conditions.** The combination $w_1\text{(ii)}-w_2\text{(iii)}$ divides by nothing and gives
$$\big(w_1^2-w_2^2\big)\big(Q+\beta^2S\big)=\beta^2a_3\big(a_2w_1^2-a_1w_2^2\big).$$
*A first derivation of this reached it by solving (iii) for $w_3$ and substituting, which needs $a_3\ne0$ and $w_1\ne0$, and typing the step as `collapse_from_pair` removed both hypotheses.* **In particular at $w_1^2=w_2^2$ the left side vanishes and the right forces $a_3=0$ or $a_1=a_2$.**

**Two nonzero entries force both $w_1$ and $w_2$ nonzero.** Setting $w_1=0$ makes (i) read $w_2^2w_3=0$, so $w_2=0$ or $w_3=0$, and either way at most one entry survives. **The case $w_1=0$ with $w_2$ and $w_3$ both nonzero is therefore impossible**, and the swap $e_1\leftrightarrow e_2$, which fixes $\hat U$ and exchanges $a_1\leftrightarrow a_2$ and $w_1\leftrightarrow w_2$ while fixing $w_3$, rules out $w_2=0$ with $w_1,w_3$ nonzero in the same way. *So on this spectrum the open case is exactly $w_1w_2\ne0$.*

## Batch 20, the value at a critical point, with the fields eliminated

**The pairing.** Each Lagrange equation is paired with its own variable. Both carry an undetermined multiple of $I$, and both multiples drop out because $\hat U$ and $V$ are traceless. From the $\hat U$ equation $3\alpha\hat U^2+\beta V^2+A\equiv3\mu\hat U$,
$$3\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat U^3+\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\langle\hat U,A\rangle=3\mu\lvert\hat U\rvert^2,$$
and from the $V$ equation $\beta\{\hat U,V\}+C\equiv\mu V$, using $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(V\{\hat U,V\}\big)=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)$,
$$2\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)+\langle V,C\rangle=\mu\lvert V\rvert^2 .$$

**The identity.** Substituting both into $\Phi=P_3+\langle\hat U,A\rangle+\langle V,C\rangle$ with $P_3=\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\hat U^3+\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UV^2)$ gives $\Phi=3\mu\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\mu\lvert V\rvert^2-2P_3$, and $3\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\lvert V\rvert^2=2T$ by the definition of the total norm, so
$$\boxed{\ \Phi=2\mu T-2P_3\ }$$
at every critical point, with the linear part $\langle\hat U,A\rangle+\langle V,C\rangle=2\mu T-3P_3$. **The fields have left the statement entirely.** Typed and proved as `value_at_critical`, and confirmed exactly in sympy.

**What it gives, stated correctly.** **The real content is that the FIELDS drop out**: at a critical point $\Phi$ is determined by the three numbers $\mu$, $T$ and $P_3$, whatever $A$ and $C$ are. *An earlier wording here said the open question becomes "a comparison of two numbers per point rather than a comparison of configurations", and that is circular: the identity gives $\mu T-P_3=\Phi/2$ exactly, so comparing $\mu T-P_3$ IS comparing $\Phi$, which is the comparison one started with.* **It is a reformulation and settles nothing on its own**, since neither $\mu$ nor $P_3$ is yet controlled at a non-commuting point. *Batch 18 bounds $\mu$ from below at a maximum, which is the wrong direction, so what is owed is an upper bound on $\mu$ or a lower bound on $P_3$, each by quantities that are NOT themselves built from $\Phi$ or from the fields* (batch 34).


## Batch 21, the joint second-order condition, and the cross term that is the only lever on non-commutativity

**Why look here.** Batch 20 reduced the open comparison to $\mu T-P_3$ and left owed an upper bound on $\mu$ or a lower bound on $P_3$. Every bound reached so far runs the other way, and each was obtained by moving **one** variable while the other was held. **The second-order condition has never been used jointly**, and its cross term is the only place where a second-order statement sees $V$ failing to commute with $\hat U$.

**The expansion.** Along $(\hat U+sX,\,V+sY)$ with $X,Y$ traceless symmetric, the cubic part has second-order coefficient $3\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX^2)+\beta\big(2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UY^2)\big)$, the norm has second-order coefficient $\tfrac32\lvert X\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert Y\rvert^2$, and the first-order coefficient of the norm is the tangency form $3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VY)$. All three exact in sympy, and the whole expansion typed as `second_variation_expansion` and `constraint_expansion`.

**Two steps this batch first left unstated** ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md)). *First*, the order-two coefficient uses $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XYV)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)$, which is **not cyclicity**: it holds because $X,V,Y$ are all symmetric, so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XYV)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((XYV)^{\mathsf T})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VYX)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)$. *Second*, the condition needs the constraint set to be a manifold near the point, both for the multiplier to exist and for every tangent vector to be realised by a curve. **It is automatic**: the constraint gradient $(3\hat U,V)$ vanishes only at $(0,0)$, which $T>0$ excludes, so the level set is a smooth sphere.

**The condition.** At a critical point the first differential of $\Phi-\mu T$ vanishes, so along any curve on the constraint surface the second derivative is $D^2(\Phi-\mu T)$ applied to the tangent vector alone, with no contribution from the curve's acceleration. Hence at every local maximum, for all traceless symmetric $X,Y$ with $3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VY)=0$,
$$6\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UX^2)+4\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XVY)+2\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UY^2)\ \le\ \mu\big(3\lvert X\rvert^2+\lvert Y\rvert^2\big).$$
**Setting $Y=0$ and $X=E_{ij}+E_{ji}$, which is tangent because $\hat U$ is diagonal, gives $\mu\ge-\alpha u_k$ at every $k$**, so $\mu\ge\alpha\lvert u_{\min}\rvert$. **This is NOT batch 18's bound improved, and the phrase first written here invited that reading** ([r958](REVIEW_r958.md)): this one is $2\alpha$ times the top Rayleigh ratio of the **off-diagonal block alone**, batch 18's is $2\beta$ times the ratio over the whole space, and **neither dominates**. At $u=(1,1,-2)$ they are $7.5$ and $4.5$, at $u=(2,-1,-1)$ they are $3.75$ and $4.5$.

**Screened, after a bug that looked like a refutation.** The first screen reported a violation of $-1.92$, which would have refuted the derivation. **The fault was the screen's tangent projection**: the constraint gradient is $(3\hat U,V)$, so the projection must divide by $\lvert(3\hat U,V)\rvert^2=9\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\lvert V\rvert^2$, and it divided by $3\lvert\hat U\rvert^2+\lvert V\rvert^2$. The directions were therefore not tangent and the condition was tested where it need not hold. With the projection corrected, over ten maximisers with criticality residual $1.2\cdot10^{-7}$ and four thousand tangent directions each, **the smallest slack is $+0.27$ and the condition is tight nowhere**. *Recorded because a wrong screen reporting a violated necessary condition is indistinguishable, at a glance, from a real refutation of the route.*

**What is owed.** The condition is a joint form, so it is a statement that a block operator is negative semidefinite on the tangent space. *Its Schur complement couples $\hat U$ and $V$ through the cross term, and whether that coupling forces $[\hat U,V]=0$ at a maximum is the next question.* **It is not settled by any specialisation to one variable**, which is all that has been extracted from it so far.

## Batch 22, the discriminant form, and the first inequality that binds $V$ to $\hat U$

**The line $(Z,tZ)$.** Batch 21's condition holds on tangent directions, and the pair $(Z,tZ)$ is tangent **for every $t$** exactly when $3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ)+t\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ)=0$ identically in $t$, that is when
$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ)=0 .$$
That is two linear conditions on the five-dimensional space of traceless symmetric $Z$, so the subspace is three-dimensional in general. **On it the whole second-order condition becomes one scalar quadratic in $t$ that must never be positive**, namely $at^2+bt+c\le0$ with
$$a=2\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ^2)-\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2,\qquad b=4\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2),\qquad c=6\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ^2)-3\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2 .$$
*The coefficient $a$ is the $X=0$ case of the same condition and $c$ is three times the $Y=0$ case, so both are already known to be at most zero.*

**The inequality.** A quadratic that is never positive, with $a<0$, satisfies $b^2\le4ac$, which is `disc_of_nonpos`. **Only $a\le0$ is established, and the case $a=0$ needs its own line** ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md), [r958](REVIEW_r958.md)): it occurs exactly when $\mu=2\beta\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L)$ with $Z$ a top eigendirection lying in $W$, and the screen's $\min(-a)=8.8\cdot10^{-4}$ shows the boundary is nearly met, so it cannot be waved away. *There $bt+c\le0$ for every $t$ forces $b=0$, whence $b^2=0=4ac$ and the inequality holds with equality.* **So it is true for every $Z\in W$**, and the batch first justified only part of that range. Writing $c=3(2\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ^2)-\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2)$ and dividing by four,
$$\boxed{\ 4\beta^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)^2\ \le\ 3\big(2\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ^2)-\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2\big)\big(2\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ^2)-\mu\lvert Z\rvert^2\big)\ }$$
for every $Z$ in that subspace. **The left side is built from $V$ alone and the right side from $\hat U$ and $\mu$ alone.** *This is the first inequality on the record that bounds a quantity of $V$ by quantities of $\hat U$, which is what batch 20 said was owed, and it arrives from second order rather than first.* Both factors on the right are nonpositive, so their product is nonnegative and the inequality is not vacuous on sign grounds.

**Screened.** At the ten maximisers, over three thousand directions of the subspace each: $\min(-a)=8.8\cdot10^{-4}$ and $\min(4ac-b^2)=2.7\cdot10^{-6}$, both nonnegative as required. **Both minima sit close to zero**, so the inequality is nearly attained somewhere on this subspace, which is either a sign that it is a sharp lever or an artefact of nearly degenerate $Z$. *That distinction is not settled and is the next thing to look at.*

**What it does not yet do.** It constrains $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)$ for $Z$ orthogonal to both $\hat U$ and $V$, and the open question needs a statement about $[\hat U,V]$. *The gap is that $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)$ over this subspace is not obviously related to the commutator*, and finding a $Z$ whose $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)$ is forced large when $[\hat U,V]\ne0$ is what would close it.


## Batch 23, the interlacing route is dead, and why it could never have worked

**The idea.** Batch 22's inequality caps $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)/\lvert Z\rvert^2$ on the subspace $W=\{Z:\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ)=0\}$, roughly by $\tfrac{\sqrt3\,\mu}{2\beta}$ once $\mu$ dominates. That quotient is the Rayleigh quotient of $V$'s Jordan operator $\mathcal L_V$ restricted to $W$, and $W$ has codimension two, so Cauchy interlacing gives $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L_V\vert_W)\ge\lambda_3(\mathcal L_V)$. Capping the left side would then cap $\lambda_3(\mathcal L_V)$ and so constrain $V$ against $\mu$, which is what batch 20 asked for.

**Why it fails, structurally.** By the spectrum computed in batch 18 for $\hat U$ and identical for $V$, the five eigenvalues of $\mathcal L_V$ on traceless symmetric matrices are $\pm\lvert V\rvert/\sqrt6$ together with $-v_1/2,-v_2/2,-v_3/2$, and those three sum to zero while the other two are symmetric about zero. **The sharp statement is $\lvert\lambda_3\rvert\le\tfrac12\lambda_{\max}$**, rather than, as this batch first said, "pinned near zero by construction" ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md), [r958](REVIEW_r958.md)). *With $\lambda=\lvert V\rvert/\sqrt6$, the three numbers $-v_k/2$ sum to zero and are bracketed by $\pm\lambda$, and maximising the median subject to those constraints gives $x=y$, $z=-2y$ and $y=\lambda/2$, attained at $v\sim(-s,-s,2s)$.* **Half the unrestricted maximum is not near zero.** Interlacing bounds the restricted maximum from *below* by that median, and half the top eigenvalue is too small to meet the cap. **The defect is INSUFFICIENT MAGNITUDE, not a wrong direction of bound**, which is how this batch first named it and which misdiagnoses a real obstruction as a formal one.

**Measured, at the ten maximisers.** $\lambda_3(\mathcal L_V)$ is negative at eight of the ten and at most $0.275$ at the others, while the cap runs over $[0.59,\,1.16]$ and the restricted maximum itself over $[0.11,\,0.54]$. *So the cap is slack by a factor of two to six everywhere, and the interlacing lower bound is vacuous against it at every point.*

**And it was not typable either.** Mathlib carries no eigenvalue interlacing and no Courant-Fischer statement under the names searched, so the step would have needed that theory built first.

**What remains.** The gap batch 22 named is unchanged and is a construction rather than a spectral inequality: **exhibit a $Z\in W$ whose $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)$ is forced large when $[\hat U,V]\ne0$.** *Interlacing was an attempt to get that for free from dimension counting alone, and dimension counting is exactly what cannot see a commutator.*

## Batch 24, the construction batch 22 asked for does not exist, and the discriminant line closes

**The natural candidate.** $K=[\hat U,V]$ is skew, and for skew $K$ and symmetric $M$ the commutator $[K,M]$ is symmetric, so $Z_1=[K,\hat U]$ is traceless symmetric and vanishes exactly when $K$ commutes with $\hat U$. It also satisfies one of the two constraints for free: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UZ_1)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat UK\hat U)-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat U^2K)=0$ by cyclicity. *So it is the obvious thing to feed into batch 22's inequality.*

**But no particular $Z$ is what is needed.** Batch 22's cap holds for **every** $Z\in W$, so a contradiction requires the *maximum* of $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)/\lvert Z\rvert^2$ over $W$ to exceed it, and that maximum is $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L_V\vert_W)$ whatever $Z$ one writes down. **The construction therefore reduces to a single question: is $\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L_V\vert_W)$ bounded below by anything increasing in $\lVert[\hat U,V]\rVert$?**

**It is not.** Over $159273$ pairs of traceless symmetric matrices, both normalised to unit Frobenius norm and kept only when $\lVert K\rVert\ge\tfrac12$, the restricted maximum descends to $7.9\cdot10^{-4}$, attained at $\lVert K\rVert=0.70$, against the unrestricted value $\lvert V\rvert/\sqrt6=0.408$. **A strongly non-commuting pair can have a restricted maximum three orders of magnitude below the unrestricted one.** *So no inequality of the shape "the commutator is large, therefore $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(VZ^2)$ is large somewhere on $W$" holds, and the construction cannot come from linear algebra alone.*

**What this closes and what it does not.** It closes the discriminant line, batches 21 to 24, as a route to $[\hat U,V]=0$: that line produces a real inequality in batch 22 and no path from it to commutativity. **It does not show the inequality is useless**, and it does not rule out a proof that consumes the criticality equations, which cut the pair down far below a generic one. **That consolation is narrower than it reads**: by [batch 28](2026-09-15-I106-m3-derivation.md) those equations admit non-commuting solutions on the open stratum, so consuming them is necessary and cannot by itself be sufficient ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md)). *What is refuted is the route that would have worked for any pair.* **The clause this batch first added, that pair-generic statements are "the only thing the second-order condition by itself can supply", overreaches** ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md)): the condition at a critical point carries $\mu$, and criticality constrains $\mu$ through batches 18 and 21, so a second-order argument is not confined to generic pairs. *The closure of the discriminant line does not rest on that clause.*

**The two readers disagree on this batch's scope, and the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved.** *[r957](REVIEW_r957.md) calls the clause "the only thing the second-order condition by itself can supply" an overreach, since the condition at a critical point carries $\mu$ and criticality constrains $\mu$. [r958](REVIEW_r958.md) reads the batch's scope as correctly drawn and treats that same clause as a claim about method rather than a theorem.* **Both agree the batch's conclusion stands**, and the clause is marked above on r957's reading.

**Three methods have now closed on this node in one day**: the three-alignment relaxation, Cauchy interlacing, and this construction. *Each died for the same underlying reason, that a bound derived without using criticality cannot see a commutator, and by the plan's own standing rule several attempts failing for one reason is the signal to return to route selection rather than to try a fourth.*

## Batch 25, eliminating the multiplier and the norm, and what the system becomes

**The elimination.** With the $u_i$ distinct and all three $w_k$ nonzero, each equation of batch 16 solves for one value of $q$:
$$q(u_k)=\frac{\beta^2\,(VKV)_{ij}}{(u_i-u_j)\,w_k},\qquad\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.$$
**And $q$ is a quadratic whose leading coefficient is $3\alpha\beta$, a constant free of $\mu$ and of $\lvert V\rvert^2$.** So its second divided difference over the three eigenvalues is forced:
$$\sum_k\frac{q(u_k)}{\prod_{m\ne k}(u_k-u_m)}=3\alpha\beta .$$
*Both unknowns of the Lagrange system disappear at once, because they sit only in the lower coefficients of $q$.*

**What is left is one polynomial equation in the block data alone.** Writing $D_1=u_2-u_3$, $D_2=u_1-u_3$, $D_3=u_1-u_2$ and $a_3=-a_1-a_2$, it is
$$3\alpha\,w_1w_2w_3\,D_1D_2D_3\ =\ \beta\Big[w_1w_2w_3\big(a_1^2D_2+2a_1a_2D_3-a_2^2D_1\big)+w_1^2w_2^2\big(a_1D_2-a_2D_1\big)$$
$$-\ w_1^2w_3^2\big(a_3D_1+a_1D_3\big)+w_2^2w_3^2\big(a_3D_2-a_2D_3\big)+w_1w_2w_3\big(w_1^2D_1-w_2^2D_2+w_3^2D_3\big)\Big],$$
exact in sympy. **The $\alpha$ side is a single monomial**, because the cubic $2u_1^3+3u_1^2u_2-3u_1u_2^2-2u_2^3$ factors as $D_1D_2D_3$, which is `alpha_part_factors`.

**Two checks it had no freedom to pass.** The coefficient of $w_1^2w_2^2$ is $a_1D_2-a_2D_1$, **exactly the combination batch 16's independent regrouping of $(VKV)_{12}$ produced**. And every term of the bracket carries a factor of some $w$, so a commuting pair satisfies the equation trivially, as it must.

**What it gives.** *The multiplier and the norm are gone, so a non-commuting critical point is now constrained by a relation among the eigenvalue gaps, the diagonal of $V$ and its off-diagonal entries alone.* **The $\alpha$ side vanishes exactly when some $w_k$ vanishes or some gap closes**, and both of those are the cases already settled, so in the open case both sides are nonzero and the equation is a genuine balance between $\alpha$ and $\beta$, whose values are $\tfrac{15}4$ and $\tfrac94$.

**What is owed.** The elimination consumed the three equations to produce one. **Exactly ONE further relation remains, not two**: interpolating the quadratic through the three points $(u_k,R_k)$, its leading coefficient is this equation, its linear coefficient merely *defines* $\mu$, and its constant term is the single genuine residual $3\mu^2-\tfrac12\beta^2\lvert V\rvert^2=A_0$, which is quadratic in the $R_k$ where this one is linear ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md), [r958](REVIEW_r958.md), reproduced by interpolation). **AND THE READING OF $w_1w_2w_3=0$ AS COMMUTATIVITY IS FALSE.** *Batch 16's open case is at least two of the $w_k$ nonzero, which strictly contains exactly two, and this elimination divides by $w_1w_2w_3$ and so silently assumes all three. The sub-stratum with exactly two was excluded without justification, and [batch 28](2026-09-15-I106-m3-derivation.md) shows it is populated.* *That is a question about a polynomial system in seven unknowns with two fixed rational constants, and it is the first form of the problem with no analysis in it at all.*


## Batch 26, the eliminated equation is invariant under the stratum's whole symmetry group

**The group.** Relabelling the three eigenvector axes acts simultaneously on the eigenvalues $u_k$, the diagonal entries $a_k$ and the off-diagonal entries $w_k$, since $w_k$ is by definition the entry opposite axis $k$. Flipping the sign of one eigenvector flips two of the three $w_k$ and fixes everything else. Together these generate $S_3$ times the sign flips. **The reason first given here was wrong**: conjugating $\hat U$ and $V$ while holding $A$ and $C$ fixed does not preserve $\Phi$. *What these maps are is a reparametrisation of the same configuration, a relabelling of which eigenvector is called first, so the equation must be equivariant by construction rather than by any invariance of $\Phi$ ([r957](REVIEW_r957.md)).*

**Batch 25's equation is invariant under all of them.** Taking the ratio of the relabelled equation to the original: both transpositions give exactly $1$, both three-cycles give exactly $1$, and the sign flip of $e_1$ gives exactly $1$. *So the equation descends to the quotient by that group, its solution set is a union of orbits, and a search for non-commuting solutions may be conducted on the quotient rather than on the seven variables.*

**Two false alarms, both of which read as a refutation.** They are recorded because each looked, at a glance, like the derivation being wrong rather than the check.
1. **Comparing numerators.** The equation was extracted as the numerator of a rational function, and **a numerator is defined only up to a unit**, so it can acquire a sign or a factor under relabelling while the equation itself is unchanged. Comparing numerators reported the symmetry broken. The meaningful comparison is the ratio of the two equations.
2. **Cycling the $w$ indices against the $u$ indices.** Since $w_k$ sits opposite axis $k$, it must be permuted exactly as the axes are. Cycling $u$ and $a$ one way while cycling $w$ the other is a different substitution altogether, and it is the only one of the five tested that fails.

**The polynomial is antisymmetric, and the equation is invariant. Both, precisely.** Batch 25's explicit polynomial transforms by the **sign character** of $S_3$: substituting into it directly, with no re-derivation, it negates under either diagonal transposition and is fixed under either three-cycle, and its $\alpha$ and $\beta$ coefficients are separately antisymmetric. That is exactly as it must be, since the $\alpha$ coefficient is the invariant $w_1w_2w_3$ times the antisymmetric Vandermonde $D_1D_2D_3$. **The equation $P=0$ is then invariant, because a sign does not move a zero set**, which is what the ratio test found.

**A third false alarm, and the one that took longest.** *Asking whether "the numerator" is symmetric is not a well-posed question*, because the numerator returned by cancelling a rational function is defined only up to a unit, so two such numerators may differ by a sign that has nothing to do with the group. Tests built that way reported the numerator antisymmetric once and symmetric once, and both readings were meaningless. **The well-posed test fixes the explicit polynomial and substitutes into that**, which is what settles it. *An earlier draft of this batch recorded the sign as an artefact of the numerator convention, and that was wrong: the antisymmetry is a real property of the polynomial.*

**What it opens.** An invariant equation is expressible in the invariants of the action, in the elementary symmetric functions of the $u_k$ and of the $a_k$, in $w_1w_2w_3$ and $\sum w_k^2$, and in the mixed invariants such as $\sum u_kw_k^2$. **Rewriting it that way would cut the seven variables down substantially.** *The mixed invariant ring of three index sets under one diagonal $S_3$ is not small, so choosing the right generating set is the work, and the cost of searching for it is not predictable in advance. It is named here rather than started.*

**Not typed.** Batch 25's equation itself is not in Lean, only `alpha_part_factors`, the cubic identity behind its $\alpha$ side. The invariance recorded here is a statement about that untyped equation.

## Batch 30, the commutator is carried by the field, and the node reduces to one commutator

**Commuting each Lagrange equation with the other variable gives two identities of the matrix ring.** From $C\equiv\mu V-\beta\{\hat U,V\}$, commuting with $\hat U$ kills the multiple of $I$ and turns $[\hat U,\{\hat U,V\}]$ into $[\hat U^2,V]=\{\hat U,K\}$, and from $A\equiv3\mu\hat U-3\alpha\hat U^2-\beta V^2$, commuting with $V$ kills $[V,V^2]$ outright:
$$\mu K-\beta\{\hat U,K\}=[\hat U,C],\qquad [V,A]=3\alpha\{\hat U,K\}-3\mu K .$$
**Neither needs symmetry, tracelessness or criticality beyond the single equation it comes from**, so both are identities of $3\times3$ matrices and are typed as such ([commutator_from_C](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I106Criticality.lean), [commutator_from_A](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/I106Criticality.lean)), proved by `module` after distributing the products.

**Eliminating $\{\hat U,K\}$ between them needs no hypotheses at all.** Taking $3\alpha$ times the first relation and $\beta$ times the second and adding cancels $\{\hat U,K\}$ with no division, so over any ring and for every $\alpha,\beta,\mu$,
$$3(\alpha-\beta)\,\mu\,K=3\alpha\,[\hat U,C]+\beta\,[V,A].$$
**What $\alpha\ne\beta$ and $\mu\ne0$ buy is the INVERSION**, $K=\big(3\alpha[\hat U,C]+\beta[V,A]\big)/\big(3(\alpha-\beta)\mu\big)$, which is what the field-frame support statement below actually uses. *Here $\alpha-\beta=\tfrac32$, and $\mu\ne0$ follows from batch 18 at a global maximum with $\hat U\ne0$, while at a general critical point it is an extra hypothesis with no source in the record ([r959](REVIEW_r959.md)).* Exact in sympy over generic symmetric $\hat U,V$ with $A$ and $C$ defined by the Lagrange forms ([screen_I106_commutator.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I106_commutator.py)).

$$(\mu+\beta u_k)\,K_{ij}=[\hat U,C]_{ij},\qquad K_{ij}=(u_i-u_j)V_{ij}.$$
**Tracelessness is load-bearing here and the screen carries the control that says so**: with $u_1,u_2,u_3$ left free the entrywise form is false while the frame-free form still holds. $$[\hat U,V]=0\iff[\hat U,C]=0 .$$

**Read in the field frame instead, the same elimination says where $K$ can live.** With $A$ and $C$ diagonal in $F$, $[\hat U,C]_{ij}=\hat U_{ij}(C_j-C_i)$ and $[V,A]_{ij}=V_{ij}(A_j-A_i)$, so
$$3(\alpha-\beta)\,\mu\,K_{ij}=3\alpha\,\hat U_{ij}(C_j-C_i)+\beta\,V_{ij}(A_j-A_i),$$
and **$K$ is supported on the pairs where the fields separate**. *In particular a critical point with both fields scalar commutes, and a pair of indices carrying $C_i=C_j$ and $A_i=A_j$ contributes nothing to the commutator whatever $\hat U$ and $V$ do there.*

## Batch 34, the upper bound on the multiplier that batch 20 owes is a field quantity, and the attempt is recorded rather than repeated
