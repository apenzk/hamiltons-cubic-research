# Review r364, Gate 2 on [post F45](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md), the multiplier families and the exhaustion claim

**U1 (the exhaustion), REFUTED.** The span sub-claim is right and trivial; the uniqueness sub-claim is right and provable unconditionally; the conclusion drawn from them, that the eighteen identities are the whole per-row apparatus, is false. **There are nine further maximum-annihilation multipliers, three per row, at every $d=3$ spectrum with distinct eigenvalues.** The apparatus is twenty-seven identities, not eighteen.

**U2 (the floor), REFUTED as stated; the weaker conclusion survives, and I supply the argument the post lacked.** A floor exists by finiteness, and **at least $129$ of the $210$ four-subsets survive the whole annihilation apparatus** by a union bound that needs no enumeration, so that instrumentation cannot close $d=3$. But $141$ is not the floor: nine identities are missing, and, decisively, **"grant every right-hand side nonzero" is not the strongest the method can be**, with $T\ge0$ the *signed* rule forbids two-element zero-patterns where the unsigned rule forbids three-element ones, killing $28$ four-subsets where the recorded rule kills $7$. Per-row constraints outside the multiplier form also exist, and one of them is on this record already.

**U3 (the classification), CONFIRMED in the scope of its own table, REFUTED as a statement about both families entire.** I derive the factorisation independently: the two-root identity $\varphi=(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$ at row $i=a$ has right-hand side $2\nu_a[(n-1)\nu_b+\theta]$, and the double root $\varphi=(x-\nu_r)^2$ at row $i=r$ has $2\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta]$; the bracket index, the "two four-type members per bracket" count and the "factor never obstructs" rider all check out. **But only nine of the eighteen right-hand sides factor at all**, the six off-diagonal double-root and the three off-row two-root ones are irreducibly quadratic in $(\nu,\theta)$, exactly as the file's own earlier "six more" row and its $\rho_1$ analysis say.

**U4 (the dichotomy), CONFIRMED, with no hypothesis, and it can be strengthened.** $|\nu_2|<|\nu_3|$ is forced whenever $\nu_3<\nu_2<0$, the $\Pi$-comparison goes the right way, and the boundary case $(n-1)\nu_2+\theta=0$ sits safely in the second branch. The sharper form: the $j=2$ bracket vanishes *only* on the locus $\{\nu_2<0,\ \theta=(n-1)|\nu_2|\}$, so off that locus **both** the $j=2$ and $j=3$ triples apply, and only the equality locus costs anything.

**U5 (what the counts are worth), one recorded claim depends on a number, and it must be withdrawn.** The sentence *"$130$ is unreachable: taking all eighteen identities nonzero floors at $141$, so that row drew on something outside the family"*, and post F45's restatement *"$130<141$ is below the floor"*, are inferences whose whole content is the value $141$. With twenty-seven identities the annihilation floor lies in $[129,141]$ and $130$ is inside that interval, so the conviction is unsupported. The figures $194$ and $178$–$179$ carry nothing downstream: the only downstream claim is that the survivor set is nonempty, which the union bound now proves outright.

## The setting, rebuilt from the definitions before reading the derivations

With $a$ in class $i$ and $\mu_j=m_j-\delta_{ij}$, write the orbit count of the type $\{i,j,k\}$ at row $i$ as $N_{ijk}=2\mu_j\mu_k$ for $j<k$ and $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$ for $j=k$. Then [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)'s three row equations read $\sum N_{ijk}t_{ijk}=2(n-2)$, $\sum N_{ijk}\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2t_{ijk}=-2\nu_i$ and $\sum N_{ijk}\nu_j\nu_kt_{ijk}=2\nu_i(\theta-\nu_i)$, the (B$_i$) weight being the arithmetic mean and the (C$_i$) weight the product, which is exactly the polarisation pair. So a multiplier $\varphi(x)=\gamma x^2+\beta x+\alpha$ applied to row $i$ puts the coefficient $N_{ijk}\,\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)$ on the type $\{i,j,k\}$ with $\psi(x,y)=\gamma xy+\beta\tfrac{x+y}2+\alpha$, and the right-hand side is

$$R(\varphi,i)\;=\;2\big[(n-2)\alpha-\beta\nu_i+\gamma\nu_i\theta-\gamma\nu_i^2\big].$$

All multiplicities are at least $3$, so $\mu_j\ge2$ and every $N_{ijk}>0$: annihilation of a type is exactly the vanishing of $\psi$ there. Two independent checks against the record confirm the formula, the diagonal double root returns $2\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta]$, which is the file's table, and the row-$3$ double root at $\nu_1$ returns $2[(n-2)\nu_1^2+2\nu_1\nu_3-\nu_3^2+\nu_3\theta]$, which is the file's $\rho_1$ line verbatim.

## U1, the span is right, the exhaustion is not

**The span is trivially right.** In the basis $(x^2,x,1)$ the double root $(x-\nu_r)^2$ is $(1,-2\nu_r,\nu_r^2)$, and the determinant over $r=1,2,3$ is $-2$ times a Vandermonde in the $\nu_r$, nonzero for distinct eigenvalues. So the three double roots at a row are a basis of that row's three-dimensional multiplier space. [r359](REVIEW_r359.md) says exactly this and it is correct; it is also weaker than it sounds, since *any* three multipliers in general position would do.

**The uniqueness is right, and it can be proved unconditionally rather than generically.** Put $X=\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2$, $Y=\nu_j\nu_k$; a multiplier is the line $\alpha+\beta X+\gamma Y=0$ and the six types of row $i$ are six points, $P_{rr}=(\nu_r,\nu_r^2)$ on the parabola $Y=X^2$ and $P_{jk}=(\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2,\nu_j\nu_k)$ strictly below it, since $\nu_j\nu_k<\big(\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2\big)^2$ for $\nu_j\ne\nu_k$. All six points are distinct. The tangent at $\nu_r$ carries $P_{rr},P_{rj},P_{rk}$ and no other, because its value at $P_{jk}$ is $(\nu_j-\nu_r)(\nu_k-\nu_r)\ne0$; the chord through $P_{aa},P_{bb}$ carries no pair-point, its value at $P_{bc}$ being $-\tfrac12(\nu_b-\nu_a)(\nu_b-\nu_c)$ and at $P_{ac}$ being $\tfrac12(\nu_c-\nu_a)(\nu_a-\nu_b)$. Three parabola points are never collinear. So the maximum is three points, attained only by the three tangents, no genericity hypothesis, and in particular no spectrum admits an accidental fourth maximum-annihilation multiplier. That closes a worry the record leaves open.

**And that same picture shows the apparatus is bigger than eighteen.** Count pairs of the six points: $\binom62=15$. The three tangents consume $3\times\binom32=9$ of them. **Six pairs remain, and each spans a line with exactly two of the six points on it**, if such a line held a third it would be one of the three-point lines, whose pairs are already consumed. The six are the three chords $\{P_{aa},P_{bb}\}$, which are the recorded two-root family, and **three more: $\{P_{aa},P_{bc}\}$, the line joining one parabola point to the *opposite* pair-point.** [r359](REVIEW_r359.md) writes "the first family's $(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$ is a generic secant hitting $2$" and stops, taking the secants to be the only two-point lines; they are half of them. [Post F43](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)(i)'s own sentence, "a line meets at most $d$ pair-points if it is a tangent and at most $2$ otherwise", is consistent with twenty-seven and was read as if it said eighteen.

**The missing multipliers are real, and their surviving sets are new.** The multiplier through $P_{aa}$ and $P_{bc}$ is the quadratic with $\varphi(\nu_a)=0$ and $\psi(\nu_b,\nu_c)=0$, two linear conditions on a three-dimensional space, hence a nonzero solution always. It annihilates exactly $\{i,a,a\}$ and $\{i,b,c\}$ and survives on $\{iab,iac,ibb,icc\}$. Transcription check at $\nu=(2,1,-3)$, $a=1$: $4\gamma+2\beta+\alpha=0$ and $-3\gamma-\beta+\alpha=0$ give $\beta=-\tfrac73\gamma$, so $\varphi=3x^2-7x+2=(3x-1)(x-2)$, whose second root $\tfrac13$ is no eigenvalue; $\psi(1,-3)=-9+7+2=0$ while $\psi(1,1)=-2$, $\psi(-3,-3)=50$, $\psi(2,1)=-\tfrac52$, $\psi(2,-3)=-\tfrac{25}2$, exactly two annihilations, as claimed. At row $1$ the three new surviving sets are $\{112,113,122,133\}$, $\{111,112,123,133\}$ and $\{111,113,122,123\}$, and none of them contains any of the nine double-root surviving triples or equals any of the nine two-root surviving quadruples. Since a surviving set at row $i$ consists of types all containing $i$, and only three of the ten types contain two given classes, no row-$i$ quadruple can coincide with a row-$j$ one. So the nine new identities forbid nine four-subsets that the eighteen do not.

**The second half of U1's question, "does spanning imply the two-root identities carry no information?", is answered no, and the confusion is instructive.** The two-root *equation* is a linear combination of the three double-root equations at that row, right-hand side included; that much follows from the span. But the *forbidding rule* is not implied by any one double-root rule: a zero-set containing the two-root surviving quadruple $\{iab,iac,ibc,icc\}$ contains none of the three double-root surviving triples. What is true is that the three double-root equations *jointly* forbid it, by consistency: fixing those four variables to zero leaves $t_{iaa},t_{ibb}$ determined twice over by the roots $b$ and $a$ and then over-determined by the root $c$, and the two-root identity is precisely the combination eliminating $t_{iaa}$ and $t_{ibb}$. So "spans" transfers to forbidding power only through joint consistency, never one identity at a time. F45 counts both families and so is not damaged by this directly, but it is the same slip, treating a spanning statement about linear content as a completeness statement about annihilation patterns, that hid the missing nine.

## U2, a floor exists, the argument for it is available, and $141$ is not it

**Existence of a floor is free, and its positivity now has an argument rather than an enumeration.** The apparatus is finite: finitely many lines meet two or more of six points, hence finitely many surviving sets. For positivity, count kills. Each of the nine three-surviving identities forbids the $\binom{7}{1}=7$ four-subsets containing its triple; each of the eighteen four-surviving identities forbids exactly one, namely its own quadruple. So at most $9\times7+18\times1=81$ of the $210$ four-subsets fall, and **at least $129$ survive**. That is the statement post F45 needs (the annihilation apparatus cannot close $d=3$), obtained without enumerating anything, and it holds a fortiori for the record's eighteen. It also brackets the true annihilation floor in $[129,141]$.

**But the post's own headline claim is false in two independent ways.** First, the nine missing identities: "no sharper analysis of any right-hand side can take the per-row multiplier apparatus below $141$" is contradicted by nine identities that were never in the tally. Second, and worse, **nonzero-ness is not the strongest use of an identity, because $T\ge0$ is a hypothesis of the whole programme.** Take row $2$ with the double root $\nu_2$: the surviving types are $\{112,123,233\}$ with $\psi$ equal to $(\nu_1-\nu_2)^2>0$, $(\nu_1-\nu_2)(\nu_3-\nu_2)<0$ and $(\nu_3-\nu_2)^2>0$, mixed signs. If the right-hand side $2\nu_2[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]$ is positive, then $t_{112}=t_{233}=0$ already contradicts it, so the forbidden zero-pattern is the *pair* $\{112,233\}$ and it kills $\binom82=28$ four-subsets rather than $7$. If it is negative, $t_{123}>0$ is forced and the forbidden pattern is the singleton $\{123\}$, killing $\binom93=84$. The file's own first version of this section said the double-root family "is the family that carries sign information"; the [r361](REVIEW_r361.md)/[r362](REVIEW_r362.md) correction is right that nonzero-ness *suffices* for the weak rule, and wrong where it was read as making sign information unusable. F45 then instrumented the method at its weakest and described that as the strongest it can ever be.

**Per-row constraints outside the multiplier form exist, and the record holds one.** [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) itself records the pair cap $\sum_k\mu_k^{(ij)}t_{ijk}\le2$ as a valid $G$-invariant linear constraint that $\Pi(\lambda)$ drops, and post F43 records that restoring it breaks the four-zeros count, so a proved per-row inequality outside the family is already written down two sections above the post that says none exists. Beyond that, L104's duality paragraph states the complete certificate as *accumulated type coefficients $\le0$*, not $=0$: the Farkas form, of which annihilation is the thin special case. The honest reading of F43 and F45 together is that the *annihilation* subfamily is spent, which is a smaller statement than "the per-row multiplier method has a floor".

**What I would still grant.** F45's practical conclusion (that closing $d=3$ needs something the per-row annihilation calculus does not supply), is well supported by the $129$ bound, and the "what a successor must clear" line survives if it is restated as *exhibit a constraint outside the per-row annihilation family, or use the signs and the pair cap that this family discards.* The boxed number and the word "floor" do not survive.

## U3, the factorisation, derived here

**Two-root, $\varphi=(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$, so $\gamma=1$, $\beta=-(\nu_a+\nu_b)$, $\alpha=\nu_a\nu_b$.** At row $i$, $R=2[(n-2)\nu_a\nu_b+(\nu_a+\nu_b)\nu_i-\nu_i^2+\nu_i\theta]$. At $i=a$ the $\nu_a^2$ terms cancel and this collapses to $2[(n-1)\nu_a\nu_b+\nu_a\theta]=\mathbf{2\nu_a[(n-1)\nu_b+\theta]}$, and symmetrically at $i=b$. **The bracket index is the root the row is not**, so for a fixed bracket $j$ the qualifying identities are the pairs $\{a,b\}\ni j$ read at the row $i\ne j$, which is exactly **two** per bracket, matching the table's "four-type members: $2$" without my having taken it on report. At $i=c\notin\{a,b\}$ nothing cancels and the right-hand side stays quadratic in $(\nu,\theta)$.

**Double root, $\varphi=(x-\nu_r)^2$.** $R=2[(n-2)\nu_r^2+2\nu_r\nu_i-\nu_i^2+\nu_i\theta]$, which at $i=r$ collapses to $\mathbf{2\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_i+\theta]}$ and at $i\ne r$ does not collapse. The file's two $\rho_1$ computations are precisely two of these six, and it handled them by substituting the $\Pi$-bound and reading a sign, which is the evidence, inside the file, that they do not factor.

**The rider.** The prefactor is $2\nu_a$ or $2\nu_i$, nonzero by kernel-freeness, so it never obstructs; the bracket $(n-1)\nu_1+\theta>0$ always, given $\nu_1>0$ and $\theta>0$; $(n-1)\nu_3+\theta$ vanishes exactly at $\theta=(n-1)|\nu_3|$, the $\Pi$-bound attained. All confirmed. What is **not** confirmed is the sentence "both families' right-hand sides factor": nine of eighteen do, nine do not, and the corrected table silently covers only the nine that do.

## U4, the dichotomy holds

Suppose $(n-1)\nu_2+\theta\le0$. With $\theta>0$ this gives $\nu_2\le-\theta/(n-1)<0$, so $\nu_2<0$ and $\theta\le(n-1)|\nu_2|$. Since $\nu_3<\nu_2<0$ we have $|\nu_2|<|\nu_3|$ (strict, because the eigenvalues are distinct) so $\theta<(n-1)|\nu_3|$ and $(n-1)\nu_3+\theta<0$: the $j=3$ bracket is nonzero and its three identities apply. The comparison goes the right way, the boundary case $(n-1)\nu_2+\theta=0$ falls in this branch and still gives $\nu_2<0$ strictly, and no hypothesis on the sign of $\nu_2$ is used anywhere. The three identities bought in either branch are the two four-type members plus the double-root diagonal $i=r=j$, which is the count I derived above independently.

**The available strengthening.** What the argument actually needs is nonzero-ness, not positivity, so the split can be made at the vanishing locus rather than at zero: the $j=2$ bracket vanishes only when $\nu_2<0$ and $\theta=(n-1)|\nu_2|$ exactly, and on that locus $\Pi$ is strict. Off it, both brackets are nonzero and **six** identities apply, not three. The recorded dichotomy discards that for no reason.

*One accounting item I could not reproduce.* The withdrawal note says "the $\nu_2<0$ branch costs **four** identities, not the one recorded". By the bracket classification the $j=2$ bracket governs exactly three of the nine factored identities, so either the fourth is one of the nine unfactored right-hand sides or the figure counts something else, a pair cut, say. It is a count inside an argument and should be stated with its derivation or dropped.

## U5, the count-dependent claim, named

**Depends on the number, and must go: the conviction of $130$.** Both the bounds-relaxations withdrawal, "$130$ is unreachable: taking all eighteen identities nonzero floors at $141$, so that row drew on something outside the family", and post F45's "$130<141$ is below the floor, so that row was drawing on something outside the family" are inferences with no content beyond the arithmetic $130<141$. With twenty-seven identities in the family the annihilation floor is somewhere in $[129,141]$, and $130$ lies inside it, so the row may well have been reachable inside the family after all. **The withdrawal of the $130$ row may still be right (it was never derived) but the stated reason is void and should be replaced by "unsourced" rather than "below the floor".**

**Depends on the number, and is the post's title: "the per-row multiplier method floors at $141$ supports".** The post disclaims the count in its own body and then puts it in the headline, in a box, and in a sentence beginning "THAT IS A FLOOR ON THE METHOD, NOT A COUNT". A number asserted to be optimal over all spectra and all sharpenings is a claim, not scaffolding, and this one is false.

**Does not depend on the numbers: everything else.** "The proved figure is $194$", "$178$–$179$ under $\nu_2>0$" and the step tables are used only to support "some support survives", which the $129$ union bound now proves directly. The three arguments the file names as what carries independently (the bracket classification, the dichotomy, and the exhaustion), are indeed arguments rather than counts; two of them survive this review and **the third, the exhaustion, is the one that fails.**

## What I would record instead

Three replacements, all derived above and none needing a run. **One:** the per-row multiplier at $d=3$ is a line against six points, three on a parabola and three strictly below it; the lines meeting at least two are exactly three tangents and six others, so the apparatus is **twenty-seven** identities per spectrum, nine per row. **Two:** at most $9\times7+18=81$ of the $210$ four-subsets fall to annihilation, so at least $129$ survive, the non-closure of the annihilation calculus, proved rather than enumerated. **Three:** the annihilation calculus is not the per-row calculus, because $T\ge0$ turns a mixed-sign identity into a two-element or one-element forbidden pattern, and because the pair cap is a per-row constraint of no multiplier form that this record already proves.
