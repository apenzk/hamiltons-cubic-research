# Step 9 literature sweep, 2026-09-10

**Run on Fable, from [the position file](2026-09-10-step8-position.md) and nothing else of the record whole.** Appended after every batch. The two targets are [I68](../plan.md), a rank bound at a maximiser of an invariant cubic, and [I37](../plan.md), a gap theorem for a defect functional, **neither had been swept before today.**

## Batch 1, both nodes stripped of names, five searches in parallel

**Searched.** Extreme points of spectrahedra and the rank of SDP optima; critical points of an invariant cubic on symmetric matrices; Z-eigenvectors and critical rank-one approximations of symmetric tensors; Hamilton's ODE and the rank of the curvature operator at a maximiser; Łojasiewicz–Simon and gap theorems for a defect functional.

**I68, the rank of an extremiser.** *The one place "extremisers have low rank" is a THEOREM is semidefinite programming*: the Barvinok–Pataki bound says an extreme point of a spectrahedron cut out by `k` affine constraints has rank `r` with `r(r+1)/2 ≤ k`, and every SDP has an optimum of rank at most `√(2k)` ([Barvinok–Pataki, strengthened by Im–Wolkowicz](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~hwolkowi/henry/reports/BPbound.pdf); [Extreme points of spectrahedra, arXiv:2410.14889](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14889)). **The mechanism is convexity plus few constraints, and the programme's maximiser is a critical point of a CUBIC on a SPHERE, which is neither.** *So the theorem does not transfer as stated. What might transfer is the mechanism, if a maximiser of `θ` could be shown to be an extreme point of some `O(n)`-invariant convex set the record already has, and the invariant convex sets it has are the Böhm–Wilking pinching cones, which are ODE-invariant rather than spectrahedra.* **Recorded as a mechanism to screen, not a route.**

**I68 through symmetric-tensor eigentheory.** *A critical point of `θ` on the sphere is a Z-eigenvector of the symmetric trilinear form `T(W,W,W) = ⟨W, Q(W)⟩`* ([Qi, spectral theory of tensors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.3424); [critical rank-one approximations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.04696)). **That theory bounds the NUMBER of eigenvectors and characterises the best rank-one approximation; it says nothing about the rank of an eigenvector viewed as an operator on a different space**, which is what I68 asks. *Nothing found.*

**I68 through invariant critical-point theory.** [Computing critical points for invariant algebraic systems](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00847) and its hyperoctahedral sequel organise critical points of an invariant polynomial by ORBIT TYPE, through the isotypic decomposition. *That is the right vocabulary and it is already the record's: the stratification by stabiliser this session ran is exactly orbit-type stratification.* **Nothing here bounds rank; what it offers is that a maximiser's orbit type is computable, which the record does by hand.**

**I68 through the Ricci-flow literature.** *The searches return Böhm–Wilking's identity and pinching cones, Hamilton's ODE, and the two-positive-curvature-operator theorem* ([Böhm–Wilking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0606187)). **No source treats the maximiser of the cubic `⟨R, R² + R^#⟩` on the unit sphere of the Weyl space, and none bounds the rank of a curvature operator at a critical point of any invariant.** *This confirms the position file's reading: the question has no literature under the programme's own name.*

**I37 through Łojasiewicz–Simon.** *The gradient inequality is the mechanism behind "energy gaps and discreteness of energies"* ([Feehan–Maridakis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03817)). **And a sharper fact sits underneath it, which is worth more than the inequality**: *`θ` restricted to the unit sphere of `𝒲_n` is a polynomial on a compact real algebraic set, so by the semialgebraic Sard theorem its set of CRITICAL VALUES is finite.* **So "strictly below the ceiling, uniformly" is automatic for any family of critical points that does not attain the ceiling**: a gap of the size of the next critical value down exists by finiteness alone. *I37 therefore reduces from a gap statement to a RIGIDITY statement at the maximum (every critical point attaining the ceiling has `S = 0` or is diagonal) and the constant `c` it asks for is a red herring.* **Recorded as a reframing to screen against [R12](../routes.md) before it is called new.**

## Batch 2, the reframing screened, the invariant cones, and the isotropy form

**Screened.** The finiteness-of-critical-values observation is **NOT on the record**: no findings file, [routes.md](../routes.md) or [plan.md](../plan.md) mentions finite or discrete critical values, and [R12](../routes.md) is a different mechanism entirely, block-diagonalising the Hessian over isotypes at a graded critical point. *So the reframing of [I37](../plan.md) counts as new.* **Its source is [Kurdyka–Orro–Simon, JDG 56 (2000)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-differential-geometry/volume-56/issue-1/Semialgebraic-Sard-Theorem-for-Generalized-Critical-Values/10.4310/jdg/1090347525.full)**: the critical-value set of a semialgebraic map is semialgebraic of lower dimension, hence finite for a polynomial on a compact real algebraic set.

**Searched.** Extreme rays of curvature cones; the Cartan 3-form's isotropic subspaces; cubic trace forms on Jordan and Lie triple systems; Schwahn's cubic invariant.

**Curvature cones.** *Böhm–Wilking's cones are ODE-invariant convex sets built by the transformations `l_{a,b}(R) = R + b Ric(R)∧id + (a−b)/n · scal(R) id∧id`, and on each, `R² + R^#` lies in the interior of the tangent cone* ([Curvature cones and the Ricci flow](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.5636); [Böhm–Wilking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0606187), on hand in [references/](../references/)). **No source bounds the rank of an extreme point of any such cone, and the Barvinok–Pataki mechanism of batch 1 needs the cone to be a spectrahedron, which the Bianchi-constrained cones are not shown to be.** *Nothing found for I68.*

**The isotropy form.** Only definitions returned; the record's own "continuous Mantel theorem" cap `⌊n²/4⌋` on an isotropic `𝔪` ([isotropy-cap.md](../findings/isotropy-cap.md)) is already sharper than anything found outward. *Nothing found.*

**Jordan and Lie triple systems.** *`S²(Λ²)` under `A∘B = ½(AB+BA)` is a Jordan algebra and `tr(W³)` is its cubic trace form, whose sphere-extremals are classical* ([Loos](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/mathematical/loos/Loos_Jordan_triple_system_R_spaces_and_bounded_symmetric_domains.pdf)), **and that is exactly the record's "cube half", already done.** *The `W^#` half is where the Lie structure enters and the Jordan literature has no word on it. Nothing new.*

## Batch 3, three fields the programme has never touched

**Searched.** The higher-dimensional algebraic classification of Weyl tensors; Hamilton's 1986 four-manifold ODE analysis and its strong maximum principle; moment maps, Kostant convexity and Palais' symmetric criticality; quantitative rigidity of Lie algebras and subalgebras.

**Alignment classification.** *[Coley–Milson–Pravda–Pravdová](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0512087) classify Weyl tensors in every dimension by aligned NULL directions and boost weight, recovering Petrov at four.* **Lorentzian throughout: a Riemannian Weyl tensor has no null directions and the classification does not apply.** *What the record does with `Λ²`-rank is the Riemannian analogue, and no source treats it. Nothing transferable.*

**Hamilton's strong maximum principle.** *Along a Ricci flow with `R ≥ 0`, null eigenvectors of `R` persist and `im R` is a Lie subalgebra of `𝔰𝔬(n)` invariant under parallel transport* ([Hamilton, JDG 24 (1986)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-differential-geometry/volume-24/issue-2/Four-manifolds-with-positive-curvature-operator/10.4310/jdg/1214440433.pdf)). **This is the one structural LOW-RANK theorem in the field, and its algebraic core is that `ker R` is preserved by `R² + R^#` under nonnegativity.** *A Weyl tensor is never nonnegative, so the theorem does not apply; whether its algebraic core survives at a critical point of `θ`, where `Q(W)` is proportional to `W` on the Weyl part, is the one mechanism this sweep found that produces a rank statement from criticality.* **Screened against the record: the record's graded/ungraded split (`𝔪` isotropic for the Cartan 3-form, which is `S = 0`), is a different Lie condition on a different subspace, so this is not that.** *Recorded as a candidate route below, with nonnegativity named as the load-bearing hypothesis the route must replace.*

**Moment maps and symmetric criticality.** *Palais' principle is the record's "symmetric criticality construction", already used; Kostant convexity concerns torus orbits and gives no rank statement; the space of invariant cubics on `𝔰𝔲(n)` is one-dimensional, spanned by `i tr(X³)`* ([moment maps and GIT](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/ccirm.4.pdf)). *Nothing new.*

**Lie algebra rigidity.** *[The survey on stability and rigidity of Lie algebras](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.7979) treats deformations of brackets and subalgebras, and Richardson's minimisation of the bracket norm is a moment-map argument.* **These are about which brackets are stable, not about extremals of a cubic. Nothing for I37.**
