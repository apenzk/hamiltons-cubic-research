## C1, linearity

**Confirmed on the definitions.** (A) and (B) are linear in $T$ with $\lambda$-built coefficients and $\theta$-free right-hand sides. (C) rearranges to $\sum_{bc}\lambda_b\lambda_cT_{abc}-2\lambda_a\theta+2\lambda_a^2=0$, linear in the pair. Nonnegativity, total symmetry and hollowness are linear. So $\Pi(\lambda)$ is a polyhedron and $\theta$ is a linear objective on it.

**Attainment holds and the derivation does not say why.** $T\ge0$ with $\sum_{bc}T_{abc}=2(n-2)$ bounds every entry by $2(n-2)$; $\lambda\ne0$ because $\lVert W\rVert=1$, so some $\lambda_a\ne0$ and (C) at that row bounds $\theta$ on both sides. $\Pi(\lambda)$ is therefore compact and the maximum is attained rather than merely a supremum. If $\Pi(\lambda)=\varnothing$ the symbol $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is undefined, and the bounding clause survives only because a critical point with spectrum $\lambda$ supplies feasibility itself, which is how the derivation uses it, so the clause is sound.

**The real defect is an omitted constraint.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) records the pair cap $\sum_\gamma T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\lVert[v_\alpha,v_\beta]\rVert^2\le2$ as a standing fact, and [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) records that the relaxation [Reviewer 324](REVIEW_r324.md) measured was "(A), (B), (C), the proved row cap and $T\ge0$". $\Pi(\lambda)$ as defined carries no cap. Every bound it produces stays valid (dropping a constraint only raises $\theta^\ast$) but "the full pinned system" is the wrong name for it, and the comparison C5 draws against the record's measurements is a comparison between two different polytopes. The cap is linear and $G$-invariant, so it survives the reduction intact: for $a$ in class $i$ and $b$ in class $j$, $\sum_cT_{abc}=\sum_k(m_k-\delta_{ik}-\delta_{jk})\,t_{ijk}\le2$, adding $\binom{d+1}2$ inequalities to the reduced programme at no cost in shape.

## C2, the reduction

**$G$-stability holds, index by index, and the derivation's step 2 is correct.** I re-derived it from the definitions before reading it. Writing $T^\sigma_{abc}=T_{\sigma a,\sigma b,\sigma c}$: (A) is a re-indexing; (B) needs $\lambda_{\sigma b}=\lambda_b$, which holds because $\sigma$ preserves classes, and $\lambda_{\sigma a}=\lambda_a$ on the right; (C) likewise, **with $\theta$ untouched**, which is the point the brief asked to be checked and it survives. Averaging over $G$ preserves nonnegativity, total symmetry and hollowness ($T^\sigma_{aab}=T_{\sigma a,\sigma a,\sigma b}=0$) and, the constraints being affine and $\theta$ held fixed, lands back in $\Pi(\lambda)$ at the same $\theta$. So the maximum is attained on the invariant slice.

**Transitivity holds in every case, including the two the brief flagged.** For $\{i,i,j\}$ the acting group is $S_{m_i}\times S_{m_j}$, not $S_{m_i}$ alone as the derivation's step 4 writes it, $S_{m_i}$ is transitive on ordered pairs of distinct elements of class $i$ and $S_{m_j}$ moves the third index, so the sentence is incomplete though the conclusion is right. For $\{i,i,i\}$, $S_{m_i}$ is transitive on ordered triples of distinct elements. For three distinct classes it is the product's transitivity.

**The correction: the count is wrong when a class is small.** A $G$-invariant hollow totally symmetric $T$ is a nonnegative function on **admissible** multisets (those in which class $i$ appears at most $m_i$ times) so $\{i,i,j\}$ needs $m_i\ge2$ and $\{i,i,i\}$ needs $m_i\ge3$. The number of variables is $\binom{d+2}3$ minus one type per class with $m_i=2$ and $d$ types per class with $m_i=1$. The derivation's step 4 carries this as a parenthetical but its theorem statement, its corollary table and C2 itself all quote the unreduced $\binom{d+2}3$. **This is not cosmetic**: the corrected count is what makes C6's dimension table right or wrong, and it is wrong in exactly the cases where the table is checked below. The value $\theta^\ast$ is unaffected, since an inadmissible type's coefficients $2\mu_j\mu_k$ and $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$ vanish identically at every row, so such a variable appears in no equation and its presence changes nothing.

## C3, the reduced system

**Every coefficient re-derived, and every one is right.** Fix $a$ in class $i$ and $\mu_j=m_j-\delta_{ij}$. Ordered pairs $(b,c)$ with $b\ne c$, both $\ne a$, of classes $\{j,k\}$: for $j\ne k$ the two orders give $\mu_j\mu_k+\mu_k\mu_j=2\mu_j\mu_k$, all automatically distinct; for $j=k$ they are the ordered pairs of distinct indices of one class, $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$. Weighting by $1$, $\lambda_b$ and $\lambda_b\lambda_c$: in $(\mathrm A_i)$ the mixed type carries $2\mu_j\mu_k$; in $(\mathrm C_i)$ it carries $2\mu_j\mu_k\nu_j\nu_k$, both orders sharing the product; in $(\mathrm B_i)$ the two orders contribute $\nu_j$ and $\nu_k$, $\mu_j\mu_k$ times each, so the coefficient is $\mu_j\mu_k(\nu_j+\nu_k)$, **the asymmetry the brief singled out is correct**, and it is exactly $2\mu_j\mu_k$ times the mean $\tfrac{\nu_j+\nu_k}2$, which is the same measure [Reviewer 337](REVIEW_r337.md) reads in [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md). The $j=k$ terms carry $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)$ times $1$, $\nu_j$, $\nu_j^2$.

## C4, the dual certificate

**The direction is right.** With $t\ge0$ and every $F_{ijk}\le0$ the left side is $\le0$, so $2(n-2)\sum\alpha_i-2\sum\beta_i\nu_i-2\sum\gamma_i\nu_i^2+2\theta\sum\gamma_i\nu_i\le0$, and dividing by $2\sum\gamma_i\nu_i>0$ preserves the sense. The multipliers are unsigned because (A), (B), (C) are equalities, which the derivation correctly leaves free.

**Two cases it does not state.** $\sum_i\gamma_i\nu_i<0$ flips the inequality and gives a **lower** bound, which is a real object at $d=2$ (both endpoints of the segment below are certificates of this family). $\sum_i\gamma_i\nu_i=0$ gives no bound on $\theta$ at all, and when its constant side is negative it is instead an **infeasibility** certificate, that is the shape [Lemma L73](../findings/bounds.md) has, since $\lambda_{\max}\le(n-2)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$ carries no $\theta$.

**Stronger than claimed.** $\theta$ is a free variable whose column sits only in the (C) rows, so the dual has the equality constraint $2\sum_i\gamma_i\nu_i=1$ after normalisation, and the sign condition $\sum_i\gamma_i\nu_i>0$ is precisely the statement that a multiplier vector can be so normalised. The family of bounds C4 describes is therefore **exactly the dual feasible set**, and by LP strong duality (primal feasible and bounded, both established in C1), its infimum **equals** $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$. So the certificate family is complete, not merely sufficient; the derivation claims only sufficiency and leaves the better half on the table.

**The accompanying claim is refuted.** *"Every spectral bound on the record is such a certificate"* fails twice. [Lemma L73](../findings/bounds.md) contains no $\theta$ and so cannot be written in the displayed form. ## C6, the rank of the reduced system

**The answer is right and the derivation's argument for it is not.** Its "no third dependency" step reasons that a further dependency would have to re-sum against $\lambda_a^2$ and that $\{2,0,0\}$ is unpinned. That is a statement about one candidate weight, not a proof that no combination of the $3d$ rows annihilates every column; [r331](REVIEW_r331.md)'s completeness theorem concerns the existence of a fourth **relation**, which is a different question from the independence of the three at a fixed spectrum. Here is the proof.

**Setting up.** A left-null vector of the reduced system is, by $G$-symmetry, class-constant; write the unreduced multipliers $\tilde\alpha_i,\tilde\beta_i,\tilde\gamma_i$. The combination $\sum_a[\tilde\alpha_{i(a)}(\mathrm A_a)+\tilde\beta(\mathrm B_a)+\tilde\gamma(\mathrm C_a)]$ acts on totally symmetric $T$ through its symmetrised kernel, and summing over the six permutations of a triple gives, with $L=\sum_{x}\nu_x$ and $E=\sum_{x<y}\nu_x\nu_y$ over the triple,

$$\Psi(\{i,j,k\})=\sum_{x}f_x+L\sum_xg_x+E\sum_xh_x,\qquad f_x=2\tilde\alpha_x-\tilde\beta_x\nu_x+2\tilde\gamma_x\nu_x^2,\quad g_x=\tilde\beta_x-2\tilde\gamma_x\nu_x,\quad h_x=2\tilde\gamma_x,$$

using $\lambda_b\lambda_c=E-\nu_xL+\nu_x^2$ for the index $x$ omitted from the pair. The map $(\tilde\alpha,\tilde\beta,\tilde\gamma)\mapsto(f,g,h)$ is invertible. The null condition is $\Psi=0$ on every admissible type, together with annihilation of the $\theta$ column, $\sum_im_i\nu_ih_i=0$.

**Solving it.** Assume every $m_i\ge3$. From type $\{iii\}$: $f_i=-3\nu_ig_i-3\nu_i^2h_i$. Substituting that into $\{iij\}$ and dividing by $\nu_i-\nu_j\ne0$ gives $-2(g_i-g_j)-4\nu_ih_i+(\nu_i+3\nu_j)h_j=0$; adding this to its $i\leftrightarrow j$ partner leaves $(\nu_j-\nu_i)(h_i-h_j)=0$, so **$h$ is constant**, $h_i=h$. Back-substituting, $g_i+\tfrac{3h}2\nu_i$ is constant, $=c$, and $f_i=-3c\nu_i+\tfrac{3h}2\nu_i^2$. The remaining types $\{ijk\}$ are then satisfied identically: $\sum f=-3cL+\tfrac{3h}2(L^2-2E)$, $\sum g=3c-\tfrac{3h}2L$, $\sum h=3h$, and the three terms cancel. The $\theta$ column gives $h\sum_im_i\nu_i=h\operatorname{tr}W=0$, automatic. **So the null space is exactly two-dimensional**, $(c,h)$, and $(c,h)=(-1,0)$ and $(0,-2)$ are the addendum's two dependencies. **Rank $=3d-2$**, and the feasible affine space has dimension $\binom{d+2}3+1-(3d-2)=\binom{d+2}3-3d+3$: **$1$ at $d=2$, $4$ at $d=3$**, $11$ at $d=4$. The table is right.

**But "never forced" is false, and the hypothesis it needs is the one C2's count drops.** Every step above used type $\{iii\}$ and both $\{iij\}$ and $\{ijj\}$, so the theorem is *rank $=3d-2$ when every $m_i\ge3$*. When a class is smaller the type set shrinks while the row count does not, and the rank can meet the column count. Screened on systems I built from the reduced coefficients: at $n=8$ with multiplicities $(26,2)$ there are $3$ types, the rank is $4$, and the **feasible dimension is $0$**, $\theta$ is forced outright. At $n=12$ with multiplicities $(30,35,1)$ there are $7$ types, rank $7$, feasible dimension $1$ rather than the table's $4$. With every $m_i\ge3$ the screen returns $(4,1)$ at $d=2$ and $(7,4)$ at $d=3$ as the theorem says. So the corollary must read: *the maximisation is genuine at every spectrum all of whose multiplicities are at least $3$*, and the record's sentence the derivation sets out to correct ("what $\theta$ does feasibility then force"), is right, not wrong, at the multiplicities where a class is small.

**Those small-multiplicity spectra are not idle cases**: a simple eigenvalue and a kernel class are both common, and [padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$](../findings/target.md) has a multiplicity-$1$ class at every $n$.

## The two-eigenvalue case, closed

**The $d=2$ polytope is a segment, and its four facets are elementary.** This follows from C6 and it is where the reduction earns its keep. Write the spectrum as $x>0$ with multiplicity $p$ and $y<0$ with multiplicity $q=N-p$ (two distinct values with $\operatorname{tr}W=0$ and $\lVert W\rVert=1$ force opposite signs), so $px+qy=0$, $px^2+qy^2=1$, hence $xy=-1/N$, and put $e=x-y$, $\rho=x/\lvert y\rvert=q/p$. Row $i$ of the reduced system is three moments (mass, mean of $\tfrac{\lambda_b+\lambda_c}2$, mean of $\lambda_b\lambda_c$), against a measure on the three pair-points $(x,x^2)$, $(\tfrac{x+y}2,xy)$, $(y,y^2)$, which are never collinear, so each row determines its three masses as affine functions of $\theta$. Solving both rows and matching, which is consistent precisely by $px+qy=0$:

$$t_{111}=\frac{2\big[x\theta-x^2+2xy+(n-2)y^2\big]}{(p-1)(p-2)e^2},\quad t_{112}=\frac{-2x\big[\theta+(n-1)y\big]}{(p-1)q\,e^2},\quad t_{122}=\frac{2x\big[\theta+(n-1)x\big]}{q(q-1)e^2},\quad t_{222}=\frac{2\big[y\theta+(n-2)x^2+2xy-y^2\big]}{(q-1)(q-2)e^2}.$$

*Screened symbolically* against the reduced system at $(n,p)=(6,6)$, $(8,12)$, $(8,4)$, $(8,8)$, $(10,20)$, $(12,30)$, $(6,5)$, $(7,9)$: all four identities exact.

**The four nonnegativity conditions are four bounds the record already knows, plus one it does not.**

- $t_{112}\ge0\iff\theta\le(n-1)\lvert y\rvert=(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$, **the $\Pi$-bound**, recovered as a facet.
- $t_{222}\ge0\iff\theta\le\lvert y\rvert\big[(n-2)\rho^2-2\rho-1\big]$, the new one, and the one that binds at the extremum.
- $t_{111}\ge0\iff\theta\ge\lvert y\rvert\big[\rho+2-(n-2)/\rho\big]$ and $t_{122}\ge0\iff\theta\ge-(n-1)x$, two lower bounds.
- Nonemptiness of the interval requires $\rho\le n-2$, which is **[Lemma L73](../findings/bounds.md)**, $\lambda_{\max}\le(n-2)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$, recovered as the feasibility boundary; the other compatibility reduces to $(n-2)\rho^2-(n+1)\rho+(n-2)\ge0$, whose discriminant $(5-n)(3n-3)$ is $\le0$ for $n\ge5$, so it never binds.

**Hence, at every two-eigenvalue spectrum,** $\theta^\ast(\lambda)=\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert\cdot\min\{\,n-1,\ (n-2)\rho^2-2\rho-1\,\}$ with $\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert=(\rho N)^{-1/2}$. The two branches cross where $(n-2)\rho^2-2\rho-n=0$, i.e. at $\rho=n/(n-2)$ exactly. On $\rho\le n/(n-2)$ the value is $\big[(n-2)\rho^2-2\rho-1\big]\rho^{-1/2}N^{-1/2}$, whose derivative carries the factor $\tfrac32(n-2)\rho^2-\rho+\tfrac12$ with discriminant $1-3(n-2)<0$, so it is strictly increasing; on $\rho\ge n/(n-2)$ the value is $(n-1)(\rho N)^{-1/2}$, strictly decreasing. **The maximum over $\rho$ is at $\rho=n/(n-2)$ and equals**

$$\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{\rho N}}=\ (n-1)\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n\cdot\tfrac12n(n-1)}}=\frac{\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}}{n}=\theta_0(n).$$

**The equality case is the balanced sphere product and nothing else.** $\rho=q/p=n/(n-2)$ with $p+q=N$ forces $p=n(n-2)/4$, $q=n^2/4$, which are integers only at even $n$; at $\rho=n/(n-2)$ both $t_{112}=0$ and $t_{222}=0$ bind simultaneously, and with $T\ge0$ these say $\langle[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h],\mathfrak m\rangle=0$ and $\langle[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m],\mathfrak m\rangle=0$, so $\mathfrak h=E_{\max}$ is a subalgebra and $(\mathfrak{so}(n),\mathfrak h)$ is a symmetric pair with $\dim\mathfrak h=n(n-2)/4$, whereupon the record's own dimension step ($p+q=n$, $\binom p2+\binom q2=n(n-2)/4$ forces $p=q$, [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) defect (iv)) gives $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$.

**Small multiplicities are excluded rather than assumed away.** $p=1$ gives $\rho=N-1>n-2$ and $p=2$ gives $\rho=(N-2)/2>n-2$ for $n\ge5$, both killed by Lemma L73. $q=1$ forces $p=n-2$ against $p=N-1$, impossible for $n\ge5$. $q=2$ leaves $t_{122}=-2[y+(n-2)x]/(p(y-x))<0$, since $-(N-2)/2+(n-2)=-(n-1)(n-4)/4<0$ for $n\ge5$. So every feasible two-eigenvalue spectrum has $\min(p,q)\ge3$ and the closed form applies.

**The omitted pair cap changes none of this.** At $d=2$ the three class pair-marginals $(p-2)t_{111}+q\,t_{112}$, $(p-1)t_{112}+(q-1)t_{122}$ and $p\,t_{122}+(q-2)t_{222}$ each have **zero $\theta$-coefficient** (the third by $px+qy=0$) so they are constant along the segment, and the genuine critical point on it already satisfies the cap. The cap is therefore implied at $d=2$, which is [r338](REVIEW_r338.md)'s doubly-stochastic reading of the pair marginal seen from the other side.

**So, offered for Gate 2 as a result in its own right.** *Let $n\ge5$ and let $W\in\mathcal W_n$ with $\lVert W\rVert=1$ satisfy $Q(W)=\theta W$ and have exactly two distinct eigenvalues. Then $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$, with equality only if the multiplicities are $\big(n(n-2)/4,\ n^2/4\big)$, hence only at even $n$, and there only at the balanced sphere product $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$.* It rests on nothing but (A), (B), (C), $T\ge0$ and the reduction; **no subalgebra hypothesis, no gradedness, no $\Delta=0$, no diagonality, no [Lemma L70](../findings/bounds.md) saturation.**

**Where it sits against the record.** [Lemma L23](../results.md) and the subalgebra family give $\theta=\tfrac2n\sqrt{rN/(N-r)}$ and their maximum $\theta_0(n)$ **only for $\mathfrak h$ a subalgebra**; [Lemma L71](../findings/bounds.md) reaches the balanced product **from Lemma L70 saturation**; [graded-half.md](../findings/graded-half.md) reaches it **within the graded enumeration**. The statement above needs none of those hypotheses. Two deduplications come with it, and both look like corrections the record wants:

- **Lemma L23's closed form *is* the $\Pi$-bound, this is AGREEMENT, not a discovery.** $\tfrac2n\sqrt{rN/(N-r)}=\tfrac{2N}{n}\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert=(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$ identically at a two-eigenvalue spectrum with $r=\operatorname{mult}(\lambda_{\max})$, which is exactly what [post F40](../findings/fence-posts.md) already states in its title. I reached it independently from the $t_{112}\ge0$ facet and confirm it.
- **But post F40's equality case is wrong, and my facet says why.** It records the $\dim E_+$ form as "saturated exactly at the balanced sphere products". $t_{112}=0\iff\theta=(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$, and $t_{112}=0$ with $T\ge0$ says $\langle[E_{\max},E_{\max}],E_{\min}\rangle=0$, i.e. $E_{\max}$ is a **subalgebra**, so **every member of the subalgebra family saturates it**, not the balanced products alone. ## What the derivation missed, beyond the per-claim verdicts

**Its explanatory corollary is refuted by the record it cites.** The corollary *"why the fixed-spectrum LP is exact at degenerate spectra and loose at generic ones"* argues that at small $d$ "the feasible segment is short and $\max\theta$ is pinned near the true value". **The segment is not short.** At the balanced $S^3\times S^3$ spectrum I get the null direction $(\delta t_{111},\delta t_{112},\delta t_{122},\delta t_{222},\delta\tau)=(\tfrac{18}5,-\tfrac85,1,-\tfrac67,300)$ through the recorded point, feasible for $u\in[-\tfrac1{36},0]$, giving $\theta\in[\sqrt{10}/18,\ \sqrt{10}/3]=[0.175682,\ 1.054093]$, a factor of six, and **exactly [Reviewer 334](REVIEW_r334.md)'s screened interval $[0.17568,\,1.054093]$ recorded in [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md)**, which the same paragraph of the record calls "an interval of width $0.878$". So the mechanism proposed is contradicted by the measurement it is meant to explain, and the record even flags that this record once "deflated" the sharpness. *The reduction does independently confirm r334's numbers by hand, which is worth recording on its own.*

**The correct mechanism, from the facets.** The LP is exact at $d=2$ not because the segment is short but because the segment's **upper endpoint is where $t_{112}$ and $t_{222}$ vanish**, and those are precisely the two structure-constant masses that a symmetric pair kills. Exactness at a catalogued point is exactness of a facet whose equality case is the point's own Lie structure, the same reason [bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) gives for Lemma L70's exactness at the balanced products, now visible as a statement about which facet binds.

**A cheap corollary the derivation should carry.** $\theta^\ast$ is $2$-homogeneous in nothing and $1$-homogeneous in $\lambda$: replacing $\lambda$ by $s\lambda$ scales (B) and (C) consistently only if $\theta\mapsto s\theta$ and $T$ is fixed, so $\theta^\ast(s\lambda)=s\,\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ for $s>0$, which is why the whole $d=2$ answer collapses to a function of the single ratio $\rho$ and a scale.

**One presentational error.** The dual-certificate corollary in [derive-sp1.md](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ap-code-private-hamiltons-cubic/d494340f-1dff-4003-801d-ae0e180c75c5/scratchpad/derive-sp1.md) writes $20\sum_i\alpha_i$, silently specialising $2(n-2)$ to $n=12$ inside a statement that is otherwise general in $n$.

## What I could not settle

**Whether the rank is $3d-2$ in general.** I proved it when every $m_i\ge3$, and screened three degenerate multiplicity vectors where it happened to remain $3d-2$ while the column count fell below it. Whether $\operatorname{rank}=\min\{3d-2,\ \#\text{types}+1\}$ in general is open, and it is what decides the "forced" cases beyond the $(26,2)$ witness.

**The $d=3$ programme.** Feasible dimension $4$, ten variables, nine equalities of rank seven: a hand computation of $\theta^\ast$ at a general three-eigenvalue spectrum is tractable by the same pair-point method, each row is again three moments against a measure on **six** pair-points now, so the rows no longer determine their masses and a genuine vertex enumeration begins. That is where the conjecture's remaining spectra live, and I did not attempt it.
