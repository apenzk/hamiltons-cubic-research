## A. Statement fidelity

- **`specFn`.** `∑ i, g (λ i) • (innerₛₗ ℝ (e i)).smulRight (e i)`, which applied to $x$ is $\sum_i g(\lambda_i)\langle e_i,x\rangle e_i$ (`specFn_apply`). This is $g(T)$ in the orthonormal eigenbasis `hT.eigenvectorBasis hn`.
- **`specFn_apply_of_eigen`.** For any $v$ with $Tv=\mu v$, symmetry gives $\lambda_i\langle e_i,v\rangle=\mu\langle e_i,v\rangle$, so each coefficient $g(\lambda_i)\langle e_i,v\rangle$ equals $g(\mu)\langle e_i,v\rangle$ (zero case split), and Parseval reassembles $g(\mu)v$. Correct, including repeated eigenvalues and non-basis eigenvectors.
- **`specFn_unique`.** Hypothesis `∀ μ v, T v = μ • v → S v = g μ • v`. It is satisfiable, `specFn` meets it by the previous theorem, so the characterisation is non-vacuous. **At $v=0$** the premise holds for every $\mu$ and the conclusion reads $S0=g(\mu)\cdot0=0$, true for every linear $S$, so the zero vector adds no constraint and is harmless. The proof checks $S$ and $g(T)$ on the eigenbasis, which is a basis, so equality follows. Since the hypothesis mentions no basis, this makes $g(T)$ independent of the eigenbasis chosen, as claimed.
- **`specFn_conj`.** Claim $g(U^{-1}TU)=U^{-1}g(T)U$ for $U$ a linear equivalence with $U^{-1}TU$ symmetric. If $U^{-1}TUv=\mu v$ then $T(Uv)=\mu Uv$, so $U^{-1}g(T)Uv=U^{-1}g(\mu)Uv=g(\mu)v$, and uniqueness applied to the symmetric $U^{-1}TU$ closes it. **The claim that orthogonality is not needed is sound**: orthogonality only supplies the symmetry of $U^{-1}TU$, which the theorem takes as a hypothesis. Non-orthogonal instances exist (any invertible $U$ with $T=\mathrm{id}$), and the statement is true for them.
- **`specFn_smul`.** Hypothesis `hg : ∀ c x, 0 ≤ c → g (c * x) = c * g x`. **At $c=0$**: `hg 0 0` gives $g(0)=0$, and $0\cdot T=0$ so $g(0\cdot T)=g(0)\,\mathrm{id}=0=0\cdot g(T)$. The proof handles this by splitting $\mu v=0$, correct. At $c>0$, $Tv=(\mu/c)v$ and $g(\mu)=c\,g(\mu/c)$. The separate hypothesis `hcT` that $cT$ is symmetric is redundant but harmless.
- **`inner_sub_eigen` and `inner_specFn_sub`.** $\langle e_i,(B-A)f_j\rangle=\langle e_i,Bf_j\rangle-\langle Ae_i,f_j\rangle=(\nu_j-\lambda_i)\langle e_i,f_j\rangle$, and the same with $g(A)$ and $g(B)$ using `specFn_isSymmetric` and `specFn_apply_of_eigen`. Both exactly as stated, $e$ the eigenbasis of $A$, $f$ that of $B$.
- **`hsSq`.** $\sum_i\lVert Xb_i\rVert^2$ over `stdOrthonormalBasis`, which is $\operatorname{tr}(X^{\top}X)$, the Hilbert–Schmidt (Frobenius) norm squared. `sum_norm_sq_eq` expands it in any two orthonormal bases, and `sum_norm_sq_basis_indep` swaps the basis for symmetric $X$, which covers both uses.
- **`hsSq_specFn_sub_le`.** Summing the mixed identity: $\lVert g(B)-g(A)\rVert_{HS}^2=\sum_{ij}(g(\nu_j)-g(\lambda_i))^2\langle e_i,f_j\rangle^2\le L^2\sum_{ij}(\nu_j-\lambda_i)^2\langle e_i,f_j\rangle^2=L^2\lVert B-A\rVert_{HS}^2$. **The constant is $L^2$ against squared norms**, correct. The hypothesis $\lvert g(x)-g(y)\rvert\le L\lvert x-y\rvert$ at $x=1,y=0$ forces $L\ge0$, so the unsquared form with constant $L$ follows by square roots.
- **`norm_apply_sq_le` and `hsSq_le_opNorm`.** $\lVert Xv\rVert^2\le\lVert X\rVert_{HS}^2\lVert v\rVert^2$ by the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz, and $\lVert X\rVert_{HS}^2\le n\lVert X\rVert^2$ termwise. Both correct.

**Concrete check.** $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $e_1=(1,0)$, $e_2=(0,1)$. $B=R_\theta AR_\theta^{\top}$ with $c=\cos\theta$, $s=\sin\theta$, $f_1=(c,s)$ for $\nu=1$ and $f_2=(-s,c)$ for $\nu=-1$. $g(x)=\max(-x,0)$, which is $1$-Lipschitz and positively homogeneous.

- $g(A)=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$, $g(B)=f_2f_2^{\top}$, so $g(B)-g(A)=\begin{pmatrix}s^2&-sc\\-sc&-s^2\end{pmatrix}$ and $B-A=\begin{pmatrix}-2s^2&2sc\\2sc&2s^2\end{pmatrix}$.
- Mixed identity, all four entries: $\langle e_1,\cdot f_1\rangle=0=(0-0)c$. $\langle e_2,\cdot f_1\rangle=-s=(0-1)s$. $\langle e_1,\cdot f_2\rangle=-s=(1-0)(-s)$. $\langle e_2,\cdot f_2\rangle=0=(1-1)c$. All pass.
- `inner_sub_eigen` at $(2,1)$: $(B-A)f_1=(0,2s)$, so $\langle e_2,\cdot\rangle=2s=(1-(-1))s$. Passes.
- Lipschitz bound: $\lVert g(B)-g(A)\rVert_{HS}^2=2s^4+2s^2c^2=2s^2\le8s^2=\lVert B-A\rVert_{HS}^2$ at $L=1$. Passes, with room.
- Eigenvector signs and Mathlib's decreasing ordering do not matter: both sides of each identity are bilinear in $(e_i,f_j)$ and pair the eigenvalue with its own vector.
