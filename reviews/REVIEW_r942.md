## 1. (a)'s subalgebra

The claim is that the characters of [L120](../findings/second-order.md#lemma-l120-l_m-is-block-diagonal-over-the-sign-characters-at-every-diagonal-critical-point)'s decomposition trivial on $H=\bigcap_i\ker\chi_{Q_i}$ are exactly $1$ and the $\chi_{Q_i}$. The characters present in $\mathcal W_n$ are the trivial one, the four-set characters $\chi_{Q'}$ and the pair characters $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$. The $\chi_{Q_i}$ and $1$ are trivial on $H$ by definition, so only the witnesses for the rest need checking.

- **Four-set $\chi_{Q'}$, $Q'$ not among the $Q_i$, with an index $a\in Q'\setminus\bigcup_iQ_i$.** The single flip at $a$ is $+1$ on every $\chi_{Q_j}$ since $a\notin Q_j$, and $-1$ on $\chi_{Q'}$. Follows.
- **Four-set $\chi_{Q'}$ with $Q'\subset\bigcup_iQ_i$.** $Q'$ meets some $Q_i$. Since $\lvert Q'\rvert=\lvert Q_i\rvert=4$ and $Q'\ne Q_i$, the set $Q_i\setminus Q'$ is nonempty, so the phrase "proper nonempty subset" is automatically satisfied by any $Q_i$ that $Q'$ meets. This covers $Q'$ meeting several $Q_i$: one $Q_i$ suffices. The witness $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$ with $a\in Q'\cap Q_i$, $b\in Q_i\setminus Q'$ has both flipped indices in $Q_i$, so it is $+1$ on $\chi_{Q_i}$, and $+1$ on $\chi_{Q_j}$ for $j\ne i$ since $Q_j\cap Q_i=\emptyset$. On $\chi_{Q'}$ exactly one flipped index lies in $Q'$, giving $-1$. Follows.
- **Pair $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$.** The pair is unordered, so the two cases "$a\notin\bigcup_iQ_i$" and "$a\in Q_i$" are exhaustive after naming. If some index lies outside the union, call it $a$: the flip at $a$ lies in every kernel and is $-1$ on the pair character. Otherwise $a\in Q_i$ for some $i$, and $Q_i\setminus\{a,b\}$ has at least two elements, so $c$ exists. The witness $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$ flips two indices of $Q_i$, hence lies in every kernel, and on $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$ it gives $(-1)(+1)=-1$ because $c\ne b$. This covers $b\in Q_i$, $b\in Q_j$ with $j\ne i$ and $b$ outside the union alike. Follows.

The list is complete, so $\mathcal D_n\oplus\bigoplus_iV_{Q_i}$ is the fixed space of $H$ and a subalgebra, since $\circ$ is $O(n)$-equivariant. A shorter route, which I checked as an independent confirmation: in $\widehat\Gamma\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^n$ the annihilator of $\bigcap_i\ker\chi_{Q_i}$ is the subgroup generated by the $\chi_{Q_i}$, whose elements are $\chi_{\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_i}$ with $\lvert\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_i\rvert=4\lvert I\rvert$, and only $\lvert I\rvert\le1$ occurs in $\mathcal W_n$.

**The products.** $\nu_i\circ\nu_j$ for $i\ne j$ has character $\chi_{Q_i}\chi_{Q_j}=\chi_{Q_i\cup Q_j}$, an eight-element set. Under the full sign group this character is distinct from every character in $\mathcal W_n$, including at $n=8$ where $Q_i\cup Q_j=[n]$ (the single flip is $-1$ on it), so the isotypic component is zero and $\nu_i\circ\nu_j=0$. $\nu_i\circ\nu_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$ is L220's step 2, which depends on $\nu_i$ alone. Both follow.

## 2. (a)'s chain

With the products of item 1, criticality $W\circ W=\theta W$ in the subalgebra splits by character into $D\circ D+\sum_j\nu_j\circ\nu_j=\theta D$ and $2D\circ\nu_i=\theta\nu_i$ for each $i$. The $\chi_{Q_i}$ equation is L220's step 3 verbatim, and the trivial one gains the terms $\nu_j\circ\nu_j$, $j\ne i$.

- **Step 4.** $L_{r_i}\nu_i\in V_{Q_i}$ by L120, and for $\nu'\in V_{Q_i}$, $\langle L_{r_i}\nu_i,\nu'\rangle=\langle r_i,\nu_i\circ\nu'\rangle$ with $\nu_i\circ\nu'\in\Pi_{Q_i}$ by polarising step 2. $r_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$ by definition, so $L_{r_i}\nu_i=0$. Follows from the stated hypotheses.
- **Step 5.** Uses only $q_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$, $L_{q_i}\nu_i=\tfrac\theta2\nu_i$, $\nu_i\ne0$, $\theta>0$. Gives $u_i^2=\theta^2/3$. Follows.
- **Step 6.** Pairing the trivial equation with $q_i$ adds $\sum_{j\ne i}\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle$. $\nu_j\circ\nu_j$ is supported on pairs inside $Q_j$ and $q_i$ on pairs inside $Q_i$, and the $\mathcal D_n$ inner product is the sum over pairs (L220's unit convention $2(x^2+y^2+z^2)=1$ is that sum over the six pairs of $Q$). With $Q_i\cap Q_j=\emptyset$ no pair is shared, so each term is $0$. Follows, and step 6 reads exactly as in L220.
- **Steps 7 and 8.** They involve only $q_i$ and $r_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$. Follow unchanged.
- **Step 9.** $1=\lVert D\rVert^2+\sum_jN_j=u_i^2+\lVert r_i\rVert^2+\sum_jN_j$, so $\lVert r_i\rVert^2=1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-\sum_jN_j\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3-N_i$. Steps 6 to 8 give $\tfrac{\theta^3}6-\tfrac\theta2N_i\le\tfrac\theta3\lVert r_i\rVert^2$, whose right side is increasing in $\lVert r_i\rVert^2$, so the smaller value only strengthens it. Substituting gives $\tfrac5{18}\theta^2\le\tfrac13+\tfrac16N_i$, and $N_i\le1-\tfrac{\theta^2}3$ still holds since $\lVert r_i\rVert^2\ge0$ and $N_j\ge0$. Then $\tfrac13\theta^2\le\tfrac12$. Follows.

(a) claims no equality case, so none is owed.

## 3. (b)1, the equations

Take $Q_1=\{a,b,c,d\}$, $Q_2=\{a,b,e,f\}$, so $Q_3=\{c,d,e,f\}$ and $n\ge6$ is exactly what this needs.

- **The four characters.** The proof asserts the list without witnesses. It holds by duality: the annihilator of $\ker\chi_{Q_1}\cap\ker\chi_{Q_2}$ in $\widehat\Gamma$ is the group generated by $\chi_{Q_1},\chi_{Q_2}$, which is $\{1,\chi_{Q_1},\chi_{Q_2},\chi_{Q_1\triangle Q_2}=\chi_{Q_3}\}$, and all four occur in $\mathcal W_n$. So $\mathcal S$ is the fixed space and a subalgebra. Follows, with one sentence of justification missing from the text.
- **The split.** $W\circ W=D\circ D+\sum_i\nu_i\circ\nu_i+2\sum_iD\circ\nu_i+2\sum_{i<j}\nu_i\circ\nu_j$. By character, $D\circ D$ and $\nu_i\circ\nu_i$ are trivial, $D\circ\nu_i$ is $\chi_{Q_i}$, and $\nu_j\circ\nu_k$ is $\chi_{Q_j}\chi_{Q_k}=\chi_{Q_i}$ for $\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}$, since $Q_j\triangle Q_k=Q_i$ for each labelling. So the trivial component is $D\circ D+\sum_i\nu_i\circ\nu_i=\theta D$ and the $\chi_{Q_i}$ component is $2D\circ\nu_i+2\nu_j\circ\nu_k=\theta\nu_i$, factor $2$ from the two orderings of the cross term. Follows.
- **$\tau=0$ at $\nu_3=0$.** Immediate from linearity of $\tau$ in $\nu_3$. The proof's route through the $\chi_{Q_3}$ equation gives the stronger fact $\nu_1\circ\nu_2=0$, correct and unused. Follows. The same linearity gives $\tau=0$ whenever any one $\nu_i$ vanishes, so the labelling of the vanishing block is immaterial.

## 4. (b)2, the pinning inequality

- **$\langle D\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle$.** $r_i=D-q_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$ and $\langle r_i\circ\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\langle r_i,\nu_i\circ\nu_i\rangle=0$. Here $r_i$ contains the parts of $q_j$ outside $\Pi_{Q_i}$, which does not matter since only orthogonality to $\Pi_{Q_i}$ is used. Follows.
- **Sign of $\tau$.** Pairing $2D\circ\nu_i+2\nu_j\circ\nu_k=\theta\nu_i$ with $\nu_i$ gives $2\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle+2\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_k,\nu_i\rangle=\theta N_i$. The trilinear form $\langle x\circ y,z\rangle$ is fully symmetric (the same symmetry L220's step 4 uses), so $\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_k,\nu_i\rangle=\tau$ for all three $i$, with one and the same sign. Hence $\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau$. Follows.
- **Direction.** L220's step 5 gives the spectrum $\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_i$ of $L_{q_i}$ on the two-dimensional $V_{Q_i}$ for any $q_i\in\Pi_{Q_i}$, so $\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_iN_i$. With $\tau\le0$, $\tfrac\theta2N_i\le\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_iN_i$, and dividing by $N_i>0$ gives $u_i\ge\theta/\sqrt3$. The direction is right, and $\tau\le0$ is used exactly here and nowhere else. Follows.

## 5. (b)3, two constants

- **The norm identity and its normalisation.** Neither L220 nor L221 states the norm on $V_Q$. The identity $2\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}(2a^2-b^2-c^2)^2=\tfrac34(2(a^2+b^2+c^2))^2$ gives $\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ only under $\lVert\nu\rVert^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)$. That normalisation is forced by the record: writing $\langle q,q'\rangle=\alpha\sum xx'$ and $\langle\nu,\nu'\rangle=\beta\sum aa'$, L220's step 6 identity $\langle\nu\circ\nu,q\rangle=\langle L_q\nu,\nu\rangle$ with the pattern of step 2 and $L_q=\tfrac12G$ of L121 reads $3\alpha\sum xa^2=3\beta\sum xa^2$, using $v^{\mathsf T}Gv=6(xa^2+yb^2+zc^2)$ at $a+b+c=0$, $x+y+z=0$. So $\beta=\alpha=2$. L220's equality case ($q=\nu=t(2,-1,-1)$, $t^2=\tfrac1{24}$, $\lVert q\rVert^2=\lVert\nu\rVert^2=\tfrac12$) agrees. *A normalisation-free derivation of the only direction used*: $\nu\circ\nu\in\Pi_Q$, so $\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert=\sup_{\hat q}\langle\nu\circ\nu,\hat q\rangle=\sup_{\hat q}\langle L_{\hat q}\nu,\nu\rangle\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$ by step 5. Follows. *Screen (seconds, sympy, reported as a screen): the Lean identity, $v^{\mathsf T}Gv=6\sum xa^2$ and $2\sum Xx=6\sum xa^2$ all expand to $0$.*
- **Each pair of the three sets shares exactly one pair.** $Q_1\cap Q_2=\{a,b\}$, $Q_1\cap Q_3=\{c,d\}$, $Q_2\cap Q_3=\{e,f\}$, each of size two, hence exactly one common pair. Follows.
- **The angle bound for every shape.** A unit pattern has entries $x,y,z$ with $x+y+z=0$, $x^2+y^2+z^2=\tfrac12$. Maximising $x^2$ gives $y=z=-x/2$ and $x^2=\tfrac13$, so every entry satisfies $\lvert K_{ab}\rvert\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ whatever the shape. The inner product of two unit patterns on $Q_i$, $Q_j$ is the product of their entries on the single shared pair, so $\lvert\langle\hat q_i,\hat q_j\rangle\rvert\le\tfrac13$, attained when both patterns are the $S^2\times S^2$ shape with the large entry on that pair. Follows.
- **The cross-term bound.** $\lvert\langle\nu_j\circ\nu_j,q_i\rangle\rvert\le\lVert\nu_j\circ\nu_j\rVert\,u_i\,\tfrac13\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}6u_iN_j=\tfrac{u_i}{2\sqrt3}N_j$, valid for either sign of the term. Follows.

## 6. (b)4, the per-block chain

- **The equality.** Pairing the trivial equation with $q_i$: $\theta\langle D,q_i\rangle=\theta u_i^2$. $\langle D\circ D,q_i\rangle=P(q_i)+2\langle r_i,q_i\circ q_i\rangle+\langle L_{q_i}r_i,r_i\rangle$, and the middle term is $0$ since $\Pi_{Q_i}$ is a subalgebra (L220 step 2) and $r_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$. $\langle\nu_i\circ\nu_i,q_i\rangle=\langle L_{q_i}\nu_i,\nu_i\rangle=\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau$ by item 4. The other two $\nu_j\circ\nu_j$ give the sum. Every term and sign of the displayed equality is correct. Follows.
- **$P(q_i)\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_i^3$.** L220 step 7 is homogeneous of degree three. Follows.
- **$\langle L_{q_i}r_i,r_i\rangle\le\tfrac{u_i}{\sqrt3}(t-u_i^2)$.** Step 8 needs only $r_i\perp\Pi_{Q_i}$, and its bound uses $\max(x,y,z)\ge0$ to drop the entries of $r_i$ outside $Q_i$'s rows, which holds as $x+y+z=0$. $\lVert r_i\rVert^2=\lVert D\rVert^2-u_i^2=t-u_i^2$ by orthogonal projection, $t=1-N$. Follows.
- **The pinning term.** It is bounded above by the spectral bound $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2u_iN_i$ of item 4, which holds for every sign of $\tau$. This is the correct choice: the equality form $\tfrac\theta2N_i-\tau$ would need an upper bound on $-\tau$, which $\tau\le0$ does not supply. Follows.
- **The cross terms.** Bounded by $\tfrac{u_i}{2\sqrt3}(N-N_i)$ from item 5. Follows.
- **Division.** $u_i\ge\theta/\sqrt3>0$ from item 4. Dividing, the $u_i^2$ coefficient is $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2-\tfrac1{\sqrt3}=\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$, the $N_i$ coefficient $\tfrac{\sqrt3}2-\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, the $N$ coefficient $-\tfrac1{\sqrt3}+\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}=-\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$, giving $\theta u_i-\tfrac{u_i^2}{2\sqrt3}\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}(1-\tfrac N2+N_i)$. Follows. *Screen: the same rearrangement expands to $0$ in sympy.*
- **Monotonicity.** $g(u)=\theta u-\tfrac{u^2}{2\sqrt3}$ has $g'(u)=\theta-u/\sqrt3\ge0$ on $u\le\sqrt3\theta$. $u_i\le\lVert D\rVert\le1$, and $1<\sqrt3\theta$ when $\theta>\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$, so both $\theta/\sqrt3$ and $u_i$ lie in the increasing range and $g(u_i)\ge g(\theta/\sqrt3)=\tfrac{5\theta^2}{6\sqrt3}$. The complementary case $\theta\le\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ gives $\theta^2\le\tfrac13<\tfrac65$ directly. Follows, and the Lean declaration per_block settles this step with the hypotheses matching the text.

## 7. (b)5, the sum

- **Three nonzero blocks.** Summing $\tfrac56\theta^2\le1-\tfrac N2+N_i$ over $i=1,2,3$ with $N=N_1+N_2+N_3$: $\tfrac52\theta^2\le3-\tfrac32N+N=3-\tfrac N2\le3$. Each of the three pinnings uses the common $\tau\le0$. Follows.
- **Two nonzero blocks.** Say $\nu_k=0$, so $N_k=0$ and $N$ is the sum of the other two. Summing the two inequalities: $\tfrac53\theta^2\le2-N+N=2$. Follows.
- **The zero block.** Its pinning is unavailable (step 2 divides by $N_k$), so its inequality is correctly left out of the sum. It enters each remaining chain only through the cross term $\tfrac{u_i}{2\sqrt3}N_k=0$, and through $N$, consistently. The case with exactly one nonzero block is excluded by hypothesis and is L220. Follows, and sum_three and sum_two settle the arithmetic.

## 8. Scope

- **Hypotheses.** $\theta>0$ is stated and used in (a)'s step 5 and (b)'s division. Unit norm and criticality in the subspace are stated, and criticality in a subalgebra is criticality in $\mathcal W_n$. Symmetry of the trilinear form is inherited from L220. I find no hypothesis used and unstated.
- **Range of $n$.** (b) needs six indices, so $n\ge6$ is exact. (a) with $m$ sets needs $n\ge4m$, and for $m\ge2$ the case $6\le n<8$ is vacuous, harmless.
- **Can $\tau\le0$ be normalised away.** A sign element $g\in\Gamma$ multiplies $\nu_i$ by $\chi_{Q_i}(g)$, and $\chi_{Q_1}\chi_{Q_2}\chi_{Q_3}=\chi_{Q_1\triangle Q_2\triangle Q_3}=\chi_\emptyset=1$, so $\tau$ is multiplied by $1$. Any $g\in O(n)$ preserving the configuration permutes the blocks, and $\tau$ is symmetric and $O(n)$-invariant. The only sign-reversing map is $W\mapsto-W$, which sends $\theta$ to $-\theta$ and leaves the hypothesis $\theta>0$. So the split $\tau\le0$ against $\tau>0$ is genuine, and the record correctly lists $\tau>0$ as open.
- **Claims beyond the proof.** "What it gives" restricts (b) to $\tau\le0$ and lists $\tau>0$, three shared indices and pair blocks as open, which matches the proof. The $\tau>0$ sizes quoted there are marked as screen. Nothing in the statement exceeds the argument.
