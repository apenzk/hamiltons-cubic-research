## 1. Step 1, the compact form and the Casimir anchor

**The resolution identity holds.** The $\varphi_{ij}$ with $i<j$ are orthonormal for $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$, so $[\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\gamma]=\sum_\delta\langle[\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\gamma],\varphi_\delta\rangle\varphi_\delta=\sum_\delta c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}\varphi_\delta$ with $\delta$ over the same unordered pairs. Total antisymmetry of $c$ follows from antisymmetry of the bracket and ad-invariance, $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}([X,Y]Z)=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X[Y,Z])$.

**The contraction gives the displayed form exactly.** $\sum_{\delta\zeta}c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}c_{\beta\varepsilon\zeta}R_{\delta\zeta}=\langle R[\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\gamma],[\varphi_\beta,\varphi_\varepsilon]\rangle$ by bilinearity of the pairing and linearity of $R$ alone, so the $\tfrac12$ of the definition passes through untouched and $(R^\#)_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12\sum_{\gamma\varepsilon}R_{\gamma\varepsilon}\langle R[\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\gamma],[\varphi_\beta,\varphi_\varepsilon]\rangle$ is the record's $\#$ and no other operator.

**The Casimir value is right.** At $R=\mathrm{id}$ the pairing is $\langle\mathrm{ad}_{\varphi_\gamma}\varphi_\alpha,\mathrm{ad}_{\varphi_\gamma}\varphi_\beta\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{ad}^2_{\varphi_\gamma}\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\beta\rangle$ by skew-adjointness of $\mathrm{ad}$, as written. With the Killing form $(n-2)\operatorname{tr}(XY)=-2(n-2)\langle X,Y\rangle$, the trace of $C:=\sum_\gamma\mathrm{ad}^2_{\varphi_\gamma}$ is $-2(n-2)\dim$, so $C=-2(n-2)$ once $C$ is known to be a scalar, and $(\mathrm{id}^\#)_{\alpha\beta}=-\tfrac12\cdot(-2(n-2))\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ is $(n-2)\mathrm{id}$ and not a multiple of it. I checked the Killing normalisation independently at $n=3$, where $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{ad}^2_{e_{12}})=-2$ and $(n-2)\operatorname{tr}(e_{12}^2)=-2$ agree, and the direct sum $\sum_{p<q}\mathrm{ad}^2_{e_{pq}}e_{12}=-2e_{12}$ matches $-2(n-2)$ at $n=3$.

**Precision note, and the reason it costs nothing.** Scalarity of $C$ is Schur's lemma on the adjoint representation, which is irreducible at every $n\ne4$ and reducible at $n=4$, where the argument needs the extra clause that the two simple factors of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ carry the same value. The block does not say this, and it does not need to: Step 2's evaluation of $B$ at $\mathrm{id}$ is an elementary computation valid at every $n$ and returns the same anchor, which is why the block calls it "a second way". The anchor is therefore established.

## 2. Step 2, the four-index form of $\#$

**The bracket formula is right.** Expanding $[E_{ij}-E_{ji},E_{pq}-E_{qp}]$ with $[E_{ij},E_{pq}]=\delta_{jp}E_{iq}-\delta_{qi}E_{pj}$ gives $\delta_{jp}e_{iq}+\delta_{iq}e_{jp}-\delta_{jq}e_{ip}-\delta_{ip}e_{jq}$, which is the block's line and also the four cases coded in `build_structure_constants`.

**The substitution of $e$ for $\varphi$ is legitimate, and the block's reason for it is loose.** What makes it work is that each sign-carrying object appears an even number of times: $[\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{pq}]=[e_{ij},e_{pq}]$, $R_{pq,rs}=\langle Re_{pq},e_{rs}\rangle$, and $\langle Re_{iq},e_{ks}\rangle=R_{iq,ks}$. The written reason, "the sign of $c$ enters twice", names the effect for the structure constants, where $c^\varphi=-c^e$ and $\#$ is quadratic in $c$, rather than for the three substitutions Step 2 actually makes. The substitution is correct either way.

**The count and the prefactor.** Each summand is even under $p\leftrightarrow q$ and under $r\leftrightarrow s$ and vanishes on both diagonals, so the unordered double sum is $\tfrac14$ of the ordered one and the compact form becomes $\tfrac18\sum_{pqrs}R_{pq,rs}\langle R[e_{ij},e_{pq}],[e_{kl},e_{rs}]\rangle$. The relabelling $p\leftrightarrow q$ carries the third bracket term onto the first and the fourth onto the second, the sign flip of $R_{pq,rs}$ paying for the sign of the term, and $r\leftrightarrow s$ does the same in the second bracket, so sixteen terms become four with prefactor $\tfrac18\cdot4=\tfrac12$. This arithmetic is left to the reader in the block, which states the mechanism and the result.

**The four terms evaluate to the stated answer.** They are $\tfrac12\sum_{qs}R_{iq,ks}R_{jq,ls}=\tfrac12B_{ijkl}$, then $\tfrac12\sum_{qr}R_{iq,lr}R_{jq,rk}=-\tfrac12B_{ijlk}$, then $\tfrac12\sum_{ps}R_{pi,ls}R_{jp,ks}=-\tfrac12B_{ijlk}$, then $\tfrac12\sum_{pr}R_{pi,rk}R_{jp,lr}=\tfrac12B_{ijkl}$, giving $(R^\#)_{ij,kl}=B_{ijkl}-B_{ijlk}$ with $B$ exactly as defined, including the index order, the first and third indices carrying $i$ and $k$. Only antisymmetry in each pair enters, so first Bianchi is unused here, which the screen of item 8 confirms by holding off $S^2_B$.

**The $\mathrm{id}$ value.** $B_{ijkl}=(n-2)\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}$ at $R=\mathrm{id}$, from $n\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}$, and the $\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}$ part is symmetric in $k,l$ so it cancels in $B_{ijkl}-B_{ijlk}$, leaving $(n-2)\mathrm{id}$.

## 3. Step 2, the $R^2$ form

**Both rewritings are the record's Bianchi identity with correct signs.** From $R_{ij,pq}+R_{ip,qj}+R_{iq,jp}=0$ and antisymmetry in the second pair, $R_{ij,pq}=R_{ip,jq}-R_{iq,jp}$. The same identity at $(p,q,k,l)$ gives $R_{pq,kl}=R_{pk,ql}-R_{pl,qk}$.

**The collapse and the identification.** Of the four products in $\tfrac12\sum_{pq}(R_{ip,jq}-R_{iq,jp})(R_{pk,ql}-R_{pl,qk})$, the relabelling $p\leftrightarrow q$ carries the third onto the second and the fourth onto the first, each needing one pair exchange $R_{qk,pl}=R_{pl,qk}$, so the prefactor $\tfrac12$ is absorbed. Then $R_{pk,ql}=R_{kp,lq}$ and $R_{pl,qk}=R_{lp,kq}$ by two antisymmetries each, which is exactly $B_{ikjl}-B_{iljk}$. So $(R^2)_{ij,kl}=B_{ikjl}-B_{iljk}$ is exact.

**Where Bianchi is needed.** In both rewritings, and nowhere else in Step 2. The pair-contraction $\sum_{pq}R_{ij,pq}R_{pq,kl}$ contracts a pair against a pair, while $B$ contracts single indices, and first Bianchi is what converts one into the other. The screen shows this claim has content: off $S^2_B$ the $B$-form of $R^2$ misses the matrix square by $48$ at $n=4$ and $258$ at $n=5$ while the $\#$ form stays exact.

## 4. Step 3, the Bianchi half

**The twelve terms, and which cancels with which.** Reading $Q_{ij,kl}=B_{ijkl}-B_{ijlk}+B_{ikjl}-B_{iljk}$ as a function of the four slots, the cyclic images are $Q_{ik,lj}=B_{iklj}-B_{ikjl}+B_{ilkj}-B_{ijkl}$ and $Q_{il,jk}=B_{iljk}-B_{ilkj}+B_{ijlk}-B_{iklj}$. The six pairings are $+B_{ijkl}$ against $-B_{ijkl}$ and $+B_{ikjl}$ against $-B_{ikjl}$ across the first and second, $-B_{ijlk}$ against $+B_{ijlk}$ and $-B_{iljk}$ against $+B_{iljk}$ across the first and third, and $+B_{iklj}$ against $-B_{iklj}$ and $+B_{ilkj}$ against $-B_{ilkj}$ across the second and third. **"No symmetry of $B$ used" is true**: every cancellation is between two occurrences of the same symbol, so only commutativity of multiplication enters. The Bianchi hypothesis on $R$ enters this half through Step 2's $R^2$ form, which is why the conclusion is stated for $R\in S^2_B$.

**The two symmetries, and what each needs.** Antisymmetry in $(i,j)$ needs nothing: $B_{jikl}=B_{ijlk}$ by inspection of the definition, so $B_{jikl}-B_{jilk}=-(B_{ijkl}-B_{ijlk})$. Antisymmetry in $(k,l)$ is immediate. Pair exchange needs self-adjointness of $R$ once in each factor: $B_{klij}=\sum_{pq}R_{kp,iq}R_{lp,jq}=\sum_{pq}R_{iq,kp}R_{jq,lp}=B_{ijkl}$.

**A one-line omission.** The block checks the symmetries of $B_{ijkl}-B_{ijlk}$, the $\#$ half, and concludes $Q(R)\in S^2_B$. The $R^2$ half carries them too, by composition of a self-adjoint bivector-preserving operator with itself, which follows from two of the four named hypotheses and is not written. The conclusion stands.

## 5. Step 4, the four contractions

**Derived independently, two ways, agreeing.** Through Step 2's form, $(R^\#)_{ac,bc}=B_{acbc}-B_{accb}$ with $B_{acbc}=\sum_{pq}R_{ap,bq}R_{cp,cq}$ and $B_{accb}=\sum_{pq}R_{ap,cq}R_{cp,bq}$. Summing over $c$ and using $\sum_cR_{cp,cq}=\operatorname{Ric}(R)_{pq}$, which is the record's definition after two antisymmetries, the first part is $A_1=\sum_{qs}R_{aq,bs}\operatorname{Ric}(R)_{qs}$ and the second is $-\sum_{cpq}R_{ap,cq}R_{cp,bq}$, which equals $A_2$ after renaming $p\to q,q\to r$ and one antisymmetry in the second pair, $R_{cq,rb}=-R_{cq,br}$.

**The prefactor claim is accurate.** Through the compact form the four surviving contractions are the four terms of item 2 at $j=l=c$ summed over $c$, namely $\tfrac12B_{acbc}$ twice and $-\tfrac12B_{accb}$ twice, so they are $\tfrac12A_1$ twice and $\tfrac12A_2$ twice, and $\tfrac12(2A_1+2A_2)=A_1+A_2$ is the block's sentence exactly. The screen puts $\operatorname{Ric}(R^\#)-(A_1+A_2)$ at exactly $0$ at $n=4$ and $n=5$ against the record's own `Rsharp_full`.

## 6. Step 4, the load-bearing Bianchi and the swap

**The rewrite.** First Bianchi at $(c;q,r,b)$ reads $R_{cq,rb}+R_{cr,bq}+R_{cb,qr}=0$, so $R_{cq,rb}=-R_{cr,bq}-R_{cb,qr}$, the block's line verbatim, no sign adjustment needed.

**The first resulting term is $-2\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)$.** $\sum_{cqr}R_{aq,cr}R_{cr,bq}=\sum_q\sum_{cr}R_{aq,cr}R_{cr,bq}$, and the inner ordered sum over $(c,r)$ is twice the unordered one, so it is $2(R^2)_{aq,bq}$ and the whole is $2\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)_{ab}$. The summand vanishes at $c=r$, which is what licenses the doubling. **This factor $2$ is load-bearing**: with $1$ in its place the final identity would keep a residual $+\tfrac12\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)$, which the block itself notes is a nonzero Gram matrix at every $R\ne0$, and closure would fail.

**The swap.** In $X=\sum_{cqr}R_{aq,cr}R_{cb,qr}$ rename $c\leftrightarrow r$ to get $\sum_{cqr}R_{aq,rc}R_{rb,qc}$, apply pair exchange to the second factor to get $\sum R_{aq,rc}R_{qc,rb}$, then antisymmetry in the second pair of the first factor and in the first pair of the second factor, whose two signs cancel, to reach $\sum R_{aq,cr}R_{cq,rb}=A_2$. So the symmetries used are one pair exchange, which is self-adjointness, and two antisymmetries, which are the bivector reading, with the dummy swap itself carrying no hypothesis.

**The conclusion follows.** $A_2=-2\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)_{ab}-A_2$ gives $A_2=-\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)_{ab}$ exactly, hence $\operatorname{Ric}(Q(R))_{ab}=\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)_{ab}+A_1+A_2=A_1$, which is the claimed right-hand side after renaming $q,s$ to $c,d$. The screen puts $X-A_2$ and $A_2+\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)$ at exactly $0$ while $|\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)|$ reaches $1.4\times10^4$ at $n=4$ and $7.1\times10^4$ at $n=5$, so the cancellation is between two large numbers.

## 7. Hypothesis inventory

**Used, and where.** Linearity of $R$: Step 1's contraction and every basis expansion. Bivector preservation: the matrix readings $(R^2)_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_\gamma R_{\alpha\gamma}R_{\gamma\beta}$ and $Q(R)_{ij,kl}$, which need $R\varphi_\gamma$ to lie in the span of the $\varphi$, and the antisymmetries of the four-index array used throughout Steps 2 and 4. Self-adjointness: the collapse of the $R^2$ expansion in Step 2, pair exchange of $Q(R)$ in Step 3, the swap $X=A_2$ in Step 4, and the self-adjointness of $R^\#$ itself, which follows from the index formula by relabelling $\gamma\leftrightarrow\varepsilon,\delta\leftrightarrow\zeta$. First Bianchi: twice in Step 2's $R^2$ form and once in Step 4, three uses in total.

**Nothing named is unused**, and each of the four is spent at a place where dropping it breaks a specific line.

**Not used.** Symmetry of $\operatorname{Ric}(R)$, which is anyway a consequence of self-adjointness rather than a hypothesis. **Ricci-flatness appears nowhere**, and the screen corroborates this at tensors whose Ricci norm is $205$ and $488$: the identity holds exactly there, so it is a statement about all of $S^2_B$ and reads off closure rather than assuming it.

**Two unnamed ingredients, both about the ambient structure.** Orthonormality and completeness of $\{\varphi_{ij}\}_{i<j}$ in $\Lambda^2$, which every resolution and every doubling uses, and ad-invariance of $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$, which gives total antisymmetry of $c$ and the Casimir anchor. **One caveat the conventions line omits**: the doubling rule as stated needs the summand to vanish on the diagonal $p=q$, otherwise diagonal terms are counted once. Every application in L209 carries a factor $R_{pq,\cdot}$ or $\varphi_{pq}$ and so vanishes there, and I checked this at each of the four uses.

## 8. Vacuity and teeth

**The identity is not vacuous.** Both sides are large and index-dependent at random Bianchi tensors, the right-hand side reaching $2.0\times10^4$ at $n=4$ and $2.2\times10^4$ at $n=5$ in my screen, and the content is a cancellation between $\operatorname{Ric}(R^\#)$ and $\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)$ rather than two vanishing halves, which I confirmed separately: $b(R^\#)$ reaches $2294$ at $n=4$ and $3964$ at $n=5$ while $b(R^\#)+b(R^2)$ is exactly $0$, so both of the block's asides about the halves hold at these instances.

**The screen, reported as a screen.** Built three Kulkarni-Nomizu tensors $S_{ik}S_{jl}-S_{il}S_{jk}$ with integer symmetric $S$ and summed them, so the tensor is in $S^2_B$ with integer entries and all arithmetic is exact. Against the record's `build_structure_constants` and `Q_full`: the four-index $\#$ against `Rsharp_full`, the $B$-form of $R^2$ against the matrix square, $b(Q(R))$, and the Ricci residual all exactly $0$ at $n=4$ and $n=5$, and the $\mathrm{id}$ anchors $\mathrm{id}^\#-(n-2)\mathrm{id}$, $\operatorname{Ric}(Q(\mathrm{id}))-(n-1)^2\delta$ and the right-hand side at $\mathrm{id}$ also exactly $0$.

**Off $S^2_B$ the identity fails, and the defect is the recorded one.** At a random integer symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2$ with Bianchi violated, the Ricci residual is $36$ at $n=4$ and $242$ at $n=5$, and it agrees with $\tfrac32\sum_{klm}\operatorname{Alt}(R)_{iklm}\operatorname{Alt}(R)_{jklm}$ to exactly $0$ at both dimensions, with $\operatorname{Alt}=\tfrac13b$ the total antisymmetrisation. **The normalisation of $\operatorname{Alt}$ is what makes that constant $\tfrac32$**, and the L209 block quotes the defect without fixing it in situ, so a reader has to take it from [ATTEMPTS.md](../ATTEMPTS.md). Under the same tensors $b(Q(R))$ is $84$ and $119$, so the Bianchi half has teeth too.

## 9. Normalisation and reach

**The compact form agrees with `Rsharp_full`.** $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_aRA_bR)=-\tfrac12\sum_{mnpq}c_{amn}c_{bpq}R_{np}R_{qm}$, and with $R_{qm}=R_{mq}$ and the rename $m\leftrightarrow n$ paying one antisymmetry of $c$ this is $+\tfrac12\sum c_{amn}c_{bpq}R_{mp}R_{nq}$, the index formula. Sign and factor both match, and the block's "by one antisymmetry of $c$" is the right account. The script builds $c$ from $e_{ij}$ rather than $\varphi_{ij}$, which flips $c$ overall, and $\#$ is quadratic in $c$, so the implementation and the block carry the same normalisation.

**The polarisation is valid.** $\mathcal W_n$ is cut out by linear conditions, bivector preservation, self-adjointness, first Bianchi and $\operatorname{Ric}=0$, so it is a subspace, $X+Y$ lies in it, and $X\circ Y=\tfrac12(Q(X+Y)-Q(X)-Q(Y))$ is a linear combination of three elements of $Q(\mathcal W_n)\subseteq\mathcal W_n$. It needs of $Q$ only that it maps $\mathcal W_n$ into $\mathcal W_n$, not that it is quadratic, and the Fox product is defined by that formula here so nothing further is owed.

**The BW comparison is corroboration, with the flip carried next door.** In a Ricci eigenframe the record's identity reads $\operatorname{Ric}(Q)_{ab}=\sum_k\operatorname{Ric}_{kk}R_{ak,bk}$, while [BW08](../references/boehm-wilking-2008-space-forms/notes.md) eq. (6) as written is $\sum_k\operatorname{Ric}_{kk}R_{kijk}$, whose first pair is in the opposite order, so read in the record's conventions it is the negative. At $R=\mathrm{id}$ the record's form gives $+(n-1)^2\delta$ and BW's literal expression gives $-(n-1)^2\delta$, and the record's $+$ is the one forced by its own definitions since $Q(\mathrm{id})=(n-1)\mathrm{id}$. The linked BW notes state this flip and the same anchor explicitly, so the block's citation is safe, although the block itself leaves the flip to the link.

## 10. The Lean statement

**`ric_Qop_eq` says the Ricci half and not something else.** Its conclusion is `Ric (Qop R) a b = ∑ c, ∑ d, R4 R a c b d * Ric R c d`, and reading the primitives in [WeylReal.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean), `phi i j` is the record's basis bivector with $-1$ at $(i,j)$, `Ric R a b` is $\sum_c\langle R\varphi_{ac},\varphi_{bc}\rangle$, `R4 R a c b d` is $R_{ac,bd}$, and `Qop` is `compOp` plus `sharpOp`. So both sides are the block's. Its hypotheses are `IsLinOp`, `hskew`, `hsym` quantified over skew arguments, and `hB` at all four indices, which is `IsWeyl` minus the Ricci clause plus linearity, matching the prose inventory with no Ricci-flatness smuggled in and nothing weakened.

**The anchors are non-vacuous.** `ric_Qop_id` and `rhs_id` are proved from `Qop_id_eq` and `Ric_id_eq` in [L208RicciFoxSquare.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L208RicciFoxSquare.lean), and `anchors_agree` chains them without touching the `sorry`, so it is a genuine instance of the statement at $R=\mathrm{id}$, at the one point where the two sides could have differed by a sign. I found no `sorry` in that dependency chain, and the compiled olean is newer than the source, so the file type-checks as written. I did not recompile, a Mathlib build being a long run.

**What the file leaves out**, all of it named in the file and in [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md): the Bianchi half, the polarisation, the tensor $B$, and, most substantively, the structure-constant form of $\#$, so the layer cannot unfold `sharpOp` and the agreement of `sharpOp` with the block's $\#$ rests on the $\mathrm{id}$ anchor rather than on a proved identity. One further gap worth naming: `lean/axioms.json` carries no entries in the `HamiltonsCubic.Results` namespace at all, so the `#print axioms` annotations at the foot of the file are comments that no recorded artifact corroborates.

## 11. What would break it

**The fragile objects are two constants and one identity.** First, the $\tfrac12$ in $(R^\#)_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12\sum cc\,RR$, equivalently the anchor $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$: rescaling $\#$ by $\lambda$ sends $A_2$ to $-\lambda\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)$ and leaves $(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Ric}(R^2)$ standing in the conclusion, which is nonzero on $\mathcal W_n$, so closure holds at $\lambda=1$ alone. Second, the doubling factor $2$ in $\sum_{cr}R_{aq,cr}R_{cr,bq}=2(R^2)_{aq,bq}$, for the same reason with $\tfrac12$ left over. Third, the exact form of first Bianchi: the swap $X=A_2$ is what turns the rewrite into a closed equation for $A_2$, and a variant identity with the pairs differently ordered would leave $A_2$ undetermined.

**What I did to look for one.** Re-derived all four steps from the definitions rather than following the prose, checked the Killing normalisation at $n=3$ two ways, checked the index formula against `Rsharp_full` by hand, and ran the exact integer screen of item 8, which would register any wrong constant as a nonzero integer residual rather than as rounding. Every quantity the chain passes through, $B$, $\#$, $R^2$, $b(Q)$, $A_1$, $A_2$, $X$ and both anchors, came back exact at $n=4$ and $n=5$, and the off-$S^2_B$ control failed in exactly the predicted amount. I found no candidate falsifier.

## Verdict

**CONFIRMED.** L209 is established by what is written, in the record's own normalisation and from the four hypotheses it names, with the Ricci half's load-bearing step, the Bianchi rewrite of $R_{cq,rb}$ followed by the swap $c\leftrightarrow r$, correct in sign, factor and symmetry use. The three imprecisions I found sit outside the chain: the Casimir sentence's scalarity clause is thin at $n=4$ and is closed by the block's own second route to the anchor, the doubling convention omits its diagonal-vanishing side condition while every application satisfies it, and Step 3 leaves the symmetries of the $R^2$ half to the reader. None of them is a gap in the argument, and I recommend the block stand as written with the defect line's $\operatorname{Alt}=\tfrac13b$ normalisation added, since that is the one number a reader cannot reconstruct from the block.
