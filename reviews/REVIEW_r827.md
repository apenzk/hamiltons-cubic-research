## 1. Claim A's three facts, all correct

**The stabiliser is exactly $4$.** Fix an oriented $4$-plane $P$ and take $U=\Lambda^+P$. Decompose $\mathfrak{so}(6)=\mathfrak{so}(P)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(P^\perp)\oplus(P\otimes P^\perp)$, of dimensions $6+1+8=15$. Now centralise $\Lambda^+P=\mathfrak{su}(2)_+$: inside $\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{su}(2)_+\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_-$ the centraliser is $\mathfrak{su}(2)_-$, since $\mathfrak{su}(2)_+$ has trivial centre; $\mathfrak{so}(P^\perp)=\mathbb R\varepsilon$ centralises everything supported on $P$; and $P\otimes P^\perp$ contributes nothing, because $\mathfrak{su}(2)_+$ acting on $P\cong\mathbb H$ by left multiplication has no nonzero fixed vector. So $\mathfrak z(\Lambda^+P)=\mathfrak{su}(2)_-\oplus\mathbb R\varepsilon$, of dimension $\mathbf 4$, which is [r824](REVIEW_r824.md) §2.2's sharp maximum. **CONFIRMED.**

**The orbit is exactly $11$.** $\dim(G\cdot p)=15-4=11$. The effective group is $O(6)/\{\pm I\}$, still of dimension $15$, and a disconnected stabiliser changes only components. **CONFIRMED.**

**The image in $M/G$ is a curve.** $G$ acts trivially on $\lambda$, so two padded points in one orbit carry the same $\lambda$, and the image of the $12$-dimensional family in $M/G$ is parametrised by the weight circle, of dimension $\mathbf 1$. **It is a curve, not a point.** *Whether it is a curve or a point does not matter for the refutation*, since a prediction of emptiness is refuted by one solution. It matters for the size: the block's own confrontation is $-10$ against $+1$, a gap of eleven, and the correct confrontation of §3 is $-6$ against $+1$, a gap of seven.

**Where the block's wording is off: "one orbit" is two.** At fixed $\lambda$ the padded set has dimension $8+3=11$ and each $G$-orbit has dimension $11$, so the orbits are its components. There are exactly two. *An element $g\in O(6)$ carries $\Lambda^\varepsilon P$ to $\Lambda^{\varepsilon'}P'$ by $\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}$, which is a Lie algebra isomorphism $\mathfrak{su}(2)\to\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and every automorphism of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ is inner, hence in $SO(3)$.* So the sign of the structure constant in $[\omega_1,\omega_2]=c\,\omega_3$ is an invariant of the frame, and the frame $(\omega_1,\omega_2,-\omega_3)$ lies in the other orbit. *Screened: $[\omega^+_1,\omega^+_2]=-\sqrt2\,\omega^+_3$ exactly, for the standard self-dual frame.* **This changes nothing**, the image in $M/G$ being a curve of two components rather than one, and it is recorded because "one orbit" appears in [r824](REVIEW_r824.md) §4 as well and a successor may lean on it.

## 2. Claim A's inference is unsound as a principle

**The principle asserted.** *"A count refuted on one stratum of the space it is computed over is refuted as a predictor for that space."*

**Counterexample, equivariant and polynomial.** Let $G=SO(2)$ act on $M=\mathbb R^2\oplus\mathbb R=\{(v,x)\}$, rotating $v$ and fixing $x$, and let $N=\mathbb R^2\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R$ carry the standard representation on the first summand and the trivial one on the others. Put
$$F(v,x)=\bigl(v,\ x\lVert v\rVert^2,\ \lVert v\rVert^4\bigr).$$
$F$ is polynomial, equivariant, and $0$ is a fixed point of the target. Its zero set is the $x$-axis, of dimension $1$.

- *The naive count over $M$*: $3-4=-1$, expect empty. **Refuted**, the zero set being $1$-dimensional.
- *The singular stratum* $\{v=0\}$, where the stabiliser is $H=SO(2)$: $M^H$ has dimension $1$ and $N^H$ has dimension $2$, so the stratified count reads $1-2=-1$ and expects empty. **Refuted there too, by exactly the padded family's mechanism**: $F(M^H)\subseteq N^H$ and the target collapses from $4$ to $2$.
- *The open principal stratum* $\{v\ne0\}$, where the stabiliser is finite: the slice has dimension $3-1=2$ against $N$ of dimension $4$, so the count reads $-2$ and expects empty. **It is exactly right**: $F=0$ forces $v=0$.

**So a count can be refuted on a stratum, refuted again after the standard equivariant correction on that stratum, and still be a correct predictor on the open stratum of the same space, carrying the same ambient dimensions.** The principle Claim A settles the disagreement with is false, and the counterexample is built to reproduce the real mechanism rather than to dodge it.

**Why this bites here.** [r824](REVIEW_r824.md) §6 derives $25$ from *"Let $p\in M$ have finite stabiliser"* and then writes, in the same section, that the repaired count *"still fails in this very problem on the non-principal strata"*, computing the padded stratum's $7$ against $5$ itself. Its verdict sentence is *"the note's conclusion comes back stronger than it went in, **on the principal stratum**"*. **Claim A refutes a claim r824 did not make.**

## 5. Claim B, step by step

**B1, reductivity.** *The theorem, stated precisely.* Let $\mathfrak g$ be a compact Lie algebra, meaning it admits an $\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}$-invariant positive definite inner product. Then every subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak g$ is compact, hence reductive, that is $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak z(\mathfrak h)\oplus[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]$ with $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]$ semisimple. *Proof in one line*: the invariant inner product restricts to $\mathfrak h$, and $\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle+\langle Y,[X,Z]\rangle=0$ holds in $\mathfrak h$ because it holds in $\mathfrak g$, so $\mathfrak h$ carries an $\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathfrak h}$-invariant inner product. **No closedness hypothesis enters**, which is the point the brief asks about: the failure people remember is that a *subgroup* of a compact group need not be closed, and that has no algebra-level analogue. $\mathfrak{so}(6)$ is compact, being semisimple with negative definite Killing form. **CONFIRMED.**

*And the hypothesis is available anyway.* $\mathfrak z(U)$ is the Lie algebra of $Z_{SO(6)}(U)=\{g:\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(g)\omega=\omega\ \forall\omega\in U\}$, which is closed, being cut out by continuous equations. So both routes are open here.

*Argue the opposite.* The step would be false with *compact* weakened to *reductive*: the Borel subalgebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)$ is a two-dimensional non-reductive subalgebra of a reductive algebra. **The block's appeal to compactness is load-bearing and correctly placed.**

**B2, dimension two forces abelian, and a $2$-torus.** A reductive $\mathfrak h$ of dimension $2$ has $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]$ semisimple of dimension at most $2$, and real semisimple algebras have dimension $0$ or at least $3$, so $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]=0$. *Independent route, avoiding reductivity*: the only non-abelian $2$-dimensional real Lie algebra is $[X,Y]=Y$, where $\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}_X$ has the real nonzero eigenvalue $1$, while in a compact algebra every $\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}$ is skew for an invariant inner product and has purely imaginary spectrum. Then, in a compact Lie algebra, every abelian subalgebra is contained in a maximal abelian one, and maximal abelian subalgebras are precisely the Cartan subalgebras, all conjugate. So $\mathfrak z(U)=\mathfrak t^2\subset\mathfrak t^3$. **CONFIRMED.**

**B3, the roots and the regular case.** The roots of $D_3$ are $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ for $i<j$, twelve of them, matching $\dim\mathfrak{so}(6)-\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}=15-3=12$. Each has exactly one zero coordinate, so **none has two**, as the block says.

**The justification is loose, and the conclusion is right.** A root $\alpha$ vanishes identically on a $2$-plane $V\subset\mathfrak t^3$ if and only if $V=\ker\alpha$, since $\ker\alpha$ is itself a $2$-plane. What *"none has two zero coordinates"* establishes is that no root is a multiple of a coordinate functional, hence that **no coordinate $2$-plane is singular**, a strictly weaker statement, and one that cannot give the conclusion, since singular $2$-planes do exist and the block names one in its next sentence. **The clean argument is one line and uses a regular element**: a regular $\mathfrak t^2$ contains an $H$ with $\alpha(H)\ne0$ for every root, so $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak t^2)\subseteq\mathfrak z(H)=\mathfrak t^3$, while $\mathfrak t^3\subseteq\mathfrak z(\mathfrak t^2)$ because $\mathfrak t^3$ is abelian. Hence $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak t^2)=\mathfrak t^3$. **CONFIRMED with the justification replaced.** *There are exactly six singular $2$-planes, the six root kernels $\ker(e_i\pm e_j)$, and they form one $W(D_3)$-orbit because $D_3$ is simply laced and irreducible, so all are $SO(6)$-conjugate.*

**B4, the bicentraliser and the contradiction.** $U\subseteq\mathfrak z(\mathfrak z(U))$ holds unconditionally, every element of $U$ commuting with every element of $\mathfrak z(U)$ by the definition of the latter. So $U\subseteq\mathfrak z(\mathfrak t^2)=\mathfrak t^3$ and $\dim U=3$ forces $U=\mathfrak t^3$, whose centraliser is $\mathfrak t^3$ itself, since $X=H+\sum_\alpha X_\alpha$ commutes with all of $\mathfrak t$ only if every $\alpha(H')X_\alpha$ vanishes. That is dimension $3$ against the hypothesis $2$. **CONFIRMED.** *Doubly so*: $U=\mathfrak t^3$ is exactly the common plane splitting that [L181](../findings/diagonal-half.md) empties.

**B5, the singular case and its centraliser.** For $\mathfrak t^2=\ker(e_1-e_2)=\{(a,a,b)\}=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(J,\varepsilon)$ with $J=e^{12}+e^{34}$ and $\varepsilon=e^{56}$, the centraliser is $\mathfrak t^3\oplus\mathfrak g_{\pm(e_1-e_2)}$, of dimension $3+2=5$. In geometric terms, $\mathfrak z(\varepsilon)=\mathfrak{so}(P)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(P^\perp)$ of dimension $7$, and inside $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ the centraliser of $J\in\Lambda^+P$ is $\mathbb R J\oplus\Lambda^-P=\mathfrak u(2)$, so
$$\mathfrak k:=\mathfrak z(\mathfrak t^2)=\mathbb R J\oplus\Lambda^-P\oplus\mathbb R\varepsilon\ \cong\ \mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1),\qquad\dim\mathfrak k=5 .$$
**The name and the dimension are both right.** *Screened: solving $[\,\cdot\,,J]=[\,\cdot\,,\varepsilon]=0$ over the fifteen basis bivectors returns a $5$-dimensional nullspace.*

**And the block is right that there is room.** As a Lie algebra $\mathfrak k\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathbb R^2$ with $\Lambda^-P$ the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(J,\varepsilon)$ the centre. Take $U=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(m_1+a,\ m_2,\ m_3)$ with $a=J/\sqrt2$ and $m_1,m_2,m_3$ an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^-P$. Then $\langle U\rangle\supseteq[\Lambda^-,\Lambda^-]=\Lambda^-$, so $\mathfrak z(U)\subseteq\mathfrak z(\Lambda^-P)=\Lambda^+P\oplus\mathbb R\varepsilon$, and within that only $\mathbb R J\oplus\mathbb R\varepsilon$ commutes with $m_1+a$. **So $\mathfrak z(U)=\mathfrak t^2$ exactly, and $3$-planes with two-dimensional centraliser genuinely exist inside $\mathfrak k$.** *Screened at that $U$: the nullspace is $2$-dimensional and is spanned by $e^{12}+e^{34}$ and $e^{56}$.* **CONFIRMED.**

## 6. Closing the singular case, which is the increment

**Lemma (r827).** *Let $P\subset\mathbb R^6$ be an oriented $4$-plane, $a\in\Lambda^+P$ a unit self-dual form, $\varepsilon$ the unit area form of $P^\perp$, and $\mathfrak k=\mathbb R a\oplus\Lambda^-P\oplus\mathbb R\varepsilon$. Let $(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3)$ be an orthonormal triple in $\mathfrak k$ and $\lambda$ a weight vector with $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$ and every $\lambda_i\ne0$. If $\sum_i\lambda_i\omega_i^2=0$ and $\sum_i\lambda_i\,\omega_i\wedge\omega_i=0$, then every $\omega_i$ lies in $\Lambda^-P$.*

**Consequences.** The span is then $\Lambda^-P$, the support is $P$ and $\dim V=4$, and the centraliser is $\Lambda^+P\oplus\mathbb R\varepsilon$ of dimension $4$. Since a two-dimensional $\mathfrak z(U)$ is a $2$-torus, regular or singular, and B4 kills the regular case while this kills the singular one, **no solution with a two-dimensional stabiliser exists at any $\dim V$**. Combined with [r824](REVIEW_r824.md) §7, **a surviving witness at $\dim V=6$ has stabiliser of dimension $0$ or $1$**, so its orbit is $14$ or $15$-dimensional, tightening the record's *"at most two"* and *"between $13$ and $15$"*.

### 6.1 The two block identities

Write $\omega=\alpha a+m+c\,\varepsilon$ with $m\in\Lambda^-P$. Since $a$ and $m$ are supported on $P$ and $\varepsilon$ on $P^\perp$, all mixed matrix products vanish, and $a$ commutes with $m$ because $[\Lambda^+,\Lambda^-]=0$. With $a^2=-\tfrac12\Pi_P$, $m^2=-\tfrac12\lVert m\rVert^2\Pi_P$, $\varepsilon^2=-\Pi_{P^\perp}$, $a\wedge a=\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}_P$, $m\wedge m=-\lVert m\rVert^2\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}_P$ and $a\wedge m=\langle\star a,m\rangle\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}_P=0$:
$$\omega^2=-\tfrac12\bigl(\alpha^2+\lVert m\rVert^2\bigr)\Pi_P+2\alpha\,(am)-c^2\,\Pi_{P^\perp},$$
$$\omega\wedge\omega=\bigl(\alpha^2-\lVert m\rVert^2\bigr)\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}_P+2c\,(\alpha a+m)\wedge\varepsilon .$$
Here $am$ is symmetric and trace-free on $P$, and $m\mapsto am$ is injective because $a$ is invertible on $P$ with $a^{-1}=-2a$. *Screened: both identities were verified exactly and symbolically in $(\alpha,m,c)$ against direct $6\times6$ matrix and $4$-form computation. Neither is asserted on the strength of the screen. Both are proved above.*

### 6.2 The system

Write $\alpha=(\alpha_i)$, $c=(c_i)$, and let $M$ be the $3\times3$ matrix whose rows are the $m_i$ in an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^-P$. Orthonormality is $\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}+MM^{\mathsf T}+cc^{\mathsf T}=I_3$, in particular $\alpha_i^2+\lVert m_i\rVert^2+c_i^2=1$. Decomposing $S^2\mathbb R^6$ into $\mathbb R\Pi_P$, $\mathbb R\Pi_{P^\perp}$ and $S^2_0(P)$, and $\Lambda^4\mathbb R^6$ into $\Lambda^4P$ and $\Lambda^2P\wedge\Lambda^2P^\perp$, and using $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$ throughout, the two constraints are exactly
$$\text{(i) }\ \alpha^{\mathsf T}\Lambda\alpha=0,\qquad\text{(ii) }\ c^{\mathsf T}\Lambda c=0,\qquad\text{(iii) }\ \alpha^{\mathsf T}\Lambda c=0,$$
$$\text{(iv) }\ M^{\mathsf T}(\Lambda\alpha)=0,\qquad\text{(v) }\ M^{\mathsf T}(\Lambda c)=0,$$
with $\Lambda=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\lambda)$. *The $\Pi_P$ component of Ricci-flatness is $-\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_i(1-c_i^2)=\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_ic_i^2$ and so repeats (ii). The $\Lambda^4P$ component of Bianchi is $2\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_i^2+\sum_i\lambda_ic_i^2$ and so gives (i) once (ii) is in hand.*

**Sanity, which the system passes.** At $\alpha=c=0$ every equation is $0=0$, and the solution set is $V_3(\Lambda^-P)$ times the whole weight circle. That is the padded family, mirrored, and it is the one inhabited family the record owns.

### 6.3 The argument

**Step 1, the isotropy step, which is the whole of it.** Let $W=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(\alpha,c)\subseteq\mathbb R^3$. Equations (i), (ii) and (iii) say precisely that the quadratic form $Q_\Lambda(x)=x^{\mathsf T}\Lambda x$ vanishes on all of $W$, since $Q_\Lambda(s\alpha+tc)=s^2\alpha^{\mathsf T}\Lambda\alpha+2st\,\alpha^{\mathsf T}\Lambda c+t^2c^{\mathsf T}\Lambda c$. **But $\lambda$ has all entries nonzero and sums to zero, so its entries are not all of one sign, and $Q_\Lambda$ is nondegenerate of signature $(2,1)$ or $(1,2)$.** A nondegenerate form of signature $(p,q)$ has maximal totally isotropic subspaces of dimension $\min(p,q)$, here $1$. **So $\dim W\le1$.**

**Step 2, the line.** Suppose $\dim W=1$, say $W=\mathbb R u$ with $u$ a unit vector, $\alpha=pu$ and $c=qu$ with $(p,q)\ne(0,0)$. Equations (iv) and (v) read $p\,M^{\mathsf T}(\Lambda u)=0$ and $q\,M^{\mathsf T}(\Lambda u)=0$, so $M^{\mathsf T}(\Lambda u)=0$, that is $\Lambda u\in\ker MM^{\mathsf T}$. Now $MM^{\mathsf T}=I-\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}-cc^{\mathsf T}=I-(p^2+q^2)uu^{\mathsf T}$, which is the identity on $u^\perp$, so its kernel is contained in $\mathbb R u$. Hence $\Lambda u=\mu u$ for a scalar $\mu$, and then $\mu=u^{\mathsf T}\Lambda u=Q_\Lambda(u)=0$ by Step 1. So $\Lambda u=0$ with $u\ne0$, against every $\lambda_i$ being nonzero. **Contradiction.**

**Step 3.** Therefore $W=0$, so $\alpha=c=0$ and every $\omega_i=m_i\in\Lambda^-P$. $\square$

**Argue the opposite.** *The one place to attack is Step 2's use of $\ker MM^{\mathsf T}\subseteq\mathbb Ru$*, which is where a defender would look for room, and it is forced: $MM^{\mathsf T}$ is a rank-one perturbation of the identity supported on $\mathbb Ru$, so it is the identity on the two-dimensional $u^\perp$. *The second place is whether $Q_\Lambda$ could be degenerate*, which would enlarge the isotropic subspaces to dimension $2$ and break Step 1 outright. That needs some $\lambda_i=0$, which the hypothesis excludes and which the record excludes on its own ground: a vanishing weight drops the rank of $W$ below three. **So the lemma stands exactly as far as the nonvanishing of the weights, and no further, which is the same footing as [r824](REVIEW_r824.md) §7.** *The third place is whether the triple must lie in a conjugate of this particular $\mathfrak k$*, and it must: the singular $2$-tori are the six root kernels, all $W(D_3)$-conjugate, so all their centralisers are $SO(6)$-conjugate to $\mathfrak k$, and the hypotheses are $SO(6)$-invariant.
