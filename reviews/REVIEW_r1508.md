## A. Fidelity

- **The record.** [diagonal-half.md](../findings/diagonal-half.md) line 1019: $W=\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i\omega_i\otimes\omega_i\in\mathcal W_n$, the $\omega_i$ orthonormal, every $\lambda_i\ne0$, then $\Phi(\omega_1)=\Phi(\omega_2)=\Phi(\omega_3)$ with $\Phi(\omega)=(\omega^2,\omega\wedge\omega)$.
- **The Lean statement** (line 77). `hskew` (each `om i` skew, so a 2-form), `hon` (`inner2 (om i) (om i') = if i = i' then 1 else 0`, orthonormality in the record's $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$), `hlam` (every weight nonzero) and `hW : IsWeyl (rankSumOp lam om)`, where `rankSumOp lam om X = ∑ i, (lam i * inner2 (om i) X) • om i` is $W$ as an operator on $\Lambda^2$ and `IsWeyl` is [WeylReal.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean) line 126 (skew-preserving, self-adjoint, Bianchi, Ricci-flat), i.e. membership in $\mathcal W_n$. These are exactly the hypotheses of L183's `collinear_of_weyl` (L183Statement.lean line 454). The conclusion `Phi (om 0) = Phi (om 1) ∧ Phi (om 1) = Phi (om 2)` is the record's, with `Phi` SelfDual.lean line 109, `(X * X, wedgeSelf X)`. `n` is a free variable, so the statement holds at every $n$.
- **`emb` as a model of $S^2\oplus\Lambda^4$.** `Idx n` keeps all index pairs and all index quadruples, and `emb` sends $(P_1,P_2)$ to the vector with entries $P_1(a,b)$ and $P_2(a,b,c,d)/\sqrt{24}$. Its squared norm is $\sum_{ab}P_{1,ab}^2+\tfrac1{24}\sum_{abcd}P_{2,abcd}^2$. On a symmetric $P_1$ the first term is the Frobenius norm. On an alternating $P_2$ the second is $\sum_{a<b<c<d}P_{2,abcd}^2=\lVert P_2\rVert_\perp^2$, each increasing quadruple appearing $24$ times among the ordered ones and the other ordered quadruples with a repeated index contributing $0$. That is the norm the record's proof names (line 1022, "multi-index-orthonormal norm on $\Lambda^4$ ... with the Frobenius norm on $S^2$") and the convention of [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) line 125. `emb` is defined on the whole ambient product, not only on symmetric and alternating arrays, which costs nothing since $\Phi$ lands in that subspace and `emb` is a linear injection of the ambient space.
- **What the Lean argument needs of the norm.** The record uses that the three points lie on one sphere and on one line. The Lean needs the same two things plus a transport: `emb_sub` and `emb_smul` carry the affine relation of `collinear_of_weyl` into the Euclidean space, and `emb_injective` carries the three distinctnesses back. The norm must come from an inner product, because `eq_of_collinear_of_norm_eq` (CollinearSphere.lean line 32) expands $\lVert z+s(y-z)\rVert^2$ as a quadratic in $s$, and in a non-Euclidean norm such as the sup norm a line can meet a sphere in a segment. The record's "a line meets a sphere in at most two points" carries the same Euclidean assumption. Any inner product in which the three images have equal norm would serve, and the $1/\sqrt{24}$ weighting is one that makes `phi_sphere` give the norm directly. The Lean argument needs nothing beyond this.
- **L195 without ω₂'s skewness.** [diagonal-half.md](../findings/diagonal-half.md) line 1041 takes $\omega_1,\omega_2$ as 2-forms. `L195` now carries `hs₁` only (section variable line 67, `include hs₁ hsq` line 130), so its hypotheses are weaker and its conclusion `w₂ = w₁ ∨ w₂ = -w₁` unchanged. Every instance of the record's lemma is an instance of `L195`, so it is a strengthening and still the record's lemma. The only declaration whose binders change otherwise is `Aop_eq`, now `omit hs₁ in` and taking only `hsq`, which is what its proof uses. The rename `Matrix.ofLp_toEuclideanLin_apply` to `Matrix.ofLp_toLpLin` (line 126) builds, with no deprecation warning left.
