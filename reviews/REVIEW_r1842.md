## Claim 1, the classification

**Candidate 1 is a weakening of R6 that inherits R6's obstruction.** It is not R6 itself.

- R6 asks for a full decomposable orthonormal eigenbasis compatible with one frame. Candidate 1 asks only for a decomposable vector in the top eigenspace. Diagonalisability implies candidate 1, and the converse fails, so "R6 under another name" overstates the identity.
- The obstruction still transfers, but the certificate the sweep cites belongs to the wrong eigenspace. The $-0.408248$ eigenspace is the bottom one. Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at unit norm has spectrum $\{2,-1,-1\}/\sqrt6$ on $\Lambda^+\mathbb R^4$, extremes $0.816497$ and $-0.408248$ (checked). Its top eigenspace is one-dimensional, spanned by $(e_{12}+e_{34})/\sqrt2$, which has rank $4$ and so contains no decomposable vector. That is the certificate candidate 1 needs, and with it the local-max argument of [diagonality.md](../findings/diagonality.md) carries over: local data cannot rule out an obstructed top eigenspace, so only the value can.
- **$t^2=\tfrac12$ in the maximal frame: CONFIRMED.** In any frame of $\mathbb R^4$ the six sectional values are $\tfrac12$ times the diagonal of $W^+$ in an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^+$, each appearing twice, so $t^2=\tfrac12\sum_k\langle W^+\omega_k,\omega_k\rangle^2\le\tfrac12\lVert W^+\rVert^2=\tfrac12$, with equality in the eigenframe. Over $O(8)$ (frames mixing $\mathbb R^4$ with its complement) a six-start BFGS screen returns $0.4999999999$, which agrees with the value. Only the frames inside $\mathbb R^4$ are proved.
- **"The small-mass node is the lifting lemma's small-mass part": PLAUSIBLE.** A coordinate-diagonal unit $W$ has $t=1$, so the lifting lemma at a maximiser rules out small maximal-frame mass. At $n\ge12$, where $\theta_0(n)\ge\Theta_n>\sqrt{3/2}$, (MG) at small mass says exactly that no global maximiser has small mass. The two statements agree on that region, and the lifting lemma is strictly stronger overall.
- **"The frame vehicle has the one non-value separator R6's criterion asks for": REFUTED as stated.** R6's criterion asks for a property of *tensors* that a maximiser has and an obstructed local max lacks. Frame maximality is a property of a *(tensor, frame) pair*, and every tensor has a maximal frame. [Post F81](../findings/fence-posts.md)'s witness is the balanced product, the maximiser itself, in a rotated frame. Frame maximality excludes that *presentation*. It separates no tensor from any other. The sweep also concedes that R6's obstruction, padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, lies outside the small-mass region, so R6's criterion has no obstruction to bite on at this node. Corrected reading: at the small-mass node, frame maximality is the one hypothesis that F81 does not fence. It meets F81's "what a successor must clear", and that is not R6's criterion.
- A minor point: (MG)'s $\sqrt{3/2}$ branch covers padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at any mass, since its $\theta$ equals $\sqrt{3/2}$. The "so it is not a small-mass point" conclusion rests on $t^2=\tfrac12$ alone.

## Claim 2, the reduction

**CONFIRMED on the pair-free subspace, derived independently.**

- Ordering the block basis as $(e_{ik},e_{il},e_{jk},e_{jl})=(e_i,e_j)\otimes(e_k,e_l)$, the Pauli coefficients $\alpha_{IX},\alpha_{XI},\alpha_{ZX},\alpha_{XZ}$ are half-sums and half-differences of the entries $W_{ik,il},W_{jk,jl},W_{ik,jk},W_{il,jl}$. Each has three distinct indices, so all are pair entries and vanish when $W$ is pair-free. The two anti-diagonal entries $W_{ik,jl},W_{il,jk}$ span $X\otimes X$ and $J\otimes J$, with $\alpha_{XX}=\tfrac12(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})$.
- Rotating both planes by $\varphi$ acts as $R\otimes R$. The $(Z,X)$ pair in each factor rotates by $2\varphi$, and $I$ and $J$ are fixed, so $\alpha'_{IZ}=c\,\alpha_{IZ}$ and $\alpha'_{ZZ}=c^2\alpha_{ZZ}+s^2\alpha_{XX}$ once the mixed terms vanish. With $u=s^2$ the change per block is $4u[-(\alpha_{IZ}^2+\alpha_{ZI}^2)-2\alpha_{ZZ}^2+2\alpha_{ZZ}\alpha_{XX}+u(\alpha_{ZZ}-\alpha_{XX})^2]$, and summing gives the stated $g$. The sectional values $a_e$ inside matching planes are invariant, so they drop out. The draft [block_angle](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ap-code-private-hamiltons-cubic/d3790d3b-3383-4c5d-9548-46ea15965c86/scratchpad/MatchingAngle.lean) types exactly this bracket.
- **The endpoints.** $f$ depends on $\varphi$ only through $s^2$, so the first derivative vanishes and $f''(0)=32g(0)$, which makes $g(0)\le0$ the second-order condition. $g(1)=S_{XX}-S_{ZZ}-S_{IZ}$. The block's diagonal mass at $\tfrac\pi4$ is $4(\alpha_{II}^2+\alpha_{XX}^2)=\tfrac14s_{ef}^2+(W_{ik,jl}+W_{il,jk})^2$, so $g(1)\le0$ is the matching inequality with its pair terms set to zero. On $(0,1]$, $ug(u)\le0$ holds iff $g\le0$ there, which for affine $g$ is iff $g(0)\le0$ and $g(1)\le0$. Correct.
- **Value check at F81's witness, $n=12$**: $t^2=0.083333$, $\sum a_e^2=5/66$, $E(M)=11/12$, largest pair entry $2\times10^{-17}$. $f(\varphi)-f(0)$ agrees with $4ug(u)$ to six decimals at $\varphi=0.1,0.3,0.5,\tfrac\pi4$. The sweep compares $E(M)=\tfrac{11}{12}$ with $t^2=\tfrac1{12}$. The inequality's actual right side is $t^2-\sum a_e^2=\tfrac1{132}$, so the violation is larger than stated, and the stated comparison suffices.
- **Scope correction.** The two-condition reduction holds only for pair-free $W$. With a pair part, $f$ carries a first-order term and interior maxima. At the screen frame of claim 3 the maximum of $f(\varphi)-f(0)$ over $\varphi$ is $0.068$ against $0.016$ at $\tfrac\pi4$, and the first-order condition fails along some $\xi_M$ ($(f(h)-f(0))/h^2=466$ at $h=10^{-3}$, a linear term of about $0.47$). The next section of the sweep writes "the matching family, second order plus the $\tfrac\pi4$ inequality along every matching" for frames of the product that are not pair-free, which applies the reduction outside its hypothesis.

## Claim 3, the screens

**REFUTED: the screen checks a relaxation of the matching inequality, and its reported frame violates the inequality itself.**

- [screen_I121_matching_feasible.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I121_matching_feasible.py) tests $V_M\le t^2$, where $V_M$ is the four-set combination plus the pair terms. The matching inequality (Givens) reads $V_M+\tfrac14\sum s_{ef}^2\le t^2-\sum_{e\in M}a_e^2$. The screen drops $\tfrac14\sum s_{ef}^2$ from the left and $\sum a_e^2$ from the right, so it tests a necessary condition. A frame passing it need not satisfy the matching inequality.
- **The refutation.** I reran the script unchanged (seed 9, `4,4 0.2`) and evaluated the full inequality at its output frames ([review_r1842_screenfull.py](../scripts/reviewers/review_r1842_screenfull.py)). Start 0 reaches $t^2=0.1923$ with relaxed room $0.089$, matching the sweep's reported range. There the full matching inequality **fails by $0.0159$** on $M=\{02,15,36,47\}$, and by $0.0133$ and $0.0077$ on two others. Start 2 ($t^2=0.1798$, relaxed room $0.078$) fails by $0.0246$. As a check on the evaluation, the full left side minus the right side equals $f(\pm\tfrac\pi4)-f(0)$ from direct rotation to five decimals on every matching printed.
- The frames also fail the first-order condition and the equal-angle family, as noted under claim 2. The screen enforces no second-order or first-order condition, so "second order plus the $\tfrac\pi4$ inequality along every matching" was never tested.
- **Consequences.** "Frames of the balanced product exist at $t^2\approx0.20$ to $0.24$ satisfying all 105 matching inequalities" is false for the reported frames. The lower-cap floor ($t^2\approx0.09$ holds, $0.08$ fails) is the floor of the relaxed test, which says nothing about the matching family's floor. Even for the relaxed test, a descent failing at $0.08$ is no proof of infeasibility, so "locate the family's floor" would overstate in any case.
- **"Every family tried today lands on the same $n^{-1/2}$ barrier" is overstated.** The matching-family figure comes from one dimension, $n=8$, and a single $n$ cannot fix a rate. F81's $t^2=\tfrac1n$ is exact, and the frame-free $0.7/n$ is two dimensions of screens ([2026-09-28b sweep](2026-09-28b-LIT-step8-sweep.md)). The matching family's rate is unmeasured.
- Schur's $0.067=6/90$ at $n=8$ checks.

## Claim 4, the recommendation's premise

**REFUTED as supported.** The weakening of "keep I121" rests on claim 3, which tested a relaxation. Whether the true matching family, the $\tfrac\pi4$ inequalities with first- and second-order conditions along every $\xi_M$, admits frames of the product at $t^2\approx0.2$ is open. No screen on record decides it.

- "Full $O(n)$ maximality is a non-value separator": it is non-value, and it is a hypothesis F81 does not fence. It separates frames, never tensors (claim 1), so in R6's sense it cannot be the tie-break against I73 as written.
- "No tractable finite family yet" holds as a description. The equal-angle matching family, correctly tested, is a tractable finite family whose strength is unknown, so the conclusion that it fails is premature.
