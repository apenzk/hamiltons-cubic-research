#### Lemma L227, the $\lvert J\rvert=3$ step of R27 at $n=5$: $\sup P=\Theta_5$ on $\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_5$, attained at commuting blocks

*Gate 2 owed. Derived 2026-09-15 on Fable for [I106](../plan.md) ([derivation](../reviews/2026-09-15-I106-derivation.md), batch 4), recorded on Opus with the alignment written as two Rayleigh bounds. The polynomial steps are kernel-checked in [L227Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L227Statement.lean) and the alignment carries a named `sorry`.*

**Statement.** *Let $J\subseteq[5]$ with $\lvert J\rvert=3$ and $\mathcal S_J$ as in [L226](second-order.md). Every unit $W\in\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_5$ has $P(W)\le\Theta_5=\tfrac{2\sqrt2}3$, and the value $\Theta_5$ is attained at a unit $W$ whose blocks $M_a$ and $S$ commute, a coordinate-diagonal tensor in a frame of $\mathbb R^5$ rotated inside $\mathbb R^J$.*

**Proof.** Write the outer set as $\{a,b\}$ and use L226's item 3.
1. **The objective at two outer indices.** L226's item 2 gives $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_a=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_b=-K_{ab}$, so $M_a=mI+X_1$, $M_b=mI+X_2$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_i=0$ and $K_{ab}=-3m$, and $S=N-\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}N)I=X_1+X_2-mI$. For a traceless $3\times3$ $X$, Cayley–Hamilton gives $\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}(mI+X)=X^2-mX+\big(m^2-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^2\big)I$ and $\det(X-mI)=\tfrac13\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^3+\tfrac12m\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X^2-m^3$. Substituting,
$$P(W)=6\big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_1^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_2^3\big)+9\big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_1^2X_2+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_1X_2^2\big)-18m\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_1X_2)-72m^3,$$
$$\lVert W\rVert^2=2\big(\lvert X_1\rvert^2+\lvert X_2\rvert^2\big)+2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_1X_2)+18m^2 .$$
2. **The change of variables.** Put $U=X_1+X_2$ and $V=X_1-X_2$, traceless and symmetric. By cyclicity of the trace, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_1^3+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_2^3=\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac34\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_1^2X_2+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X_1X_2^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UX_1X_2)=\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3-\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X_1X_2)=\tfrac14\big(\lvert U\rvert^2-\lvert V\rvert^2\big)$, so
$$P(W)=\tfrac{15}4\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^3+\tfrac94\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)-\tfrac92m\lvert U\rvert^2+\tfrac92m\lvert V\rvert^2-72m^3,\qquad\lVert W\rVert^2=\tfrac32\lvert U\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert V\rvert^2+18m^2 .$$
**At fixed $m$, $U$ and $\lvert V\rvert$, the frame of $V$ enters only through $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)$, and the norm not at all.**
3. **The alignment.** Take an orthonormal eigenframe $Q\in SO(3)$ of $U$ with eigenvalues $u_1\ge u_2\ge u_3$, let $d_i=(Q^{\mathsf T}V^2Q)_{ii}$, and let $w_1\ge w_2\ge w_3\ge0$ be the eigenvalues of $V^2$, the squares of $V$'s eigenvalues $\nu_i$ indexed so that $\nu_i^2=w_i$. By Rayleigh, $d_1\le w_1$ and $d_3\ge w_3$, and $\sum d_i=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}V^2=\sum w_i$, so $d_1+d_2\le w_1+w_2$. Then
$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)=\sum_iu_id_i=u_3\sum_id_i+(u_2-u_3)(d_1+d_2)+(u_1-u_2)d_1\le\sum_iu_iw_i .$$
The matrix $V'=Q\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)Q^{\mathsf T}$ is traceless and symmetric, commutes with $U$, has $\lvert V'\rvert=\lvert V\rvert$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV'^2)=\sum_iu_iw_i\ge\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)$.
4. **The comparison tensor.** $X_1'=\tfrac12(U+V')$ and $X_2'=\tfrac12(U-V')$ are traceless, symmetric and diagonal in $Q$, and with the same $m$ and $K$ they define $W'\in\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_5$ by L226's item 2, with $\lVert W'\rVert=\lVert W\rVert$ and $P(W')-P(W)=\tfrac94\big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV'^2)-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV^2)\big)\ge0$ by step 2. The rotation of $\mathbb R^J$ by $Q^{\mathsf T}$ conjugates every $M_a$ and, since $Q\in SO(3)$ acts on the frame $\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31},\varphi_{12}$ by itself, conjugates $S$ as well, so it carries $W'$ to a tensor whose blocks and $S$ are diagonal. That tensor is coordinate-diagonal, a unit element of $\mathcal D_5$ at unit norm, and [L57](problem.md) gives $P(W)\le P(W')\le\Theta_5$. **Attainment**: $\mathcal D_5\subseteq\mathcal S_J$, and L57's maximiser, the vertex tensor of $S^2\times S^3$, has value $\Theta_5$. $\square$

#### Lemma L226, the cubic on the block subalgebra $\mathcal S_J$: every coupling carries the coefficient $3$, and at $\lvert J\rvert=3$ Ricci-flatness fixes $R_J$ as $N-\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}N)I$

*Gate 2 passed: [r948](../reviews/REVIEW_r948.md) and [r949](../reviews/REVIEW_r949.md) on identical briefs, every step CONFIRMED by both, with the definition of $\mathcal S_J$, three links of the chain and the scope of Gate 1 and of the Lean file repaired on their reports. The operator and the bridge to $P$ are typed in [L226Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L226Statement.lean).*

**Setting.** Fix $J\subseteq[n]$ with $\lvert J\rvert=k\ge2$, outer set $A=[n]\setminus J$ and $m=n-k$. Put $V_a=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\varphi_{pa}:p\in J\}$, identified with $\mathbb R^J$ by $x\mapsto\sum_px_p\varphi_{pa}$, and for $x,y\in\mathbb R^J$ put $x\wedge y=\sum_{p,q}x_py_q\varphi_{pq}\in\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$. Here $\mathcal S_J$ is the fixed space of $H_J$ in the symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$. The block note's character argument uses no Weyl condition and gives the same block form on it, and the block note's $\mathcal S_J$ is $\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$ in this notation. By the block note, $W\in\mathcal S_J$ is block-diagonal on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n=\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A}V_a\oplus\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$: a symmetric $R_J$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, a symmetric $M_a$ on $V_a$ with $(M_a)_{pq}=W_{pa,qa}$, and $W\varphi_{ab}=K_{ab}\varphi_{ab}$. So $\lVert W\rVert^2=\lvert R_J\rvert^2+\sum_a\lvert M_a\rvert^2+\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^2$. $\Lambda^2M$ is the operator $x\wedge y\mapsto Mx\wedge My$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$.

**Statement.**
1. *For every $W\in\mathcal S_J$,* $$P(W)=P(R_J)+\sum_{a\in A}\Big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_a^3+3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(R_J\,\Lambda^2M_a\big)\Big)+\sum_{a<b}\Big(K_{ab}^3+3K_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_aM_b)\Big)+3\sum_{a<b<c}K_{ab}K_{ac}K_{bc},$$ *where $P(R_J)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}R_J^3+\langle R_J,R_J^\#\rangle$ is the cubic of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J=\mathfrak{so}(k)$.*
2. *$W\in\mathcal S_J$ lies in $\mathcal W_n$ exactly when $R_J$ satisfies the first Bianchi identity on $\mathbb R^J$, $\rho(R_J)+\sum_aM_a=0$ with $\rho(R_J)_{pq}=\sum_{r\in J}\langle R_J\varphi_{pr},\varphi_{qr}\rangle$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_a+\sum_{b\ne a}K_{ab}=0$ at every $a\in A$.*
3. *At $k=3$, write $R_J$ in the orthonormal frame $\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31},\varphi_{12}$ as a symmetric $S$. Then $P(R_J)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S^3+3\det S$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(R_J\Lambda^2M)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}M)$, $\rho(R_J)=(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S)I-S$, and Bianchi holds automatically. So on $\mathcal S_J\cap\mathcal W_n$, with $N=\sum_aM_a$, $S=N-\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}N)I$ and* $$P(W)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S^3+3\det S+\sum_a\Big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_a^3+3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(S\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}M_a)\Big)+\sum_{a<b}\Big(K_{ab}^3+3K_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_aM_b)\Big)+3\sum_{a<b<c}K_{ab}K_{ac}K_{bc}.$$

**Proof.**
1. **$P$ in an eigenbasis.** Let $v_\alpha$ be an orthonormal eigenbasis of $W$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_\alpha$, and $c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\langle[v_\alpha,v_\beta],v_\gamma\rangle$, totally antisymmetric. The index formula of [conventions.md](conventions.md), $(W^\#)_{\alpha\beta}=\tfrac12\sum c_{\alpha\gamma\delta}c_{\beta\epsilon\zeta}W_{\gamma\epsilon}W_{\delta\zeta}$, gives $\langle W,W^\#\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^2$ over ordered triples, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3=\sum\lambda_\alpha^3$. *At $W=\mathrm{id}$ the first is $\tfrac12\cdot2(n-2)\dim\Lambda^2$, which is $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$.* $W\in\mathcal S_J$ is block-diagonal, so the eigenbasis is chosen block by block, taking $\varphi_{ab}$ itself as the eigenvector on each line $\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$ of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$.
2. **Brackets between blocks.** From $[\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{cd}]=\delta_{ac}\varphi_{bd}+\delta_{bd}\varphi_{ac}-\delta_{ad}\varphi_{bc}-\delta_{bc}\varphi_{ad}$, for distinct $p,q\in J$ and distinct $a,b,c\in A$: $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{qa}]=\varphi_{pq}$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{pb}]=\varphi_{ab}$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{qb}]=0$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{ab}]=-\varphi_{pb}$, $[\varphi_{pa},\varphi_{bc}]=0$, $[\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{ac}]=\varphi_{bc}$, $[\varphi_{pq},\varphi_{ra}]=\delta_{pr}\varphi_{qa}-\delta_{qr}\varphi_{pa}$, $[\varphi_{pq},\varphi_{rs}]\in\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, and $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ commutes with $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$. So $[V_a,V_a]\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ with $[x,y]=x\wedge y$, $[V_a,V_b]=\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$ with $[x,y]=\langle x,y\rangle\varphi_{ab}$, $[\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J,V_a]\subseteq V_a$, $[V_a,\varphi_{ab}]\subseteq V_b$, and $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ and $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^A$ are subalgebras. Hence $c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\ne0$ only when the three blocks are, up to order, three copies of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, one of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ with two of one $V_a$, the three blocks $V_a,V_b,\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$, or three pairs of $A$ forming a triangle.
3. **The four sums.** *Three copies of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$*: the structure constants are those of $\mathfrak{so}(k)$, so these triples give $\langle R_J,R_J^\#\rangle$ and, with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}R_J^3$, $P(R_J)$. *$\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ with two of $V_a$*: the one $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ vector takes $3$ positions in an ordered triple. For orthonormal eigenvectors $v_\beta,v_\gamma$ of $M_a$, $v_\beta\wedge v_\gamma$ is an eigenvector of $\Lambda^2M_a$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma$, and since $\langle x\wedge y,u\wedge v\rangle=\langle x,u\rangle\langle y,v\rangle-\langle x,v\rangle\langle y,u\rangle$ these form an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ over $\beta<\gamma$, so for a unit eigenvector $\omega$ of $R_J$ with eigenvalue $\mu$, $\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma\langle v_\beta\wedge v_\gamma,\omega\rangle^2=2\langle\Lambda^2M_a\,\omega,\omega\rangle$. Summing, $\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot2\sum_\omega\mu\langle\Lambda^2M_a\omega,\omega\rangle=3\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(R_J\Lambda^2M_a)$. *$V_a,V_b,\mathbb R\varphi_{ab}$*: $6$ orders, and $\sum_{\beta,\gamma}\lambda_\beta(M_a)\lambda_\gamma(M_b)\langle v_\beta,v_\gamma\rangle^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_aM_b)$, giving $\tfrac12\cdot6\,K_{ab}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_aM_b)$. *Triangles in $A$*: $6$ orders and $c^2=1$, giving $3K_{ab}K_{ac}K_{bc}$. With $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}R_J^3+\sum_a\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_a^3+\sum_{a<b}K_{ab}^3$ this is item 1.
4. **The Weyl conditions.** $W$ is symmetric and preserves $\Lambda^2$ by construction. An entry $W_{ij,kl}$ can be nonzero only when its index multiset lies in $J$, is $\{p,q,a,a\}$, or is $\{a,b,a,b\}$. A Bianchi sum with a repeated index follows from the pair symmetries, and one over four distinct indices not all in $J$ meets only entries of none of these shapes, so Bianchi reduces to $R_J$'s. For Ricci, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_{pq}=\rho(R_J)_{pq}+\sum_a(M_a)_{pq}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_{aa}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}M_a+\sum_{b\ne a}K_{ab}$, while $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_{pa}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_{ab}$ pair vectors of different blocks and vanish. This is item 2.
5. **$k=3$.** The frame $\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31},\varphi_{12}\mapsto e_1,e_2,e_3$ carries $x\wedge y$ to $x\times y$, and it carries the bracket of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$ to the cross product, since the bracket formula gives $[\varphi_{23},\varphi_{31}]=\varphi_{12}$ and its cyclic images. So every distinct orthonormal triple has $c^2=1$, and $\langle R_J,R_J^\#\rangle=\tfrac12\cdot6\det S$. The cofactor identity $Mx\times My=\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}(M)(x\times y)$ for symmetric $M$ makes $\Lambda^2M$ the adjugate. Entry by entry, $\rho_{11}=S_{22}+S_{33}$ and $\rho_{12}=-S_{12}$, so $\rho(S)=(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S)I-S$. Every symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^3$ satisfies Bianchi, since four indices from a set of three repeat one and step 4's repeated-index case applies. Taking traces in $(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S)I-S=-N$ gives $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}S=-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}N$, hence $S=N-\tfrac12(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}N)I$. $\square$

**The domain is a bijection, and here is why.** A coordinate-diagonal Weyl operator is determined by its vertex matrix $S$; first Bianchi is automatic on that locus, and Ricci-flatness is exactly $S\mathbf1=0$, so $\mathcal D_n\leftrightarrow$ the coordinate-diagonal part of $\mathcal W_n$ both ways. *Asserted throughout the record and proved nowhere until this audit.*

**Attainment, at every $n$ and both parities, in one closed form.** For any sphere product,
\[
\theta\big(S^p\times S^q\big)^2=\frac{2(n-1)(p-1)(q-1)}{(n-2)\,pq},\qquad p+q=n,
\]
from criticality with $\lambda_V=1-n$ independent of the split. Let $\mathcal D_n=\{S\ \text{symmetric, hollow},\ S\mathbf1=0\}$ be the vertex matrices of coordinate-diagonal Weyl operators. Then
\[
\langle Q(W),W\rangle\;\le\;\Theta_n\,\|W\|^3\qquad\text{for every diagonal }W\text{ at every }n\ge4,
\]
\[
\Theta_n^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-2)}{n^2}\ \ (n\text{ even}),\qquad
\Theta_n^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-3)}{(n+1)(n-2)}\ \ (n\text{ odd}),
