## Claim by claim

**0. Coordinates.** Raw $a=W_{ij,kl}$, $b=W_{ik,jl}$, $c=W_{il,jk}$, $a=b-c$. With $b=X+\tfrac J2$, $c=X-\tfrac J2$ one gets $b-c=J=a$, and $a^2+b^2+c^2=\tfrac32J^2+2X^2$, so $2(a^2+b^2+c^2)=3J^2+4X^2$. For the other pairings: on $(ik|jl)$ the raw triple is $(b,a,-c)$ and on $(il|jk)$ it is $(c,-a,-b)$, each satisfying $a'=b'-c'$ by the original Bianchi, so `rect_norm` applies to each. Four-set entries do not enter the Ricci contraction, so every $\nu_Q\in V_Q$ is Weyl.

**1. Only rectangles couple.** $Q$ is $O(n)$-equivariant, so $T$ is invariant under sign flips, and $T(\nu_{Q_1},\nu_{Q_2},\nu_{Q_3})$ picks up $\chi_{Q_1}\chi_{Q_2}\chi_{Q_3}=\chi_{Q_1\triangle Q_2\triangle Q_3}$. Trivial character forces every vertex to lie in $0$ or $2$ of the blocks. All three equal gives $\chi_Q\neq1$. Two equal forces the third empty. Three distinct forces $Q_3=Q_1\triangle Q_2$, $\lvert Q_1\cap Q_2\rvert=2$, six vertices in all, and the pairwise intersections are three disjoint edges. Conversely three disjoint edges determine the three blocks, so unordered block triples and rectangles are in bijection, and total symmetry of $T$ gives the $3!=6$.

**2. The $R^2$ part.** $T$'s $R^2$ part is $\langle\tfrac12(XY+YX),Z\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(XYZ)$ for symmetric operators, and $\operatorname{tr}(ACB)=\operatorname{tr}(ABC)$ by transposition, so the six orders agree. $\nu_Q(x,y)\neq0$ needs $x,y$ complementary 2-subsets of $Q$, and the forcing $x=e_2$, $y=e_1$, $z=e_3$ is correct. The product $J_{12}J_{13}J_{23}$ is invariant under each edge's orientation. The restatement $\operatorname{tr}B_f^3=6\sum_RJJJ$ also needs $\operatorname{tr}\nu_Q^3=0$ (true: $\nu_Q$ is three $2\times2$ off-diagonal blocks) and $\operatorname{tr}(\nu_Q^2\nu_{Q'})=0$ (character), both of which step 1 covers.

**3. The $\#$ part.** The calibration is right: on a diagonal tensor $R_{ijij}=K_{ij}$ contributes $\sum_{p\neq i,j}K_{ij}K_{ip}K_{jp}$ and $R_{ijji}$ contributes $0$ (its third factor is $R_{jjii}$), so $I_3=6\sum_{\text{tri}}$, and with `P_PiQ` $\kappa=\tfrac12$. Step 4 is right: the three factors' index sets pairwise meet in $\{i,k\}$, $\{j,l\}$, $\{p,q\}$, which are therefore the three edges ($6$ assignments, matching the $6$ assignments of blocks to factors), each factor is a crossing entry $X_m+\tfrac{s_m}2J_m$ with $s_1=\sigma_1\sigma_2$, $s_2=\sigma_1\sigma_3$, $s_3=\sigma_2\sigma_3$ relative to fixed reference orientations, and the bit sum is $8XXX+JJJ$ (`rect_bits`, whose exponents have exactly this pattern). So $I_3^{\text{mixed}}=48XXX+6JJJ$.

**5. Whole $P$ at pair-free $W$.** $T(A,A,\nu_Q)$ carries $\chi_Q\neq1$ and $T(A,\nu_Q,\nu_{Q'})$ carries $\chi_{Q\triangle Q'}\neq1$ for $Q\neq Q'$, so both vanish. $2a^2-b^2-c^2=2J^2-2X^2-\tfrac12J^2=\tfrac32J^2-2X^2$. Summing over $Q$ and its three pairings is the sum over unordered pairs of disjoint edges. The weights $3\cdot\tfrac32=\tfrac92$ and $3\cdot(-2)=-6$ follow. The check script reproduces $7.0944991067$ both ways at $n=6$.

**6. Raw reading.** The inner product of two $V_{Q_3}$ elements with pairing coefficients $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ and $(a_3,b_3,c_3)$ is $2(\alpha a_3+\beta b_3+\gamma c_3)$, which turns I104's halves into the note's expression. With $e_1=(i,j)$, $e_2=(k,l)$, $e_3=(m,p)$ every raw entry in I104's formula is $X\pm\tfrac J2$ with the sign the note uses (for example $b_3=W_{km,lp}=X_{23}+\tfrac{J_{23}}2$, $c_2=W_{ip,jm}=X_{13}-\tfrac{J_{13}}2$), so `rect_raw` applies. Two points strengthen the note's conclusion. First, I104's coefficient triple satisfies Bianchi, $\beta-\gamma=\tfrac12(b_1-c_1)(b_2-c_2)=\tfrac12a_1a_2=\alpha$, so it is an element of $V_{Q_3}$. Second, the only entries of character $\chi_{Q_3}$ are those of $V_{Q_3}$, so $\nu_1\circ\nu_2\in V_{Q_3}$, and agreement of $\tau$ for all $\nu_3\in V_{Q_3}$ pins the whole product. So the raw formula is settled as an identity of operators, not only of $\tau$.

**7. Product constant.** $x^2+j^2=(4X^2+3J^2)/N=1$, $\tfrac32/(3\sqrt3)=\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}$ and $4/8=\tfrac12$ are right, and `rect_unit` at $c=\tfrac1{\sqrt3}<1$ gives the bound. Equality case: the chain in `rect_unit` is tight only if $c\lvert j_1j_2\rvert\lvert j_3\rvert=\lvert j_1j_2\rvert$, which with $c<1$ forces $j_1j_2=0$, then $\lvert j_1j_2\rvert=\tfrac12(j_1^2+j_2^2)$ forces $j_1=j_2=0$, then $\lvert x_3\rvert=1$ forces $j_3=0$. So equality holds exactly at $x_i=\pm1$, and this agrees with the I104 equality case ($a_1=a_2=0$, signed $b=-c$, that is raw $b=c$, $J=0$).

**8. Matching form.** Three disjoint edges extend to $(n-7)!!$ perfect matchings of the rest. For a hollow symmetric matrix $\operatorname{tr}Y^3=6\sum_{\text{triples}}YYY$, so $\tfrac32\operatorname{tr}J_M^3+4\operatorname{tr}X_M^3=\sum_{R\subset M}(9JJJ+24XXX)$. Orientation flips conjugate $J_M$, $X_M$ by a diagonal sign matrix, so the traces are well defined. $E(M)=\sum4X^2=2\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2$. The matching inequality gives $E(M)\le t^2$, so $\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2\le\tfrac{t^2}2$. $\sum_M\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2=2(n-5)!!\sum_{(e,f)}X_{ef}^2=2(n-5)!!\cdot\tfrac38\lVert B_f\rVert^2=\tfrac34(n-5)!!\lVert B_f\rVert^2$, using $\sum_\pi4X_\pi^2=\tfrac32\lVert\nu_Q\rVert^2$ and orthogonality of blocks. Then $\lvert4\operatorname{tr}X_M^3\rvert\le4\lVert X_M\rVert_F^3\le\tfrac{4t}{\sqrt2}\lVert X_M\rVert_F^2$, and $(n-5)!!/(n-7)!!=n-5$ gives $\tfrac3{\sqrt2}(n-5)t\lVert B_f\rVert^2$. The section silently assumes $n$ even (perfect matchings), which the F81 context supplies. Stating it would help.
