## 1. The Casimir identity, Schur, and $n=4$

**The identity is true at every $n$, and the cheapest proof uses no representation theory.** Write it out in the pair basis, where $(A_a)_{mp}=c_{amp}$ and $c$ is totally antisymmetric:
$$\Big(\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a\Big)_{mp}=\sum_{a,q}(A_a)_{qm}(A_a)_{qp}=\sum_{a,q}c_{aqm}c_{aqp}.$$
A term is nonzero only if $\{a,q,m\}$ and $\{a,q,p\}$ are each the three pairs on three distinct indices. The two pairs $a$ and $q$ already span three indices, so both triples are the index set $a\cup q$, and $m$ and $p$ are both its third pair, forcing $m=p$. **So every off-diagonal entry vanishes identically.** For $m=p$ the count is as follows: $n-2$ index triples contain the pair $m$, each contributes the two orderings of the remaining two pairs, and each constant has modulus one, giving $2(n-2)$.

**The written justification is incomplete at $n=4$ and stands at every other $n$.** $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4=\Lambda^2_+\oplus\Lambda^2_-$ under $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, so the $\mathfrak{so}(4)$-invariant operators on $\Lambda^2$ form a two-dimensional algebra and Schur delivers only $c_+\pi_++c_-\pi_-$. **The repair is one clause**: the sum $\sum_a\mathrm{ad}_{\varphi_a}^2$ is independent of which orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is used, hence invariant under $\mathrm{Ad}_g$ for every $g\in O(n)$, and a reflection reverses orientation and exchanges $\Lambda^2_\pm$, which forces $c_+=c_-$. At $n\ge5$ and at $n=3$ the adjoint module is irreducible and the line as written is sound, and at $n=2$ both sides vanish. **The repair is needed at $4$ only, and the index count above makes it unnecessary at every $n$.**

**Screen** (a scratch script outside the repository, seconds, on the record's own [build_structure_constants](../scripts/lib/weyl.py)): at $n=4,5,6,7,8$ the residual $\lVert\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a-2(n-2)\mathrm{Id}\rVert_\infty$ is exactly $0$, the total antisymmetry residual is exactly $0$, and every nonzero $c$ has modulus one. **This covers $n=4$, the case the block's own Gate 1 skips, and the two odd dimensions it skips as well.**

## 2. The count, and whether a factor in $c$ would move the bound

**The count is right.** Nonzero constants correspond to ordered triples of distinct pairs lying on a common index triple, which is $6\binom n3=n(n-1)(n-2)$, and the block's gloss "one nonzero constant of modulus one for each ordered triple of distinct indices" names the same number by the other bijection. Screened exact at $n=4,5,6,7,8$ above.

**Modulus one is the record's own normalisation and it is verified rather than assumed.** [weyl.py](../scripts/lib/weyl.py) builds $c_{abg}=\langle[\varphi_a,\varphi_b],\varphi_g\rangle$ with $\varphi_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{ji}$ and $\langle A,B\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A^{\mathsf T}B)$, under which the $\varphi$ are orthonormal and $[\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{jk}]=\varphi_{ik}$, so $c$ takes values in $\{0,\pm1\}$.

**A factor would rescale the statement and not break it, and the numeral is pinned independently.** Under $c\mapsto\beta c$ both $X^{\#}$ and the Casimir scalar carry $\beta^2$, so the derived inequality reads $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le\beta^2(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2$ and matches the rescaled $\#$: the derivation is covariant. What fixes the numeral is [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md)'s mandatory anchor $\mathrm{id}^{\#}=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$, and **the lemma's own basis-free formula delivers it exactly**: $(\mathrm{id}^{\#})_{ab}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_aA_b)=\tfrac12\langle A_a,A_b\rangle_F=(n-2)\delta_{ab}$, screened at $n=4,5,6,7,8$ with residual exactly $0$. **So the factor-of-two trap that file records against the index formula does not bite here.**

## 3. Invariance, and the scalar read off the trace

**Both halves hold.** $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a=-\sum_a\mathrm{ad}_{\varphi_a}^2$ is the Casimir operator of the adjoint module for an invariant form, and invariance follows from basis independence rather than from anything special about $\Lambda^2$: replacing $\{\varphi_a\}$ by $\{\mathrm{Ad}_g\varphi_a\}$ leaves the sum unchanged because $\mathrm{Ad}_g$ is orthogonal for that form. The trace reading is right as well, $\operatorname{tr}\big(\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a\big)=\sum_{a,m,p}c_{amp}^2=n(n-1)(n-2)$ against $N=\binom n2=n(n-1)/2$, and $n(n-1)(n-2)/(n(n-1)/2)=2(n-2)$. **The trace step is sound and it is the scalarity step alone that owes the $n=4$ clause.**

## 4. The first Cauchy--Schwarz, and what antisymmetry is for

**It holds exactly as written, with $P=A_aX$ and $Q=A_bX$.** The correct pairing is $\operatorname{tr}(PQ)=\operatorname{tr}((P^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}Q)=\langle P^{\mathsf T},Q\rangle$, and the Frobenius norm is transpose-invariant, so $\lvert\operatorname{tr}(PQ)\rvert\le\lVert P^{\mathsf T}\rVert_F\lVert Q\rVert_F=\lVert P\rVert_F\lVert Q\rVert_F$. **The antisymmetry of $A_a$ is not what carries this step**, and it does carry two others: $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a=-\sum_aA_a^2$ identifies the sum as the Casimir, and the total antisymmetry of $c$ is what makes the sum the same numbers as $\mathrm{id}^{\#}$ (section 10). The companion step $\sum_a\lVert A_aX\rVert^2=\operatorname{tr}(X^{\mathsf T}(\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a)X)$ carries its transposes correctly too.

## 5. The second step is an identity

**Confirmed, and no inequality is doubled.** With $u_a=\lVert A_aX\rVert^2\ge0$, the bound of section 4 applied once to each of the $N^2$ terms gives $\sum_{ab}\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(A_aXA_bX)^2\le\tfrac14\sum_{ab}u_au_b$, and $\sum_{ab}u_au_b=(\sum_au_a)^2$ is an exact factorisation of a product of two independent sums. The arithmetic closes: $\tfrac14(2(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2)^2=(n-2)^2\lVert X\rVert^4$, so $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2$. **The single Cauchy--Schwarz of section 4 is used once per term and nowhere twice.**

## 6. Step 1, and an equality case that is vacuous where it is invoked

**The inequality is right and needs only that $X$ is symmetric**, which $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ gives before $\mathcal W_n$ is mentioned: $\lVert X^2\rVert^2=\operatorname{tr}X^4=\sum_i\lambda_i^4\le(\sum_i\lambda_i^2)^2=\lVert X\rVert^4$.

**The equality clause is true of symmetric operators and says nothing about $\mathcal W_n$.** Equality in $\sum\lambda_i^4\le(\sum\lambda_i^2)^2$ holds when at most one eigenvalue is nonzero, so rank one and also the trivial $X=0$. **No nonzero rank-one algebraic Weyl tensor exists**: for $X=\pm vv^{\mathsf T}$ with $v\in\Lambda^2$ the Ricci contraction is $\pm\sum_jv_{ij}v_{kj}=\pm(vv^{\mathsf T})_{ik}$ as an $n\times n$ matrix, of trace $\pm\lVert v\rVert^2\ne0$, so Ricci-flatness fails. **And rank one kills the other half exactly**: $(X^{\#})_{ab}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_avv^{\mathsf T}A_bvv^{\mathsf T})=-\tfrac12(v^{\mathsf T}A_bv)(v^{\mathsf T}A_av)=0$ because each $A$ is antisymmetric. **So the two halves of step 3 cannot saturate together at any $X$ whatever**, which is a structural reason the sum $(n-1)$ is unattained and belongs beside the lossiness note rather than inside step 1.

## 7. Is $\kappa$ the constant the estimate needs

**The second supremum dominates the first, and every link is true.** $\kappa=\sup\{\lVert\pi^\perp(X\circ X)\rVert:X\in\mathcal D_n^\perp,\lVert X\rVert=1\}$ and the lemma bounds $\sup\lVert Q(X)\rVert$ over unit $X$. First, $X\circ X=Q(X)$: [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) fixes $X\circ Y=\tfrac12(Q(X+Y)-Q(X)-Q(Y))$, and $Q$ is quadratic homogeneous, so $X\circ X=\tfrac12(4Q(X)-2Q(X))=Q(X)$. Second, $\pi^\perp$ is an orthogonal projection, hence a contraction, so $\lVert\pi^\perp(Q(X))\rVert\le\lVert Q(X)\rVert$. Third, and this is the link that makes the domination robust, **the lemma's proof never uses $\mathcal W_n$**: step 2 uses no property of $X$ at all and step 1 uses only symmetry, so the bound holds on all of $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2)$ and covers a unit element of $\mathcal D_n^\perp$ however that perp is taken. Hence $\kappa\le n-1$.

**The block states the conclusion and none of the three links.** It writes "So the constant $\kappa$ of [I96](../plan.md)'s isolation estimate is at most $n-1$" directly under the display, leaving a reader to supply the polarisation identity, the contraction, and the observation that the hypothesis $X\in\mathcal W_n$ is unused. **The third is worth stating rather than eliding, because it is a strengthening**: the lemma as proved is a statement about symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2$, and stating it on $\mathcal W_n$ understates it and hides why the $\kappa$ domination does not depend on $\mathcal D_n^\perp\subseteq\mathcal W_n$.

## 8. The radius, which is refuted as written

**The dichotomy needs three things at the configuration where it is applied**: [L214](../findings/second-order.md)'s off-diagonal equation, which is given and passed Gate 2; a lower bound $\lVert(2L_D-\theta)N\rVert\ge\gamma\lVert N\rVert$, which needs $\gamma$ to be the transverse gap **at the actual $D$ and the actual $\theta$** and needs $N$ transverse to the kernel; and $\kappa\le n-1$, which the lemma supplies.

**The given $\gamma=2\theta_0(n)/(n-2)$ is the gap at the balanced product, and in a split with $N\ne0$ the operator is not that one.** At $\lVert W\rVert=1$ the split $W=D+N$ has $\lVert D\rVert^2=t<1$ whenever $N\ne0$, so $D$ is not the unit balanced product, and $\theta=P(W)/\lVert W\rVert^2$ is not $\theta_0$. Under a rescaling that makes $\lVert D\rVert=1$, $\theta$ still moves off $\theta_0$, and $2L_D-\theta=2L_{W_0}-\theta_0+(\theta_0-\theta)\mathrm{Id}$ has its old kernel directions carrying the eigenvalue $\theta_0-\theta$ rather than the gap $\gamma$. **So on the very directions the estimate must control, the available lower bound degrades to the size of the perturbation**, which is why the perturbation step is the owed item and not a formality. **The configurations at which the radius is established are therefore exactly those with $N=0$, where it asserts nothing.**

**Worse, the sentence as written is false, and the record supplies the falsifier.** The block writes: "no critical point sits at a positive distance below it from the diagonal locus in that frame." Since $\pi_F$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal D_n$, $\lVert N\rVert$ **is** that distance. Take the balanced product $W_0$ at $n=8$ and rotate it inside $SO(n)$ by $\exp(\varepsilon\varphi_{04})$, a generator with a nontrivial sign character, so the orbit direction lies in $\mathcal D_n^\perp$, a fact this record already carries in [L214](../findings/second-order.md)'s own block. Equivariance of $Q$ makes every rotate critical with the same $\theta$.

The witness lives in the kernel, because the gauge orbit is a kernel direction by construction, so **the vanishing excess does not rescue the statement**: the excess hypothesis buys "kernel equals gauge orbit", and the gauge orbit is itself the falsifier. **The honest conclusion is isolation modulo gauge**, either by quantifying over frames, $\inf_F\lVert N_F\rVert\ge\gamma/\kappa$ or $=0$, whose conclusion reads "$W$ is coordinate-diagonalisable in some frame", or by carrying "$N$ orthogonal to the gauge orbit" as a stated hypothesis. *The first route runs straight into this record's recorded trap about substituting $\max_Ft_F$ into a formula whose $t_F$ means one frame's own value, so it owes a first-order argument at the minimising frame rather than a substitution.*

## 9. The dichotomy's logic

**The step is valid and it carries two side conditions the block leaves implicit.** From $\gamma\lVert N\rVert\le\kappa\lVert N\rVert^2$: if $N\ne0$ then $\lVert N\rVert>0$ and dividing gives $\gamma\le\kappa\lVert N\rVert$, hence $\lVert N\rVert\ge\gamma/\kappa$ provided $\kappa>0$. The relaxation $\gamma/\kappa\ge\gamma/(n-1)$ needs both $\kappa\le n-1$, which the lemma gives, and $\gamma>0$.

**At $\gamma\le0$ the whole chain is empty.** The hypothesis $\gamma\lVert N\rVert\le\kappa\lVert N\rVert^2$ then holds automatically for every $N$, the conclusion $\lVert N\rVert\ge\gamma/\kappa$ is a statement about a non-positive number and excludes nothing, and the relaxation to $\gamma/(n-1)$ reverses direction. **The direction of every inequality in the chain is the one the estimate needs**, $\gamma$ from below on the linear part and $\kappa$ from above on the quadratic part, which is exactly the direction [r928](REVIEW_r928.md) found the screen getting wrong, and the lemma does fix that half. **What the block never writes is the transversality of $N$ itself**, and section 8 is what that omission costs.

## 10. It is on the record, three ways, and the $n=4$ defect was found here before

**Searched by value and by shape rather than by the word Casimir.** I grepped [findings/](../findings/), [reviews/](.) and [references/](../references/) for $2(n-2)$, for $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a$, for $\mathrm{id}^{\#}$, and for norm bounds of the form $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le$ and $\lVert Q(X)\rVert\le$.

1. **It is the mandated anchor $\mathrm{id}^{\#}=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$, up to a factor of two, and the two are one identity read at two contractions.** By the total antisymmetry of $c$, $c_{aqm}=c_{maq}$, so $\big(\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a\big)_{mp}=\sum_{a,q}c_{maq}c_{paq}=\langle A_m,A_p\rangle_F=2(\mathrm{id}^{\#})_{mp}$. **The Casimir identity is literally the same $N^2$ numbers as the check [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) makes mandatory before any $\#$ formula is used.**
2. **That anchor is proved at every $n$ in Lean, `sorry`-free, as [sharpOp_id](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/StructureConstants.lean) through [sum_inner2_ad2](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/StructureConstants.lean)**, which is $\sum_{ij}\langle\mathrm{ad}_X\mathrm{ad}_Y\varphi_{ij},\varphi_{ij}\rangle=2(n-2)\operatorname{tr}(XY)$, recorded in [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) and used by [L209](../findings/curvature-invariants.md).
3. **The same contraction is tabled in [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) as the first orbit moment.** With $g_k=\lVert[\mathrm{ad}(v_k),X]\rVert^2$ and $X$ symmetric, $[\,A_k,X]$ is symmetric and $\sum_kg_k=4(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2-4\operatorname{tr}(X^{\#})$, which with the record's $\operatorname{tr}W^{\#}=-\lVert W\rVert^2$ is the tabled $\sum_kg_k=4(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$, flagged there as needing no criticality. **The first half of that identity is step 2's $\sum_a\lVert A_aX\rVert^2=2(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2$ verbatim.**

## 11. The lossiness, understated by the headline and removable by the method

**The headline is false at $n=6$ on the block's own numbers.** The quoted ratios are $4/0.504=7.9$ and $5/0.620=8.1$ at $n=6$, against $6/0.352=17.0$ and $7/0.442=15.8$ at $n=8$. **Two of the four quoted pairs are inside an order of magnitude**, so the claim holds at $n=8$ and not at $n=6$. Two further points about those numbers: $400$ random samples in a space of dimension $84$ or more measure a lower bound on the supremum and not a maximum, so the true lossiness is smaller than quoted, and **the quoted maxima are not specific to $\mathcal W_n$** (my screen over random symmetric unit $X$ reproduces them, $0.469$ at $n=6$ and $0.356$ at $n=8$ on $200$ samples), which is consistent with section 7's finding that the proof never uses the module.

**A sharper constant is visibly available from the same two ingredients, and it is $\sqrt{n-2}$.** Set $T_a=A_aX$ and $M_{ab}=\operatorname{tr}(T_aT_b)=\langle T_a^{\mathsf T},T_b\rangle$, so that $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert=\tfrac12\lVert M\rVert_F$ exactly. Let $\mathcal A$ have rows $\mathrm{vec}(T_a^{\mathsf T})$ and $\mathcal B$ rows $\mathrm{vec}(T_b)$, so $M=\mathcal A\mathcal B^{\mathsf T}$ and $\lVert M\rVert_F\le\lVert\mathcal A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert\mathcal B\rVert_F$. The Casimir identity gives $\lVert\mathcal B\rVert_F=\sqrt{2(n-2)}\lVert X\rVert$ unchanged. For the other factor, $\lVert\mathcal A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{\lVert u\rVert=1}\lVert\mathrm{ad}_uX\rVert_F\le\sup_u\lVert\mathrm{ad}_u\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert X\rVert$, and $\mathrm{ad}_u$ is skew on $\Lambda^2$, so its operator norm is its spectral radius: with $\pm ia_k$ the skew eigenvalues of $u$ and $\sum_ka_k^2=\lVert u\rVert^2=1$, the eigenvalues of $\mathrm{ad}_u$ on $\Lambda^2$ are $\pm i(a_k\pm a_l)$ and $\pm ia_k$, so $\lVert\mathrm{ad}_u\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\max_{k\ne l}(a_k+a_l)\le\sqrt2$. Hence
$$\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le\tfrac12\cdot\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt{2(n-2)}\,\lVert X\rVert^2=\sqrt{n-2}\,\lVert X\rVert^2,\qquad\lVert Q(X)\rVert\le\big(1+\sqrt{n-2}\big)\lVert X\rVert^2.$$
**Screened at $n=5,6,8,10$**: $\sup_u\lVert\mathrm{ad}_u\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$ reaches $1.4142$ at two equal blocks and never exceeds it on $400$ random directions, and neither the intermediate nor the final bound is violated on $300$ random symmetric unit $X$ per dimension. **The radius it gives is $2\theta_0/((n-2)(1+\sqrt{n-2}))$, which is $0.1107$ at $n=8$ and $0.0594$ at $n=12$**, factors of $2.0$ and $2.6$ over the block's numbers, from an $O(\sqrt n)$ constant in place of an $O(n)$ one.

**Where the loss sits, stated positively.** The block attributes it to two Cauchy--Schwarz steps. The second is the expensive one, and the reason is legible: the bound $\lVert M\rVert_F\le\sum_a\lVert T_a\rVert^2$ is tight only if every $T_a^{\mathsf T}$ is parallel to every $T_b$, which for $N\ge2$ forces all $T_a$ to be multiples of one rank-one matrix. **The record's own anchor measures the cost**: at $X=\mathrm{id}$ the truth is $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert=(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2/\sqrt N$, so the sum step alone gives away a factor $\sqrt{\binom n2}$. **And section 6 adds a second, structural, source**: the two halves of step 3 have disjoint maximisers, $X^2$ saturating at rank one where $X^{\#}$ vanishes identically, so $n-1$ is unattained for a reason independent of either Cauchy--Schwarz.

## 12. What would break it, and what I did to look

**The bound's load-bearing objects, each checked.** *(i) The normalisation of $c$*, which is the record's live trap in this area: a factor $\beta$ moves the numeral, and I verified from the lemma's own basis-free formula that $\mathrm{id}^{\#}=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$ exactly at $n=4,5,6,7,8$, so the lemma sits on the pinned convention and not on the doubled one. *(ii) The total antisymmetry of $c$*, which carries $A_a^{\mathsf T}=-A_a$, the identification of the sum as a Casimir, and the bridge of section 10, screened exact. *(iii) $X\circ X=Q(X)$*, which a polarisation factor of two would break and which section 7 derives from the tabled definition. *(iv) The scalarity of the Casimir sum*, whose failure would be fatal and which the index count of section 1 settles at every $n$ without Schur. **I looked for a falsifier of the bound over $400$ random symmetric unit $X$ at $n=6$ and $n=8$ and $300$ more at $n=5,10$, at the identity, and at rank one, and found none, which is expected, since the proof is correct.**

## 13. The Lean file types the arithmetic shadow, and says so
