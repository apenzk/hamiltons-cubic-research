### Reviewer 20: the $SO(n)$-invariance vanishing, and what it does not establish

**The proof stands; the consequence drawn from it does not.** Five of six attack points survived, rebuilt from scratch, structure constants (exact match), the $\#$ operation ($5.3\times10^{-15}$), the trilinear form, the Weyl basis, $u$ constructed by hand ($P(u)=1.224744871392$), and $\rho(A)=\mathrm{ad}_A$ (exact match against the repo's version).

The derivation identity is sign-convention-free, all three terms carrying the same sign; $\mathrm{Ad}(SO(n))$-invariance holds at $7.8\times10^{-14}$, while a random $SO(N)$ rotation on $\Lambda^2$ breaks it by $-31.0$ and a flipped-sign control by $-111$. Building $T$ by polarizing $P$ alone gives $\langle A\circ B,C\rangle/T(A,B,C)=0.9999999999999992$, so there is no factor slip, the factor-two trap lives in $\mathrm dQ(u)=2F_u$, elsewhere. The derivation $X\mapsto[\rho(A),X]$ preserves symmetry exactly and is Frobenius-skew to $8\times10^{-17}$; a sharpening worth keeping is that $\langle[R,X],X\rangle=0$ by trace cyclicity for **any** $R$, so skewness is not what that step needs. The orbit tangent and the $\tfrac12\theta^*$-eigenspace coincide with principal cosines $1.000000000000$, and the inclusion is proven dimension-free at $2.2\times10^{-16}$.

**The scope is exactly as narrow as the proof suggests.** The identity fails for generic $x$ ($0.33$) and for sums across eigenspaces ($0.29$), and the exact law is $T(x,y,[\rho(A),u])=(\lambda-\mu)\theta^*\langle A\!\cdot\!x,y\rangle$, vanishing on the diagonal, reaching $0.91$ off it.

**The refutation.** The identity's content is one column of the $7\times7$ coupling table, $\Pi_{V_{1/2}}Q(V_\lambda)=0$ for every $\lambda$, while the containment $Q(V_{1/3})\subseteq\ker F_u\oplus\langle u\rangle$ needs five components to vanish, with the $\langle u\rangle$ component forced and nonzero at $\theta^*/3=0.408$. It holds on every eigenspace while the containment fails on four rows, for $x\in V_{-1/6}$, $Q(x)$ carries a $0.29$ component in $V_{1/3}$ and $0.045$ in $V_{-1}$ against an orbit component of $10^{-15}$, so a statement true where the conclusion is false cannot force it.

**And it is inert downstream.** In the Remark 4.18 subdivision $W_2$ lies in $(\mathbb Ru\oplus T_u\mathcal O)^\perp$ and therefore has no $V_{1/2}$ component by construction; the coupling step needs $Q(W_1)$ orthogonal to the three negative eigenspaces, which this identity does not reach. It proves a fifth, previously unlisted statement rather than any of the four the bound rests on.

**Two findings beyond the brief.** The argument never uses criticality of $u$ (it holds at a random non-critical base point across all 84 eigenvectors) and it does prove $P\equiv0$ on $V_{1/2}$, which the original write-up did not claim.
