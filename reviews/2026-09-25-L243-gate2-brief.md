#### Lemma L238, the Gaussian form of a seven-frame's bracket total, and the isotropic bound 42

*Derived 2026-09-23 at effort `high` (`$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `high`), for the seven-frame lemma owed by section 10 of [the summed-pair note](../reviews/2026-09-23-I73c-summed-pairs.md). **Gate 2 by [r1113](../reviews/REVIEW_r1113.md) and [r1114](../reviews/REVIEW_r1114.md)**: (i) to (iii) CONFIRMED by both, with $i\neq j$, $d\ge5$ and the $d=5$ value added, and the claim that both known $42$-frames are isotropic REFUTED by the pencil frame.*

**Setting.** $X_1,\dots,X_7$ orthonormal in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ under $\langle X,Y\rangle=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}XY$; $Q_i=-X_i^2$, $P=\sum_iQ_i$ (so $\operatorname{tr}P=14$), $S_{ij}=X_iX_j+X_jX_i$, $d=\operatorname{rank}P$; $v$ a standard Gaussian vector and $G(v)$ the $7\times7$ Gram matrix of $X_1v,\dots,X_7v$; $T=\sum_{i\neq j}\lVert[X_i,X_j]\rVert^2$, the ordered total. A seven-frame does not fit in $\mathfrak{so}(4)$, whose dimension is $6$, so $d\ge5$.

**Statement.** (i) For each pair $i\neq j$, $\lVert[X_i,X_j]\rVert^2=2\operatorname{tr}(Q_iQ_j)-\tfrac12\lVert S_{ij}\rVert_F^2=\mathbb E\,\lvert X_iv\wedge X_jv\rvert^2-4$, hence
$$T=\mathbb E\big[(\operatorname{tr}G)^2-\lVert G\rVert_F^2\big]-168 .$$
(ii) With $r=\min(7,d-1)$, $T\le(1-\tfrac1r)\big(196+2\operatorname{tr}P^2\big)-168$. (iii) **When $P$ is a multiple of the identity on its range, $T\le42$**: the bound of (ii) is $336/d$ for $d\ge8$ and $37.8,\ 41.07,\ 42$ at $d=5,6,7$. It is attained at $d=8$ by the octonion frame $L_{e_i}/2$ ($P=\tfrac74 I_8$) and at $d=7$ by the complement of $\mathfrak g_2$ in $\mathfrak{so}(7)$ ($P=2I_7$ by $G_2$-irreducibility of $\mathbb R^7$).

**Proof.** (i) For skew $X,Y$, $\lVert[X,Y]\rVert^2=\operatorname{tr}(X^2Y^2)-\operatorname{tr}(XYXY)$ and $\operatorname{tr}S^2=2\operatorname{tr}(XYXY)+2\operatorname{tr}(X^2Y^2)$ by cyclicity, which is the first form. Isserlis' fourth moment $\mathbb E[(v^{\mathsf T}Av)(v^{\mathsf T}Bv)]=\operatorname{tr}A\operatorname{tr}B+2\operatorname{tr}AB$ for symmetric $A,B$ gives $\mathbb E[G_{ii}G_{jj}]=4+2\operatorname{tr}Q_iQ_j$ and, for $i\ne j$, $G_{ij}=-\tfrac12v^{\mathsf T}S_{ij}v$ with $\operatorname{tr}S_{ij}=-4\langle X_i,X_j\rangle=0$, so $\mathbb E G_{ij}^2=\tfrac12\lVert S_{ij}\rVert_F^2$. Summing over $i\neq j$ gives the displayed identity. (ii) Each $X_i$ vanishes on $\ker P$ and maps into $\operatorname{ran}P$, so restrict to $W=\operatorname{ran}P\cong\mathbb R^d$ with $v$ Gaussian there. $X_iv\perp v$ puts the seven vectors in a $(d-1)$-space, so $G(v)$ has rank at most $r$, and Cauchy–Schwarz on its eigenvalues gives $(\operatorname{tr}G)^2-\lVert G\rVert_F^2\le(1-\tfrac1r)(\operatorname{tr}G)^2=(1-\tfrac1r)(v^{\mathsf T}Pv)^2$. Isserlis again gives $\mathbb E(v^{\mathsf T}Pv)^2=196+2\operatorname{tr}P^2$. (iii) $\operatorname{tr}P^2=196/d$ in the isotropic case, and the arithmetic is case by case. $\square$

**(ii) at $r=7$ needs no Gaussian.** With $M_{ij}=\operatorname{sym}(X_i^{\mathsf T}X_j)-\delta_{ij}P/k$ for a family of $k$ skew matrices, the per-pair identity and $\sum_iQ_i=P$ give the exact identity $$\sum_{i\neq j}\lVert[X_i,X_j]\rVert^2=2\big(1-\tfrac1k\big)\operatorname{tr}P^2-2\sum_{i,j}\lVert M_{ij}\rVert_F^2,$$ orthonormality unused. It is the Gaussian identity with $\mathbb E\lVert G-\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}G}7I\rVert_F^2$ evaluated, so it proves (ii) at $r=7$ and (iii) for $d\ge8$ outright, and it names the deficiency: equality holds exactly when the $X_i$ pairwise anticommute and every $Q_i=P/k$. The Gaussian is needed only for the rank refinement at $d\le7$.

**What it does.** *It splits the seven-frame lemma.* The isotropic class is closed at $42$, sharp at the octonion and $\mathfrak g_2$-complement frames. **The remainder, anisotropic $P$, contains a frame at $42$ as well**, the pencil frame $X_i=u\wedge w_i$ (all through one vector $u$, distinct from section 10's star-realised frames), where step (ii) loses: it has $T=42$, $\operatorname{tr}P^2=56$, and (ii) returns $96$, because $G(v)=xx^{\mathsf T}+t^2I$ has one eigenvalue far from the rest. **Closing the lemma needs a pointwise bound on $(\operatorname{tr}G)^2-\lVert G\rVert_F^2$ that sees the spread of $G$'s spectrum**, or a reduction of an arbitrary frame to an isotropic one that does not lower $T$. *Literature: the DDVV-type bound for skew matrices ([Ge 2014](../references/ge-2014-ddvv-skew-symmetric/notes.md), constant $\tfrac23$) gives $65.3$ for a seven-frame, being sharp at three matrices; the Clifford-span theorem of Ge–Li–Zhou ([survey, Thm 3.10](../references/ge-li-tang-zhou-2024-ddvv-survey/README.md)) gives $42$ only inside a Clifford span; the Lu–Wenzel row bounds ([notes](../references/ge-li-lu-zhou-2019-lu-wenzel-conjectures/notes.md)) are conjectures, and in this norm they give a row at most $14$ and a top-six sum at most $16$, weaker than $\kappa_6=8$. None bounds a seven-frame below $54.064$.* **The lemma holds in any orthogonal representation $\rho$ of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$**, with $\rho(X_i)$ renormalised by the index $\iota_\rho$ ($-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\rho(X)\rho(Y)=\iota_\rho\langle X,Y\rangle$) and $T$ replaced by $T/\iota_\rho$. In the spin representation, $\iota=N/8$, the pencil frame becomes isotropic and its bound is exactly $42$. *Screened: on ascent-maximised frames at $n=8$ the smaller of the vector and spin bounds still reaches $56.6$, so the two representations together do not close the lemma.*

#### Lemma L243, the Gaussian subspace cap at every dimension, in class form

*Gate 2 owed. The class-form arithmetic is typed in [L243GaussianSubspaceCap.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L243GaussianSubspaceCap.lean), taking the Gaussian form of [L238](../findings/second-order.md#lemma-l238-the-gaussian-form-of-a-seven-frames-bracket-total-and-the-isotropic-bound-42) (i) as a hypothesis, as that file does.*

**Statement.** Let $X_1,\dots,X_k$ be orthonormal in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$, any $k$, with $P=\sum_i(-X_i^2)$ and $T=\sum_{i\ne j}\lVert[X_i,X_j]\rVert^2$. Then
$$T\ \le\ \Big(1-\frac1r\Big)\big(4k^2+2\operatorname{tr}P^2\big)-4k(k-1),\qquad r=\min(k,\,n-1).$$
**In class form**, for $V$ the sum of the eigenspaces in a set $S$ of classes, $k_S=\sum_{a\in S}m_a$:
$$\sum_{a,b\in S}\sum_c\tau_{abc}\ \le\ \Big(1-\frac1{r_S}\Big)\Big(4k_S^2+2\sum_{a,b\in S}G_{ab}\Big)-4k_S(k_S-1),\qquad r_S=\min(k_S,n-1),$$
**a constraint linear in the class values** $(\tau,G)$.

**Proof.** L238's proof uses $k=7$ nowhere. (i) gives $T=\mathbb E[(\operatorname{tr}G)^2-\lVert G\rVert_F^2]-4k(k-1)$ with $G(v)$ the Gram of $X_1v,\dots,X_kv$, since each ordered pair contributes $\mathbb E\lvert X_iv\wedge X_jv\rvert^2-4$. The $k$ vectors lie in $v^\perp$, so $\operatorname{rank}G\le\min(k,n-1)$, and Cauchy–Schwarz on $G$'s eigenvalues with Isserlis' $\mathbb E(v^{\mathsf T}Pv)^2=(\operatorname{tr}P)^2+2\operatorname{tr}P^2$, $\operatorname{tr}P=2k$, gives the bound. In class form $P=\sum_{a\in S}A_a$, so $\operatorname{tr}P^2=\sum_{a,b\in S}G_{ab}$, and $T$ is the full bracket total over $V$, $\sum_{a,b\in S}\sum_c\tau_{abc}$. $\square$

**Checks.** *Tight on the whole algebra*: $k=\binom n2$, $P=(n-1)I$, and at $n=12$ both sides are $1320$. **It excludes [L242](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md#lemma-l242-the-capped-three-value-class-system-reaches-above-sqrt32-under-every-valid-seven-frame-cap)'s point**, which was already unrealisable: $S=\{0,2\}$, $k=24$, $\sum G=288$, left side $464$, right side $\tfrac{28800}{11}-2208\approx410.2$. [L241](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md#lemma-l241-off-l173s-ray-the-capped-three-value-class-system-still-reaches-above-sqrt32-with-an-exact-witness) satisfies it ($196\le\approx264.7$).

**What it does to (c), on a screen.** Added for all six proper class unions to the $n=12$ screen with section 5 and $T_7\le54.064$: **234 of 2080 triples stay feasible at $\theta=1.2248$**, against 237 without it ([screen_I73c_capped_offray.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I73c_capped_offray.py) `12 10 54064/1000 sec5 gauss`). *It removes points far above $\sqrt{3/2}$ and leaves those near the threshold.* **Owed**: the cap for frames drawn partly from a class, whose $\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}P^2$ needs the per-element $\operatorname{tr}(\Psi\Psi^{\mathsf T})^2\le2$ bound.
