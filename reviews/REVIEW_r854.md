## 3. (A) The setup, and the two strict steps it crosses

The dichotomy itself is invoked correctly. Given the supposition, each eigenspace is a subspace every element of which is decomposable, which is exactly Theorem 6's hypothesis at $\ell=2$, and the conclusion is exactly *a pencil $f\wedge U$ or $\Lambda^2U$ with $\dim U=3$*. Two things the block leaves unsaid are true and load-bearing. First, the type is determined: a pencil $f\wedge U'$ has the common factor $f$ dividing every element, and no element of $\Lambda^2U$ at $\dim U=3$ divides all the others, so no eigenspace is counted in both sums and the partition of the index set in the equation is well posed. Second, all-decomposability at $\ell=2$ does deliver an orthonormal decomposable basis, since a pencil takes $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ over an orthonormal basis of $U'$ and $\Lambda^2U$ takes its three coordinate planes, so the supposition is strong enough to feed [L180](../findings/diagonal-half.md)(1) with the specialisation. Nothing in the invocation is wrong.

What is wrong is that this is not the locus. [r852](REVIEW_r852.md) C1 and its summary establish that the three conditions all-decomposable, orthonormal-decomposable-basis and decomposable-basis are strictly nested in that order, and I36's open half is the converse of the $\Lambda^4$ test, which the same review records as *a necessary condition for an orthonormal decomposable basis*. So the block's own opening clause, *Gate 2 settled the within-eigenspace half*, cites the review whose strictness result forbids the clause that follows it. The upgrade crosses two strict steps at once: from $\mathfrak b(P_\mu)=0$ to the middle condition, which is I36's open half, and from the middle condition to the top, which r852's witness refutes.

**The upgrade is lossy, and the record's own witness shows it in closed form.** Take $n=4$ and $W=\sum_{a<b}\lambda_{ab}\,\varphi_{ab}\otimes\varphi_{ab}$ with $\lambda_{12}=\lambda_{34}=2$ and $\lambda_{13}=\lambda_{24}=\lambda_{14}=\lambda_{23}=-1$. Ricci-flatness reads $\sum_{a<b}\lambda_{ab}\Pi_{\{a,b\}}=0$, whose $a$-th diagonal entry is $\sum_{b\ne a}\lambda_{ab}$, and that is $2-1-1=0$ at every $a$. First Bianchi reads $\sum\lambda_{ab}\,\varphi_{ab}\wedge\varphi_{ab}=0$ by [L179](../findings/diagonal-half.md)(b), and every $\varphi_{ab}\wedge\varphi_{ab}$ vanishes because $e_a\wedge e_b\wedge e_a\wedge e_b=0$. So $W$ is a nonzero element of $\mathcal W_4$, its eigenbasis $\{\varphi_{ab}\}$ is orthonormal and decomposable, and it sits on the locus I36b is about. Its eigenvalue-$2$ eigenspace is $\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_{12},\varphi_{34}\}$, which is r852's own witness C2: it carries an orthonormal decomposable basis and is not all-decomposable, since $\varphi_{12}+\varphi_{34}$ has self-wedge $2\,\varphi_{12}\wedge\varphi_{34}\ne0$. The block's supposition therefore excludes a member of the locus, and it excludes it at the place where the difficulty lives, namely a repeated eigenvalue.

**And the upgrade buys nothing that the weaker hypothesis does not already buy.** Section 5's $\Theta$ is defined on any subspace and computed from any orthonormal basis, so on the orthonormal-decomposable-basis locus the eigenspace sum is $\Theta(E_\mu)=\sum_{i\in E_\mu}\Pi_{P_i}$, a closed form and an invariant of $E_\mu$, with no Structure Theorem in sight. What the Structure Theorem adds is the packaging of $\Theta$ as $r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}$ or $2\Pi_U$. That is a convenience, and it is paid for with the cases in the last paragraph.

## 4. (B) The pencil sum, and where orthonormality is used and of what

Write $P=\mathrm{Id}-ff^{\mathsf T}$ for the projection off a unit $f$. From the Gram formula, which the conventions fix: with $X=a\wedge b=ba^{\mathsf T}-ab^{\mathsf T}$,
$$\langle a\wedge b,\,c\wedge d\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\big((ab^{\mathsf T}-ba^{\mathsf T})(dc^{\mathsf T}-cd^{\mathsf T})\big)=\langle a,c\rangle\langle b,d\rangle-\langle a,d\rangle\langle b,c\rangle,$$
and at $a=c$, $b=d$ this is $\lvert a\rvert^2\lvert b\rvert^2-\langle a,b\rangle^2$, which is $1$ on $\varphi_{ab}$ as the conventions require. Hence for unit $f$,
$$\langle f\wedge u_i,\,f\wedge u_j\rangle=\langle u_i,u_j\rangle-\langle f,u_i\rangle\langle f,u_j\rangle=\langle Pu_i,\,Pu_j\rangle.$$

**So $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ orthonormal says exactly that $\{Pu_i\}$ is orthonormal, and nothing more.** It does not force $u_i\perp f$ and it does not force $\lvert u_i\rvert=1$: the substitution $u_i\mapsto u_i+c_if$ leaves $f\wedge u_i$ unchanged for every $c_i$, so the component of $u_i$ along $f$ is invisible to the hypothesis. The identity as written is therefore false for a general representative. At $n=3$ with $f=e_1$ and $u_1=e_2+e_1$, the form $f\wedge u_1=\varphi_{12}$ is a unit and $\Pi_{\langle f,u_1\rangle}=\Pi_{\{1,2\}}=e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}+e_2e_2^{\mathsf T}$, whereas $ff^{\mathsf T}+u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}$ has trace $3$ and is not a projection at all.

**With the representatives normalised the identity is immediate.** Put $u_i':=Pu_i$, so $f\wedge u_i'=f\wedge u_i$, the plane is unchanged, $\{u_i'\}$ is orthonormal and lies in $f^\perp$, and $U':=\mathrm{span}\{u_i'\}=U\cap f^\perp$ has dimension $r$. Then $f,u_i'$ are orthonormal, so $\Pi_{\langle f,u_i'\rangle}=ff^{\mathsf T}+u_i'u_i'^{\mathsf T}$ by the specialisation, and
$$\sum_{i=1}^r\Pi_{\langle f,u_i\rangle}=\sum_{i=1}^r\big(ff^{\mathsf T}+u_i'u_i'^{\mathsf T}\big)=r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}.$$
Traces agree at $2r$ on both sides, as they must, each summand being a plane projection.

So the identity needs two hypotheses the block does not state, $f$ unit and $\{u_i\}$ orthonormal inside $f^\perp$, and both are free: the first by rescaling, the second by projecting. The verdict is **CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA** rather than CONFIRMED because the sentence as written is true only of the normalised representatives. That the reselection is free is [r852](REVIEW_r852.md) D1, uncited here.

## 5. The unifying object, which the block is missing

For a subspace $E\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ set $\Theta(E):=\sum_i\omega_i\omega_i^{\mathsf T}$ over an orthonormal basis $\{\omega_i\}$ of $E$.

1. **Well defined.** If $\omega_i'=\sum_jO_{ij}\omega_j$ with $O$ orthogonal then $\sum_i\omega_i'\omega_i'^{\mathsf T}=\sum_{j,k}\big(\sum_iO_{ij}O_{ik}\big)\omega_j\omega_k^{\mathsf T}=\sum_j\omega_j\omega_j^{\mathsf T}$. Equivalently, $\omega\otimes\omega\mapsto\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}$ is the diagonal of the symmetric bilinear map $(\omega,\eta)\mapsto\tfrac12(\omega\eta^{\mathsf T}+\eta\omega^{\mathsf T})$, so it extends linearly to $S^2(\Lambda^2)\to S^2(\mathbb R^n)$ and $\Theta(E)$ is its value at $\Pi_E$.
2. **Additive.** $E=E_1\perp E_2$ gives $\Theta(E)=\Theta(E_1)+\Theta(E_2)$, by concatenating bases.
3. **Trace.** $\operatorname{tr}\Theta(E)=2\dim E$, since $\operatorname{tr}\omega\omega^{\mathsf T}=2\lVert\omega\rVert^2$.
4. **Decomposable evaluation.** If $E$ has an orthonormal decomposable basis with planes $P_i$ then $\Theta(E)=\sum_i\Pi_{P_i}$, by the specialisation.

Every claim in the block is a value of $\Theta$. (B) is $\Theta(f\wedge U')=r\,ff^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{U'}$, (C) is $\Theta(\Lambda^2U)=2\Pi_U$, (F) is $\Theta(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$, and the equation itself is $\sum_\mu\lambda_\mu\Theta(E_\mu)=0$. Point 1 is also what makes the eigenspace sums independent of which orthonormal decomposable basis of the eigenspace is chosen, a well-posedness question the block does not raise and needs.

## 6. (C) The $\Lambda^2U$ sum, and the docstring's reason

Let $\dim U=3$ with orthonormal frame $h_1,h_2,h_3$ and $\Pi_k=h_kh_k^{\mathsf T}$. The three coordinate planes are $\langle h_1,h_2\rangle$, $\langle h_2,h_3\rangle$, $\langle h_1,h_3\rangle$, and
$$(\Pi_1+\Pi_2)+(\Pi_2+\Pi_3)+(\Pi_1+\Pi_3)=2(\Pi_1+\Pi_2+\Pi_3)=2\,\Pi_U.$$
**The docstring's reason is correct**: each of the three directions lies in exactly two of the three planes, which is the coefficient $2$. It is also the $n=3$ case of (F), since $\Theta(\Lambda^2U)=(\dim U-1)\Pi_U$ inside $U$, so (C) and (F) are one statement at two dimensions rather than two facts.

Two strengthenings the block does not take. First, by section 5 point 1 the sum is the same for **every** orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2U$, so the restriction to coordinate planes is inert. Second, that is not an accident: $\star$ inside $U$ carries an orthonormal basis $\{\eta_k\}$ of $\Lambda^2U$ to an orthonormal basis $\{g_k\}$ of $U$ with $\eta_k=\pm g_i\wedge g_j$, so every orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2U$ **is** the coordinate-plane basis of some orthonormal frame of $U$, and $\Pi_{P_k}=\Pi_U-g_kg_k^{\mathsf T}$ gives $\sum_k\Pi_{P_k}=3\Pi_U-\Pi_U=2\Pi_U$ a second time. That is [r852](REVIEW_r852.md) D2, uncited here.

## 7. (D) The assembled equation

Take $W$ on the block's locus and let $E_1,\dots,E_k$ be the eigenspaces with $\lambda_\mu\ne0$. Choose in each an orthonormal decomposable basis, available by section 3. Concatenated, these are orthonormal, span the image of $W$, and give $W=\sum_i\lambda_i\,\omega_i\otimes\omega_i$ in the spectral form [L180](../findings/diagonal-half.md)(1) requires, with every $\lambda_i\ne0$. L180(1) is then $\sum_i\lambda_i\,\omega_i\omega_i^{\mathsf T}=0$, and since $\lambda$ is constant on each eigenspace this regroups as $\sum_\mu\lambda_\mu\Theta(E_\mu)=0$. Substituting (B) at the pencils and (C) at the $\Lambda^2$ types gives the displayed equation. **It follows.** The specialisation is applied at unit decomposable forms and at the record's normalisation, which is where it is true, and the regrouping is legitimate because $\Theta(E_\mu)$ does not depend on the basis chosen inside the eigenspace.

**The zero eigenspace is handled, and by accident rather than by argument.** L180(1) quantifies over the nonzero spectrum, so the kernel never enters its sum, and the equation's omission of the kernel's projections is exactly right: those terms would carry $\lambda=0$. The provenance blockquote at [diagonal-half.md:1116](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1116) already records that L180 extends over zero eigenvalues for free, so both conventions are on the record. The defect is that the block never says which it uses, and section 8 shows the two counts use the other one.

## 8. (E) The two counts

**The trace identity is correct and is a dimension count.** By section 5 point 3, $\operatorname{tr}\Theta(E_\mu)$ is $2\dim E_\mu$, which is $2r_\mu$ at a pencil and $6$ at a $\Lambda^2$ type, so the trace of (F) reads $\sum_\mu 2r_\mu+6\cdot\#\{\Lambda^2\}=\operatorname{tr}\big((n-1)\mathrm{Id}\big)=n(n-1)$, that is $\sum_\mu r_\mu+3\cdot\#\{\Lambda^2\}=\binom n2$. Note that it is the trace of the **unweighted** identity: the trace of the weighted equation is $\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha=0$, which is tracelessness and not a count. So $\binom n2$ is right **only if the index set is every eigenspace, the kernel included**, because the identity is $\sum_\mu\dim E_\mu=\dim\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ and nothing else. With the kernel excluded the right side is $\binom n2-\dim\ker W$ and the stated identity is false at every $W$ with a kernel. Verdict **CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA**, with the kernel clause supplied.

**The $n/2$ count does not survive a zero eigenspace.** The dimension bound is [r852](REVIEW_r852.md)'s reading of Corollary 7, that an all-decomposable subspace of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ has dimension at most $\max\{n-1,3\}=n-1$ for $n\ge4$, and the arithmetic $\binom n2/(n-1)=n/2$ is right. What the count needs beyond that is that the eigenspaces it divides among exhaust $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ and that **each** of them meets the bound. Two readings, and both fail.

- **Kernel counted.** Then $\ker W$ is an all-decomposable eigenspace, so $\dim\ker W\le n-1$ and $\operatorname{rank}W\ge\binom n2-n+1$. That is an enormous restriction, presented as free, and it is exactly the trap [W1](../findings/INDEX-traps.md) names. Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $n=5$ has $\dim\ker=7$ against $n-1=4$, so the class the record keeps as its standing counterexample generator is outside the hypothesis before any decomposability question is asked.
- **Kernel not counted.** Then the eigenspaces sum to $\operatorname{rank}W$, not to $\binom n2$, and the division gives $\#\{\text{eigenspaces}\}\ge\operatorname{rank}W/(n-1)$, which is $2$ or more only because Ricci-flatness forces mixed signs, and $2<n/2$ for every $n\ge5$. The count is false as stated.

Since the equation in (D) uses the second index set and the trace identity uses the first, the block asserts three things under one word *eigenspace* and needs two different meanings of it. Verdict **REFUTED AS STATED**, repairable by stating the kernel convention and dropping the $n/2$ count or its hypothesis.

## 9. (F) The unweighted total, proved and generalised

**Claim.** For every orthonormal basis $\{\omega_\alpha\}$ of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, $\sum_\alpha\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$. Decomposability is not needed anywhere, and at a decomposable basis the left side is $\sum_\alpha\Pi_{P_\alpha}$, which is the recorded statement.

**Proof.** By section 5 point 1, $\Theta(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)$ is independent of the orthonormal basis, so evaluate it on $\{\varphi_{ab}\}_{a<b}$. With $\varphi_{ab}=E_{ba}-E_{ab}$ and $a\ne b$,
$$\varphi_{ab}\varphi_{ab}^{\mathsf T}=(E_{ba}-E_{ab})(E_{ab}-E_{ba})=E_{ba}E_{ab}+E_{ab}E_{ba}=E_{bb}+E_{aa},$$
the cross terms $E_{ba}E_{ba}$ and $E_{ab}E_{ab}$ vanishing. Summing over $a<b$, each index $a$ lies in exactly $n-1$ pairs, so the total is $(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$. $\square$

Three consequences the record should carry instead of the screen. The identity is basis-free, so it says nothing about the basis and cannot be used to detect decomposability. It is also the parent of (C), which is the same statement inside a three-space. And by additivity it holds over any orthogonal decomposition of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ into eigenspaces, decomposable or not, which is what makes it available as a normalisation for the equation at all.

## 10. (G) The Lean file against its row

```

```

so the row's *kernel-checked, no compiler-trusting tactic* is right: no `sorryAx`, no `native_decide`, only the three standard axioms. The scratch file was removed after the run.

**What the two theorems are about.** `planeProj u v` is **defined** as `outer u + outer v`, and neither theorem carries a hypothesis. So `sum_planeProj_pencil` unfolds to
$$\sum_{i<r}\big(\mathrm{outer}\,f+\mathrm{outer}\,u_i\big)=r\cdot\mathrm{outer}\,f+\sum_{i<r}\mathrm{outer}\,u_i,$$
which is the distributivity of a finite sum over a constant summand and uses no property of `outer` whatever. I verified that reading by proving, with the theorem's own proof script unchanged, the statement
$$\sum_{i<r}(A+B_i)=r\cdot A+\sum_{i<r}B_i \qquad\text{for arbitrary } A,B_i\in\mathrm{Matrix}\,\iota\,\iota\,\mathbb R,$$
and likewise $(A+B)+(B+C)+(A+C)=2(A+B+C)$ for the second theorem. Both compiled. So the pencil theorem is an instance of a generic matrix identity, and the $\Lambda^2$ theorem is an instance of a three-term one.

**So `sum_planeProj_pencil` does not state (B).** (B) has two pieces of content, that $\Pi_{\langle f,u_i\rangle}=ff^{\mathsf T}+u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$ at orthonormal $f,u_i$, and that $\sum_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_{U'}$ at an orthonormal basis of $U'$. The file assumes the first away by definition and **never forms the second at all**: the right side keeps $\sum_i\mathrm{outer}\,(u_i)$ unreduced, so $\Pi_{U'}$ appears nowhere in the Lean statement. The theorem is true and trivial rather than vacuous, its hypotheses being absent rather than unsatisfiable, and it is true of objects that are not projections: at $\iota=\mathrm{Fin}\,2$ and $u=v=e_0$, `planeProj u v` is $2\,e_0e_0^{\mathsf T}$, which I also checked in Lean. The $\Lambda^2$ theorem is the better of the two, since the coefficient $2$ is the whole combinatorial content of (C), and it still stops short of $2\Pi_U$.

**The row.** It discloses the definitional gap in its own words, *it assumes rather than proves that the vectors are orthonormal, `planeProj` being the outer-product sum by definition rather than a projection derived from a plane*, and it correctly says the equation itself is not stated. That much is honest. Two things are not. The caps headline *BOTH EIGENSPACE SUMS PROVED OUTRIGHT AT EVERY DIMENSION* describes a sum over plane projections, and what is proved at every dimension is that a constant summand can be pulled out of a finite sum. And the row does not disclose that the pencil theorem never mentions $\Pi_{U'}$, so a reader who accepts the orthonormality caveat still overestimates what is there by one of the identity's two halves.

## 11. (H) Is *a reduction* accurate

No, on three counts.

- **Direction.** The equation is a consequence of the hypotheses and is not equivalent to anything that remains. It is Ricci-flatness itself, rewritten in the pencil parameters by (B) and (C), so it consumes a hypothesis and produces no obligation. A reduction of the remaining half would have to run the other way, showing that every solution of the equation yields a common frame.
- **Object.** The remaining obligation, as [diagonal-half.md:1110](../findings/diagonal-half.md#L1110) states it, is existential over decomposable orthonormal eigenbases: that **some** such basis lies in a common frame. The equation quantifies over none of that. Solving it completely would leave the frame existential untouched, because the equation is satisfied by every locus member including any hypothetical non-diagonal one.
- **Wording.** *one linear identity in the pencil data* is wrong twice. The unknowns are $\lambda_\mu$, the unit vectors $f_\mu$ and the subspaces $U'_\mu,U_\nu$, and the equation is quadratic in the vectors, $ff^{\mathsf T}$ and $\Pi_{U'}$ being quadratic in a frame. It is linear only in the eigenvalues with the geometry fixed. And it is not one identity but $\binom{n+1}2$ scalar equations, one per entry of a symmetric matrix.

What the block has is a **reformulation of Ricci-flatness on a proper sub-locus**, in coordinates adapted to the Structure Theorem. That is worth recording. Calling it a reduction of the remaining half overstates it, and the honest version of the sentence is available: it names the shape a proof would have to argue about, on the cases the supposition keeps.

The clause *it is what a proof of I36b at $n\ge5$ has to spend* is a necessity claim with no support. A proof could spend Ricci-flatness in any other form, for instance as $\operatorname{diag}(M^{\mathsf T}\Lambda M)=0$ in [L181](../findings/diagonal-half.md)'s reading.

## 13. Item 11, and the finding that most threatens the route

**What would break the route.** The equation is a necessary condition satisfied by every member of the block's locus. It has content only if that locus has nonzero members. Three ways the route fails: the locus is empty, in which case the equation is an equation about nothing and I36b on this sub-locus has to be proved by an emptiness argument that the equation does not supply; the locus has a member that is not coordinate-diagonal, in which case I36b is false and the equation is satisfied by the counterexample; or the equation has solutions in the pencil data that come from no $W$, which is harmless because it is necessary only.

**What I did to look for it, and what I found.** The first case holds at $n=4$.

**Claim.** No nonzero coordinate-diagonal Ricci-flat $W$ at $n=4$ has all of its eigenspaces all-decomposable.

**Proof.** Let $W=\sum_{a<b}\lambda_{ab}\,\varphi_{ab}\otimes\varphi_{ab}$. Ricci-flatness is $\sum_{a<b}\lambda_{ab}\Pi_{\{a,b\}}=0$, whose diagonal entries are the four conditions $\sum_{b\ne a}\lambda_{ab}=0$ and whose off-diagonal entries vanish identically. The coefficient matrix of those four conditions on the six unknowns is the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix of $K_4$, which has rank $4$ because $K_4$ is connected and contains an odd cycle, so the solution space has dimension $2$. The two-parameter family $\lambda_{12}=\lambda_{34}=t$, $\lambda_{13}=\lambda_{24}=s$, $\lambda_{14}=\lambda_{23}=-t-s$ consists of solutions and has dimension $2$, so it is the whole solution space. Since $W$ is diagonal in $\{\varphi_{ab}\}$, each eigenspace is the span of the coordinate forms carrying one value, hence a union of complete opposite pairs $\{\varphi_{ab},\varphi_{cd}\}$ with $\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\}=\emptyset$. Any such eigenspace contains $\varphi_{ab}+\varphi_{cd}$, whose self-wedge is $2\,\varphi_{ab}\wedge\varphi_{cd}\ne0$, so that element is not decomposable and the eigenspace is not all-decomposable. This holds of the zero eigenspace as well, so the conclusion is independent of the kernel convention. $\square$

**Consequence.** I36 is proved at $n=4$ by [r850](REVIEW_r850.md), so every member of the all-decomposable locus at $n=4$ is coordinate-diagonal, and the claim then gives that **the block's locus at $n=4$ contains only $W=0$**. The cross-eigenspace equation therefore has no nonzero instance at the one dimension where the record can check it, and the case it drops relative to the real locus is exactly the case the $n=4$ proof has to handle, a repeated eigenvalue whose eigenspace is a pair of orthogonal planes.

**A second consequence, which reframes the route.** On the block's locus, I36b's conclusion is coordinate-diagonality, and if the diagonal part of that locus is empty at $n\ge5$ as well then I36b restricted to it is equivalent to the locus being empty. The route's target would then be an emptiness theorem and not a frame construction, and the equation would be the tool for proving emptiness rather than a reduction of the frame problem. I attempted the general statement by hand. On the diagonal, the condition is that the edge set of $K_n$ be partitioned into intersecting families, which is what all-decomposability of a coordinate-spanned eigenspace means, since a family of pairs with two disjoint members carries a non-decomposable sum, and a pairwise intersecting family of pairs is a star or a triangle, matching Theorem 6's two types exactly. The requirement is a weighting with every vertex sum zero, distinct values on distinct parts. Every configuration I tried by hand collapsed to zero, at $n=4$ provably and at $n=5$ and $n=6$ in the several cases I worked, including the two edge-disjoint triangles of $K_6$ plus a triangle decomposition of the octahedron, where the four triangles pairwise share a vertex and the sign condition would need a proper two-colouring of $K_4$.

**This is where I stop, and it is a computation the standing rule blocks.** Deciding whether the block's locus has a coordinate-diagonal member at $n=5,6,7$ means enumerating the partitions of $E(K_n)$ into stars and triangles and solving the vertex-sum-zero system on each. **An empty answer would mean the route reduces I36b on this sub-locus to an emptiness theorem, and a nonempty answer would give the equation its first instance and a test object for anything derived from it.** Either outcome changes what the next attempt should do, so it is worth the run, and I do not open it.

## 14. Arguing the opposite, and what is withdrawn

- **Against A2 and A3.** The block writes *suppose*, so it is not asserting that the locus is all-decomposable, and a reader could take the whole block as conditional. That defence holds for the sentence and not for the block, whose closing clause calls the result *a reduction of the remaining half*, which the supposition makes false: the remaining half is the wider locus. The verdict stands as REFUTED AS STATED rather than REFUTED for exactly that reason.
- **Against B.** One could say $\{u_i\}$ is meant as an orthonormal basis of $U'$ throughout, in which case the identity is stated correctly and my objection is pedantry. Against that: the block writes the basis as $\{f\wedge u_i\}$ and the sum as $\sum_i\Pi_{\langle f,u_i\rangle}$, so the $u_i$ are introduced through the $2$-forms, whose orthonormality is the only stated hypothesis and does not give what is needed. Hence CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA and not REFUTED.
- **Against E1.** The charitable reading counts the kernel, on which the count is true. It is then true with an unstated hypothesis that no padded critical point satisfies, which is a defect of the same size. Both readings are answered in section 8, so the verdict does not depend on the choice.
- **Against G2.** One could say the Lean layer is only meant to type the shape, and the row says as much. Against that: the row's status is **proved** and its headline says the eigenspace sums are proved, so a reader auditing the record's Lean coverage would count (B) as machine-checked. The disclosure is in the row's fine print and the overstatement is in its caps.
- **Against section 13.** The claim depends on eigenspaces of a diagonal $W$ being coordinate-spanned, which holds because $W$ is diagonal in the orthonormal basis $\{\varphi_{ab}\}$, and on the rank of $K_4$'s unsigned incidence matrix, which is $4$ for any connected non-bipartite graph. Both checked twice. Nothing withdrawn.
- **Withdrawn.** I considered reporting the equation as **VACUOUS** on the strength of section 13. I cannot defend that at $n\ge5$, where the locus may well be inhabited and where the block scopes its claim. The $n=4$ emptiness is reported as what it is, a closed-form fact about one dimension with a consequence for how the route should be described.
- **Not a defect.** The equation's grouping, the specialisation, the normalisation and the type-disjointness of the two sums are all correct, and the two closed forms are right. The mathematics of the block is sound. What fails is the scope, the two counts and the label.

## Overall verdict

**The three identities are true, the equation follows, and the block's account of what it has is wrong in scope and in kind.** (B), (C), (D), (E2) and (F) survive, and (F) survives in a stronger and cheaper form than either of its two sites records. (A)'s supposition is a strict upgrade of the locus across two steps that [r852](REVIEW_r852.md) proved strict, it excludes a nonzero member of the locus exhibited here in closed form, and section 5 shows the upgrade is not needed for any of the closed forms. The $n/2$ count is unsound as written under either kernel convention, and the trace identity holds only under the convention the equation itself does not use. The Lean file builds cleanly on the three standard axioms and proves two matrix-bookkeeping identities, one of which is an instance of a statement about arbitrary matrices and mentions no projection, so the row's headline claim that both eigenspace sums are proved outright should go. *Reduction* should become *reformulation of Ricci-flatness on a proper sub-locus*, and the block should carry the $n=4$ emptiness so that the next attempt knows the equation has no instance yet.
