## A. Fidelity

- **The definitions.** [L81Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L81Statement.lean) line 128: `cc x` is `{u | Skew u ∧ inner2 x u = 0 ∧ br x u = 0}`, the block's c = z(x) ∩ x^⊥ taken inside so(m). Line 149: `zPerp x` is `{u | Skew u ∧ ∀ w ∈ cc x, br u w = 0 ∧ ∀ w ∈ cc x, inner2 u w = 0}`, the block's z(c) ∩ c^⊥ inside so(m). `inner2 X Y = ½ tr(XᵀY)` is a positive multiple of the invariant form, so the orthogonal complements agree. **Faithful.**
- **The right side of `L160`.** It reads `∀ x, Skew x → ∀ y ∈ zPerp x, ∀ z, x *ᵥ z = 0 → y *ᵥ z = 0`, which says both the Hom(K,V) and the so(K) components of y vanish. The block speaks of Hom(K,V) alone. **The two agree** because [SkewCentraliser.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/SkewCentraliser.lean)'s `kernel_kernel` (line 433) kills the kernel-kernel entries of every y ∈ z(c) ∩ c^⊥ with no hypothesis on m, the rotation `rotK` of two kernel vectors lying in c. So "y kills K" is equivalent to "the mixed part of y is zero" at every m, and the iff has the block's content.
- **The per-x reading.** The block's claim is per nonzero x. `L160` quantifies over every skew x, including x = 0, where `cc 0` is all of so(m) and `zPerp 0` is zero, so the extra case is trivially true and costs nothing. The per-x form lives in the components: `L160_support` (line 64) is the forward direction at each skew x, and `L160_three` (line 162) the converse at **every** nonzero x in so(3), exhibiting `rot (N.u j) z` with z ∈ ker x and `rot_mulVec` giving y z = -‖z‖² u ≠ 0 with u ∈ im x. Since dim K = 1 at m = 3, so(K) = 0 and the moved direction is a genuine Hom(K,V) contribution. **`L160_three`'s per-x form is right**, and it rests on `cc_three` (c = 0), which matches the block's case (r, k) = (1, 1) where the mixed part supplies the two dimensions beyond ℝx.
- **Hypotheses inhabited.** `L160_support`: m = 4, x = rot(e₀, e₁), y = x (x ∈ zPerp x, L81Statement.lean line 46), z = e₂ ∈ ker x. `cc_three` and `L160_three`: m = 3, x = rot(e₀, e₁), nonzero, the same object `L160`'s converse builds at line 186. `L160`: any m, e.g. m = 4 on the left.
- **Gap A-1.** [L160.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L160.lean) lines 15 to 16: "it needs a second kernel direction, so `dim K ≥ 2`, which at rank two is `m ≠ 3`". At rank two dim K = m - 2, so m ≠ 3 is m = 2 or m ≥ 4, and at m = 2 dim K = 0. The correct reading is "dim K ≥ 2, which at rank two with K nonzero is m ≠ 3". `kernel_one` itself handles z = 0 separately, so the proof is unaffected.
- **Gap A-2.** [L160.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L160.lean) lines 181 to 183: the docstring of `L160` says "the mixed summand `Hom(ker x, im x)` contributes nothing" while the statement kills all of ker x. The bridge (`kernel_kernel` at every m) is used at line 70 and mentioned at lines 21 to 22 as a consequence, but no sentence says that it is what makes the stronger right side equal to the block's claim.

## C. Records

- **Partial is accurate.** The block's statement has two conjuncts, the iff and the displayed identity z(c) ∩ c^⊥ = z_{so(V)}(c_V) ∩ c_V^{⊥_V}. The iff is proved. The identity is untyped, and `L160_support` gives exactly the inclusion into so(V) that the row says it gives: a skew y killing K preserves V = K^⊥.
- **Gap C-1, "the same two facts".** [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) line 414: "The layer splits the cases by the number of planes and the block by $\dim K$; the two facts are the same and fail together exactly at rank $2$ with $\dim K=1$". The two facts are distinct: F1, c_V has trivial common kernel on V, true iff rank x ≥ 4, and F2, so(K) fixes no vector, true iff dim K ≥ 2. The block uses F2 on the region dim K ≥ 2 and F1 on dim K = 1. The layer uses F1 on the region rank ≥ 4 (`kernel_two`, any dim K) and F2 on rank 2 (`kernel_one`). What is shared is the **pair** of facts and the **joint-failure locus** (rank 2, dim K = 1, which is m = 3). The row's own preceding sentences attribute `kernel_two` and `kernel_one` to two different facts correctly, so the clause contradicts its own row. Correct form: "the two splits use the same two facts on different case regions, and both fail together exactly at rank 2 with dim K = 1".
- **Gap C-2, the owed identity needs m ≠ 3.** [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) line 414 names as owed the identity "as an equality of subspaces of so(V)" with no hypothesis. At m = 3 it is false: z(c) ∩ c^⊥ = so(3), dimension 3 (`cc_three`), while V is a plane, c_V = 0 and the right side is so(V), dimension 1. The block itself leaves the scope implicit (line 111), so the owed item should carry m ≠ 3 explicitly. [INDEX-results.md](../findings/INDEX-results.md) line 176 writes "the displayed identity owed" and inherits the same ambiguity.
- **Gap C-3.** [isotropy-cap.md](../findings/isotropy-cap.md) line 126: "Machine-checked: `L160` ... proves the iff at every $m$" is accurate as far as it goes, but placed after the proof's $\square$ it reads as covering the lemma, and it does not say the displayed identity is not machine-checked. INDEX-results.md line 176 and lean/INDEX.md line 414 both say so. One clause ("the displayed identity is owed") would align the three.
- **Gap C-4.** [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) line 414 ends at "**statement: typed**" with no closing ` |`, unlike every neighbouring row.
- `L160_support` "placing z(c) ∩ c^⊥ inside so(V) at m ≠ 3" (isotropy-cap.md line 126) and the INDEX-results citation are otherwise accurate.
