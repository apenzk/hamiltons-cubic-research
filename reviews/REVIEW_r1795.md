## Findings per check

**1. `rotR_of_hasChar`.** `HasChar c S R` ([L120Hess.lean:37](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Hess.lean)) is $S\,\mathrm{ext}R(S^{\mathsf T}ZS)\,S^{\mathsf T}=c\,\mathrm{ext}R(Z)$ on skew $Z$. `rotR U V` ([FrameOp2.lean:45](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/FrameOp2.lean)) is $X\mapsto U\,V(U^{\mathsf T}XU)\,U^{\mathsf T}$ through `cU` ([FrameOp2.lean:36](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/FrameOp2.lean)), and `extend_rotR` ([L222Swap.lean:83](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L222Swap.lean)) states that formula for any $U$. The two sides of the conclusion are the two sides of the hypothesis evaluated at skew $X$, so the theorem holds for every matrix $S$ with no orthogonality. The proof uses only `extend_rotR` and `extend_apply` and matches this.

**2. `Tsym_char_zero`.** `Tsym_rotR` ([FrameOp2.lean:137](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/FrameOp2.lean)) gives $T(Ux,Uy,Uz)=T(x,y,z)$ at orthogonal $U$ and Weyl arguments. Substituting the scalings and trilinearity gives $abc\,T=T$, so $-T=T$ and $T=0$. Correct. The Weyl hypotheses are inherited from `Pcubic2_rotR` ([FrameOp2.lean:87](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/FrameOp2.lean)) and are all supplied at every use in `L224_cubic`.

**3. `L224_cubic`.**
- *Objects.* `SignChar (fun _ => 1) D` with `IsWeyl2 D` is $\mathcal D_n$ by `trivial_block_iff` ([L120Ident.lean:49](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Ident.lean)), and `SignChar (setChar {j,k}) ν` with `IsWeyl2 ν` is $V_{jk}$ by `pair_block_iff` ([L120Ident.lean:64](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L120Ident.lean)), the `pairMap` image of the zero-sum vectors, which is the record's $\nu_x$ with $\sum x=0$. So $D+\nu$ ranges over $\mathcal S_{jk}\cap\mathcal W_n$, the record's $W$.
- *$E$ and $D^-$.* $\sigma=$ `rotR (swapM j k)` is an involution (`swapM_sq`, used at the `hσσ` step), so $\tfrac12(D\pm\sigma D)$ are the projections onto its $\pm1$ eigenspaces, and the decomposition of $D$ into a swap-even and a swap-odd part is unique. The record's $E$ is swap-even and its $D^-_y$ is swap-odd (the swap exchanges $K_{ja}$ and $K_{ka}$ and so negates $D^-_y$), hence $E=\tfrac12(D+\sigma D)$ and $D^-_y=\tfrac12(D-\sigma D)$ exactly. `swapM` is symmetric (`swapM_T`), so the direction convention of the frame change is immaterial. The identification of $D^-$ with the coordinate tensor $D^-_y$ is step 1's coordinates, owed (O6).
- *$T$ and $P$.* `Tsym` ([L214Op.lean:40](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L214Op.lean)) is the symmetrisation of `cubicTri`, and `Tsym_diag` ([L214Op.lean:89](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L214Op.lean)) is $T(R,R,R)=P(R)$, the record's $P(W)=T(W,W,W)$.
- *The vanishing.* The expansion of $T(E+M+\nu)^{\otimes3}$ has 27 ordered terms, 7 of which have an even count of $M$ and of $\nu$ ($EEE$, three orders of $EMM$, three of $E\nu\nu$). The proof kills the other 20 at [L224Cubic.lean:90 to 129](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L224Cubic.lean). The 13 odd in $M$ use the swap (scalings $1,-1,1$ on $E,M,\nu$), and the 7 even in $M$ and odd in $\nu$ use `signMat (sgn j)` (scalings $1,1,-1$). I checked each of the 20 against this assignment and each product is $-1$. This is the record's two reasons: $\sigma$ reverses $D^-_y$ and fixes $E$ and $\nu_x$ (the last by `pair_swap_even`, L222 item 1's swap parity), and the flip at $j$ is the reflection in $e_j$ with character $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k=-1$ there, which is the record's italic "reflections in $e_j-e_k$ and in $e_j$". The mixed terms odd in both are killed by the swap, where the record allows either reason.
- *Strengthening.* No unit hypothesis and no $n\ge4$ (O3).

## Non-trivial object

- **Compiled.** In a scratch file under `import HamiltonsCubic.Results.L224Cubic`, for any `IsWeight w` and $j\ne k$, $D=$ `restrict (diagOp_isWeyl hw)` meets `hD` through `trivial_block_iff.mpr ⟨w, hw, rfl⟩`, $\nu=0$ meets `hν` through `pair_block_iff.mpr` with $c=0$, and `L224_cubic` then elaborates. It asserts $P(D)=P(E)+3T(E,D^-,D^-)$ for every diagonal Weyl tensor: the cubic of a diagonal tensor carries no term odd in its $(j,k)$ swap-odd part. This checks the hypotheses are satisfiable by the whole of $\mathcal D_n$.
- **By hand, nonzero $\nu$.** r1788's $V_*=\varphi_{02}\odot\varphi_{12}-\varphi_{03}\odot\varphi_{13}$ at $n=5$, $(j,k)=(0,1)$, is Weyl of character $\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_1$, so with $D=0$ the theorem asserts $P(V_*)=0$. That is right: the flip at $0$ carries $V_*$ to $-V_*$, and $P$ is odd and $O(n)$-invariant. A hand evaluation, not a screen.

## What would falsify each headline

- `rotR_of_hasChar`: a matrix $S$, an operator $A$ and a skew $Z$ with $S\,\mathrm{ext}A(S^{\mathsf T}ZS)S^{\mathsf T}=c\,\mathrm{ext}A(Z)$ for all skew $Z$ but $(\mathrm{rotR}\,S\,A)(Z)\ne cA(Z)$. Impossible, the two are the same expression.
- `Tsym_char_zero`: an orthogonal $U$ and Weyl $x,y,z$ with $Ux=ax$, $Uy=by$, $Uz=cz$, $abc=-1$ and $T(x,y,z)\ne0$, which would contradict the $O(n)$-invariance of $P$ on $\mathcal W_n$.
- `L224_cubic`: a Weyl $D$ of character $1$ (a diagonal Weyl tensor) and a Weyl $\nu$ of character $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$ (a $\nu_x$ with $\sum x=0$) with $P(D+\nu)\ne P(E)+3T(E,D^-,D^-)+3T(E,\nu,\nu)$, equivalently a nonzero term of $T$ with an odd count of $D^-$ or of $\nu$.
