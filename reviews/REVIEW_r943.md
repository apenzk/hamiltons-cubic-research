## 1. Item 1, the subalgebra and the rotation

**Conventions used throughout.** $\Gamma$ acts on $\Lambda^2$ by $e_p\wedge e_q\mapsto\varepsilon_p\varepsilon_qe_p\wedge e_q$, so on an operator entry $R_{pq,rs}$ by $\varepsilon_p\varepsilon_q\varepsilon_r\varepsilon_s$. $O(n)$ acts on $\mathcal W_n$ by algebra automorphisms of $Q$, which is what makes the fixed space of any subgroup a subalgebra. Given 1 (Lemma L120) supplies the summands and that each carries a single character.

**The chain, link by link.**
- *The fixed space of $H=\ker(\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k)$ is the sum of the summands whose character is trivial on $H$.* Follows from Given 1, each summand being isotypic. The characters trivial on $H$ are $1$ and $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$ (duality in the elementary abelian group), so the fixed space is $\mathcal D_n\oplus V_{jk}$, provided every other character of Given 1 is nontrivial on $H$, which the witnesses supply.
- *Four-set witness.* For any four-set $Q$, $Q\setminus\{j,k\}$ has at least two elements. The flip $f_i$ at $i\in Q\setminus\{j,k\}$ has $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k(f_i)=1$, so $f_i\in H$, and $\chi_Q(f_i)=-1$. Follows.
- *Pair witness.* $\{a,b\}\ne\{j,k\}$ makes $\{a,b\}\setminus\{j,k\}$ nonempty, the flip at an index of it lies in $H$ and gives $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b=-1$. Follows. The alternative witness $\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_c$ with $c\notin\{a,b,j,k\}$ is correct when $a\notin\{j,k\}$ and is never needed, since the single flip always exists.
- *Subalgebra.* The fixed space of a group of automorphisms is closed under $\circ$. Follows, though the proof leaves this step implicit.
- *Rotation.* $H$ is generated by the flips at indices outside $\{j,k\}$ and by $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$, which is $-I$ on the $(j,k)$-plane and $I$ off it. A rotation $R$ in that plane commutes with each generator, so $R$ maps $H$-fixed tensors to $H$-fixed tensors. Follows.

**Verdict CONFIRMED.**

## 2. Item 2, $\nu\circ\nu=G_x$

**The element.** $\nu$ is the symmetric operator with $\nu_{ja,ka}=\nu_{ka,ja}=x_a$ and no other entry, $e_{pa}=e_p\wedge e_a$. Its Frobenius norm is $2\lVert x\rVert^2$, matching the stated norm. Its nonzero components have index multiset $\{j,k,a,a\}$, so the totally antisymmetric part vanishes and the first Bianchi identity holds, and the only Ricci entry that can be nonzero is at $(j,k)$, equal up to sign to $\sum_ax_a=0$. So $\nu\in\mathcal W_n$, with character $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$. Conversely every entry of character $\varepsilon_j\varepsilon_k$ has index multiset odd exactly at $j,k$, which forces the pattern $(ja,ka)$, so $V_{jk}$ is exactly this family and has dimension $n-3$.

**The sharp formula and its sign, derived from the definition.** With $\langle R^\#\omega,\eta\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\langle[R\varphi_\alpha,R\varphi_\beta],\omega\rangle\langle[\varphi_\alpha,\varphi_\beta],\eta\rangle$ over an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2\cong\mathfrak{so}(n)$, and $[E_{pq},E_{rs}]=\delta_{qr}E_{ps}-\delta_{qs}E_{pr}-\delta_{pr}E_{qs}+\delta_{ps}E_{qr}$, the only brackets with an $e_{ab}$ component are $[e_{ac},e_{cb}]=e_{ab}$ and its reverse, so $(R^\#)_{ab,ab}=\sum_c\langle[Re_{ac},Re_{cb}],e_{ab}\rangle$. Expanding $Re_{ac}=\tfrac12\sum_{p,q}R_{pq,ac}e_{pq}$, the four bracket terms contribute equally by antisymmetry, and $\langle E_{ps},E_{ab}\rangle=\delta_{pa}\delta_{sb}-\delta_{pb}\delta_{sa}$ gives
$$(R^\#)_{ab,ab}=\sum_{c,q}\big[R_{aq,ac}R_{qb,cb}-R_{bq,ac}R_{qa,cb}\big]=\sum_{c,c'}\big[R_{ac,ac'}R_{bc,bc'}-R_{ac,bc'}R_{bc,ac'}\big],$$
the brief's formula, after $R_{qb,cb}=R_{bq,bc}$, $R_{qa,cb}=R_{aq,bc}$ and renaming. **The relative sign of the two terms comes from the antisymmetry of $\langle E_{ps},E_{ab}\rangle$ and is independent of any normalisation or of the sign of the bracket**, which enters squared. A rescaling of the bracket scales both terms together, and the diagonal reduction fixes it.
- *Diagonal reduction.* On a diagonal $R$, $R_{ac,ac'}=\delta_{cc'}K_{ac}$, and $R_{ac,bc'}\ne0$ forces $c=b$, where $R_{bc,ac'}=R_{bb,\cdot}=0$. So the entry is $\sum_cK_{ac}K_{bc}=(K^2)_{ab}$, which with $(R^2)_{ab,ab}=K_{ab}^2$ is DGK's (b) and the diagonal product of Given 2 at $A=B$. Follows. **The diagonal check does not see the second term's sign**, which is why the derivation above is needed. Two independent checks fix it as well: $G_x$ has zero row $j$ only with $K_{jk}=-\lVert x\rVert^2$, which L209 requires, and Lemma L122's pair block reproduces $G_x$ (below).

**Each entry.** A factor $\nu_{pc,pc'}$ with a shared first index is nonzero only when $p=m\notin\{j,k\}$ and $\{c,c'\}=\{j,k\}$, with value $x_m$ (two orientation flips cancel).
- $(\nu^2)_{ja,ja}=\nu_{ja,ka}^2=x_a^2$, likewise at $(ka)$. $(\nu^2)_{jk,jk}=(\nu^2)_{ab,ab}=0$, as those rows of $\nu$ vanish. Follows.
- $(ab)$, $a,b\notin\{j,k\}$: first term $\nu_{aj,ak}\nu_{bj,bk}+\nu_{ak,aj}\nu_{bk,bj}=2x_ax_b$. Second term: $\nu_{ac,bc'}\ne0$ needs $a=m$, $c\in\{j,k\}$ and then $b\in\{j,k\}$, impossible. So $2x_ax_b$. Follows.
- $(jk)$: first term needs $j\notin\{j,k\}$, zero. Second term: $\nu_{jc,kc'}\ne0$ forces $c=c'=m$ with value $x_m$, and $\nu_{km,jm}=x_m$, so $-\sum_mx_m^2$. Follows, and this is the entry whose sign matters.
- $(ja)$: first term zero as at $(jk)$. Second term: $\nu_{jc,ac'}\ne0$ forces $c=a$, $c'=k$, and then $\nu_{ac,jc'}=\nu_{aa,\cdot}=0$. So $K_{ja}=x_a^2$ from $\nu^2$ alone, likewise $K_{ka}$. Follows. The proof's "every other diagonal entry vanishes" is correct and these are all the pair types.
- *Off-diagonal part.* $Q(\nu)$ transforms by $\chi(f)^2=1$ under every flip, so it is $\Gamma$-fixed, and a $\Gamma$-fixed operator has every index of every entry with even multiplicity, which with $p\ne q$, $r\ne s$ forces $\{p,q\}=\{r,s\}$. So it is diagonal. Follows.

**Cross-check against prior art.** Lemma L122 gives the pair block of $L_D$ in the basis $f_a$ as $\tfrac12[\operatorname{diag}(K_{ja}+K_{ka})-K_{jk}\mathrm{Id}+K^{(jk)}]$. Then $\langle L_D\nu,\nu\rangle=2x^{\mathsf T}Lx=\sum_a(K_{ja}+K_{ka})x_a^2-K_{jk}\lVert x\rVert^2+\sum_{a\ne b}K_{ab}x_ax_b$, which is $\langle D,G_x\rangle$ term by term, so $\langle D,\nu\circ\nu\rangle=\langle D,G_x\rangle$ for every $D\in\mathcal D_n$, and both lie in $\mathcal D_n$. Item 2 is therefore also a corollary of L122 by total symmetry.

**Norm.** Over unordered pairs: $\sum_a(x_a^4+x_a^4)+\lVert x\rVert^4+\sum_{a<b}4x_a^2x_b^2=2p_2+p_1^2+2(p_1^2-p_2)=3p_1^2$. So $\lVert\nu\circ\nu\rVert=\sqrt3\lVert x\rVert^2=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. Follows, and is the Lean G_norm_sq identity.

**Verdict CONFIRMED.**

## 3. Item 3, the pinning and the odd equation

- *Split of the critical equation.* A unit critical point has $Q(W)=\theta W$ with $\theta=P(W)$, the Lagrange condition for $P(W)=\langle Q(W),W\rangle$ on the sphere. As $\mathcal S_{jk}$ is a subalgebra, criticality in $\mathcal S_{jk}$ and in $\mathcal W_n$ coincide. $Q(D+\nu)=D\circ D+2D\circ\nu+G_x$ with $D\circ D,G_x\in\mathcal D_n$ and $D\circ\nu\in V_{jk}$ by Given 1 at the $\Gamma$-fixed $D$. Projecting gives $D\circ D+G_x=\theta D$ and $L_D\nu=\tfrac\theta2\nu$. Follows.
- *$\langle L_D\nu,\nu\rangle=\langle D,\nu\circ\nu\rangle$.* Total symmetry of $\langle A\circ B,C\rangle$ for the trace inner product, which holds for $W^2$ and for the Lie-bracket sharp. Follows.
- *Pinning.* $\langle D,G_x\rangle=\tfrac\theta2\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, and dividing by $\lVert G_x\rVert=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\lVert\nu\rVert^2\ne0$ (the hypothesis $\nu\ne0$) gives $\theta/\sqrt3$. Follows.
- *Swap parity.* $\sigma$ is a permutation matrix in $O(n)$, so it acts on $\mathcal W_n$ by an algebra automorphism preserving $\mathcal D_n$, with $K\mapsto K\circ\sigma$. The odd part has $K_{ka}=-K_{ja}$, $K_{jk}=-K_{kj}=0$ and $K_{ab}=0$, and row $a$ reads $K_{ja}+K_{ka}=0$ automatically, row $j$ reads $\sum_aK_{ja}=0$. $G_x$ is even by its entries. $\sigma(D^+\circ D^+)=D^+\circ D^+$, $\sigma(D^-\circ D^-)=(-D^-)\circ(-D^-)=D^-\circ D^-$, $\sigma(D^+\circ D^-)=-D^+\circ D^-$, so $(D\circ D)^-=2D^+\circ D^-$ and the odd part of the $\mathcal D_n$ equation is $2L_{D^+}D^-=\theta D^-$. Follows.

**Verdict CONFIRMED.**

## 4. Item 4, the odd action of $L_{G_x}$

- *The odd part is $\{y:\sum y=0\}$.* Shown in section 3: an odd diagonal tensor is supported on the pairs $(ja),(ka)$ with $K_{ja}=-K_{ka}=y_a$, row $a$ is automatic and row $j$ is $\sum_ay_a=0$. The identification scales the norm uniformly, $\lVert Y\rVert^2=2\lVert y\rVert^2$, so it carries an operator's eigenvalues and self-adjointness unchanged. Follows.
- *$(G\circ Y)_{ja}$.* $G_{ja}Y_{ja}=x_a^2y_a$. In $\sum_cG_{jc}Y_{ca}$ only $c\in\{j,k\}$ has $Y_{ca}\ne0$, $c=j$ gives $G_{jj}=0$ and $c=k$ gives $G_{jk}Y_{ka}=(-\lVert x\rVert^2)(-y_a)=\lVert x\rVert^2y_a$. In $\sum_cY_{jc}G_{ca}$ only $c\notin\{j,k\}$, $c\ne a$ contributes, $y_c\cdot2x_cx_a$, total $2x_a(\langle x,y\rangle-x_ay_a)$. Halving the sum and adding: $\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2y_a+x_a\langle x,y\rangle$. Follows, with the sign of $G_{jk}$ from item 2 entering exactly once.
- *Even part vanishes.* By parity: $\sigma(G\circ Y)=G\circ(-Y)$, so $G\circ Y$ is odd. Directly: at $(jk)$ the sum is $\sum_a[x_a^2(-y_a)+y_ax_a^2]=0$, and at $(ab)$ the $c=j$ and $c=k$ contributions $x_a^2y_b+y_ax_b^2$ and $-x_a^2y_b-y_ax_b^2$ cancel, while $G_{ab}Y_{ab}=0$. Follows.
- *The image lies in the odd part.* $\sum_a(\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2y_a+x_a\langle x,y\rangle)=0$ by $\sum y=\sum x=0$, and $y\mapsto\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2y+x\langle x,y\rangle$ is symmetric, consistent with $L_{G_x}$ being self-adjoint. Follows. The two eigenvalues the Lean file checks are then $\tfrac32\lVert x\rVert^2$ on $y=x$ and $\tfrac12\lVert x\rVert^2$ on $x^\perp$.

**Verdict CONFIRMED.**

## 5. Item 5, the commutators and the minimising angle

- *The action of $\Lambda^2\xi$.* The derivation extension of $\xi$ with $\xi e_j=e_k$, $\xi e_k=-e_j$ sends $e_j\wedge e_a\mapsto e_k\wedge e_a$, $e_k\wedge e_a\mapsto-e_j\wedge e_a$, $e_j\wedge e_k\mapsto e_k\wedge e_k-e_j\wedge e_j=0$ and $e_a\wedge e_b\mapsto0$. So the brief's action is the one for $\xi=e_ke_j^{\mathsf T}-e_je_k^{\mathsf T}$. **This is a convention, and it is opposite to the common $E_{jk}=e_je_k^{\mathsf T}-e_ke_j^{\mathsf T}$ and to the convention of the orbit lemma in [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) line 1083, whose commutator coefficient is $M_{ki}-M_{ji}$.** Only the first clause of item 5, "coordinates $K_{ja}-K_{ka}$", depends on it, and both conditions at the minimum are invariant under $\xi\mapsto-\xi$. The statement should name the convention it uses.
- *$\xi\cdot W=[\Lambda^2\xi,W]$.* The rotation acts by $W\mapsto\Lambda^2g\,W\,\Lambda^2g^{-1}$, whose derivative at $g=e^{\varphi\xi}$, $\varphi=0$, is the commutator. Follows.
- *$\xi\cdot D$.* With $A=\Lambda^2\xi$, $A_{ka,ja}=1$, $A_{ja,ka}=-1$, and $D$ diagonal, $[A,D]_{PR}=A_{PR}(K_R-K_P)$, which is $K_{ja}-K_{ka}$ at $(ka,ja)$ and $-(K_{ka}-K_{ja})=K_{ja}-K_{ka}$ at $(ja,ka)$, zero elsewhere. The coordinates sum to $(-K_{jk})-(-K_{kj})=0$ by the rows $j$ and $k$. So $\xi\cdot D\in V_{jk}$ with $\lVert\xi\cdot D\rVert^2=2\sum_a(K_{ja}-K_{ka})^2$. Follows.
- *$\xi\cdot\nu$.* $(A\nu)e_{ja}=A(x_ae_{ka})=-x_ae_{ja}$, $(\nu A)e_{ja}=\nu e_{ka}=x_ae_{ja}$, so $[A,\nu]e_{ja}=-2x_ae_{ja}$, and likewise $[A,\nu]e_{ka}=2x_ae_{ka}$, with every other basis vector annihilated. Diagonal with $K_{ja}=-2x_a$, $K_{ka}=2x_a$, rows summing to zero, norm$^2$ $8\lVert x\rVert^2=4\lVert\nu\rVert^2$. Follows. It is swap-odd with $y=-2x$.
- *$\xi^2$ preserves each summand.* $\xi$ maps $\mathcal D_n\to V_{jk}$ and $V_{jk}\to\mathcal D_n$ by the two bullets above. Follows.
- *The expansion.* $W(\varphi)=W+\varphi\,\xi\cdot W+\tfrac{\varphi^2}2\xi^2\cdot W+O(\varphi^3)$, and projecting to $V_{jk}$ keeps $\nu$, $\varphi\,\xi\cdot D$ and $\tfrac{\varphi^2}2\xi^2\cdot\nu$, the term $\xi\cdot\nu$ and $\xi^2\cdot D$ lying in $\mathcal D_n$. Then $\lVert\nu+\varphi a+\tfrac{\varphi^2}2b\rVert^2=\lVert\nu\rVert^2+2\varphi\langle\nu,a\rangle+\varphi^2(\lVert a\rVert^2+\langle\nu,b\rangle)+O(\varphi^3)$. The action is orthogonal, so $\xi$ is skew-adjoint on $\mathcal W_n$ and $\langle\nu,\xi\cdot(\xi\cdot\nu)\rangle=-\lVert\xi\cdot\nu\rVert^2$. Follows, sign included.
- *Both consequences.* The angle function is smooth on a circle, so a minimiser exists, and translating the angle puts it at $\varphi=0$ because $e^{(\varphi_0+t)\xi}=e^{t\xi}e^{\varphi_0\xi}$. There $f'(0)=0$ gives $\langle\nu,\xi\cdot D\rangle=2\langle x,(K_{ja}-K_{ka})_a\rangle=0$, and $f''(0)\ge0$ gives $2\sum_a(K_{ja}-K_{ka})^2\ge4\lVert\nu\rVert^2$, which is the stated bound. Follows. **The hypothesis the statement leaves implicit is that $D$, $K$ and $x$ are those of the rotated tensor at the minimising angle**, which is how the proof uses them, and the row difference $K_{j\cdot}-K_{k\cdot}$ is to be read on the indices $a\notin\{j,k\}$ (its entries at $j$ and $k$ meet no coordinate of $x$).

**Verdict CONFIRMED**, with the two wording repairs above.

## 6. Item 6, the pairing

- *$Q(D)=\theta D$.* A unit diagonal critical point satisfies the Lagrange condition, and $Q(D)=D\circ D$. Follows.
- *$\theta\langle D,G_x\rangle=\langle D\circ D,G_x\rangle=\langle G_x\circ D,D\rangle$.* Pairing, then total symmetry of $\langle A\circ B,C\rangle$, the same fact as in section 3. Follows, and $\langle K,G_x\rangle$ is the Frobenius pairing $\langle D,G_x\rangle=\sum_{p<q}K_{pq}(G_x)_{pq}$.
- *Hypotheses.* The identity is linear in $G_x$, so "unit $x\perp\mathbf1$" is used only to normalise, and "unit $D$" is used only through $\theta=P(D)$. With item 4 and the vanishing of $\langle L_{G_x}D^+,D^-\rangle$ by parity it gives $\theta\langle D,G_x\rangle=\langle L_{G_x}D^+,D^+\rangle+\lVert x\rVert^2\lVert y\rVert^2+2\langle x,y\rangle^2$ at the odd coordinates $y$ of $D$, which is the form the note uses.

**Verdict CONFIRMED.**

## 7. Scope, unstated hypotheses and What it gives

- *$n\ge5$.* No step uses it. At $n=4$, $\mathcal D_4$, $V_{1234}$ and the six $V_{jk}$ have dimensions $2+2+6=10=\dim\mathcal W_4$, every witness of item 1 exists, and items 2 to 6 are identities valid whenever $V_{jk}\ne0$, which is $n\ge4$. The hypothesis is stronger than needed and harmless.
- *$\theta$.* Items 3 and 6 use $\theta=P(W)$ through $Q(W)=\theta W$, the record's convention, and no sign of $\theta$.
- *Given 1 at a non-critical $D$.* Item 3 uses that $L_D$ preserves $V_{jk}$ at the $\Gamma$-fixed $D$, which Given 1 states without criticality. Correct use.
- *Prior art.* Lemma L122 is the pair block of $L_D$ and implies item 2 by total symmetry, as section 2 shows. The orbit lemma at line 1083 of [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) already records the commutator of item 5 for diagonal $D$, with the opposite sign convention. The lemma cites neither, and a citation to L122 would give item 2 a second proof in one line.
- *What it gives, REFUTED as written.* It says the pair-block stratum "reduces, with these identities, to one open inequality". The items prove identities at a critical point of $\mathcal S_{jk}$ (items 3, 5) and at a diagonal critical point (item 6), and no step connects a gap at diagonal critical points to the exclusion of critical points with $\nu\ne0$ above $\sqrt{3/2}$. The linked [note](2026-09-14-I104-pair-block.md) derives no such implication either, and its own account of what a hand proof needs is larger: the gap must be "stable under the perturbation the chain allows" (note line 151), since a critical point with small $\nu\ne0$ has $D^+$ only close to a diagonal critical point, and the closure for $\lVert\nu\rVert^2\ge0.2$ rests on sampled even constants, listed as "Owed for a lemma: the even constants derived rather than sampled, and a bridge" (note line 122). The step from the gap to the even-form bound is sound: with $x\perp y$, which is $x$ orthogonal to the orbit direction $2y$, items 4 and 6 give exactly the note's inequality $Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2\le\theta^2-2\gamma\theta$. The over-reach is the word "reduces" and "one". In addition $\gamma$ is undefined in the lemma, and "the pair-block form" means $\langle K,G_x\rangle$, twice the Rayleigh quotient of $L_D$ on $V_{jk}$ at unit $x$, which is why its bound reads $\theta-2\gamma$ against an eigenvalue gap $\tfrac\theta2-\gamma$.
- *Correction proposed.* "**What it gives.** *Items 4 and 6 rewrite a quantitative pair-block gap at a unit diagonal critical point $D$, top eigenvalue of $L_D$ on $V_{jk}$ off the orbit direction at most $\tfrac\theta2-\gamma$ for a constant $\gamma>0$, as the inequality $Q_{\mathrm{even}}(D^+)+\tfrac12\lVert D^-\rVert^2\le\theta^2-2\gamma\theta$ over unit $x\perp\mathbf1$ with $x\perp y$. The pair-block stratum needs that gap for every diagonal critical point above $\sqrt{3/2}$, its stability under the perturbation the critical equations allow, and derived constants for the chain at large $\lVert\nu\rVert$ ([note](../reviews/2026-09-14-I104-pair-block.md)).*" followed by the existing sentence on screens.

**Verdict REFUTED** for the What it gives sentence. The six items of the lemma and its $n\ge5$ scope stand.
