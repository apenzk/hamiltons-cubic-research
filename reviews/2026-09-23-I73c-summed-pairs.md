# I73 sub-step (c): the eigenspace-summed pair inequality, and what it needs beyond $(\lambda,T)$

*Working note, derived 2026-09-23 at effort `xhigh`, which the user set. Gate 2 by [r1095](REVIEW_r1095.md) and [r1096](REVIEW_r1096.md): sections 1 to 4 CONFIRMED, the rank argument of section 3 supplied, and the R35 paragraph repaired in place.*

## Setting

$W$ is a unit maximiser with $\theta>0$ and $e=W/\theta$, and $E_a$ is an eigenspace of $e$ with value $\varepsilon_a$, multiplicity $m_a$ and orthonormal basis $\{\psi_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in a}$, the matrices being $\Psi_\alpha$. **Put $A_a=\sum_{\alpha\in a}\Psi_\alpha\Psi_\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ (the isotropy matrix of $E_a$), $G_{ab}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aA_b)$, and $\tau_{abc}=\sum_{\alpha\in a,\beta\in b,\gamma\in c}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$, $\tau_{ab}=\sum_c\tau_{abc}$.** For $\alpha\in a$, $\beta\in b$ with $a\ne b$, $Y_0=\psi_\alpha\odot\psi_\beta$ and $X=\pi_{\mathcal W_n}Y_0$, with $\langle W,X\rangle=0$.

## 1. The summed inequality

[L125](../findings/second-order.md) holds for every such $X$. Summing over all $\alpha\in a$, $\beta\in b$ gives a necessary condition at a maximiser that depends on the eigenspaces only:
$$S_{ab}:=\sum_{\alpha\in a,\beta\in b}\Big(\langle X,e\circ X\rangle-\tfrac12\lVert X\rVert^2\Big)\le0 .$$
*This is the class-level form, matching the per-index rows' class averaging at three values ([L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md)).*

## 2. The per-pair norms, derived

With $g=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(U^2V^2)$ for the pair's matrices $U,V$:
$$\lVert N_4\rVert^2=\tfrac16(1-g),\qquad \lvert h\rvert^2=g-\tfrac12\textstyle\sum_\gamma T_{\alpha\beta\gamma},\qquad \lVert X\rVert^2=\tfrac12-\frac{\lvert h\rvert^2}{n-2}-\tfrac16(1-g).$$
**Proof.** $N_4=\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(u\otimes v)$, and $\langle u\otimes v,\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(u\otimes v)\rangle$ splits over the three pair-partitions of four indices. The identity partition gives $\lvert u\rvert^2\lvert v\rvert^2$, the exchange gives $\langle u,v\rangle^2=0$, and each crossing contraction is $\pm g$. For $h=-\tfrac12\{U,V\}$, use $\lVert\{U,V\}\rVert_F^2+\lVert[U,V]\rVert_F^2=2(\lVert UV\rVert_F^2+\lVert VU\rVert_F^2)=4g$ at skew $U,V$, and $\lVert[U,V]\rVert_F^2=2\sum_\gamma T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$. The norm of $X$ is then [L234](../findings/second-order.md)'s. $\square$ *Screened exact at random orthonormal pairs, $n=5,6,7$, residuals $\le4\cdot10^{-16}$. Both extreme coordinate cases, $\tfrac13$ disjoint and $\tfrac{n-3}{2(n-2)}$ sharing, are recovered.*

**Summed**, since $\sum_{\alpha\in a}\Psi_\alpha^2=-A_a$:
$$\sum_{\alpha\in a,\beta\in b}\lVert X\rVert^2=\tfrac12m_am_b-\frac{G_{ab}-\tfrac12\tau_{ab}}{n-2}-\tfrac16(m_am_b-G_{ab}).$$
**The norm side needs one datum beyond $(\lambda,m,\tau)$: the isotropy Gram $G$.**

## 3. At three values $G$ has ONE free parameter

$\sum_bA_b=(n-1)I$, since the identity on $\Lambda^2$ has Ricci $(n-1)g$. $\sum_b\varepsilon_bA_b=0$, which is $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(e)=0$. And $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A_a=2m_a$. Taking traces against $A_a$ gives $\sum_bG_{ab}=2(n-1)m_a$ and $\sum_b\varepsilon_bG_{ab}=0$, six linear equations on the six entries of a symmetric $3\times3$ matrix. **Exactly one is dependent**: the homogeneous system $G\mathbf1=0$, $G\varepsilon=0$ has kernel spanned by $zz^{\mathsf T}$ with $z=\mathbf1\times\varepsilon$, so the rank is $5$ and $G_{12}$ is a valid coordinate since $z_1z_2\ne0$ ([r1096](REVIEW_r1096.md)). So $G$ is fixed by $(\varepsilon,m)$ up to one parameter, say $G_{12}$. It is further constrained by $G_{ab}\ge0$, $G_{ab}^2\le G_{aa}G_{bb}$ and $G_{aa}\ge4m_a^2/n$.

## 4. The $e$-side, and where it meets R35

The unprojected part is pinned, $\sum\langle Y_0,e\circ Y_0\rangle=\tfrac14m_am_b(\varepsilon_a+\varepsilon_b)-\tfrac14\sum_c\varepsilon_c\tau_{abc}$. **The two corrections are not**: they are cubic frame contractions of $(e,P_a,P_b)$, $P_a=\sum_{\alpha\in a}\psi_\alpha\otimes\psi_\alpha$, of index patterns other than the $\#$-pattern that $T$ carries ([L234](../findings/second-order.md), r1093/r1094). *They were not reduced here.*

**They are BOUNDED by two spectral quantities of $e$.** By L234, $\langle N_r,e\circ N_r\rangle=\frac1{2(n-2)}\langle h,\mathring eh\rangle\le\frac{\mathring\mu}{2(n-2)}\lvert h\rvert^2$, with $\mathring\mu$ the top eigenvalue of the second-kind operator $h\mapsto\sum_{jl}e_{ijkl}h_{jl}$ on $S^2_0$. **This is the $\mu/\theta$ of [I113](../plan.md)(a)'s identity (1.5), R35's quantity.** Likewise $\langle N_4,e\circ N_4\rangle\le\tfrac38\nu\lVert N_4\rVert^2$, with $\nu$ the top of $\omega\mapsto\sum e_{ijkl}\omega_{ijab}\omega_{klab}$ against $\lVert\omega\rVert^2_{\mathrm{op}}$ on $\Lambda^4$. So every pair satisfies
$$\tfrac14\Big[(\varepsilon_\alpha+\varepsilon_\beta)-\textstyle\sum_\gamma\varepsilon_\gamma T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-1\Big]\ \le\ \frac{\mathring\mu-1}{2(n-2)}\lvert h\rvert^2+\Big(\tfrac38\nu-\tfrac12\Big)\tfrac16(1-g),$$
and summed over classes this is an inequality in $(\varepsilon,m,\tau,G,\mathring\mu,\nu)$ alone.

**The link to R35 is a TENSION on one quantity, and the record already caps it from above.** *The bound through $\mathring\mu$ is a relaxation: the exact term is $\langle h,\mathring eh\rangle$ at the pair's own $h$, which a bound on the top eigenvalue does not determine.* **The second-kind projection estimate $\tfrac{n-1}n\mu\le\Theta_n$ is PROVED at both parities** ([target.md](../findings/target.md), [bispectral.md](../findings/bispectral.md)), so $\mathring\mu=\mu/\theta\le\tfrac n{n-1}\Theta_n/\theta$, which is at most $\tfrac n{n-1}$ wherever $\theta\ge\Theta_n$, and the Ricci slack is then at most $\frac1{2(n-1)(n-2)}\lvert h\rvert^2$ ([r1096](REVIEW_r1096.md)). **That cap belongs in the class-level system.** *R35's unproved dichotomy would push $\mathring\mu$ the other way, to at least $\tfrac n{n-1}$ above $\sqrt{3/2}$, and the two together force equality, which is R35's own closing branch.*

**Setting.** Three values $\varepsilon_1<\varepsilon_2<\varepsilon_3$, gaps $p=\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_1$ and $q=\varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_2$, and $P_a=L_a(e)$ with $L_a(x)=\ell_a\prod_{c\ne a}(x-\varepsilon_c)$ and $\ell_a=\prod_{c\ne a}(\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_c)^{-1}$.

**Lemma (the quintic moment enters one pair through $\ell_a\ell_b$).** Let $F$ be $\Phi$ or $\Omega$, and $M^F_{ij}=F(e;e^i,e^j)$ for $0\le i,j\le2$. Then
$$F(e;P_a,P_b)=\sum_{i,j}[L_a]_i[L_b]_jM^F_{ij}=\widehat F_{ab}+\ell_a\ell_b\,M^F_{22},$$
where $\widehat F_{ab}$ is the same sum with the $(2,2)$ term removed, and $\widehat F_{ab}$ is class-level: its five moments are section 5's reduced entries, $M^\Phi_{00}=0$, $M^\Phi_{01}=12\lVert e\rVert^2$, $M^\Phi_{02}=12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}e^3$, $M^\Phi_{11}=8\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}e^3-4\langle e,e^\#\rangle$, $M^\Phi_{12}=4\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}e^2\rvert^2-4m_{22}-2m_{211}$, and likewise for $\Omega$. At three values these are $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}e^k=\sum_am_a\varepsilon_a^k$, $\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}e^2\rvert^2=\sum_{a,b}\varepsilon_a^2\varepsilon_b^2G_{ab}$ (since $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(e^2)=\sum_a\varepsilon_a^2A_a$), and T-moments that are sums of $\tau_{abc}$ against monomials in $\varepsilon$.
**Proof.** $F$ is bilinear in its last two arguments, since its summand is quadratic in $U$ and in $V$ and so depends on $X=\sum x_\alpha\psi_\alpha\otimes\psi_\alpha$ only as a tensor, **and symmetric in them**, since $\{U,V\}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(u\otimes v)$ are symmetric in $u,v$, which gives $M^F_{10},M^F_{20},M^F_{21}=M^F_{01},M^F_{02},M^F_{12}$. With $P_a=\sum_i[L_a]_ie^i$ the reconstruction follows, and the $x^2$ coefficient of $L_a$ is $\ell_a$. $\square$

**The signs.** $\ell_1\ell_2=-\frac1{p^2q(p+q)}$, $\ell_1\ell_3=\frac1{pq(p+q)^2}$ and $\ell_2\ell_3=-\frac1{pq^2(p+q)}$. So $M_{22}$ enters the pairs $(1,2)$ and $(2,3)$ with one sign and the pair $(1,3)$ with the other, and
$$p\,\ell_1\ell_2+(p+q)\,\ell_1\ell_3=0,\qquad q\,\ell_2\ell_3+(p+q)\,\ell_1\ell_3=0.$$

**Proposition (two exact class-level inequalities).** At a unit maximiser with three values, with $S_{ab}$ section 1's summed pair quantity,
$$p\,S_{12}+(p+q)\,S_{13}\le0\qquad\text{and}\qquad q\,S_{23}+(p+q)\,S_{13}\le0,$$
and in both the corrections may be replaced by their class-level parts $\widehat\Phi_{ab}$, $\widehat\Omega_{ab}$, so both are exact inequalities in $(\varepsilon,m,\tau_{abc},G_{12})$ with no bound on $\mathring\mu$ or $\varepsilon_{\max}$, the quintic moments cancelling as formal symbols whatever their values. **Every nonnegative combination of the three pair inequalities that is free of the quintic entries is a nonnegative combination of these two.**
**Proof.** $S_{ab}=U_{ab}-\frac1{8(n-2)}\Phi(e;P_a,P_b)-\frac38\Omega(e;P_a,P_b)-\tfrac12N_{ab}$, with $U_{ab}$ section 4's pinned unprojected sum and $N_{ab}$ section 2's summed norm. Each $S_{ab}\le0$, the weights are nonnegative, and by the Lemma the $M^\Phi_{22}$ and $M^\Omega_{22}$ terms carry the factor $w_{12}\ell_1\ell_2+w_{13}\ell_1\ell_3+w_{23}\ell_2\ell_3$, which vanishes on both weight vectors. For the last clause, that linear form vanishes on nonnegative weights exactly when $w_{13}=(p+q)\big(\tfrac{w_{12}}p+\tfrac{w_{23}}q\big)$, which is $\tfrac{w_{12}}p$ times the first weight vector plus $\tfrac{w_{23}}q$ times the second. $\square$

*Screened: $F(e;P_a,P_b)$ against the Vandermonde reconstruction to $6\times10^{-14}$ on a random three-valued operator at $n=6$ and to $5\times10^{-8}$ at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, $n=8$ (eigenvalue rounding), and the $M_{22}$ coefficient of both combinations zero to $10^{-15}$, a check on the Lagrange matrix alone and so the same for $\Phi$ and $\Omega$.*

**What it buys.** Section 5's per-pair inequalities stay valid and carry two relaxations. These two carry none. *They trade the pair resolution for exactness: $S_{13}$ is only ever seen alongside $S_{12}$ or $S_{23}$.*

## 7. Eliminating $G_{12}$: the gap-weighted sum, and it is spectral at every $W$

*Derived 2026-09-23 at `xhigh` (`$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh` at the start of the step). Gate 2 by [r1104](REVIEW_r1104.md) and [r1105](REVIEW_r1105.md): the Gram elimination, the identity and the corollary CONFIRMED; the corollary's reach, the Fourier–Motzkin paragraph and one catalogue sentence repaired below. **$G_{12}$ enters the two L235 inequalities with opposite signs.** Along the free Gram direction of section 3, $dG=-zz^{\mathsf T}\,dG_{12}/(q(p+q))$ with $z=(\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3,\varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2)$, so $d\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}e^2\rvert^2/dG_{12}=-p^2q(p+q)$, while $\sum_{a<b}w_{ab}\,dG_{ab}=0$ for both weight vectors of section 6. In $S_{ab}$ the Gram enters through $N_{ab}$ with coefficient $-\tfrac12\big(\tfrac16-\tfrac1{n-2}\big)$ and through $M_{12}$ of both corrections, whose $\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}e^2\rvert^2$ parts combine to $\tfrac1{2(n-2)}-\tfrac1{12}$. Both are multiples of $n-8$, and the $M_{12}$ coefficient of pair $(a,b)$ is $-\ell_a\ell_b(2\sigma-\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_b)$ with $\sigma=\sum\varepsilon_c$, which on weights with $\sum w_{ab}\ell_a\ell_b=0$ (section 6) sums to $\sum w_{ab}\ell_a\ell_b(\varepsilon_a+\varepsilon_b)$; that is $\tfrac1{p(p+q)}$ on $(w_{12},w_{13},w_{23})=(p,p+q,0)$ and $-\tfrac1{q(p+q)}$ on $(0,p+q,q)$. The corrections are subtracted in $S_{ab}$, which flips the sign of $\tfrac1{2(n-2)}-\tfrac1{12}$. **So the $G_{12}$ coefficients are $-c\,pq$ and $+c\,p^2$ with $c=\frac{n-8}{12(n-2)}$**, and $p$ times the first inequality plus $q$ times the second is free of $G_{12}$. *Checked exactly in sympy: the null direction, the derivative, both vanishing $G$-sums and the cancellation* ([screen_I73c_gram_elim.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I73c_gram_elim.py)). **At $n=8$ both inequalities are $G$-free on their own.**

**That combination is the gap-weighted sum.** $p\,(p,p+q,0)+q\,(0,p+q,q)=(p^2,(p+q)^2,q^2)$, i.e. $w_{ab}=(\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_b)^2$, which is half of $\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\varepsilon_\alpha-\varepsilon_\beta)^2s_{\alpha\beta}$ over ordered eigenpairs, pairs inside one eigenspace carrying weight $0$. The weight $(x-y)^2=x^2-2xy+y^2$ reaches only the moments $M_{02}$ and $M_{11}$, never $M_{12}$ or $M_{22}$, so the sum is class-level at **every** spectrum, with no three-value hypothesis.

**Proposition (the gap-weighted identity).** For every unit $W\in\mathcal W_n$ with $\theta=P(W)>0$ and $e=W/\theta$, with $s_{\alpha\beta}=\langle X,e\circ X\rangle-\tfrac12\lVert X\rVert^2$ at $X=\pi_{\mathcal W_n}(\psi_\alpha\odot\psi_\beta)$ and the sum over ordered eigenpairs,
$$\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\varepsilon_\alpha-\varepsilon_\beta)^2s_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{n+4}6\Big[(n-1)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}e^3-(n-3)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}e^2\Big].$$
**Proof.** Sum each piece of $s_{\alpha\beta}$ against $w(x,y)=(x-y)^2$, writing $E_k=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}e^k$ ($E_1=0$), $N=\binom n2$, and using three facts: row (A), $\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=2(n-2)$; row (B), $\sum_{\beta\gamma}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\varepsilon_\beta=-2\varepsilon_\alpha$; and $\langle e,e^\#\rangle=E_2-E_3$, the definition of $P$ at unit $W$, equivalently $m_{111}=2(E_2-E_3)$. With $\sum_\alpha x_\alpha\Psi_\alpha\Psi_\alpha^{\mathsf T}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X)$, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)I$, $\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(e)=0$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(X)=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X$:
- $\sum w\,g=2\langle\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}e^2,\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}\mathrm{Id}\rangle-2\lvert\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}e\rvert^2=4(n-1)E_2$, and $\sum w\sum_\gamma T=4(n-2)E_2-2m_{110}=4(n-1)E_2$ since $m_{110}=-2E_2$, so $\sum w\lvert h\rvert^2=2(n-1)E_2$ and $\sum w\lVert X\rVert^2=\frac{(n-1)(n^2-10)}{3(n-2)}E_2$.
- $\sum w\,\langle Y_0,e\circ Y_0\rangle=\tfrac N2E_3-\tfrac14\big(-4E_3-2m_{111}\big)=\tfrac N2E_3+E_2$.
- $\sum w\,\Phi=2M^\Phi_{02}-2M^\Phi_{11}=24E_3-2(12E_3-4E_2)=8E_2$, and $\sum w\,\Omega=\tfrac29\big[(n-1)(n-8)E_3+4E_2\big]$.
Then $\sum ws=\tfrac N2E_3+E_2-\tfrac{E_2}{n-2}-\tfrac1{12}\big[(n-1)(n-8)E_3+4E_2\big]-\frac{(n-1)(n^2-10)}{6(n-2)}E_2$, whose $E_3$ coefficient is $\tfrac{(n-1)(n+4)}6$ and whose $E_2$ coefficient is $\frac{-n^3+n^2+14n-24}{6(n-2)}=-\tfrac{(n+4)(n-3)}6$. $\square$

*Screened exact ([screen_I73c_gapweighted.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I73c_gapweighted.py)), by direct projection over all eigenpairs: $S^3\times S^3$, $S^2\times S^4$, padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ at $n=6,8$, $S^3\times S^4$, and F39's saddle, residuals $\le6\times10^{-15}$; and at a random NON-critical Weyl tensor, residual $6\times10^{-14}$, confirming that the identity needs no criticality: row (B) is $W^\#\mathrm{Id}=-W$, a Weyl identity ([r1104](REVIEW_r1104.md), [r1105](REVIEW_r1105.md)). **Basis-free, the ordered sum is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal W_n}\big(\pi\,\mathrm{ad}_e^2\,\pi\,(L_e-\tfrac12)\big)$** ([r1105](REVIEW_r1105.md), screened at $n=6$), with $\pi\,\mathrm{ad}_e^2\,\pi\succeq0$ killing $W$, so the corollary is L125 traced against that operator.*

**Corollary (a spectral necessary condition at every maximiser).** L125 makes every $s_{\alpha\beta}\le0$ with $\varepsilon_\alpha\ne\varepsilon_\beta$, so a unit maximiser satisfies
$$(n-1)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3\le(n-3)\,\theta,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad 2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W^3\le(n-3)\langle W,W^\#\rangle .$$

## 9. F76's successor in linear form: the Ky Fan bracket cap, and what it does to L173's family

*Derived 2026-09-23 at `xhigh` (`$CLAUDE_EFFORT` read `xhigh`). **Lemma (the Ky Fan bracket cap).** For a unit $X_0$ and orthonormal $X_1,\dots,X_r\in\mathfrak{so}(n)$ (orthogonality to $X_0$ is not needed), in this record's norm,
$$\sum_{j=1}^r\lVert[X_0,X_j]\rVert^2\ \le\ \kappa_r:=\max_{\lVert X\rVert=1}\big(\text{sum of the $r$ largest eigenvalues of }-\mathrm{ad}_X^2\big),$$
**with $\kappa_1=2$, $\kappa_2=4$, $\kappa_4=6$, $\kappa_6=8$**, and $\kappa_{2k-1}\le\kappa_{2k}$. The case $r=1$ is [L31](../findings/stratum-two-negative.md)'s pair cap.
**Proof.** $\lVert[X_0,X_j]\rVert^2=\langle X_j,-\mathrm{ad}_{X_0}^2X_j\rangle$ and the $X_j$ are orthonormal, so Ky Fan's maximum principle gives the bound. For the constants: with the angles $s$ of $X$ ($\sum s_a^2=1$), the spectrum of $-\mathrm{ad}_X^2$ consists of the slot values $(s_a+s_b)^2$ and $(s_a-s_b)^2$ for $a<b$ and, at odd $n$, $s_a^2$, **each with multiplicity $2$** (the proof of L31 lists the roots). So $\kappa_{2k}$ is twice the largest sum over $k$ distinct slots, and a sum over slots is $s^{\mathsf T}Ms$ with $M=D+A_+-A_-+\Lambda$: $D$ the slot degrees, $A_\pm$ the adjacencies of the $\pm$ slots, $\Lambda$ the diagonal of the $s_a^2$ slots. The largest such sum is $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ maximised over configurations of $k$ slots. For $k=1$ it is $2$; for $k=2$ it is $3$ (the path $P_3$, against $\tfrac{3+\sqrt5}2$ for an edge with a loop and $2$ for the $\pm$ pair); **for $k=3$ it is $4$**, attained by the triangle and by the star $K_{1,3}$ (signless-Laplacian index $4$ for both, and $\le\max_{uv}(d_u+d_v)=4$ for any simple graph with three edges), and every other configuration is lower: $(s_a\pm s_b)^2\le(\lvert s_a\rvert+\lvert s_b\rvert)^2$ reduces a configuration without a $\pm$ pair to the unsigned bound, a $\pm$ pair contributes $2(s_a^2+s_b^2)\le2$ with the third slot $\le2$, and with an $s_a^2$ slot at most two edges remain, so Weyl gives $\le3+1$ ([r1109](REVIEW_r1109.md)). **Attainment**: $\kappa_1,\kappa_2$ at $(1,1)/\sqrt2$, $\kappa_4$ at $(2,1,1)/\sqrt6$, $\kappa_6$ at $(1,1,1)/\sqrt3$ ($n\ge6$) or $(3,1,1,1)/\sqrt{12}$ ($n\ge8$). $\square$ *Screened: random search with local ascent at $n=7,8,9$ approaches $2,4,6,8$ from below and never exceeds them; it also suggests $\kappa_3=3+\sqrt3$ and $\kappa_5=4+2\sqrt2$, not proved and not used* ([screen_I73c_kyfan_kappa.py](../scripts/misc/screen_I73c_kyfan_kappa.py)).
