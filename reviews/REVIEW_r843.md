## 0. Conventions, fixed once, quoted from the brief

$\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n\cong\mathfrak{so}(n)$ carries $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$, under which $\{\varphi_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}\}_{a<b}$ is orthonormal, and $N=\dim\Lambda^2=n(n-1)/2$. $W$ is symmetric on $\Lambda^2$ with orthonormal eigenbasis $\{v_k\}$, $Wv_k=\lambda_kv_k$. Write

$$c_{abc}=\langle[v_a,v_b],v_c\rangle,\qquad T_{abc}=c_{abc}^2,\qquad R_{ab}=\sum_cT_{abc}=\lVert[v_a,v_b]\rVert^2,\qquad S_k=\sum_{\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta^2T_{k\beta\gamma}=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^2R_{k\alpha}.$$

$c$ is totally antisymmetric, so $T$ is totally symmetric, nonnegative and hollow. The index formula is $(A\#B)_{ab}=\tfrac12\sum_{cdef}c_{acd}c_{bef}A_{ce}B_{df}$, normalised so that $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\,\mathrm{id}$, and $W^\#=W\#W$. $\mathrm{Ric}$ is the adjoint of $\cdot\wedge\mathrm{id}$ with $\mathrm{Ric}^*=2(\cdot\wedge\mathrm{id})$, and $\mathrm{Alt}$ is the orthogonal projection of $S^2(\Lambda^2)$ onto $\Lambda^4$, $\mathrm{Alt}(R)_{ijkl}=\tfrac13(R_{ijkl}+R_{iklj}+R_{iljk})$. The operator pairing is Frobenius, $\lVert W\rVert^2=\operatorname{tr}(W^2)=\sum_k\lambda_k^2$ and $P(W)=\operatorname{tr}(W^3)+\operatorname{tr}(WW^\#)$. Relations (A), (B), (C) and the definition $g_k=\lVert[\mathrm{ad}(v_k),W]\rVert^2=\sum_{\alpha\beta}(\lambda_\alpha-\lambda_\beta)^2T_{k\alpha\beta}$ are as quoted in my brief from [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md).

## 1. The expansion, done here rather than quoted

$$g_k=\sum_{\alpha\beta}(\lambda_\alpha-\lambda_\beta)^2T_{k\alpha\beta}=\sum_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_\alpha^2T_{k\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_\beta^2T_{k\alpha\beta}-2\sum_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta T_{k\alpha\beta}.$$

$T_{k\alpha\beta}=T_{k\beta\alpha}$ makes the first two sums equal, each being $S_k$, so the first two terms give $2S_k$. The third is $-2\cdot2(W^\#)_{kk}$ by the index formula read at $A=B=W$: at diagonal $A,B$ the formula collapses to $(A\#B)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_{cd}c_{kcd}^2A_{cc}B_{dd}=\tfrac12\sum_{cd}T_{kcd}A_{cc}B_{dd}$, so $\sum_{\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta\lambda_\gamma T_{k\beta\gamma}=2(W\#W)_{kk}$. Hence $g_k=2S_k-4(W^\#)_{kk}$, exactly as the block writes it. Hollowness is not needed at this step, and the block does not claim it. **C1 CONFIRMED.**

The collapse of the index formula at diagonal arguments deserves the line the block gives it: $A_{ce}=A_{cc}\delta_{ce}$ and $B_{df}=B_{dd}\delta_{df}$ kill the $e$ and $f$ sums, leaving $c_{kcd}c_{kcd}$, and the surviving weight is $A_{cc}B_{dd}$ as claimed.

## 2. The index-formula step, and the $\tfrac12$ the record warns about

At $A=W^2$, $B=I$ the same collapse gives $(W^2\#I)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_{cd}T_{kcd}\lambda_c^2=\tfrac12S_k$, so $S_k=2(W^2\#I)_{kk}$. **C2 CONFIRMED**, and there is no off-by-two anywhere, for three independent reasons.

- The same $\tfrac12$ returns the normalisation the brief fixes: $(I\#I)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_{cd}T_{kcd}=\tfrac12\cdot2(n-2)=n-2$ by (A), which is $\mathrm{id}^\#=(n-2)\,\mathrm{id}$.
- It returns relation (B) as $(W\#I)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_{\beta\gamma}\lambda_\beta T_{k\beta\gamma}=-\lambda_k$, which is $W\#I=-W$ read on the diagonal.
- It returns relation (C) as $2(W^\#)_{kk}=2\lambda_k(\theta-\lambda_k)$, which is the diagonal of $Q(W)=\theta W$.

Three of the record's own tabled facts are recovered from one reading of the constant, so the constant is pinned rather than chosen. $W^2$ is diagonal in the same basis because $W$ is symmetric and $\{v_k\}$ is its eigenbasis, with $(W^2)_{kk}=\lambda_k^2$, which is also the entry the L131 substitution consumes.

## 3. The asymmetric weight

$\lambda_\beta^2$ is indeed asymmetric in $(\beta,\gamma)$ and $T$ is symmetric, and no symmetrisation is performed. The index formula at $B=I$ produces exactly the asymmetric weight $A_{cc}\cdot1$, with $c$ the slot the block calls $\beta$, and the identity $S_k=2(W^2\#I)_{kk}$ is between the asymmetric object and the $\#$ product as written. Symmetrising by hand, $\tfrac12(\lambda_\beta^2+\lambda_\gamma^2)$, returns $S_k$ unchanged by the symmetry of $T$, so even the symmetrised reading moves no constant. **C3 CONFIRMED.** The one place a reader could slip is to conflate $S_k$ with the doubly weighted $\sum\lambda_\beta^2\lambda_\gamma^2T_{k\beta\gamma}$, which is $2(W^2\#W^2)_{kk}$ and is a degree-$4$ object outside this lemma.

## 4. The scope claim

Every step is available at an arbitrary symmetric $W$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$: the expansion uses only the symmetry of $T$, the two $\#$ readings use only the index formula at diagonal arguments, and L131 is quantified over every symmetric $R$, which is what lets it be read at $W^2$ even though $W^2$ satisfies no Bianchi identity and is not trace-free. $\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)$ and $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)$ are defined for any element of $S^2(\Lambda^2)$, and a symmetric operator on $\Lambda^2$ is such an element, so both terms of the first display are meaningful without a Weyl hypothesis. **C4 CONFIRMED**, and the block is right that $W\#I=-W$ is nowhere in it.

**One caveat the block should carry.** $g_k$, $T_{k\alpha\beta}$, $(\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\wedge\mathrm{id})_{kk}$ and $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)_{kk}$ all depend on the choice of $v_k$ inside a repeated eigenspace, so at a degenerate spectrum the identity is a statement about a chosen unit eigenvector rather than about an eigenvalue. Both sides move together, so the identity survives, and the boxed bound is then a bound holding at **every** unit eigenvector, which is a strengthening rather than an ambiguity. The block's "at every index $k$" is honest under the stated hypothesis "an orthonormal eigenbasis", and saying so would cost one clause.

## 5. Both moment sums, derived

Write the identity in the form the two $\#$ readings give, before L131 is applied: $g_k=4(W^2\#I)_{kk}-4(W^\#)_{kk}$.

**Weight $1$.** By total symmetry, $\sum_k\sum_{cd}T_{kcd}\lambda_c^2=\sum_c\lambda_c^2\sum_{kd}T_{ckd}=2(n-2)\operatorname{tr}(W^2)$ by (A), so $\operatorname{tr}(W^2\#I)=(n-2)\lVert W\rVert^2$. And $\operatorname{tr}(W^\#)=\tfrac12\sum_c\lambda_c\sum_{dk}\lambda_dT_{cdk}=\tfrac12\sum_c\lambda_c(-2\lambda_c)=-\lVert W\rVert^2$ by (B). Hence $\sum_kg_k=4(n-2)\lVert W\rVert^2+4\lVert W\rVert^2=4(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$. **Reproduced exactly.**

**Weight $\lambda_k$.** $\sum_k\lambda_k(W^2\#I)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_c\lambda_c^2\sum_{kd}\lambda_kT_{ckd}=\tfrac12\sum_c\lambda_c^2(-2\lambda_c)=-\operatorname{tr}(W^3)$ by (B) at index $c$, which is the block's $\sum_{abc}\lambda_a\lambda_b^2T_{abc}=-2\operatorname{tr}W^3$ halved. And $\sum_k\lambda_k(W^\#)_{kk}=\operatorname{tr}(WW^\#)$ because $W$ is diagonal in this basis. Hence $\sum_k\lambda_kg_k=-4\operatorname{tr}(W^3)-4\operatorname{tr}(WW^\#)=-4P(W)$. **Reproduced exactly.**

**The conventions each constant carries.** $\operatorname{tr}(W\#W)=-\lVert W\rVert^2$ is relation (B) contracted, so it is a **Weyl** statement carrying first Bianchi and Ricci-flatness, not a general one. $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\wedge\mathrm{id})=(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$ needs only (A), because $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Alt}(R))=0$ for every symmetric $R$: $\mathrm{Alt}(R)$ is totally antisymmetric in its four indices, so its operator diagonal entries $\mathrm{Alt}(R)_{ijij}$ vanish. Under $\mathrm{Ric}^*=2(\cdot\wedge\mathrm{id})$ the same number reads $\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Ric}^*\mathrm{Ric}(W^2))=2(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$, and quoting it without naming which of the two is meant is exactly the collision [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) exists to prevent.

**The defect this exposes.** Both moments consume (B), so both hold at a Weyl tensor and at nothing wider, one paragraph after the block asserts in bold that there is no hypothesis anywhere. The first display is hypothesis-free and its two contractions are not, and the block reads as though the second inherited the first's freedom. Screened at a random symmetric $W$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^5$: the identity holds to $1.4\times10^{-14}$ while $\sum_kg_k=549.17$ against $4(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2=670.61$. **C5 CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA, MIS-SCOPED IN THE PROSE.** The repair is one clause: "at a Weyl tensor, where (B) is available".

**A second, smaller mis-scoping.** The trace of the boxed right side, $\sum_k\big[(\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\wedge\mathrm{id})_{kk}+3\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)_{kk}\big]=(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$, is a general fact needing only (A), and my brief attributes it to "the identity plus $\operatorname{tr}W=0$". Trace-freeness is not used: the derivation above is $\operatorname{tr}(W^2\#I)+\operatorname{tr}(W^2)$ with $\mathrm{Alt}$ traceless.

## 6. $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)$ on the coordinate-diagonal locus

**The claim is true and the proof is one line.** $\mathrm{Alt}$ lands in $\Lambda^4$, so $\mathrm{Alt}(R)$ is totally antisymmetric in $(i,j,k,l)$ and vanishes unless all four indices are distinct. A coordinate-diagonal $W$ has $W_{ijkl}=0$ unless $\{i,j\}=\{k,l\}$, so it has no four-index-distinct entry, and neither does its operator square, because the composition of two operators diagonal in the basis $\{\varphi_{ij}\}$ is diagonal in that basis with entries $\lambda_{ij}^2$. So every entry of $W^2$ on four distinct indices vanishes, and $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)=0$ as an element of $\Lambda^4$, not merely on the diagonal $kk$. **C6 CONFIRMED**, and yes, $W^2$ is coordinate-diagonal when $W$ is.

**"And only there" is false, in closed form.** At $n=4$, $\Lambda^4$ is one-dimensional and $\mathrm{Alt}(R)=\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}(R\star)}{6}\star$ with $\star=\Pi^+-\Pi^-$ and $\lVert\star\rVert^2=6$, both quoted from [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md). For a Weyl tensor $W$ at $n=4$, $W$ commutes with $\star$ and $\operatorname{tr}(W^2\star)=\lVert W^+\rVert^2-\lVert W^-\rVert^2$. So **every** $n=4$ Weyl tensor with $\lVert W^+\rVert=\lVert W^-\rVert$ has $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)=0$, whether or not it is coordinate-diagonal. Take $u_1,u_2,u_3$ the standard basis of $\Lambda^+$ and $w_1,w_2,w_3$ of $\Lambda^-$, and set

$$W=\tfrac1{\sqrt{12}}\big(2u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}-u_2u_2^{\mathsf T}-u_3u_3^{\mathsf T}\big)+\tfrac1{\sqrt{12}}\big(-w_1w_1^{\mathsf T}+2w_2w_2^{\mathsf T}-w_3w_3^{\mathsf T}\big).$$

It is a unit Weyl tensor, both blocks are trace-free, and $\lVert W^+\rVert=\lVert W^-\rVert$ gives $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)=0$. It is not coordinate-diagonal: $2u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}-w_1w_1^{\mathsf T}=\tfrac12(u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}+w_1w_1^{\mathsf T})+\tfrac32(u_1u_1^{\mathsf T}-w_1w_1^{\mathsf T})$, and the second summand is $\tfrac32(\varphi_{12}\varphi_{34}^{\mathsf T}+\varphi_{34}\varphi_{12}^{\mathsf T})$, an off-diagonal pair entry that no other term cancels. **C7 REFUTED.** The correct clause is "exact wherever $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)$ vanishes, which includes the whole coordinate-diagonal locus", and the locus is a proper subset of the vanishing set.

## 7. The boxed bound

$g_k\ge0$ is automatic: $g_k=\lVert[\mathrm{ad}(v_k),W]\rVert^2$ is a squared norm, and independently $g_k=\sum(\lambda_\alpha-\lambda_\beta)^2T_{k\alpha\beta}$ is a sum of nonnegative terms since $T=c^2$. Dividing the second display by $4$ and moving $\theta\lambda_k$ across gives the boxed bound exactly, so the deduction is sound and the direction is right. **C8 CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA.**

**Where it has content.** By §2 the right side is $(W^2\#I)_{kk}+\lambda_k^2=\tfrac12\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^2R_{k\alpha}+\lambda_k^2$, which is $\ge\lambda_k^2\ge0$ at every $k$, since $T\ge0$. So at $\theta\ge0$ the bound is vacuous at every index with $\lambda_k\le0$, and the advertised gain over a bound stated at $\lambda_{\max}$ is a gain over the positive part of the spectrum only. It is strongest where $\theta\lambda_k$ is largest against its own right side, and at padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ that is the top index, where it is an equality. At $\theta<0$ the negative indices carry the content instead and the display is still correct, so no sign hypothesis is missing.

## 8. The $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ evaluation

Under the brief's normalisation, at $n=4$ the self-dual triple satisfies $[u_a,u_b]=\sqrt2\,\epsilon_{abc}u_c$: relation (A) forces it, since $\Lambda^-$ commutes with $\Lambda^+$ and $\sum_\beta\lVert[u_1,u_\beta]\rVert^2=2\kappa^2=2(n-2)=4$. So $T_{abc}=2$ on distinct $a,b,c\in\Lambda^+$ and $0$ otherwise. With $\lambda=(2,-1,-1)/\sqrt6$ on $\Lambda^+$ and $0$ on $\Lambda^-$:

| index | $(W^2\#I)_{kk}=\tfrac12\sum_{cd}T_{kcd}\lambda_c^2$ | $\lambda_k^2$ | right side | $\theta\lambda_k$ | $g_k$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $u_1$ | $\lambda_2^2+\lambda_3^2=\tfrac13$ | $\tfrac23$ | $1$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $u_2$, $u_3$ | $\lambda_1^2+\lambda_3^2=\tfrac56$ | $\tfrac16$ | $1$ | $-\tfrac12$ | $6$ |
| $\Lambda^-$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |

$\theta$ itself comes out of the same table: $(W^\#)_{11}=2\lambda_2\lambda_3=\tfrac13$ and criticality gives $\lambda_1^2+(W^\#)_{11}=1=\theta\lambda_1$, so $\theta=\sqrt6/2$, as [L198](../findings/bounds.md#L266) records. So at $n=4$ the block's sentence is exactly right, the count is $3\cdot1+3\cdot0=3=(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2$, and $\theta\lambda_{\max}\le1$ is recovered with equality and with $g_{\max}=0$.

**At every padded $n\ge5$ the sentence is false, and the count is the thing that shows it.** [L198](../findings/bounds.md#L266)'s own block says the equality holds "at every $n\ge4$", so "padded" ranges over $n\ge5$, where $\sum_k$ of the right side must be $(n-1)\lVert W\rVert^2=n-1$ and the block's six listed values sum to $3$. The missing mass sits on the mixed indices. At $n=5$, take $k=\varphi_{15}$. Then $[\varphi_{15},u_c]$ is orthogonal to $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$ for every $c$, so $c_{kcd}=0$ whenever both $c$ and $d$ carry nonzero eigenvalue, giving $(W^\#)_{kk}=0$ and confirming that padding preserves criticality on the diagonal. But $R_{k c}=\lVert[\varphi_{15},u_c]\rVert^2=\tfrac12$ for each $c=1,2,3$, since $[\varphi_{15},\varphi_{12}]=\varphi_{25}$ and the other half of $u_1$ commutes with $\varphi_{15}$. Hence

$$(W^2\#I)_{\varphi_{15}\varphi_{15}}=\tfrac12\sum_c\lambda_c^2R_{kc}=\tfrac14\lVert W\rVert^2=\tfrac14,$$

so the right side is $\tfrac14$ at each of the four indices $\varphi_{i5}$, and the count closes: $3\cdot1+3\cdot0+4\cdot\tfrac14=4=n-1$. **C9 REFUTED AS STATED.** What survives, and it is the substantive half, is that the top index still reads $\theta\lambda_{\max}=1=$ right side at every $n$, so L198's equality case is genuinely recovered. The repair is to say "at $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ on $\mathbb R^4$", or to add the padded indices with their $\tfrac14$.

## 13. The open question is closed, and L199 refines L198

The block ends by calling $(\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\wedge\mathrm{id})_{kk}+3\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)_{kk}\le1$ open at unit norm, and concludes that L198 and L199 are independent. **Both statements are wrong, and the proof is two lines from facts L198's own proof spends.** By L131 the left side is $(W^2\#I)_{kk}+\lambda_k^2=\tfrac12\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^2R_{k\alpha}+\lambda_k^2$. $T$ is hollow, so $R_{kk}=0$, and the pair cap of [L31](../findings/stratum-two-negative.md) gives $R_{k\alpha}=\lVert[v_k,v_\alpha]\rVert^2\le2$. Hence

$$(\mathrm{Ric}(W^2)\wedge\mathrm{id})_{kk}+3\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)_{kk}\ \le\ \sum_{\alpha\ne k}\lambda_\alpha^2+\lambda_k^2\ =\ \lVert W\rVert^2\ =\ 1 .$$

So the boxed bound implies $\theta\lambda_k\le1$ at **every** index, which is L198's conclusion at $k=\arg\max$, and L199 is a strict refinement rather than an independent statement. **C13 REFUTED**, and the correction strengthens the lemma: the note should read that L199 refines L198 index by index, the loss in L198 being the two steps that replace $\tfrac12\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^2R_{k\alpha}$ by its cap.

**Equality is characterised by the same two lines**, and it explains §8's table. Equality needs $R_{k\alpha}=2$ for every $\alpha\ne k$ carrying $\lambda_\alpha\ne0$.
