## G1, the count is right, the deduction is not, and the frame needs an argument the note does not give

**Reproduction, reported as a screen and not as evidence for a positive claim.** I implemented $B(i,j)=\{k:\{i,j,k\}\notin Z\}$ and the inclusion $\bigcup_{m\in B(i,j)}B(m,k)\subseteq\big(\bigcup_{m\in B(j,k)}B(i,m)\big)\cup\big(\bigcup_{m\in B(i,k)}B(j,m)\big)$ from the brief's text, before reading the session's script, and got $121$ of $210$, with the split symmetric pair passing. The two auxiliary numbers [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) reports also reproduce: none of the $121$ has every $B(i,j)$ a singleton, and $21$ have some $B(i,j)$ empty. Seconds, on objects constructed for the purpose.

**The deduction fails, and it fails twice over.** Jacobi gives the *subspace* inclusion $[[E_i,E_j],E_k]\subseteq[E_i,[E_j,E_k]]+[E_j,[E_i,E_k]]$. The index inclusion is a statement about the *upper bounds* on the supports of the two sides, and passing from one to the other needs the left side to attain its upper bound. It need not. Write $p$ for an index in the left union and not in the right; the $p$-component of the left side is $\sum_{m}P_p\big[P_m[x,y],\,w\big]$ over $x\in E_i$, $y\in E_j$, $w\in E_k$. Membership $p\in\bigcup_{m\in B(i,j)}B(m,k)$ says only that *some* $m$ has $P_m[E_i,E_j]\ne0$ and *some* pair in $E_m\times E_k$ has a nonzero $E_p$-component. **Neither of the two steps that would be needed follows**: the particular element $P_m[x,y]$ may lie in the kernel of $P_p[\cdot,E_k]$, and even if no single term vanishes the sum over $m$ may. A composite of two nonzero bilinear maps is routinely zero, and eigenspace decompositions of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ are exactly where that happens.

**Consequence, stated as the correction.** The index inclusion is a *genericity* filter, not a necessary condition, so [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md)'s "the index inclusion is a **necessary** condition, so $121$ is an *upper bound* on the admissible supports" is unproved in that direction: a realisable exact vanishing set can fail the inclusion through cancellation, and the true admissible count is not known to be $\le121$. The honest reading is that $121$ counts the supports consistent with Jacobi *at the level of index supports in general position*. The test is also strictly weaker than Jacobi in the other direction, since it reads no quantitative relation. **It is incomparable with Jacobi, not implied by it.**

**Does total symmetry interact correctly? Yes.** $T_{abc}=c_{abc}^2$ with $c_{abc}$ totally antisymmetric ([conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md)), so a type is a multiset and $B(i,j)=B(j,i)$ automatically; the test never needs a reading-dependent rule, which is exactly the error the $\mathbb Z_3$ half of [DERIVE-sp3b](DERIVE-sp3b.md) made.

**Is "exact vanishing set" the right frame? It is, and here is the argument the note owes.** [DERIVE-sp3](DERIVE-sp3.md)'s vertex has *at least* four zeros, so the true vanishing set $S$ may have five or more, and $S$ is then not in the $210$; the enumeration covers such a configuration only if some four-subset of $S$ survives the test, which is not automatic. I screened every $Z$ with $\lvert Z\rvert\ge5$ that passes the test as an exact vanishing set: **exactly three of them have no passing four-subset**, namely $\{112,113,122,123,133\}$, $\{112,122,123,223,233\}$ and $\{113,123,133,223,233\}$, the three patterns "all types meeting $i$ except $\{i,i,i\}$ vanish", which say $[E_i,E_j]=0$ for $j\ne i$ and $[E_i,E_i]\subseteq E_i$, i.e. $E_i$ is a direct-summand ideal. **$\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is simple at every $n\ge5$**, so no such configuration is realisable, and the four-subset frame loses nothing. *[DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) noticed the ideal pattern (it is why the first negative control was worthless), without noticing that it is precisely the frame's only escape.*

**The caveat that matters most for what the list is used for.** The Jacobi cut is a *realisability* argument, and realisability does not transfer to $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$. Three statements, in order: $\theta(W)\le\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ by [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md); $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$ is attained at a vertex of $\Pi(\lambda)$; that vertex need not be realisable. Removing the non-realisable vertices does not lower $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$, and $W$'s own averaged $T$ is a point of $\Pi(\lambda)$ that need not be a vertex at all, so **the maximum of $\theta$ over the surviving $110$ supports is not an upper bound for $\theta$ at a $d=3$ critical point**. What it is: a classification of candidate partial gradings, which is what [I15](../plan.md) wants. If the plan's next step is "the surviving supports evaluated at the $n=12$ witness spectrum", the number that comes out bounds nothing until either $W$'s $T$ is shown to sit on a named face or the realisable set is shown to be a union of faces. *The nine-identity cut of G4 is exempt from this and is the one that does narrow $\theta^\ast$; see below.*

## G2, the retraction is right, and it stops in the right place

**The core claim, re-derived from the definitions rather than checked.** Take three pairwise orthogonal eigenspaces (eigenspaces of a symmetric $W$ with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal) carrying a $\mathbb Z_m$-grading, so $[\mathfrak g_a,\mathfrak g_b]\subseteq\mathfrak g_{a+b}$. Because the decomposition is orthogonal for a positive-definite form, $\langle[\mathfrak g_a,\mathfrak g_b],\mathfrak g_c\rangle\ne0$ forces $c\equiv a+b$. Because $\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle$ is totally antisymmetric, the same triple read as $(b,c,a)$ forces $a\equiv b+c$, and the two together give $2b\equiv0$; reading it as $(c,a,b)$ gives $2a\equiv0$. **So a nonvanishing type at an orthogonal real grading has $2a\equiv2b\equiv2c\equiv0\pmod m$**, which is the retraction's condition, obtained without appeal to any automorphism. At $m=3$ only $a=0$ survives, so every type meeting $\mathfrak g_1$ or $\mathfrak g_2$ vanishes; then $[\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_1]=[\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_1]=[\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2]=0$ and $\mathfrak g_1\oplus\mathfrak g_2$ would be central, impossible in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$. **$\mathfrak{so}(n)$ admits no orthogonal $\mathbb Z_3$-grading with three nonzero parts, so the four-zero vertex claimed for it does not exist.** The retraction is correct, and it is correct for a reason stronger than the one it gives, it needs no order-$3$ automorphism at all.

**One precision on the two conditions, which the retraction runs together.** "$a+b+c\equiv0$" is the condition when the invariant form pairs $\mathfrak g_a$ with $\mathfrak g_{-a}$, the complexified picture, where the Killing form is nondegenerate on $\mathfrak g_a\times\mathfrak g_{-a}$. "$c\equiv a+b$" is the condition in the record's own setting, where the form is positive-definite and pairs $\mathfrak g_a$ with itself. The two coincide precisely under $2a\equiv0$, which is the retraction's own conclusion. Stating both in one breath is harmless here but is the kind of thing that has cost this record a cycle; the setting the $t_{ijk}$ live in is the second.

**It has not over-retracted, and one thing should be added back as a positive statement.** The criterion $2a\equiv0$ says the real orthogonal gradings of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ are exactly those by elementary abelian $2$-groups, so **the $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ alternative named in [DERIVE-sp3](DERIVE-sp3.md) survives untouched**, the retraction kills only the $\mathbb Z_3$ half, as it says. Working the $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ case through: with $E_1=\mathfrak g_{00}$, $E_2=\mathfrak g_{01}$, $E_3=\mathfrak g_{10}\oplus\mathfrak g_{11}$ one gets zeros $\{113,123,223,333\}$ (the split symmetric pair again, because $E_1\oplus E_2$ is then the even part) and with the eigenvalue split *aligned* to the grading one gets the six zeros $\{112,113,123,222,223,333\}$, a degenerate vertex. *That degenerate case is the $d=3$ analogue of the balanced product's own degeneracy at $d=2$, where both facets bind at once, and it is where I would look for the optimum.* I screened it against G1's test: $10$ of its $15$ four-subsets pass, so it is visible to the enumeration.

**What the withdrawn triples actually were, verified.** With an order-$3$ $g\in SO(n)$ of eigenvalue multiplicities $(p;q,q)$, $n=p+2q$, the centraliser algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak u(q)$ of real dimension $\binom p2+q^2$. At $n=12$: $q=1,\dots,6$ gives $46,32,24,22,26,36$, and $66$ minus each, halved, gives $10,17,21,22,20,15$, **all six triples of [DERIVE-sp3b](DERIVE-sp3b.md), on the nose.** So the numbers are right and their reading was wrong: the second and third entries are complex dimensions of conjugate eigenspaces, exactly as the retraction says. **And the real decomposition they do describe is $(\dim\mathfrak g_0,\dim\mathfrak m)$, two classes, e.g. $(46,20)$, which is $d=2$, where [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) already gives $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$.** Nothing is lost by the withdrawal, and the withdrawal of the $(30,20,16)$ exclusion is right with it.

**The split symmetric pair, checked independently.** With $E_3=\mathfrak m$ and $E_1\oplus E_2=\mathfrak h$, a type vanishes exactly when it contains an odd number of $3$s: $113$, $123$, $223$, $333$. **Exactly four**, and the other six, $111,112,122,222$ inside $\mathfrak h$, and $133,233$ from $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak m$, are unconstrained by the grading. Confirmed.

**One assertion in that sentence is not established: "a genuine vertex."** A vertex needs the four active constraints to be linearly independent on the $4$-dimensional feasible affine set, i.e. the four coordinate functionals $t_{113},t_{123},t_{223},t_{333}$ must be independent there, which is a rank condition on the reduced system at the given $(\nu,m)$ and is not supplied. Passing the Jacobi test says nothing about it. *Downgrade "a genuine vertex" to "a Jacobi-consistent four-zero support" until the rank is checked.*

## G3, confirmed, with its two hypotheses named

**The identity, re-derived rather than checked.** Applying multipliers $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ to $(\mathrm A_i,\mathrm B_i,\mathrm C_i)$ of [Lemma L104](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) gives every mixed type $\{i,j,k\}$ the coefficient $2\mu_j\mu_k\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)$ and every diagonal type $\{i,j,j\}$ the coefficient $\mu_j(\mu_j-1)\varphi(\nu_j)$, where $\varphi(x)=\gamma x^2+\beta x+\alpha$ and $\psi(x,y)=\gamma xy+\tfrac\beta2(x+y)+\alpha$ is its polarisation, the same structure [Lemma L105](../findings/bounds-relaxations.md) uses at $d=2$. For $\varphi(x)=(x-\nu_1)(x-\nu_2)$ one has $\psi(x,y)=\tfrac12[(x-\nu_1)(y-\nu_2)+(x-\nu_2)(y-\nu_1)]$, and row $1$ gives coefficients $-\mu_1\mu_2(\nu_1-\nu_2)^2$, $-\mu_1\mu_3(\nu_1-\nu_2)(\nu_1-\nu_3)$, $+\mu_2\mu_3(\nu_1-\nu_2)(\nu_2-\nu_3)$, $+\mu_3(\mu_3-1)(\nu_1-\nu_3)(\nu_2-\nu_3)$ on $t_{112},t_{113},t_{123},t_{133}$, with right-hand side $2(n-2)\nu_1\nu_2+2\nu_1(\nu_1+\nu_2)+2\nu_1\theta-2\nu_1^2=2\nu_1[(n-1)\nu_2+\theta]$. **Every coefficient and the right-hand side match [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) exactly**; a symbolic screen of the same computation agrees.

**The sign argument is valid.** At $\nu_1>\nu_2>0>\nu_3$ and $\theta>0$ the two negative coefficients multiply $t_{112},t_{113}\ge0$, so moving them across leaves a sum of two nonnegative terms equal to a strictly positive quantity, and $t_{123}=t_{133}=0$ is impossible. **Two conditions ride on it and should be carried with the cut**: $\mu_3\ge2$, which holds under the standing hypothesis that every multiplicity is at least $3$; and $\nu_2>0$, without which the right-hand side can vanish or reverse, **at a two-negative-eigenvalue spectrum the cut is void**, and the $112$ and $110$ counts are conditional on the sign pattern. [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) does say "the honest list at $\nu_2>0$, $\theta>0$", so this is a matter of carrying the hypothesis into the RESULT line, where it is dropped.

## G4, the mechanism is right, the accounting sentence is false, and the working hypotheses are the wrong ones

**"Exactly four survive" is right in all nine cases, and here is why.** Row $i$ carries the six types $\{i\}\cup\{j,k\}$. The multiplier $\varphi(x)=(x-\nu_a)(x-\nu_b)$ kills a coefficient only through $\varphi(\nu_j)$ at a diagonal type or $\psi(\nu_j,\nu_k)$ at a mixed one. It kills exactly the two diagonal types $\{i,a,a\}$ and $\{i,b,b\}$; the third diagonal type $\{i,c,c\}$ has coefficient $\mu_c(\mu_c-1)(\nu_c-\nu_a)(\nu_c-\nu_b)\ne0$, and the three mixed types have $\psi(\nu_1,\nu_2)=-\tfrac12(\nu_1-\nu_2)^2$-type values, each a product of two nonzero differences. **Four survive, never three and never five**, provided every multiplicity is at least $3$ so that no admissible type has $\mu_c(\mu_c-1)=0$, at a spectrum with a multiplicity $2$ the type count itself changes and the $\binom{10}4$ frame goes with it. I re-derived all nine rows symbolically: the nine four-subsets and all nine right-hand sides match [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md)'s table exactly, six of the form $2\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_j+\theta]$ and three of the form $2[(n-2)\nu_a\nu_b-\nu_i^2+\nu_i(\nu_a+\nu_b+\theta)]$.

**The forbidding argument is valid, and it is the most valuable thing in these four commits.** If the four surviving types vanish, the left side of the identity is identically zero because the other two coefficients were annihilated, so the right side must vanish. **This uses only the equalities of $\Pi(\lambda)$ and the named zeros, no realisability, no genericity, no sign of any coefficient.** It therefore genuinely removes vertices from $\Pi(\lambda)$ and genuinely narrows $\theta^\ast(\lambda)$, which the Jacobi cut does not (G1 above). *That distinction is worth recording: the two cuts intersected into "$110$" have different logical statuses, and only one of them can be used in a bound.*

**The accounting sentence is false.** "$3$ of the nine were already excluded by Jacobi, so the count falls from $121$ to $118$", screened: **six** of the nine are Jacobi-excluded and **three** survive, so $121-3=118$ is right and the reason given for it is the number's complement. The final $110$ is also right: the $123$-and-$133$ cut removes $9$ of the $121$, exactly one of which ($\{123,133,233,333\}$) is among the three, so $121-(3+9-1)=110$. **The numbers stand; the sentence does not.**

**And the hypothesis under which the cut is claimed does not cover the cut that is actually made.** The three subsets that survive Jacobi (the only three the identities remove from the $121$), are exactly $\{111,112,113,123\}$, $\{122,123,222,223\}$ and $\{123,133,233,333\}$: **the three with quadratic right-hand sides.** All six of the "$\nu_i[(n-1)\nu_j+\theta]$" identities, whose degeneracy [DERIVE-sp3c](DERIVE-sp3c.md) analyses so carefully as $\Pi$-saturation, remove nothing that Jacobi had not already removed. The quadratic right-hand sides vanish on a locus that is **not** $\Pi$-saturation: for row $1$ against $(x-\nu_2)(x-\nu_3)$ it is $\theta=\nu_1-(\nu_2+\nu_3)-(n-2)\nu_2\nu_3/\nu_1$, and at the $n=12$ witness spectrum of [DERIVE-t11](DERIVE-t11.md), values $(1.2m,0.375m,-m)$ with $m=0.127984$, that is $\theta=0.6335$, positive and comfortably inside the $\Pi$-bound $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_3\rvert=1.4078$. **So "at a kernel-free three-eigenvalue critical point with $\theta>0$ and the $\Pi$-bound strict, the nine four-subsets above are not vertex supports" is not established**: the three that matter need three further nonvanishing conditions, one per identity, none of which is $\Pi$-saturation.

**A second gap in the same RESULT line, smaller.** Among the six simple right-hand sides, those with $\nu_j=\nu_2$ degenerate at $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_2\rvert$ when $\nu_2<0$, which is *strictly inside* the $\Pi$-bound $\theta\le(n-1)\lvert\nu_{\min}\rvert$ ([Lemma L107](../findings/bounds.md)) and so is not excluded by "the $\Pi$-bound strict" either. Under the sign pattern $\nu_2>0$ that G3 assumes this is void, which is one more reason to carry that hypothesis explicitly.

**Corrected statement, which I would record in place of the RESULT line.** *At a kernel-free three-eigenvalue spectrum with every multiplicity at least $3$, each of the nine four-subsets is excluded whenever its own right-hand side is nonzero; the six simple ones are automatic at $\theta>0$ when the corresponding $\nu_j$ is positive and otherwise degenerate exactly at $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\nu_j\rvert$; the three quadratic ones are excluded off an explicit codimension-one locus in $(\lambda,\theta)$ named above.* The bookkeeping $210\to121\to110$ then reads: $210\to201$ by the identities alone, which is the part that bounds $\theta^\ast$, and $\to110$ after the genericity filter, which classifies rather than bounds.

## G5, the mechanism is right, the counting argument is not, and its companion claim is false at the witness

**The action, re-derived.** $E_{jk}=e_j\otimes e_k-e_k\otimes e_j$ sends $e_k\mapsto e_j$, $e_j\mapsto-e_k$, and kills every other $e_i$; extending as a derivation to $\Lambda^2$ gives $\rho(E_{jk})e_{ki}=e_{ji}$ and $\rho(E_{jk})e_{ji}=-e_{ki}$ for $i\notin\{j,k\}$, while $e_{jk}\mapsto-e_k\wedge e_k+e_j\wedge e_j=0$ and $e_{il}\mapsto0$ for $i,l\notin\{j,k\}$. **Confirmed exactly as stated.** With $M$ diagonal, $[\rho(E_{jk}),M]e_{ki}=(M_{ki}-M_{ji})e_{ji}$ and $[\rho(E_{jk}),M]e_{ji}=(M_{ki}-M_{ji})e_{ki}$, so the orbit tangent is $\sum_i(M_{ki}-M_{ji})f_i$ in the pair-block basis $f_i=e_{ji}\odot e_{ki}$. **Confirmed.**

**The conclusion is right but the counting is not the reason.** $A\mapsto[\rho(A),M]$ is linear and sends each basis element $E_{jk}$ into the block $\{j,k\}$, so its whole image lies in $\bigoplus_{\{j,k\}}V_{jk}$, **immediately, with no count and no independence claim.** Hence no orbit direction lies in a $4$-set block or in $\mathcal D_n$, and the nine $\tfrac12$-eigenvectors [Lemma L121](../findings/second-order.md) puts in the $(2,2)$ $4$-set blocks at $S^3\times S^3$ are genuinely off-orbit. *The claim survives; the argument offered for it should be replaced by this one, which is two lines and unconditional.*

**"One orbit direction per block" is false, and the record already contains the refutation.** The block-$\{j,k\}$ orbit direction vanishes identically when $M_{ki}=M_{ji}$ for every $i$. Since the map is block-diagonal, its kernel (the isotropy algebra of $M$ in $\mathfrak{so}(n)$), is spanned by exactly those coordinate rotations $E_{jk}$, and $\dim(\text{orbit})=\#\{\text{blocks with }X_{\mathrm{orb}}\ne0\}$, which is $\binom n2$ only at a diagonal point with finite isotropy. **At $S^3\times S^3$ the isotropy contains $\mathfrak{so}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)$, so $6$ of the $15$ pair blocks (the $3+3$ within-factor ones), carry no orbit direction and the orbit is $9$-dimensional**, which is the very number [second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md) records ("$\dim E(\tfrac12)=18$ against a $9$-dimensional orbit") and which $\binom62=15$ contradicts. So the note's sentence "there are $\binom n2$ pair blocks and $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n)=\binom n2$ orbit directions, one per block" conflates $\dim\mathfrak{so}(n)$ with the orbit dimension and contradicts an indexed statement of the record.

**What that costs downstream.** "Each pair block contains exactly one orbit direction, so the criterion on that block is $L_e\le\tfrac12$ on an explicit $(n-4)$-dimensional complement" must become: *at most one, and at a block where the orbit direction vanishes the criterion applies to the full $(n-3)$-dimensional block.* At symmetric points (the ones the route is aimed at) that is not a corner case but the majority behaviour for within-factor blocks. **The consolation is a corollary worth keeping**: the isotropy algebra of a diagonal critical point is always spanned by coordinate rotations $E_{jk}$, and the within-factor blocks at $S^p\times S^q$ are exactly the ones where the pair-block criterion has no orbit direction to discount, which is where the predicted $(p-2)/(2(p-1))$ is supposed to live.
