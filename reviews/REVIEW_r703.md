## C1, CONFIRMED, by two routes, and the hypothesis list is shorter than the batch says

**The identity the batch reads off is the right one.** The index pattern in [ATTEMPTS.md](../ATTEMPTS.md)'s $D(R)$ is fixed by consistency at $R=\mathrm{Id}$, and it fixes the sign too. Writing $T_R(k,i,l,j)=R_{kilj}$ and using $R_{kilj}=R_{ikjl}$ (two pair swaps, two sign flips), the subtracted term is $\sum_{k,l}\mathrm{Ric}(R)_{kl}R_{ikjl}=\mathring R(\mathrm{Ric}R)_{ij}$ in the batch's convention $\mathring R(s)_{ik}=\sum_{jl}R_{ijkl}s_{jl}$. Check at $R=\mathrm{Id}$, where $\mathrm{Alt}(\mathrm{Id})=0$ forces $D=0$: $\mathrm{Ric}(\mathrm{Id}^2+\mathrm{Id}^\#)=\mathrm{Ric}((n-1)\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)^2\mathrm{id}$ and $\mathring{\mathrm{Id}}(\mathrm{Ric}\,\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)\mathring{\mathrm{Id}}(\mathrm{id})=(n-1)^2\mathrm{id}$. They cancel. The transposed pattern $R_{kijl}$ would leave $D(\mathrm{Id})=2(n-1)^2\mathrm{id}\ne0$ and is therefore excluded. **So the batch's reading of the $\tfrac32$ and of the index pattern is correct.**

**Route 1, subtraction, as the batch does it.** $\mathrm{Ric}(R^2+R^\#)=\mathring R(\mathrm{Ric}R)+\tfrac32G(R)$ with $G(R)_{ij}=\sum_{klm}\mathrm{Alt}(R)_{iklm}\mathrm{Alt}(R)_{jklm}$, read at $R=\lvert W\rvert$ and $R=W$. At $R=W$ both terms die, by $\mathrm{Ric}(W)=0$ and $\mathrm{Alt}(W)=0$. With $\lvert W\rvert^2=W_+^2+W_-^2=W^2$ and $\lvert W\rvert^\#-W^\#=4W_+\#W_-=4C$, and $\mathrm{Alt}(\lvert W\rvert)=\mathrm{Alt}(W)+2\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)=2\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)$ so that $G(\lvert W\rvert)=4\mathcal A$, the difference is $4\mathrm{Ric}(C)=\mathring{\lvert W\rvert}(h)+6\mathcal A$. The coefficient $6=\tfrac32\cdot4$ is forced.

**Route 2, polarisation, which the batch does not run and which is an independent check on the $6$.** Polarising $D$ gives $\mathrm{Ric}(AB+BA+2A\#B)=\mathring A(\mathrm{Ric}B)+\mathring B(\mathrm{Ric}A)+3G^s(A,B)$. At $A=W_+$, $B=W_-$: the products vanish, $\mathrm{Ric}(W_\pm)=g$, and $\mathrm{Alt}(W_+)=\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)$ (again from $\mathrm{Alt}(W)=0$) makes $G^s(W_+,W_-)=\mathcal A$. So $2\mathrm{Ric}(C)=\mathring{\lvert W\rvert}(g)+3\mathcal A$, which is C1 after doubling and $h=2g$. **The two routes agree, so the $6$ is not a fitted constant.**

**Correction to the hypothesis list.** The batch says "for every Weyl $W$ (no criticality)". Criticality is indeed unused, but Bianchi is used twice and should be stated: $\mathrm{Alt}(W)=0$ is what kills $G(W)$ *and* what turns $\mathrm{Alt}(\lvert W\rvert)$ into $2\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)$. For a merely Ricci-flat symmetric $W$ the statement is false as written.

**Citation slip.** The $D(R)$ identity is **Result 2** of the "Lifting Jäger's §3.3 in trace form" section of [ATTEMPTS.md](../ATTEMPTS.md), at the line beginning $D(R)_{ij}:=$. Result 4 of that section is the confidence paragraph's "load-bearing unproven statement", i.e. the $J_5$ inequality itself. Batch 4 and the brief both cite Result 4. Low severity, but it points a reader at the conjecture instead of at the theorem it rests on.

**A convention hazard that is worth a line in the record.** Batch 4's $\mathring R(s)_{ik}=\sum_{jl}R_{ijkl}s_{jl}$ is the $ikjl$ (Bourguignon–Karcher) order. [findings/conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) declares the record's order to be Li's $iklj$, and states that the two differ by a sign. Batch 4 is internally consistent, it anchors on $\mathring R(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{Ric}(R)$, which is true in its own order and false in Li's, where $\mathring R(\mathrm{id})=-\mathrm{Ric}(R)$, but **$\langle\mathring{\lvert W\rvert}(h),h\rangle\ge0$ imported into a file written in the record's declared order becomes $\le0$.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) names this exact trap. Either state the local order in the boxed identity or convert it before it reaches [findings/](../findings/).

## C2, CONFIRMED, with one clarification about the frame

**The algebra.** $J_5=\langle\mathrm{Ric}(X_{\mathrm{inv}}),h\rangle$, $X_{\mathrm{inv}}=4C-2\theta W_--2W_-^2$, $2\mathrm{Ric}(W_-)=h$, and C1 give
$$\mathrm{Ric}(X_{\mathrm{inv}})=\mathring{\lvert W\rvert}(h)+6\mathcal A-\theta h-2\mathrm{Ric}(W_-^2),$$
and pairing with $h$ is C2 term for term. I checked it against the $g$-normalisation as well ($J_5=2\langle\mathrm{Ric}(X_{\mathrm{inv}}),g\rangle$ with $h=2g$) and the factors of two close.

**Both nonnegativity claims hold.** $\langle\mathring{\lvert W\rvert}(h),h\rangle\ge0$ is C3's lemma applied to $\lvert W\rvert\succeq0$ and $h=2\mathrm{Ric}(W_-)\succeq0$. $\langle\mathcal A,h\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal Ah)\ge0$ because both factors are PSD, and $\mathcal A$ **is** a genuine Gram matrix: $\mathcal A_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle$ with $v_i=(\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)_{iklm})_{klm}\in\mathbb R^{n^3}$, the free index sitting in the same slot of both factors and all three remaining indices contracted. The contraction really does make it one.

**The $h$-frame reading, checked.** In an $h$-eigenframe, $\langle\mathring R(h),h\rangle=\sum_a\eta_a\mathring R(h)_{aa}=\sum_{a,b}\eta_a\eta_bR_{abab}$, and $w_{aa}=0$ gives the batch's $\sum_{a\ne b}$. $w_{ab}=\lvert W\rvert_{abab}\ge0$ by $\lvert W\rvert\succeq0$, and $\mathbf w$ is symmetric. So $\eta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf w\eta=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf w^3\mathbf 1$ and $\lVert h\rVert^2=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf w^2\mathbf 1$, as stated.

**The clarification the brief asks for.** $h_{aa}=\mathrm{Ric}(\lvert W\rvert)_{aa}=\sum_b\lvert W\rvert_{abab}=(\mathbf w\mathbf 1)_a$ holds **in every orthonormal frame**; it is the definition of $\mathrm{Ric}$ read on the diagonal. What the eigenframe buys is two separate things: that $\eta$ is $h$'s *spectrum* rather than its diagonal, and the disappearance of the off-diagonal contributions $\sum_{a\ne b,c\ne d}\lvert W\rvert_{acbd}h_{cd}h_{ab}$ from the quadratic form. Batch 4's sentence bundles these; they should be separated, because the first is frame-free and the second is not.

**What is new beyond the sign.** The sweep's batch 2 already had $J_5=4[\langle\mathring W_+g,g\rangle-\theta\lVert g\rVert^2+\langle\mathrm{Ric}(W_-^\#),g\rangle]+(\text{Alt terms})$, the same shape, with $-\theta\lVert h\rVert^2$ identical after $h=2g$. Substituting the $D$ identity at $R=W_-$ turns the sweep's form into C2 exactly, and the "(Alt terms)" it left unnamed are precisely the $+6\langle\mathcal A,h\rangle$ that the two dropped $\tfrac32\mathcal A$'s reassemble into. **So the genuinely new content is: the defect term named, given its coefficient, and signed; $\mathring W_+$ replaced by $\mathring{\lvert W\rvert}$; and the $\mathbf w$-matrix reading.** The claim that this is a new decomposition, rather than the sweep's decomposition completed, would overstate it.

**One overreach in item (1)'s advertising sentence.** *"The sectional part of the grant is a statement about one nonnegative $n\times n$ matrix and the scalar $\theta$"* is not right even for the reduced condition the batch derives from it: that condition carries $\lambda_{\max}(W_-)$, which is not a function of $\mathbf w$. And the two remaining terms of $J_5$ ($\mathrm{Ric}(W_-^2)$ and $\mathcal A$), are not functions of $\mathbf w$ either. Nor is every hollow nonnegative symmetric $\mathbf w$ realisable by a critical Weyl $W$, so the reduction cannot be attacked as a free matrix problem without dropping the constraint that carries the content.

## C3, CONFIRMED as mathematics; the error is real, the corollary is overstated

**The identity.** For $R=\omega\otimes\omega$ the 4-tensor is $R_{ijkl}=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$, so $\mathring R(g)_{ik}=\sum_{jl}\omega_{ij}g_{jl}\omega_{kl}=(\omega g\omega^{\mathsf T})_{ik}$ and $\langle\mathring Rg,g\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\omega g\omega^{\mathsf T}g)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(MM^{\mathsf T})=\lVert M\rVert_F^2$ with $M=g^{1/2}\omega g^{1/2}$, using $(g^{1/2}\omega g^{1/2})^{\mathsf T}=g^{1/2}\omega^{\mathsf T}g^{1/2}$. Since $\omega^{\mathsf T}=-\omega$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\omega g)^2)=-\lVert M\rVert_F^2\le0$. **The quantity the sweep calls indefinite has a definite sign for every $g\succeq0$ and every skew $\omega$.** Screened on three random $(\omega,g)$ pairs at $n=6$: the two sides agree to all printed digits. *Screen, on objects constructed for the screen; the proof above is the evidence.*

**It is not a convention mismatch.** The anchor $\mathring{\mathrm{Id}}(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{Ric}(\mathrm{Id})=(n-1)\mathrm{id}$ holds in batch 4's order: $\sum_a\omega_a\omega_a^{\mathsf T}$ over $\omega_a=E_{ij}-E_{ji}$, $i<j$, equals $\sum_{i<j}(E_{ii}+E_{jj})=(n-1)\mathrm{id}$. And the sign question is moot for the verdict: whichever order one adopts, $\sum_a\lambda_a\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\omega_ag)^2)$ with $\lambda_a\ge0$ and $g\succeq0$ is *signed*, one way or the other. **"Indefinite for every $a$" is wrong in both orders.** The sweep's error is that it applied $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(N^2)=\lVert N_{\mathrm{sym}}\rVert^2-\lVert N_{\mathrm{skew}}\rVert^2$ as though the two halves were free, when $N=\omega g$ with $\omega$ skew and $g$ symmetric ties them: $N_{\mathrm{sym}}=\tfrac12(\omega g-g\omega)$, $N_{\mathrm{skew}}=\tfrac12(\omega g+g\omega)$, and the difference of their squared norms is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\omega g)^2)\le0$ whenever $g\succeq0$. **The sweep's own check confirms this rather than the claim it was offered for**: at $g=E_{11}$ the term is $0$ because $M=E_{11}\omega E_{11}=\omega_{11}E_{11}=0$, degeneracy at a rank-one $g$, not a change of sign.

**Where C3 overstates.** *"The route the sweep closed on that ground is open"*, the sweep closed it on two grounds, and the second one is untouched: *"one more rearrangement of the grant through $W$'s spectral parts, which is the class [post F56](../findings/second-order.md) closes"*. Batch 4's own item (3) then confirms that second ground on its own terms: the sectional part does not carry the grant off the diagonal locus. So the accurate statement is **the sweep's stated reason was wrong and the rewrite does yield a signed, quantified decomposition, but it does not by itself reopen the grant**, which is what "open" will be read as. The correction to [plan.md](../plan.md)'s I4(a) is owed either way: "F56-class, dropped" repeats a false reason.

## C4, sufficiency and sphere-product exactness CONFIRMED and now proved; the closing clause REFUTED

**Sufficiency, checked.** In the $h$-eigenframe, $\mathrm{Ric}(W_-^2)_{aa}=\sum_b\lVert W_-(e_a\wedge e_b)\rVert^2$, and $\lVert W_-\xi\rVert^2\le\lambda_{\max}(W_-)\langle W_-\xi,\xi\rangle$ for $W_-\succeq0$ gives $\mathrm{Ric}(W_-^2)_{aa}\le\tfrac12\lambda_{\max}(W_-)\eta_a$, hence $2\langle\mathrm{Ric}(W_-^2),h\rangle\le\lambda_{\max}(W_-)\lVert\eta\rVert^2$, using $\eta_a\ge0$. Dropping $6\langle\mathcal A,h\rangle\ge0$ then gives the stated condition. **Two remarks.** The condition is sufficient *unconditionally*, not only when $\mathcal A=0$, dropping a nonnegative term costs nothing at any point, so the qualifier in C4 understates its own result. And the derivation is scale-consistent: both sides are homogeneous of degree three under $W\mapsto tW$, since $\theta\mapsto t\theta$.

**Exactness at every sphere product, proved by hand, no computation.** Take $r697$'s block-scalar $W=(a,b,c)$ on $\mathfrak{so}(p)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(q)\oplus M$ with $c<0$, so $W_-=\lvert c\rvert\Pi_M$, $\lambda_{\max}(W_-)=\lvert c\rvert$, $\lvert W\rvert=a\Pi_p+b\Pi_q+\lvert c\rvert\Pi_M$, and $a(p-1)=\lvert c\rvert q$, $b(q-1)=\lvert c\rvert p$, $\theta=\lvert c\rvert(n-1)$. In the product frame the sectional matrix is $w_{\alpha\beta}=a$ inside the first block, $b$ inside the second, $\lvert c\rvert$ across. Then

- $\eta_\alpha=a(p-1)+\lvert c\rvert q=2\lvert c\rvert q$ for $\alpha\le p$, and $\eta_\alpha=b(q-1)+\lvert c\rvert p=2\lvert c\rvert p$ for $\alpha>p$, so $h$ is scalar exactly when $p=q$, and the unbalanced products are genuine non-scalar-$h$ territory;
- $\lVert\eta\rVert^2=p(2\lvert c\rvert q)^2+q(2\lvert c\rvert p)^2=4c^2pqn$;
- $(\mathbf w\eta)_\alpha=\lvert c\rvert q\,(u+v)=2c^2qn$ for $\alpha\le p$ and $2c^2pn$ for $\alpha>p$, where $u+v=2\lvert c\rvert n$;
- $\eta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf w\eta=4\lvert c\rvert^3pqn^2$.

And $(\theta+\lambda_{\max}(W_-))\lVert\eta\rVert^2=\lvert c\rvert n\cdot4c^2pqn=4\lvert c\rvert^3pqn^2$. **Equal, at every $(p,q)$.** The mechanism is visible: $\mathcal A=0$ on the diagonal locus and $W_-$ is $\lvert c\rvert$ times a projection, so *both* discarded inequalities are tight, which is what $X_{\mathrm{inv}}\equiv0$ there demands. This upgrades batch 4's screened "$1.8=1.8$ at $S^2\times S^4$" to a family statement. Arithmetic control at $(p,q,\lvert c\rvert)=(2,4,1)$: $a=4$, $b=2/3$, $\eta=(8,8,4,4,4,4)$, $\lVert\eta\rVert^2=192$, $\eta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf w\eta=1152=(5+1)\cdot192$.

**The closing clause is refuted.** *"Any proof of $J_5\ge0$ must use $\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)$ quantitatively, and must be exact at $\mathbb{HP}^2$."*

- **$\mathbb{HP}^2$ has scalar $h$**, [ATTEMPTS.md](../ATTEMPTS.md) lists it among the points where $\mathrm{Ric}(W_-)$ is scalar and the slack is exactly $0$, and batch 2 of this very file records "$\mathbb{HP}^2$ has $h$ scalar". **So [L154](../findings/bounds.md)(ii) already proves $J_5=\lambda\mathop{\mathrm{scal}}(X_{\mathrm{inv}})\ge0$ at $\mathbb{HP}^2$**, by a purely spectral block argument in which $\mathrm{Alt}$ never appears. A proof of the grant at the point cited as forcing $\mathrm{Alt}$ to be quantitative exists on the record and is $\mathrm{Alt}$-free. The same holds at $SU(3)$ and at the balanced sphere products.
- **What the fence actually establishes** is narrower and should be stated that way: *within this decomposition, no bound that discards $6\langle\mathcal A,h\rangle$ can close the grant, because the sectional remainder $J_5^{\mathrm{sec}}$ is negative at $\mathbb{HP}^2$ and at the $n=5$ point.* That is a real fence over a class of relaxations. It says nothing about proofs that never pass through the split, and [L154](../findings/bounds.md)(ii) is one such proof, covering a locus that contains two of the three points the batch names.
- **"Must be exact at $\mathbb{HP}^2$" is true but does not follow from anything in C4.** It follows from $J_5=0$ at $\mathbb{HP}^2$, which the position file already records; every proof of a non-strict inequality is tight at its equality points. Presenting it as an output of the new fence gives it more weight than it has.
- **One check the fence still needs, and it is free.** Is $h$ scalar at the $n=5$ critical point? If it is, then *every* point the batch offers as evidence lies inside [L154](../findings/bounds.md)(ii)'s already-proven locus, and the fence rests on no point where the grant is actually open. If it is not, the $n=5$ point is the fence's sole genuine witness and should be named as such. The record does not say, and the answer is a property of an object already on disk.
