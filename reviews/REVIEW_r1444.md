## A. Θ_n is the sphere-product maximum

**The record.** [problem.md](../findings/problem.md) line 170: *"$\theta_0(n)^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-2)}{n^2}\quad(n\text{ even}),\qquad\Theta_n^2=\frac{2(n-1)(n-3)}{(n-2)(n+1)}\quad(n\text{ odd})$"*. Line 213: *"the sphere-product maximum is $\Theta_n$ (machine-checked at both parities: `sphere_theta_le_odd`, `sphere_theta_le_even` in [L16Max.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L16Max.lean), with attainment in L57Attain.lean)"*. Line 440, in the Conjecture L16 block: *"the sphere-product maximum is $\Theta_n$, and at **odd** $n$ that is strictly smaller"*. The L16 row of [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md) (line 469): *"$\theta_{p,q}^2\le\Theta_n^2$ for all integers $p,q\ge2$ with $p+q=n$, from $\theta_{p,q}^2=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\big(1-\frac{n-1}{pq}\big)$ and $pq\le(n^2-1)/4$, respectively $n^2/4$"*.

**The definitions, printed from the kernel.**
- `ThetaSqOdd n = 2(n−1)(n−3)/((n−2)(n+1))`, the record's odd $\Theta_n^2$ exactly.
- `theta0Sq n = 2(n−1)(n−2)/n²`, the record's even $\Theta_n^2=\theta_0(n)^2$ exactly.
- `thetaSplitSq p q = 2(p+q−1)(p−1)(q−1)/((p+q−2)pq)`. Since $(p-1)(q-1)=pq-(n-1)$, this is the row's $\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\big(1-\frac{n-1}{pq}\big)$, which `thetaSplitSq_pq` proves.
- By hand at the extremal splits: $p=q=m$ gives $\frac{2(n-1)(n-2)^2/4}{(n-2)n^2/4}=\theta_0(n)^2$, and $(m,m+1)$ at $n=2m+1$ gives $(p-1)(q-1)=\frac{(n-3)(n-1)}4$, $pq=\frac{n^2-1}4$, so $\frac{2(n-1)^2(n-3)}{(n-2)(n^2-1)}=\Theta_n^2$. The record's $\Theta_5^2=\tfrac89$ and $\Theta_7^2=\tfrac65$ come out of `thetaSplitSq 2 3` and `thetaSplitSq 3 4`.

**The argument.** $\theta_{p,q}^2$ is increasing in $pq$ at fixed $n$ (the coefficient $\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}$ is positive and $-\frac{n-1}{pq}$ increases), so the bound follows from $pq\le B$. `thetaSplitSq_le_of_pq` is that step for any $B\ge pq$. At odd $n$ the integer fact $4pq\le(p+q)^2-1$ holds because $p\ne q$ forces $(p-q)^2\ge1$, and `hodd` is exactly what supplies $p\ne q$. At even $n$ AM-GM gives $pq\le n^2/4$, which needs no parity. This is the record's argument.

**The range $p,q\ge2$ is right.** A factor $S^1$ is flat and $S^1\times S^{n-1}$ is conformally flat, so its Weyl tensor is zero and it has no point on the unit sphere. The formula agrees, giving $0$ at $p=1$. The hypothesis also keeps the denominators $|S|-1$ and $|S^c|-1$ of `sphereWeight` nonzero.

**The sphere-product statements.** `sphere_theta_le_odd` and `sphere_theta_le_even` quantify over every `Finset T` of `ι` with $|T|,|T^c|\ge2$ and every $t>0$ with `normSq (diagOp (t • sphereWeight T)) = 1`. That is every split and every coordinate placement of a unit sphere product in the diagonal model. $t<0$ is $-W$, where $\theta$ changes sign, so omitting it loses nothing. The value is `theta_sphereProduct_value`, which is L57's. Paired with `L57_attained_odd` and `L57_attained_even` (whose right-hand sides are `thetaOddSq`, equal to `ThetaSqOdd` by `thetaOddSq_eq`, and $\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$), this is "maximum, attained", which is what line 213 claims. *That `diagOp (t • sphereWeight T)` is the Weyl tensor of the Riemannian product is the family layer's identification and is inherited from L57, not re-examined here.*

**Hypotheses.** `hp`, `hq` are used for positivity and nonzero denominators. `hodd` is used in the odd case to force $p\ne q$. `sphere_theta_le_even` carries no parity hypothesis and proves $\theta\le\theta_0(n)$ at every $n$. At odd $n$ that is a true but weaker bound, and the docstring and the L16 row both say it is $\Theta_n$ at even $n$ only, so nothing is overclaimed. `thetaSplitSq_le_of_pq`'s $B$ is forced positive by $0<pq\le B$. None is vacuous.

## B. L117

**The record** ([bounds.md](../findings/bounds.md) lines 726 to 728): *"(1) $D(v)=0$ puts the mass $2(n-2)$ on $E_{\min}\times E_{\max}$, and relation (B) with total symmetry gives $(n-2)(M-m)=2m$, so $M=nm/(n-2)$. (2) L70's boxed identity then gives $\theta=(n-1)m$ ... (3) Summing the chord inequality $(\lambda_a+m)(M-\lambda_a)\ge0$ over all $N=\binom n2$ eigenvalues, with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$ and $\lVert W\rVert=1$, gives $1\le mMN$ and hence $\theta=(n-1)m\ge\frac{\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}}n=\theta_0(n)$, equality iff $W$ has two eigenvalues and no kernel."* The source statement (line 742): *"If $D(v)=0$ at a critical unit $W\in\mathcal W_n$ then $\lambda_{\max}=n\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert/(n-2)$, $\theta=(n-1)\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$ and $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$."*

**"D(v) = 0" is `hsat`.** L70's boxed identity (bounds.md line 218) is $\theta=\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}-D(v)/\lvert\lambda_{\min}\rvert$ at every critical unit $W$ and every unit $v\in E_{\min}$. With $\lambda_{\min}<0$ this makes $D(v)=0$ equivalent to $\theta=\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}$, and since the right side does not depend on $v$, $D(v)=0$ at one $v$ is $D(v)=0$ at all. The record says this in so many words (line 748): *"the hypothesis "saturates Lemma L70" is the scalar spectral equation $\theta=\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}$"*. In the kernel, `saturation_iff_support` proves `hsat` iff every nonzero bracket coefficient $c_{v\gamma_0ab}$ pairs $\lambda_{\min}$ with $\lambda_{\max}$, which is the record's "$\mathrm{ad}(v)$ supported on $E_{\min}\otimes E_{\max}$", and the corner-minimum equality case (derived in [L70Statement.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/L70Statement.lean)) makes that support condition $D(v)=0$. So `hsat` is faithful.

**The eigenvalue binders pin $\lambda_{\min}$ and, on every satisfiable instance, $\lambda_{\max}$.** `hγ` and `hlo` make `lmin` an attained lower bound, so `lmin` is $\lambda_{\min}$. `hhi` makes `lmax` an upper bound only, with no attainment. That is harmless: if `lmax` exceeded the true $\lambda_{\max}$ then L70's bound $\theta\le\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}$ would make `hsat` false, so such instances are empty. `hneg` and `hpos` are free on $\mathcal W_n\setminus\{0\}$ by tracelessness and exclude only $W=0$. `hn : 3 ≤ n` is weaker than the record's $n\ge4$, and at $n=3$ the Weyl space is zero, so it adds no instance.

**θ is the critical multiplier.** `IsCritical W θ` is $Q(W)\varphi_{ij}=\theta\,W\varphi_{ij}$ for all $i,j$, so $\theta$ is homogeneous of degree one, like the eigenvalues. `L117_mass` and `L117_theta` carry no unit-norm hypothesis and are the scale-covariant form of steps (1) and (2), which is a generalisation. At unit norm `theta_of_critical_unit` gives `theta W = θ`, the record's $\theta=P/\lVert W\rVert^3$. `L117_bound` and `L117_eq` carry `hunit`, which is what step (3) and the identity need.

**Step by step.**
- (1) `L117_mass`: $(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})(n-2)=-2\lambda_{\min}$. With $m=-\lambda_{\min}$, $M=\lambda_{\max}$ this is $(n-2)(M-m)=2m$, the record's relation. The proof is the record's: `support_of_saturation` puts every nonzero term on an $(E_{\min},E_{\max})$ pair, and rows (A) (`killing_frame`, mass $2(n-2)$) and (B) (`aW2_frame`, `aW2_mid`) are summed in the eigenframe.
- (2) `L117_theta`: $\theta=(n-1)(-\lambda_{\min})$, from `hsat` and (1). The record reaches it through L70's boxed identity, which at $D=0$ is `hsat` itself, so the two derivations coincide.
- (3) `L117_bound`: $\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n\le\theta$, the record's $\theta_0(n)$ literally. The chord terms $(\mu_j-\lambda_{\min})(\lambda_{\max}-\mu_j)\ge0$ are summed over `Pairs n` with `Fintype.card (Pairs n) = n.choose 2`, `sum_mu_zero` ($\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}W=0$) and `sum_mu_sq` with `hunit` ($\sum\mu^2=1$), giving `hA`, which is $1\le mMN$. Then $M=nm/(n-2)$ gives $n^2m^2(n-1)/(2(n-2))\ge1$, i.e. $\theta^2=(n-1)^2m^2\ge\theta_0(n)^2$.

**The equality case.** `L117_eq` proves $\theta=\theta_0(n)$ iff every eigenvalue is `lmin` or `lmax`, through $\theta^2=\theta_0(n)^2(1+C)$ with $C$ the summed chord terms. This is the record's "two eigenvalues and no kernel":
- Lean to record. `lmin < 0 < lmax` excludes $0$, so there is no kernel. `lmin` is attained at `γ₀`, and if `lmax` were not attained every eigenvalue would be `lmin` and the trace would be negative, so there are exactly two.
- Record to Lean. With two eigenvalues they are $\lambda_{\min}=$ `lmin` and $\lambda_{\max}$, which is `lmax` on every satisfiable instance as above.
- *A remark, not a defect*: on $\mathcal W_n\setminus\{0\}$ "no kernel" follows from "two eigenvalues", since a trace-zero two-valued spectrum $\{a,0\}$ forces $a=0$.

The multiplicities r324 attached to the equality case, $\dim E_{\min}=n^2/4$ and $\dim E_{\max}=n(n-2)/4$, are **not** in `L117_eq`, and the box does not claim them. By hand from `L117_eq`'s right side, $L117$'s mass law and the trace: $-am+bnm/(n-2)=0$ with $a+b=\binom n2$ gives $b=n(n-2)/4$ and $a=n^2/4$, and $n(n-2)/4$ is an integer only at even $n$. So the equality clause is empty at odd $n$ as a consequence of L117's own equality case. The L117 row gets that emptiness from L96's balanced product instead, and says so.

**Whether the L117 row may be marked proved.** Yes. The record's L117 is "$D(v)=0$ at a critical unit $W$ gives $M=nm/(n-2)$, $\theta=(n-1)m$, $\theta\ge\theta_0(n)$, with equality iff two eigenvalues and no kernel". All four are proved for the real operator in the spectral-theorem eigenframe, with the hypothesis in the form the record itself defines. The block's surrounding claims (L71's "for $n\ge5$ this is an equality", the dimension counts) are L71's and r324's, not L117's statement.

**Witness (hand screen, reported as a screen).** No Lean object in the layer carries both `IsCritical` and a computed Mathlib spectrum, so the witness is by hand.
- $S^2\times S^2$, $n=4$. The curvature operator has eigenvalue $1$ on the two factor area forms and $0$ on the four mixed planes, and $\mathop{\mathrm{scal}}/(n(n-1))=\tfrac13$, so $W$ has $\tfrac23$ twice and $-\tfrac13$ four times, a two-valued spectrum with ratio $2=n/(n-2)$. Normalised, $m=1/\sqrt{12}$, $M=2/\sqrt{12}$, so $\sum\mu=0$, $\sum\mu^2=4m^2+2M^2=1$, and $\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}=3/\sqrt{12}=(n-1)m=\theta_0(4)$. Every hypothesis holds, and `L117_eq`'s two sides are both true.
- $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, $n=4$. Spectrum $(2c,-c,-c,0,0,0)$ with $6c^2=1$. $\lambda_{\min}+(n-2)\lambda_{\max}=3c=\sqrt{3/2}$, the record's $\theta(W_{\mathbb{CP}^2})$, so it saturates as the block says. Mass law: $(c)(2)=2c=-2\lambda_{\min}$. $\theta=3c=(n-1)m$. $\theta_0(4)=\sqrt{12}/4<\sqrt{3/2}$, strict, and the right side of `L117_eq` fails on the kernel. **This is the three-eigenvalue saturating point the block names, and L117's statement handles it correctly.**
