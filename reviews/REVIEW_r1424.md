## 1. The Weyl hypothesis in `sharpQF_frame`

The original proof calls `weyl_lin hW` to push `W` through $\sum_\alpha c_\alpha v_\alpha$ (twice, in `hlin`), through a single scalar multiple (in `hWs`, at `Fin 1`), and through the frame expansion of $[\eta,v_\alpha]$ (in `hWb`). It calls `hW.1` once, to know that `W (v α)` is skew so that $[\eta,W v_\alpha]$ is a bivector and `weyl_lin` applies to it. No other clause of `IsWeyl` (symmetry, Bianchi, Ricci) appears. `sharpQF_frame_lin` substitutes `hWl` for each `weyl_lin` call and `hsk` for `hW.1`, and the rest of the script is unchanged. Symmetry of $W$ on $\Lambda^2$ is not lost: an orthonormal eigenframe with real eigenvalues implies it.

## 2. Polarisation and the rotation

`sharpBil` is defined in [WeylReal.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Base/WeylReal.lean) as the symmetric polarisation of `sharpQF`, the record's $\langle R^\#X,Y\rangle$ since $R^\#$ is self-adjoint. With $a_{\alpha\beta}=\omega_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ and $b_{\alpha\beta}=\omega_{\alpha\beta\delta}$, $(a+b)^2-a^2-b^2=2ab$, and the outer $\tfrac12$ and the frame form's $\tfrac12$ leave $\tfrac12\sum\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta ab$. The rotation $\langle[v_\gamma,v_\alpha],v_\beta\rangle=-\langle v_\alpha,[v_\gamma,v_\beta]\rangle=\langle[v_\alpha,v_\beta],v_\gamma\rangle$ uses ad-invariance and symmetry of the pairing, which `omegaF_rot`'s script follows.

## 3. `XinvRec` and $\lvert W\rvert$

The record defines $X_{\mathrm{inv}}$ for symmetric $W$ on $\Lambda^2$ with $P_\pm$ the spectral projections. In an orthonormal eigenframe these are diagonal with entries $[\lambda_\gamma>0]$ and $[\lambda_\gamma<0]$, which is the two `if` factors. For $\lvert W\rvert$ the file takes an operator with the same frame and eigenvalues $\lvert\lambda\rvert$. Two facts make that the right reading. First, uniqueness: for skew $X$, completeness gives $X=\sum\langle X,v_\gamma\rangle v_\gamma$, and linearity with the eigen clause gives $\mathrm{Wabs}\,X=\sum\lvert\lambda_\gamma\rvert\langle X,v_\gamma\rangle v_\gamma$, which is the record's $\lvert W\rvert X$. Second, `sharpQF` applies its operator to $\varphi_{ij}$ and to brackets of bivectors only, so values off $\Lambda^2$ never enter. The screen built `absOp` from an arbitrary `EigenFrame W v lam` and proved `absOp_lin`, `absOp_skew` and `absOp_frame` in a scratch file, so the three `Wabs` hypotheses follow from `hf` alone. That is why item 4c calls the row's "$\lvert W\rvert$ sharing it" a construction rather than a debt.
