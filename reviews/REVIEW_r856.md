## Conventions, quoted from the brief

- $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ is realised as skew matrices. $\varphi_{ab}=E_{ba}-E_{ab}$ and it is a UNIT bivector. The inner product is $\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$.
- $u\wedge v=vu^{\mathsf T}-uv^{\mathsf T}$. At orthonormal $u,v$ this is a unit bivector and $(u\wedge v)(u\wedge v)^{\mathsf T}=uu^{\mathsf T}+vv^{\mathsf T}=\Pi_P$, the orthogonal projection onto $P=\mathrm{span}\{u,v\}$. **This holds for exactly this normalisation**: the $1/\sqrt2$ convention returns $\tfrac12\Pi_P$.
- $\omega$ is **decomposable** when $\omega=u\wedge v$, equivalently $\omega\wedge\omega=0$. The record's wedge is TWICE the Pfaffian.
- $\mathcal W_n$ is the space of algebraic Weyl tensors: symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, first Bianchi, Ricci-flat, trace-free. $P(W)=\langle W,W^2+W^\#\rangle$, $\theta=P(W)/\lVert W\rVert^3$, and criticality on the unit sphere is $Q(W)=W^2+W^\#=\theta W$.
- $\sqrt{3/2}=1.224745$ and $\theta_0(n)=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$.

One convention is used in the block that the brief does not declare, and it matters for the constant. $\Xi$'s expansion $\lVert\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha\otimes\Pi_\alpha\rVert^2=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)^2$ is the UNHALVED Frobenius product $\langle A,B\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)$ on the symmetric $n\times n$ matrices $\Pi_\alpha$, not the halved product the brief declares for skew matrices. Read with the halved product the value would be $n^2(n-1)/4$. The block is self-consistent, and the hazard is only that one block carries two inner products.

## 1. The per-index identity

Write $N=\binom n2$ and $\Pi_\alpha=\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}=u_\alpha u_\alpha^{\mathsf T}+v_\alpha v_\alpha^{\mathsf T}$, using the record's normalisation. Given 1 supplies $\sum_\beta\Pi_\beta=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$, and I checked that value directly: in the coordinate basis each index $i$ lies in $n-1$ of the pairs, so $\sum_{i<j}(E_{ii}+E_{jj})=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$, and basis-independence is part of the given. Then

$$\sum_\beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Big(\Pi_\alpha\sum_\beta\Pi_\beta\Big)=(n-1)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Pi_\alpha=2(n-1),$$

and the diagonal term is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha^2)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\Pi_\alpha=2$ by idempotence, leaving $2(n-1)-2=2(n-2)$. The block's parenthetical $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha((n-1)\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_\alpha))=2(n-1)-2$ is the same computation.

At the coordinate configuration, $\alpha=\{1,2\}$ has $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{12}\Pi_{1k})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{12}\Pi_{2k})=1$ for the $2(n-2)$ indices $k\ge3$ and $0$ on the disjoint pairs, giving $2(n-2)$.

At given 4's rotated witness, with $g_2=(0,\cos t,\sin t,0)$ and $g_3=(0,-\sin t,\cos t,0)$, the six projections are $\Pi_1=E_{11}+g_2g_2^{\mathsf T}$, $\Pi_2=E_{11}+g_3g_3^{\mathsf T}$, $\Pi_3=E_{11}+E_{44}$, $\Pi_4=E_{22}+E_{33}$, $\Pi_5=E_{22}+E_{44}$, $\Pi_6=E_{33}+E_{44}$. Their sum is $3\,\mathrm{Id}=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$, since $g_2g_2^{\mathsf T}+g_3g_3^{\mathsf T}=E_{22}+E_{33}$. At $\alpha=1$ the five traces are $1,1,1,\cos^2t,\sin^2t$, summing to $4=2(n-2)$. Taking $\cos t=3/5$ and $\sin t=4/5$ gives the exact list $1,1,1,9/25,16/25$ with sum $4$. So the identity holds on a configuration that is not integral, which is the point: the identity is unconditional, and integrality is what turns it into a degree count of exactly $2(n-2)$ neighbours.

The use of $\Theta(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)=(n-1)\mathrm{Id}$ is legitimate and it does need the configuration to be a basis rather than an orthonormal set. For a proper orthonormal subset $E$, $\Theta(E)$ has trace $2\dim E$ but is not a multiple of the identity, and the screen in the hypothesis inventory shows the identity failing by $1$ against $4$ at $n=4$. The Lean lemma sidesteps the issue honestly by taking $\sum_a P_a=(n-1)\bullet 1$ as a hypothesis, and that hypothesis in fact pins the cardinality: taking traces gives $2N=n(n-1)$, so $N=\binom n2$ is derivable and the free `N` in the Lean statement is not a widening.

## 2. The principal-angle reading

Set $X=u\wedge v=vu^{\mathsf T}-uv^{\mathsf T}$ and $Y=u'\wedge v'$. Since $X^{\mathsf T}=-X$,

$$\langle X,Y\rangle=\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)=-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(XY)=(u\cdot u')(v\cdot v')-(u\cdot v')(v\cdot u')=\det M,\qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}u\cdot u'&u\cdot v'\\ v\cdot u'&v\cdot v'\end{pmatrix}.$$

At $u'=u$, $v'=v$ this gives $1$, confirming the unit normalisation. The singular values of $M$ are the cosines of the principal angles between $P$ and $P'$, so $\lvert\det M\rvert=\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2$. The block's identity therefore holds in absolute value and fails in sign: at $\omega'=v\wedge u=-\omega$ the left side is $-1$ and the right side is $1$. The sign is fixed by a choice of orientation on each plane, and every use in the block is of the vanishing, so the misstatement is cosmetic. It is still a misstatement, and the corrected form is $\langle u\wedge v,u'\wedge v'\rangle=\pm\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2$, with the sign the relative orientation.

For the trace, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\lVert M\rVert_F^2=\cos^2\vartheta_1+\cos^2\vartheta_2$, so the block's reading is correct. Equivalently, and more directly, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\lvert\Pi_\alpha u'\rvert^2+\lvert\Pi_\alpha v'\rvert^2$, a form I use repeatedly below.

Orthogonality gives $\det M=0$, hence $\cos\vartheta_1\cos\vartheta_2=0$, hence at least one right angle. Under orthogonality the trace is the remaining $\cos^2\vartheta\in[0,1]$, it is $1$ exactly when the smallest principal angle vanishes, which is exactly $P_\alpha\cap P_\beta\ne0$, and it is $0$ exactly when $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta=0$, because $\lVert\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta\rVert_F^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\beta\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)$ by idempotence and cyclicity. The block's justification of the second reading is exactly this, and it is right.

Both equality readings need orthogonality, and the screen is in the hypothesis inventory: two planes with trace $1$ and no shared line exist as soon as their bivectors are allowed to be non-orthogonal. Anyone quoting "trace one means a shared line" outside the orthonormal-basis setting quotes a false statement.

## 3. The fixed total and the identification of $\Xi$

Summing C1 over the $N=\binom n2$ indices gives $\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\tfrac{n(n-1)}2\cdot2(n-2)=n(n-1)(n-2)$. The second route is $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha)^2)-\sum_\alpha\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha^2)=n(n-1)^2-n(n-1)=n(n-1)(n-2)$. Both are right and they agree.

$\lVert\sum_\alpha\Pi_\alpha\otimes\Pi_\alpha\rVert^2=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\Pi_\alpha,\Pi_\beta\rangle^2=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)^2$ on the induced product of the unhalved Frobenius product, as noted in the conventions section.

At a coordinate configuration the diagonal terms are $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha^2)^2=4$ apiece, contributing $4N=2n(n-1)$, and the off-diagonal terms are all $0$ or $1$, so their squares sum to the same $n(n-1)(n-2)$. Hence $\Xi=n(n-1)(n-2)+2n(n-1)=n^2(n-1)$. Two checks in closed form: at $n=3$, $\Xi=3\cdot4+6\cdot1=18=9\cdot2$, and at $n=4$, $\Xi=6\cdot4+24=48=16\cdot3$.

## 4. The bound and its equality case

The termwise argument applies to the off-diagonal terms only. There $t=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)\in[0,1]$ gives $t^2\le t$ and $\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}t^2\le n(n-1)(n-2)$. The diagonal terms are $t=2$, which is not in $[0,1]$, and their contribution $4N=2n(n-1)$ is an identity rather than a bound. The block's sentence "Every term lies in $[0,1]$, so $t^2\le t$ termwise" is therefore false as written for $\binom n2$ of the $n^2(n-1)^2/4$ terms, while the displayed inequality $\Xi\le n(n-1)(n-2)+2n(n-1)$ shows the diagonal was in fact accounted for separately and correctly. The fix is one word: every OFF-DIAGONAL term.

With that reading the bound $\Xi\le n^2(n-1)$ holds, and equality holds if and only if $t^2=t$ for every off-diagonal term, that is if and only if every off-diagonal trace is $0$ or $1$, which is integrality. That equivalence is unconditional and is the content of the Lean lemma `sum_sq_eq_sum_iff` at the scalar level.

The clause "and by the theorem below it takes it at no other" is a different statement, and it is the theorem. The chain is: $\Xi=n^2(n-1)$ if and only if integrality (proved), and integrality implies a common frame (the theorem, whose proof in Lean is a `sorry`). So the bound's sharpness at coordinate configurations and the claim that nothing else attains it are not two facts but one fact and one conjecture-with-a-written-proof. They must not be cited as mutually corroborating.

## 5. The theorem's four steps

Throughout, $\{\omega_\alpha\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ by decomposable forms, $n\ge3$, with integrality. Note first that distinct indices give distinct planes: $P_\alpha=P_\beta$ forces $\omega_\beta=\pm\omega_\alpha$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=2$, excluded both by orthonormality and by integrality.

**Step 1 holds.** If $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=1$ then by section 2 the principal cosines are $1$ and $0$, so $P_\alpha\cap P_\beta=\mathbb Ru$ is a line. Choose the unit $v\in P_\beta\cap u^\perp$. Then $1=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)=\lvert\Pi_\alpha u\rvert^2+\lvert\Pi_\alpha v\rvert^2=1+\lvert\Pi_\alpha v\rvert^2$, so $v\perp P_\alpha$. The block's own route is the same fact read off the determinant: with $\omega_\alpha=u\wedge u'$ one has $\langle u\wedge v,u\wedge u'\rangle=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&v\cdot u'\end{pmatrix}=\langle v,u'\rangle$, so orthogonality gives $v\perp u'$, and $v\perp u$ holds by construction. Both are correct.

**Step 2 holds, with one case left unwritten.** For two neighbours $\beta,\beta'$ of $\alpha$, step 1 gives $u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\in P_\alpha$ and $v_\beta,v_{\beta'}\perp P_\alpha$, so the cross terms vanish and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\beta\Pi_{\beta'})=\langle u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\rangle^2+\langle v_\beta,v_{\beta'}\rangle^2$, while $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality reads $\det=\langle u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\rangle\langle v_\beta,v_{\beta'}\rangle=0$. If $\langle u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\rangle\ne0$ then $\langle v_\beta,v_{\beta'}\rangle=0$ and the trace is $\langle u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\rangle^2$, which integrality forces to be $1$. So in every case $\lvert\langle u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\rangle\rvert\in\{0,1\}$ and the shared directions are pairwise parallel or orthogonal, hence they occupy at most two orthogonal lines of the two-plane $P_\alpha$.

Compressing completeness by $\Pi_{P_\alpha}$ kills the non-neighbours, because trace $0$ gives $\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\gamma=0$, and reduces each neighbour to $u_\beta u_\beta^{\mathsf T}$, leaving $\Pi_{P_\alpha}+\sum_{\beta\sim\alpha}u_\beta u_\beta^{\mathsf T}=(n-1)\Pi_{P_\alpha}$, that is $\sum_{\beta\sim\alpha}u_\beta u_\beta^{\mathsf T}=(n-2)\Pi_{P_\alpha}$. The trace of that is $2(n-2)$, which recovers C1's degree count.

Here is the lacuna. The text says "at most two such lines" and then speaks of "whose two-line split", never excluding the single-line case. The exclusion is one line: if every $u_\beta$ lay on one line $\mathbb Rx$ the left side would be $2(n-2)xx^{\mathsf T}$, of rank $1$, against $(n-2)\Pi_{P_\alpha}$ of rank $2$, which needs $n\ge3$. With two lines $\mathbb Rx,\mathbb Ry$ carrying $k$ and $m$ neighbours, $kxx^{\mathsf T}+myy^{\mathsf T}=(n-2)(xx^{\mathsf T}+yy^{\mathsf T})$ gives $k=m=n-2$. So the conclusion is right and the argument for it is complete only after that rank remark, which the reader has to supply.

**Step 3 holds.** For two neighbours on the same line, $\lvert\langle u_\beta,u_{\beta'}\rangle\rvert=1$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\beta\Pi_{\beta'})=1+\langle v_\beta,v_{\beta'}\rangle^2\ge1$, and integrality forces the value $1$ and $\langle v_\beta,v_{\beta'}\rangle=0$. The $n-2$ vectors $v_\beta$ are then orthonormal in the $(n-2)$-dimensional $P_\alpha^\perp$, so they are a basis of it.

The saturation argument is correct. For unit $x$, $\sum_\gamma\lvert\Pi_{P_\gamma}x\rvert^2=x^{\mathsf T}\big(\sum_\gamma\Pi_\gamma\big)x=n-1$, every term lies in $[0,1]$, and the $n-1$ planes $P_\alpha$ and $\mathrm{span}\{x,v_\beta\}$ each contribute exactly $1$. Hence every other term is $0$, so every other plane lies in $x^\perp$, and there is no plane with a partial value either.

The count is right: $\binom n2-(n-1)=\binom{n-1}2$, which is $\dim\Lambda^2(x^\perp)$, so the remaining forms are an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2(x^\perp)$ by decomposables. Orthogonality and integrality are inherited verbatim, the embedding $\Lambda^2(x^\perp)\subseteq\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ being isometric.

The inherited completeness is the step I most expected to be assumed rather than derived, and it is derived. The $n-1$ planes through $x$ are $\mathrm{span}\{x,y\}$ and the $\mathrm{span}\{x,v_\beta\}$, where $y$ is the second line of step 2 and $\{y\}\cup\{v_\beta\}$ is an orthonormal basis of the $(n-1)$-dimensional $x^\perp$. Their projections sum to $(n-1)xx^{\mathsf T}+\Pi_{x^\perp}$. Subtracting from $(n-1)\mathrm{Id}=(n-1)xx^{\mathsf T}+(n-1)\Pi_{x^\perp}$ leaves $\sum_{\text{rem}}\Pi_\gamma=(n-2)\Pi_{x^\perp}$, which is exactly the completeness relation of given 1 in dimension $n-1$. So the sub-configuration inherits precisely the hypotheses the induction consumes, with nothing assumed.

**Step 4 holds, and it needs $n\ge4$.** Each plane through $x$ is $\mathrm{span}\{x,w\}$ with $w\in\{y\}\cup\{v_\beta\}$, an orthonormal basis of $x^\perp$, and the induction returns some other orthonormal frame $f_1,\dots,f_{n-1}$ of $x^\perp$ whose coordinate planes are the remaining $\binom{n-1}2$ planes. For $Q=\mathrm{span}\{f_i,f_j\}$ one has $x\perp Q$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{\langle x,w\rangle}\Pi_Q)=\lvert\Pi_Qw\rvert^2=c_i^2+c_j^2$ where $w=\sum_kc_kf_k$. Integrality gives $c_i^2+c_j^2\in\{0,1\}$ for every pair, with $\sum_kc_k^2=1$. If two coefficients are nonzero, say $c_1,c_2$, then $c_1^2+c_2^2=1$ and all others vanish, and testing the pair $(1,3)$ gives $c_1^2\in\{0,1\}$, hence $c_2=0$, a contradiction. So $w=\pm f_i$.

That last test needs a third index in $x^\perp$, that is $n-1\ge3$. The inductive case is $n\ge4$, so the requirement is met, and the base $n=3$ is handled separately, but the text does not flag the dimension. The remaining unwritten line is that $w\mapsto f_i$ is a bijection: the $n-1$ vectors $w$ are pairwise orthogonal, so distinct $w$ give distinct $f_i$, and counting closes it. Then $\{x,f_1,\dots,f_{n-1}\}$ is a frame carrying both families of planes as coordinate planes.

**The base case holds.** At $n=3$ every $2$-form is decomposable and $\star:\Lambda^2\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ carries the unit $\varphi_{ab}$ to a unit $\pm e_c$, so it maps orthonormal bases to orthonormal bases. An orthonormal basis $\{\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3\}$ therefore has $\omega_i=\pm h_j\wedge h_k$ for an orthonormal frame $\{h_i\}$, and $P_i=h_i^\perp$ is a coordinate plane. Integrality is not used and is automatic there, every pair of distinct planes in $\mathbb R^3$ sharing a line and every trace being $1$.

**The one place the four steps do not obviously chain.** Step 3 fixes one line $\mathbb Rx$ of the two produced by step 2 and never returns to the other. Nothing is lost: the neighbours on the second line $\mathbb Ry$ are $\mathrm{span}\{y,v'\}$ with $y\perp x$ and $v'\perp P_\alpha\ni x$, so they lie in $x^\perp$ and are among the $\binom{n-1}2$ planes the induction handles. That is worth a clause in the text, since a reader tracking the count will otherwise look for them.

So the written proof establishes the theorem. My verdict on it is CONFIRMED AS ALGEBRA rather than CONFIRMED only because three links are one line short in the prose: the single-line exclusion in step 2, the dimension requirement and the bijection in step 4. None is a gap in the mathematics.

## 6. Vacuity and triviality

The theorem is not vacuous. The coordinate configuration $\{e_i\wedge e_j\}$ satisfies every hypothesis: it is an orthonormal basis of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, every element is decomposable, completeness holds with the constant $n-1$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{ij}\Pi_{kl})=\lvert\{i,j\}\cap\{k,l\}\rvert\in\{0,1\}$. Its conclusion holds with the standard frame.

For the conclusion to be empty the hypotheses would have to be jointly unsatisfiable, and the shape of that failure would be a degree count that cannot be met: each index needs exactly $2(n-2)$ neighbours among $\binom n2-1$ other indices, which is $2$ of $2$ at $n=3$, $4$ of $5$ at $n=4$ and $6$ of $9$ at $n=5$, all satisfiable. A vacuous version would also be visible in the statement, in that the hypotheses would carry a numerical constraint contradicting the count, for instance an $N$ exceeding $\binom n2$ or a required degree exceeding $N-1$. Neither happens.

The theorem is also not trivial, since integrality is a proper restriction on orthonormal decomposable bases. Given 4's rotated witness is such a basis with two traces equal to $\cos^2t$ and $\sin^2t$, neither in $\{0,1\}$ when $\sin2t\ne0$, and its planes lie in no common frame. So both the hypothesis and the conclusion cut something out, and the theorem relates the two cuts.

## 7. The kernel

The theorem's statement mentions no operator, no eigenvalue and no multiplicity, and the Lean statement confirms that reading: its hypotheses are orthonormality of the pairs, completeness, pairwise $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality and integrality. So $\ker W\ne0$ is invisible to it, and nothing in the four steps assumes $\lambda_\alpha\ne0$ or a simple spectrum. A zero eigenvalue simply labels some of the planes, and the conclusion survives untouched.

The kernel does bite in two places downstream. First, in the reformulation, the kernel is one more eigenspace to reselect inside, so $\dim\ker\ge2$ puts $W$ outside the simple-spectrum branch and leaves the supremum over $\prod_\mu O(\dim E_\mu)$, and the block states this correctly. Second, in the last paragraph's chain of equivalences, the conditions $\mathrm{Alt}(W^k)=0$ for $k\ge1$ say nothing about the kernel directly, because every $W^k$ annihilates it. The repair is available and unwritten: $\mathrm{Alt}(\mathrm{Id})=0$ always, since the coordinate basis is all-decomposable and $\mathrm{Alt}$ is basis-independent by given 6, so $\sum_\mu\mathfrak b(E_\mu)=0$ and the kernel's term is minus the sum of the others. With that step the equivalence reaches the kernel, and without it the kernel's eigenspace is unconstrained.

## 8. Necessity, and integrality as a property of the basis

Integrality is necessary at a coordinate configuration, by the count $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{ij}\Pi_{kl})=\lvert\{i,j\}\cap\{k,l\}\rvert$, which is $1$ when the pairs meet and $0$ when they are disjoint. So the condition discards no coordinate-diagonal operator, and the equality case of the bound is a characterisation and not merely a sufficient condition.

The block is right that integrality is a property of the chosen basis. I verified given 4's witness independently. Its weights $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1,-1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ on $(e_1\wedge g_2,e_1\wedge g_3,e_1\wedge e_4,e_2\wedge e_3,e_2\wedge e_4,e_3\wedge e_4)$ satisfy $\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha\Pi_\alpha=0$, which is Ricci-flatness by given 2: the row sums are $\tfrac12+\tfrac12-1$, $\tfrac12-1+\tfrac12$, $\tfrac12-1+\tfrac12$ and $-1+\tfrac12+\tfrac12$, all zero. Since $e_1\wedge g_2$ and $e_1\wedge g_3$ carry the same weight and span the same plane in $\Lambda^2$ as $e_1\wedge e_2$ and $e_1\wedge e_3$, the operator is the coordinate-diagonal one with weights $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1,-1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ on the coordinate bivectors, and it is a genuine algebraic Weyl tensor: trace-free, $\tfrac12\cdot4-1\cdot2=0$, and first Bianchi automatically, every coordinate bivector being decomposable so that given 6's sum vanishes term by term. The rotated basis is an admissible reselection inside the four-dimensional $\tfrac12$-eigenspace, it consists of decomposable forms, and it is not integral. So one operator has both an integral and a non-integral orthonormal decomposable eigenbasis, and integrality is not a function of $W$.

## 9. The $\sup\Xi$ reformulation

At a simple spectrum every eigenspace is one-dimensional, so the unit eigenvectors are unique up to sign and $\Xi$, which depends on the $\Pi_\alpha=\omega_\alpha\omega_\alpha^{\mathsf T}$ and hence not on the signs, is a function of $W$. That much is right.

Both directions of "coordinate-diagonality is $\sup\Xi=n^2(n-1)$" hold, with one unwritten step. Forward: if $W$ is diagonal in the frame $f$ then $\{f_i\wedge f_j\}$ is an admissible orthonormal decomposable eigenbasis, it is integral, and $\Xi=n^2(n-1)$, which the bound says is the maximum. Backward: the value must be ATTAINED by an actual reselection before the equality case can be applied, and a supremum alone does not give that. The repair is compactness: the admissible reselections form a closed subset of the compact $\prod_\mu O(\dim E_\mu)$, the decomposability constraints $\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha=0$ being closed, so a non-empty set of them is compact and the supremum is a maximum. Then equality gives integrality and the theorem gives the frame. The block should write "maximum" or add the compactness clause.

The sentence about the simple-spectrum branch is where I have a substantive finding rather than a stylistic one. **At $n=4$ that branch is empty for the operators the programme is about.** A coordinate-diagonal Ricci-flat $W$ at $n=4$ has $w_{12}=w_{34}$, $w_{13}=w_{24}$ and $w_{14}=w_{23}$, which is the block's own contrast claim and which I derive in section 10. Every eigenvalue therefore has multiplicity at least two, so the spectrum is never simple. Consequently, at $n=4$, "where the spectrum is simple, coordinate-diagonality is exactly the single equation $\Xi(W)=n^2(n-1)$" is true only because both sides are impossible: a Ricci-flat $W$ with simple spectrum is not coordinate-diagonal, and it cannot satisfy $\Xi=48$ either. The branch that meets I73's target of spectral simplicity from the other side therefore has no $n=4$ instances, and the reformulation at $n=4$ is unavoidably the supremum over reselections, with $\prod_\mu O(\dim E_\mu)$ of dimension at least three.

The branch is not empty in general. At $n=5$ I exhibit a coordinate-diagonal Ricci-flat trace-free Weyl operator with all $\binom52=10$ weights distinct, in closed form, by solving the five row conditions $\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}=0$:

$$w_{12}=1,\quad w_{13}=2,\quad w_{14}=-103,\quad w_{15}=100,\quad w_{23}=-603,\quad w_{24}=402,\quad w_{25}=200,\quad w_{34}=301,\quad w_{35}=300,\quad w_{45}=-600.$$

The rows read $1+2-103+100=0$, $1-603+402+200=0$, $2-603+301+300=0$, $-103+402+301-600=0$ and $100+200+300-600=0$, so $\sum_\alpha\mu_\alpha\Pi_\alpha=0$ by given 2, the trace $\tfrac12\sum_i(\text{row}_i)=0$, and first Bianchi holds term by term because every coordinate bivector is decomposable. All ten weights are distinct, so the spectrum is simple and the single-equation form of the reformulation has content at $n=5$. What fails at $n=4$ is the opposite-plane pairing, which is special to $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$ and its chirality splitting.

One further scope point on the same sentence. $\Xi$ is defined on orthonormal decomposable bases and on nothing wider, so the "single equation" is a single equation only relative to membership of the all-decomposable locus, which is the vanishing of $\omega_\alpha\wedge\omega_\alpha$ for every $\alpha$ and is a system rather than one scalar. The block's opening sentence scopes itself correctly, to "an operator with an orthonormal decomposable eigenbasis", and the later "exactly the single equation" drops the qualifier. Since the block's own last paragraph reports that qualifier failing at a real local maximum, the drop matters.

## 10. The $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ paragraph

**$\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)$ and chirality.** For unit $\eta_\pm=(\omega_{12}\pm\omega_{34})/\sqrt2$ one computes $\eta_\pm\wedge\eta_\pm=\pm\,\omega_{12}\wedge\omega_{34}$, and $\Lambda^\pm$ are orthogonal for the wedge pairing as well as the metric, so every unit self-dual form has $\omega\wedge\omega=+\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}$ and every unit anti-self-dual form $-\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}$, with one non-zero constant $\kappa$. A symmetric $A$ that preserves the splitting, which $W^2$ does because $W$ does by given 3, has an eigenbasis inside $\Lambda^+\cup\Lambda^-$, and given 6 then reads $\mathrm{Alt}(A)=\tfrac\kappa6\big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A\Pi_+)-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A\Pi_-)\big)\mathrm{vol}$. With $A=W^2$ this is proportional to $\lVert W^+\rVert^2-\lVert W^-\rVert^2$, as claimed.

**The arithmetic.** With $\mathcal A=c\,\mathrm{diag}(2,-1,-1)$ and $\mathcal B=0$: $\lVert W\rVert^2=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal A^2)+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal B^2)=c^2(4+1+1)=6c^2$. $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(W^3)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal A^3)=c^3(8-1-1)=6c^3$. Given 3 gives $W^\#\vert_{\Lambda^+}=2\mathcal A^2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal A^2)\mathrm{Id}_3$, so $\langle W,W^\#\rangle=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal A^3)-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal A^2)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal A)=12c^3-0$, the block being trace-free. Hence $P=6c^3+12c^3=18c^3$ and $\theta=18c^3/(6c^2)^{3/2}=18c^3/(6\sqrt6\,c^3)=3/\sqrt6=\sqrt{3/2}$ for $c>0$. All three figures confirm, and $\theta_0(4)=\sqrt{12}/4=\sqrt3/2$, so this critical point sits strictly above $\theta_0(4)$ and exactly AT $\sqrt{3/2}$, not above it.

**The failure of the test.** $\lVert W^+\rVert^2-\lVert W^-\rVert^2=6c^2-0=\lVert W\rVert^2\ne0$, so $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)\ne0$. Since an orthonormal decomposable eigenbasis would give $\mathrm{Alt}(A)=0$ for every $A$ diagonal in it, $W^2$ included, $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has none. The direct reason is the same one: its eigenvectors lie in $\Lambda^+$, and a unit self-dual form has $\omega\wedge\omega=\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}\ne0$, so no eigenvector is decomposable at all. Given 5 supplies criticality and local maximality, so the conclusion stands: the $\Lambda^4$ test fails at a genuine local maximum, and the only thing separating that instance from the theorem's intended use is that its $\theta$ equals $\sqrt{3/2}$ rather than exceeding it. The block's reading, that the missing link must consume the strict threshold, is the right reading of its own example.

**The contrast claim.** For coordinate-diagonal $W$ with weights $w_{ij}$, Ricci-flatness is $\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}=0$ for each $i$ by given 2. At $n=4$, subtracting row $2$ from row $1$ gives $w_{13}+w_{14}=w_{23}+w_{24}$, subtracting row $4$ from row $3$ gives $w_{13}+w_{23}=w_{14}+w_{24}$, and adding those two gives $w_{13}=w_{24}$. The same two steps on the other pairings give $w_{12}=w_{34}$ and $w_{14}=w_{23}$. So opposite-plane weights are forced equal, exactly as the block says. Then $W(\omega_{12}\pm\omega_{34})=w_{12}(\omega_{12}\pm\omega_{34})$ and similarly for the other two pairs, so both chirality blocks are $\mathrm{diag}(w_{12},w_{13},w_{14})$, each trace-free by row $1$. Hence $\mathcal A=\mathcal B$, $\lVert W^+\rVert=\lVert W^-\rVert$, $\mathrm{Alt}(W^2)=0$, and the test passes. Given 4's witness is the instance: $w_{12}=w_{34}=\tfrac12$, $w_{13}=w_{24}=\tfrac12$, $w_{14}=w_{23}=-1$, and both blocks are $\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1)$. The contrast with $\mathcal B=0$ is as sharp as claimed.

## 11. What would break the theorem, and where I looked

A counterexample is an orthonormal basis $\{\omega_\alpha\}$ of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge3$, by decomposable forms, with $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)\in\{0,1\}$ for all $\alpha\ne\beta$, and no orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$ whose coordinate $2$-planes are the $P_\alpha$. Concretely it would be $\binom n2$ two-planes, pairwise either meeting in a line or mutually orthogonal, with the bivectors pairwise $\Lambda^2$-orthogonal, that nevertheless do not close into a single frame.

Where such a thing could live is determined by the proof. Steps 2 and 3 are rank and saturation arguments against the completeness relation, and there is no room in them: the rank of $(n-2)\Pi_{P_\alpha}$ is $2$ whatever the configuration, and $\sum_\gamma\lvert\Pi_{P_\gamma}x\rvert^2=n-1$ with terms in $[0,1]$ leaves no slack once $n-1$ planes contain $x$. The only step with a choice in it is step 4, the reconciliation of the pencil directions with the recursively produced frame, so a counterexample would have to be a pencil through a line $x$ whose $n-1$ directions form one frame of $x^\perp$ while the planes inside $x^\perp$ are the coordinate planes of a genuinely different frame.

That is exactly the shape of given 4's rotated witness, and I checked it in closed form rather than searching for it. Its planes are the pencil $\mathrm{span}\{e_1,g_2\},\mathrm{span}\{e_1,g_3\},\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_4\}$ through $e_1$, with directions $\{g_2,g_3,e_4\}$ an orthonormal frame of $e_1^\perp$, together with the three coordinate planes of the different frame $\{e_2,e_3,e_4\}$. Integrality fails on precisely the pairs step 4 tests: $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{\langle e_1,g_2\rangle}\Pi_{\langle e_2,e_4\rangle})=\cos^2t$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_{\langle e_1,g_2\rangle}\Pi_{\langle e_3,e_4\rangle})=\sin^2t$, both in $(0,1)$ when $\sin2t\ne0$, and both forced away from $\{0,1\}$ by step 4's coefficient argument. So the family that realises the only structural freedom in the proof is excluded by the hypothesis, in closed form, for every $t$. I opened no search, computed no sweep and constructed no random configuration, per the analysis-only footing.

## 12. The Lean file

The build is clean and the axiom profile is what the brief says. `sum_trace_off`, `sum_sq_le_sum` and `sum_sq_eq_sum_iff` depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. `integrality_forces_frame` depends additionally on `sorryAx`.

**`sum_trace_off` is the per-index identity, and it is strictly wider than the prose.** It asks only that each `P a` be idempotent of trace two and that the family sum to $(n-1)\bullet1$. There is no wedge, no bivector, no $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality and not even symmetry of the `P a`, so it holds for families of oblique rank-two projections that are nothing to do with planes. The widening is sound, the proof is the two-line trace computation, and the docstring says as much. Two remarks. The free `N` is not a genuine widening, since taking traces in `hsum` forces $2N=n(n-1)$. And the docstring's clause "so under integrality every index has exactly `2(n-2)` neighbours" is prose: integrality appears nowhere in the statement.

**`sum_sq_le_sum` and `sum_sq_eq_sum_iff` are the scalar half and only that.** They are statements about an arbitrary `t : Fin N → ℝ` with `0 ≤ t b ∧ t b ≤ 1` on a finset, and that range is a HYPOTHESIS. So the geometric content of the block's second identity, namely that $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Pi_\alpha\Pi_\beta)$ lies in $[0,1]$ with the two equality readings, is assumed by these lemmas rather than established anywhere in the file. The lemmas are correct and they are what their docstring says.

**`integrality_forces_frame` is the theorem, and its typing is faithful.** `hu`, `hv`, `huv` make each pair orthonormal, so `planeProj (u a) (v a) = outer (u a) + outer (v a)` really is the rank-two orthogonal projection, which is what the earlier `planeProj` file left open. `horth` is $(u_a\cdot u_b)(v_a\cdot v_b)-(u_a\cdot v_b)(v_a\cdot u_b)=0$, and by section 2 that determinant is exactly $\langle\omega_a,\omega_b\rangle$ in the record's normalisation, so it is $\Lambda^2$-orthogonality and not something weaker. `hint` and `hcomplete` are the two remaining hypotheses of the prose statement. `horth` is not redundant given `hint`: two planes with both principal cosines $1/\sqrt2$ have trace $1$ and determinant $\pm\tfrac12$. The conclusion asserts an orthonormal `f` and index maps with `i a ≠ j a` and each plane equal to a coordinate plane, which is marginally weaker than the prose's "as exactly its coordinate $2$-planes", since no injectivity is stated. The difference is recoverable, because `hint` forces the planes to be distinct and there are $\binom n2$ of each, so the map is a bijection.

**Where the record's description of the file is wrong.** The block says the file is one "where the two identities and the scalar equality case are proved and the theorem's proof is the file's one gap". Only the FIRST identity is typed. The second identity, the one carrying the principal-angle reading, the $[0,1]$ range and the equivalences with sharing a line and with mutual orthogonality, appears nowhere. Neither does $\Xi$, in any form, and so neither does the bound $\Xi\le n^2(n-1)$ or its value at coordinate configurations. What the file contains is one geometric identity in a wider algebraic form, two generic scalar lemmas that take the geometric range as an input, and the theorem as a `sorry`. The file's own docstring describes this accurately, calling the scalar lemmas "the scalar half", and the findings block does not.

## Verdict

The scalar spine of the claim is sound. C1, C3, C4, C5, C7, C10, C11, C12, C13, C15, C17, C18, C19 and C20 all confirm, most of them from short derivations I redid independently, and the four-step proof of the frame theorem survives line-by-line attack: step 3's inherited completeness, the step most likely to have been assumed, comes out of compressing the completeness relation and equals the induction's hypothesis exactly.

Five corrections are owed. The inner-product identity of C2 is off by a sign that the principal-angle formulation cannot carry, and it should be written with $\pm$ or with absolute values. C6's "every term lies in $[0,1]$" is false for the $\binom n2$ diagonal terms, which the displayed constant shows were handled separately, so the sentence and not the arithmetic needs fixing. C8's "at no other" is the theorem restated and must not be cited beside the bound as independent support. C14's supremum needs to be a maximum, or needs the compactness clause. And C16's single-equation branch drops the all-decomposable qualifier and, at $n=4$, is empty for Ricci-flat coordinate-diagonal $W$, since opposite-plane weights are forced equal and no such operator has a simple spectrum. The branch recovers content at $n=5$, where I exhibit a Ricci-flat coordinate-diagonal Weyl operator with all ten weights distinct.

The record's account of its Lean layer is the finding I would act on first. One identity is typed rather than two, the $[0,1]$ range enters as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion, and $\Xi$ and its bound are not in the file at all, so any later citation of "the identities and the bound are machine-checked" would be false. Three small textual lacunae in the theorem's proof, in step 2's single-line case, step 4's dimension requirement and step 4's bijection, are each one line and worth writing down before the proof is transcribed into Lean.
