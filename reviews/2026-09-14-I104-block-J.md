## The block subalgebra $\mathcal S_J$, derived

For $J\subseteq[n]$ let $H_J\subset\Gamma$ be the sign changes constant on $J$ and free off it, and $\mathcal S_J$ its fixed space, a subalgebra invariant under $O(J)$, which commutes with $H_J$. Reading characters entry by entry, a component $R_{pq,rs}$ survives exactly when its index multiset is trivial on $H_J$:

- $R_{pa,qa}$ with $p,q\in J$, $a\notin J$: for each $a$ a symmetric $\lvert J\rvert\times\lvert J\rvert$ matrix $M_a$;
- $R_{pq,rs}$ with all indices in $J$: the curvature-type block $R_J$ on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$;
- $R_{ab,ab}$ with $a,b\notin J$: the outer sectional entries $K_{ab}$.

Every other entry carries a character nontrivial on $H_J$: $R_{pa,qb}$ ($\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$), $R_{pq,ra}$ ($\varepsilon_J\varepsilon_a$), $R_{ab,pq}$ ($\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$), and outer entries off the diagonal. So as an operator $W=\bigoplus_aM_a\oplus R_J\oplus\operatorname{diag}(K_{ab})$, and $O(J)$ conjugates every $M_a$ and $R_J$ at once. $\mathcal S_{\{i\}}=\mathcal D_n$ and $\mathcal S_{[n]}=\mathcal W_n$.

## L224 read in these coordinates

At $J=\{j,k\}$, $M_a=\begin{pmatrix}K_{ja}&x_a\\x_a&K_{ka}\end{pmatrix}$, whose traceless part is $\begin{pmatrix}y_a&x_a\\x_a&-y_a\end{pmatrix}$ with $y_a=\tfrac12(K_{ja}-K_{ka})$, rotated at double angle by $SO(2)$, which is L224's $(y,x)$ up to the factor in $y$. **L224 says the supremum on $\mathcal S_J$ is attained where every $M_a$ and $R_J$ commute**, rank one in the Gram matrix being all traceless parts proportional, hence simultaneously diagonal. *At $J=[n]$ that sentence is the root node itself, so L224 is its case $\lvert J\rvert=2$.*

## Why the Gram argument stops at $\lvert J\rvert=3$, derived

$\operatorname{tr}W^3=\sum_a\operatorname{tr}M_a^3+\operatorname{tr}R_J^3+\sum K_{ab}^3$, and $\langle W^\#,W\rangle$ contains no term cubic in a single $M_a$: $[e_{pa},e_{qa}]=\pm e_{pq}\in\Lambda^2\mathbb R^J$, on which $W$ acts by $R_J$, so every bracket term through one $M_a$ twice carries $R_J$, and one through $M_a$ and $M_b$ carries $K_{ab}$ via $[e_{pa},e_{pb}]=\pm e_{ab}$. **So $P$ contains $\sum_a\operatorname{tr}M_a^3$.** Writing $M_a=m_aI+M_a^0$, $\operatorname{tr}M_a^3=\lvert J\rvert m_a^3+3m_a\operatorname{tr}(M_a^0)^2+\operatorname{tr}(M_a^0)^3$. At $\lvert J\rvert=2$ the last term vanishes identically, which is why L224's value is quadratic in the traceless parts and linear in their Gram matrix. **At $\lvert J\rvert\ge3$, $\operatorname{tr}(M^0)^3$ is a nonzero cubic invariant of $O(J)$, so $P$ on $\mathcal S_J$ is not a function of any Gram matrix and the rank-one step has nothing to act on.** *Whether the Weyl constraints on the $M_a$ cancel it in $P$ is not settled here: the bracket terms cannot, by the count above, but a constraint tying $M_a^0$ to $R_J$ could restrict the admissible configurations.*

## What this leaves

- **A block induction on $\lvert J\rvert$** is the route this suggests, $\mathcal S_{\{i\}}$ being L57 and $\mathcal S_{\{j,k\}}$ being L224, each step asking that the supremum on $\mathcal S_J$ is attained at commuting blocks. Its first open step, $\lvert J\rvert=3$, must handle $\sum_a\operatorname{tr}(M_a^0)^3$. *Proposing it as a route is the user's decision, at step 9.*
- **L224 does not place a general maximiser in any $\mathcal S_{\{j,k\}}$**, so it closes a stratum and not the root node.
