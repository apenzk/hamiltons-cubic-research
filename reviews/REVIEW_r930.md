**Summary.** The bound is true and its chain is sound, the direction of every step is conservative, and the two things wrong with the block are that its engine is a numbered lemma of this record quoted as a new observation, and that its radius is asserted as a theorem at configurations where the hypothesis it rests on forces the conclusion's other branch.

## 1. The Casimir identity and Schur at $n=4$

**The identity holds at $n=4$.** Built from [weyl.py](../scripts/lib/weyl.py)'s structure constants and split on the self-dual and anti-self-dual bases of $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$, $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a$ restricts to $4\,\mathrm{Id}_3$ on each of $\Lambda^2_+$ and $\Lambda^2_-$, with zero off-diagonal block and zero residual. Over $n=3\ldots12$ the full deviation $\max\lvert\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a-2(n-2)\mathrm{Id}\rvert$ is $0$ in floating point at every $n$, so the value is not in doubt.

**The block's justification is nonetheless incomplete at $n=4$ and unnecessary at every $n$.** The brief's objection is right as far as it goes: $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$ splits under $\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$, so an invariance argument gives one scalar per summand, $c_+\mathrm{Id}\oplus c_-\mathrm{Id}$, and a trace gives only $c_++c_-$. Two repairs are available and the second is the one the record already owns.

- **Widen the group.** $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ is irreducible under $O(n)$ at every $n\ge3$, a reflection in $O(4)\setminus SO(4)$ swapping $\Lambda^2_\pm$, and $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a$ is $O(n)$-invariant for the same reason it is $\mathfrak{so}(n)$-invariant. Since the operator is symmetric, its eigenspaces are invariant subspaces, so irreducibility forces a scalar with no appeal to the real Schur alternatives.
- **Cite [Lemma L153](../findings/bounds.md#lemma-l153--the-killing-row-marginal-sum_betalvertv_alphav_betarvert22n-2), which needs no representation theory at all.** L153 states $\sum_\beta\lVert[v,v_\beta]\rVert^2=2(n-2)\lVert v\rVert^2$ for **any** $v\in\mathfrak{so}(n)$ at every $n\ge2$. Because $A_av=\pm\mathrm{ad}(v)\varphi_a$, that is $\langle v,(\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a)v\rangle=2(n-2)\lVert v\rVert^2$ for every $v$, and a symmetric operator is determined by its quadratic form, so the operator identity follows by polarisation. Applied column by column it gives the matrix form $\sum_a\lVert A_aX\rVert^2=2(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2$ that step 2 actually uses.

**So the repair needed is not at $n=4$ only, it is a replacement of the justification everywhere.** L153's proof is two lines, orthonormality for the Frobenius trace and ad-invariance for the sign flip, and it covers $n=2$ and $n=4$ with no case distinction. The block's route has a hole at $n=4$ and buys nothing at the other $n$.

## 2. The count, and whether it depends on the normalisation

**The count is exact and its description is exact.** At $n=3\ldots12$, $\sum_{a,m,p}c_{amp}^2$ equals $n(n-1)(n-2)$ to the last digit, the number of nonzero entries is exactly $n(n-1)(n-2)$, and $\max\lvert c\rvert=1$, so "one nonzero constant of modulus one for each ordered triple of distinct indices" is literally what the array holds. The combinatorics agree: a pair $a$ admits $2(n-2)$ partners $m$ sharing exactly one index, each fixing $p$, and $\binom n2\cdot2(n-2)=n(n-1)(n-2)$.

**It depends on the normalisation, and the record's is pinned rather than chosen.** [weyl.py](../scripts/lib/weyl.py) builds $c_{abg}=\langle[\varphi_a,\varphi_b],\varphi_g\rangle$ with $\varphi_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{ji}$ and the inner product $\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{\mathsf T}B)$, which makes the $\varphi$ orthonormal and the constants $\pm1$. Under $c\to\lambda c$ the whole derivation is homogeneous: $A_a\to\lambda A_a$, the Casimir scalar goes to $2\lambda^2(n-2)$, and $X^{\#}$ as computed by $-\tfrac12\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aXA_bX)$ goes to $\lambda^2X^{\#}$, so the bound's constant scales exactly with the object it bounds. The bound is therefore insensitive to a consistent rescaling and highly sensitive to an inconsistent one, which is item 12.

**The record's own gate confirms the normalisation in force.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) requires every $\#$ formula to be checked at the identity before use, and the formula L215 quotes returns $\mathrm{id}^{\#}=(n-2)\,\mathrm{id}$ with zero residual at $n=4,6,8,10$. That is the test that settles the $\tfrac12$ and the basis together.

## 3. Invariance, and the scalar read off the trace

**Invariance holds, on the hypothesis that makes it true.** With $A_a$ the matrix of $\mathrm{ad}(\varphi_a)$ in the pair basis and $\{\varphi_a\}$ orthonormal for the same form used to define the transpose, $\mathrm{Ad}_g$ is orthogonal and $\mathrm{Ad}_g\,\mathrm{ad}(\varphi_a)\,\mathrm{Ad}_g^{-1}=\mathrm{ad}(\mathrm{Ad}_g\varphi_a)$, so conjugation permutes the summands of $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a$ and fixes the sum. The orthonormality is load-bearing and the block does not say so, which matters here precisely because this record's $c$ lives in a non-Frobenius normalisation.

**The trace reading is arithmetically right.** $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a)=\sum_a\lVert A_a\rVert^2=\sum_{a,m,p}c_{amp}^2=n(n-1)(n-2)$, and dividing by $N=\binom n2=n(n-1)/2$ gives $2(n-2)$. Measured $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}/N$ is $2(n-2)$ exactly at every $n$ from $3$ to $12$. The reading is valid only once the operator is known to be scalar, which is section 1's point and not a defect in the arithmetic.

## 4. Step 2's first Cauchy--Schwarz

**Valid as written, with the transpose in the right place.** $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aXA_bX)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(((A_aX)^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}(A_bX)\big)=\langle(A_aX)^{\mathsf T},A_bX\rangle$, and Cauchy--Schwarz in the Frobenius inner product gives $\lvert\cdot\rvert\le\lVert(A_aX)^{\mathsf T}\rVert\,\lVert A_bX\rVert=\lVert A_aX\rVert\,\lVert A_bX\rVert$, the last equality being transpose-invariance of the Frobenius norm. **Antisymmetry of $A_a$ is not needed and neither is symmetry of $X$**: the inequality holds for arbitrary square matrices, which is worth saying because the block's surrounding prose leans on both properties elsewhere.

## 5. Step 2's second step

**It is an identity and nothing is doubled.** With $u_a=\lVert A_aX\rVert$, the termwise bound gives $\sum_{ab}\tfrac14\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aXA_bX)^2\le\sum_{ab}\tfrac14u_a^2u_b^2$, and $\sum_{ab}u_a^2u_b^2=(\sum_au_a^2)^2$ exactly, the sum factoring. So the chain contains **one** inequality, applied to each of the $N^2$ entries, followed by an exact factorisation and the Casimir substitution. The arithmetic then closes: $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert^2\le\tfrac14(2(n-2))^2\lVert X\rVert^4$, so $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2$.

**The block's own account of this step is wrong**, and it is the sentence in the lossiness note: *"Two Cauchy--Schwarz steps cost that, the first in the trace pairing and the second in pulling the supremum out of the sum."* There is no second Cauchy--Schwarz and no supremum is pulled out of anything. All of the loss is in the first step, which replaces each Gram entry $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aXA_bX)$ by the product of the two norms, and which cannot be tight at more than a few of the $N^2$ index pairs at once. Section 11 turns that observation into a better constant.

## 6. Step 1, and the equality case

**Step 1 is correct and needs exactly what $\mathcal W_n$ gives.** $X$ symmetric makes $\lVert X^2\rVert^2=\sum\lambda_i^4$ and $\lVert X\rVert^2=\sum\lambda_i^2$ in an eigenbasis, and $\sum\lambda_i^4\le(\sum\lambda_i^2)^2$ because the cross terms are nonnegative. $\mathcal W_n\subset\mathrm{Sym}^2(\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n)$ supplies the symmetry.

**The equality case is right about symmetric operators and empty inside the lemma's own scope.** Equality forces at most one nonzero eigenvalue, so rank at most one rather than rank exactly one, the zero operator being the other case. Inside $\mathcal W_n$ neither is available for a nonzero $X$: Ricci-flatness makes the scalar curvature vanish, hence $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}_{\Lambda^2}X=0$, and a trace-free symmetric operator of rank at most one is zero. Screened at random Weyl elements, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X$ is $10^{-16}$ with full rank and $\lVert X^2\rVert\approx0.19$ against the bound $1$.

**And the two equality cases of step 3 exclude each other.** For a rank-one symmetric $X=vv^{\mathsf T}$, $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aXA_bX)=(v^{\mathsf T}A_av)(v^{\mathsf T}A_bv)=0$ because each $A_a$ is antisymmetric, so $X^{\#}=0$ exactly, measured at $3\times10^{-17}$ at $n=6,8$. So wherever step 1 is tight, step 2's contribution vanishes, and the sum $(n-1)$ of the two constants is unattainable even in principle.

## 7. Is $\kappa$ the constant the estimate needs

**The second supremum dominates the first, so $\kappa\le n-1$ is the right kind of statement.** Three facts do it. $X\circ X=Q(X)$ by the definition of the polarisation, so the two maps are the same map. $\pi^\perp$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal D_n^\perp$, which the [I96](../plan.md) notes confirm by using $\pi^\perp(D\circ D)=0$ for $D\circ D\in\mathcal D_n$, so it is a contraction. And $\mathcal D_n^\perp$ is the orthogonal complement of $\mathcal D_n$ **inside** $\mathcal W_n$, so the smaller supremum ranges over a subset of the larger one's domain. Hence $\sup_{\mathcal D_n^\perp}\lVert\pi^\perp(X\circ X)\rVert\le\sup_{\mathcal W_n}\lVert Q(X)\rVert\le n-1$.

**The block states none of the three.** It asserts the conclusion in one clause at [second-order.md:710](../findings/second-order.md#L710). Each step is a line, and this record has been burned by exactly this class of omission before, so they belong in the block. The estimate does not in fact need $\kappa$ as an intermediate at all: $N\in\mathcal W_n$, so $\lVert\pi^\perp(N\circ N)\rVert\le\lVert Q(N)\rVert\le(n-1)\lVert N\rVert^2$ directly.

## 8. The radius, which is where the block claims more than it has

**What is established.** The implication is sound at any pair $(D,\theta)$ where two things hold: the gap of $2L_D-\theta$ transverse to its kernel is at least some $\gamma>0$, and the $N$ in question has no component in that kernel. Under those two, $\lVert N\rVert\ge\gamma/(n-1)$ or $N=0$.

**What is not established, and it is the whole content.** The only $D$ for which this record holds a value of $\gamma$ is the unit balanced product itself, with $\theta=\theta_0$, which is where the given $\gamma=2\theta_0(n)/(n-2)$ is computed: the [I96](../plan.md) table reads $\gamma=\theta\cdot\min\lvert2\lambda-1\rvert$ off $L_e$ at the idempotent $e=W_0/\theta_0$. But in the application $D=\pi_FW$ with $\lVert W\rVert=1$, so $\lVert D\rVert^2=t$ and $\lVert N\rVert^2=1-t$. **If $D$ is the unit balanced product then $t=1$ and $N=0$**, so the branch carrying the number is empty, and if $N\ne0$ then $\lVert D\rVert<1$, so $D$ is not the configuration whose $\gamma$ is in hand. The radius is therefore established at no configuration where it says anything.

Three further conditions sit under it, none of them visible in the displayed claim.

- **The closed form $\gamma=2\theta_0/(n-2)$ is itself two floating-point measurements** at $n=6$ and $n=8$, as the block twelve lines below says in terms ("two floating-point measurements are two points"). The brief hands it as a given and this review accepts it as given, so the point is only that the radius inherits its status.
- **The vanishing excess is needed and provably fails at $n=6$**, where the kernel is $18$-dimensional against a $9$-dimensional gauge orbit. So the displayed claim, which carries no hypothesis, is unsupported at the smallest $n$ it is written for.
- **Gauge fixing is needed and unstated.** Even with kernel equal to gauge orbit, an $N$ with a component along the orbit defeats the estimate, so a normalisation choosing the frame to make $N$ orthogonal to the orbit is part of the argument.

**The wording claims more than that.** *"The isolation radius is therefore positive and explicit"*, and *"Small, and a theorem: no critical point sits at a positive distance below it from the diagonal locus in that frame"*. The second sentence is an unconditional statement about every critical point, in an unspecified frame, and what is in hand is a conditional whose hypotheses are satisfied at no non-trivial configuration. The numbers reproduce exactly, $2\theta_0/((n-2)(n-1))=0.022473$ at $n=12$ with $\theta_0(12)=1.236033081$ and $0.054554$ at $n=8$ with $\theta_0(8)=1.145643924$, both computed here at criticality residual $5\times10^{-16}$, so the arithmetic is right and the status of the statement is the finding. The block's own closing note lists the perturbation step as owed, which makes the displayed claim inconsistent with the paragraph below it rather than merely optimistic.

## 9. The dichotomy's logic

**The step is correct and every direction is conservative.** From $\gamma\lVert N\rVert\le\lVert(2L_D-\theta)N\rVert=\lVert\pi^\perp(N\circ N)\rVert\le\kappa\lVert N\rVert^2$, either $\lVert N\rVert=0$ or division by $\lVert N\rVert>0$ gives $\lVert N\rVert\ge\gamma/\kappa$. Passing to $\kappa\le n-1$ enlarges the denominator and so shrinks the radius, and the lemma supplies an upper bound on $\kappa$ where the earlier screen supplied a lower one. **Both directions are the safe ones**, which is the specific failure the previous round found and which is not repeated here.

**Two conditions the written step omits.** The lower bound $\lVert(2L_D-\theta)N\rVert\ge\gamma\lVert N\rVert$ holds for $N$ orthogonal to the kernel and fails outright for $N$ inside it, where the left side is zero and the conclusion would be $0\le\kappa\lVert N\rVert^2$, true and empty. And $\gamma\le0$ makes the second branch vacuous rather than false, since $\lVert N\rVert\ge\gamma/(n-1)$ then holds for every $N$, so a sign error in $\gamma$ would degrade the theorem silently instead of breaking it.

## 10. It is on the record, twice, and one of the two is in this file

**The Casimir identity is [Lemma L153](../findings/bounds.md#lemma-l153--the-killing-row-marginal-sum_betalvertv_alphav_betarvert22n-2).** Its statement is $\sum_\beta\lVert[v,v_\beta]\rVert^2=2(n-2)\lVert v\rVert^2$ for any $v\in\mathfrak{so}(n)$ at every $n\ge2$, it is proved in two steps, and Gate 2 confirmed it and rebuilt it cold two ways ([r695](REVIEW_r695.md), [r696](REVIEW_r696.md)). It is the operator identity in quadratic-form guise, and a symmetric operator is its quadratic form, so L215's $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a=2(n-2)\mathrm{Id}$ is L153 polarised. That also disposes of section 1's $n=4$ hole for free.

**Both ingredients also stand at [second-order.md:357](../findings/second-order.md#L357), 355 lines above L215 in L215's own file**, and stand there as the thing the bounds run on: *"What survives transporting $c$ to the eigenbasis is not its $0,\pm1$ entries but the two invariants $\lVert c\rVert^2=2(n-2)N$ and $\sum_{\alpha\gamma}c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}c_{\alpha\beta'\gamma}=2(n-2)\delta_{\beta\beta'}$, and those are what the bounds use."* The first invariant is L215's count, $2(n-2)\binom n2=n(n-1)(n-2)$. The second is L215's Casimir identity in indices, since $\sum_{\alpha\gamma}c_{\alpha\beta\gamma}c_{\alpha\beta'\gamma}=\sum_\alpha(A_\alpha A_\alpha^{\mathsf T})_{\beta\beta'}$ and $A^{\mathsf T}=-A$ makes $A A^{\mathsf T}=A^{\mathsf T}A$.

**So the block's "engine", asserted by Schur plus a count and certified by a five-dimension numerical check, is a proved numbered lemma of this record and a displayed invariant of this file.** [Lemma L153](../findings/bounds.md#lemma-l153--the-killing-row-marginal-sum_betalvertv_alphav_betarvert22n-2)'s own block says the record carried this fact in five guises across three files and that naming the parent is what makes the others citable rather than restatable. L215 adds a sixth guise and cites none of them. This is the third item of the brief's warning realised: the thing about to be derived was already present.

**The scalar shadow of the bound is also recorded, in stronger form.** [second-order.md:361](../findings/second-order.md#L361) gives $\langle W,W^{\#}\rangle\le(n-2)y$ with $y=\lVert\lambda\rVert_4^2\le1$, so pairing L215's bound against $X$ yields $(n-2)$ at unit norm and the recorded bound yields $(n-2)y$, which is never worse. [Lemma L142](../findings/bounds.md#lemma-l142-withdrawn-dominated-by-bounds-already-on-the-record) was **withdrawn** for arriving at the same worst case $n-1$ that L215's step 3 produces. **L215 is not dominated**, because $\lVert Q(X)\rVert$ is genuinely stronger than $\langle X,Q(X)\rangle$ at unit norm and is what the vector-valued estimate needs, and the norm-level statement is the block's real contribution. That is the finding: what is new is the norm, not the constant and not the engine, and the block presents all three as new.

## 11. The lossiness, which is understated in one place and misdiagnosed in another

**The quoted factor fails at $n=6$ on the block's own numbers.** $4/0.504=7.9$ and $5/0.620=8.1$, both below ten, so "more than an order of magnitude" is true at $n=8$ ($6/0.352=17.0$ and $7/0.442=15.8$) and false at $n=6$.

**The quoted maxima are one sample's maxima and are exceeded easily.** At $400$ random unit Weyl elements under seed $930$, $\max\lVert X^{\#}\rVert$ is $0.6112$ at $n=6$ and $0.4158$ at $n=8$, against the block's $0.504$ and $0.352$, with $\max\lVert Q\rVert$ at $0.7058$ and $0.4617$. Sampling bounds the supremum from below only, so neither set of figures measures the loss, and the honest form of the sentence is that the loss is at least this large.

**The loss grows with $n$ rather than sitting at an order of magnitude.** The ratio of bound to sampled maximum is $6.5$ at $n=6$, $14.4$ at $n=8$ and above $25$ at $n=10$, consistent with a true supremum that decreases in $n$ against a bound that grows linearly. That matters for the radius, whose stated $n^{-2}$ decay is an artefact of the constant rather than of the geometry.

**A sharper constant is visibly available from the same two ingredients, and it changes the growth rate.** Write $G_{ab}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_aXA_bX)$, so $X^{\#}=-\tfrac12G$ and $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert=\tfrac12\lVert G\rVert_F$. With $U$ the map $\xi\mapsto A_\xi X$ and $V$ the map $\xi\mapsto(A_\xi X)^{\mathsf T}$, $G=V^{*}U$, so $\lVert G\rVert_F\le\lVert V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert U\rVert_F$. The Casimir identity gives $\lVert U\rVert_F^2=2(n-2)\lVert X\rVert^2$, and $\lVert V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sigma\lVert X\rVert$ with $\sigma=\max_{\lVert\xi\rVert=1}\lVert\mathrm{ad}_\xi\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$, which is at most $2$ by $\lVert[\xi,\eta]\rVert\le2\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert\eta\rVert$ together with $\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert\xi\rVert$ for antisymmetric $\xi$. Hence

$$\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le\sqrt{2(n-2)}\,\lVert X\rVert^2 ,$$

proved with one extra elementary line and strictly better than $n-2$ for every $n\ge5$: $2.83$ against $4$ at $n=6$, $3.46$ against $6$ at $n=8$, $4.47$ against $10$ at $n=12$. **Screened, $\sigma$ is $\sqrt2$ at $n=4,6,8,10,12$ to six figures**, which would give $\sqrt{n-2}$, and the chain $\lVert G\rVert_F\le\lVert V\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lVert U\rVert_F$ was verified directly at random symmetric $X$ (worst ratio $0.687$ at $n=4$, falling with $n$) together with $\lVert X^{\#}\rVert\le\sqrt{n-2}\lVert X\rVert^2$. The block's claim that "a sharp constant would need the extremal $X$ rather than a norm inequality" is therefore too strong: a better norm inequality gives $O(\sqrt n)$ in place of $O(n)$, and it takes the radius from $n^{-2}$ to $n^{-3/2}$. **[Lemma L143](../findings/bounds.md#lemma-l143-suplvert-prvertle1sqrtn-2-from-the-two-recorded-marginals) reached $\sqrt{n-2}$ at the scalar level from these same two marginals**, which is where the shape of the improvement was visible on the record already.

## 12. What would break it, and what was done to look

**The single falsifier is the reading of the Killing marginal.** [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) records that the marginal has two readings, $2(n-2)$ over ordered pairs and $n-2$ over unordered, that consumers use both, and that one must convert before comparing constants. Had L215 taken the unordered reading, the Casimir scalar would have been $(n-2)$ and the claimed bound $\tfrac{n-2}{2}\lVert X\rVert^2$, that is, **twice as strong as its own argument supports**, and the block's screen could not have caught it: the sampled maxima are around $0.6$ at $n=6$, so a halved bound violates nothing that was measured. This is the same factor of two that [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) says has entered this record twice by two routes. The block took the ordered reading, and it is the correct one for $\sum_aA_a^{\mathsf T}A_a$, verified at zero residual at $n=3\ldots12$.

**Second candidate, the $\tfrac12$ in the $\#$ formula**, which [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) flags as the other route to the same error. Checked by the record's own mandated test: the formula L215 quotes returns $\mathrm{id}^{\#}=(n-2)\mathrm{id}$ exactly at $n=4,6,8,10$, so the normalisation in the block is the record's.

**Third candidate, the availability of $\gamma$**, covered in section 8, and it is where the claim actually gives way.

**What was done to look.** Rebuilt the structure constants and the Casimir sum independently at $n=3\ldots12$ and at $n=4$ blockwise on $\Lambda^2_\pm$; rebuilt $\theta_0(n)$ and the radius at $n=6,8,10,12$ from criticality; sampled $400$ unit Weyl elements at $n=6,8$ under a different seed from the block's; tested the rank-one equality case and the trace-free obstruction to it; and verified the sharper chain of section 11 at random symmetric $X$ at $n=4,6,8,10$. ## 13. The Lean file types the arithmetic shadow, and says so
