## 0. Conventions, fixed once

Quoted from the brief:

- $\mathcal W_n$ is the space of algebraic Weyl tensors on $\mathbb R^n$: 4-tensors with the curvature symmetries $W_{ijkl}=-W_{jikl}=-W_{ijlk}=W_{klij}$, the first Bianchi identity $W_{ijkl}+W_{iklj}+W_{iljk}=0$, and every Ricci contraction zero, with the Frobenius inner product. $\mathcal W_n=0$ for $n\le3$.
- $O(n)$, and its subgroups $SO(n)$ and the signed permutation group $B_n$, act on $\mathcal W_n$ by $(g\cdot W)_{ijkl}=g_{ia}g_{jb}g_{kc}g_{ld}W_{abcd}$. Zero-padding sends $W\in\mathcal W_{n_0}$ to the tensor on $\mathbb R^n$ with the same components on indices in $[n_0]$ and zero elsewhere.
- $\mathrm{Sym}^d\mathcal W_n^*$ is the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$ on $\mathcal W_n$.
- The step: "for every $n\ge n_0$, an $SO(n_0)$-invariant form $F$ of degree $d$ on $\mathcal W_{n_0}$ has an $SO(n)$-invariant extension to $\mathcal W_n$, a form restricting to $F$ on the padded copy."

Repeated indices are summed. $\mathrm{pad}:\mathcal W_{n_0}\to\mathcal W_n$ denotes zero-padding, and $r=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)\in O(n_0)$.

## 1. Hypothesis inventory

1. **Padding lands in $\mathcal W_n$.** Stated in the Setting of Passage A and in the conventions. It is also immediate: every symmetry, Bianchi and Ricci relation of $\mathrm{pad}(V)$ either involves only indices in $[n_0]$, where it is a relation of $V$, or has every term carrying an index above $n_0$, where every term is zero. Checked.
2. **Padding is equivariant for block embeddings.** For $g\in O(n_0)$ and $\hat g=\mathrm{blkdiag}(g,g')$ with $g'\in O(n-n_0)$, $\hat g\cdot\mathrm{pad}(V)=\mathrm{pad}(g\cdot V)$. A component of $\hat g\cdot\mathrm{pad}(V)$ is $\hat g_{ia}\hat g_{jb}\hat g_{kc}\hat g_{ld}\,\mathrm{pad}(V)_{abcd}$. The summand vanishes unless $a,b,c,d\in[n_0]$, and then $\hat g_{ia}=0$ unless $i\in[n_0]$, where $\hat g_{ia}=g_{ia}$. So the result is $\mathrm{pad}(g\cdot V)$, whatever $g'$ is. Checked.
3. **$O(n_0)$ is generated by $SO(n_0)$ and $r$.** $O(n_0)=SO(n_0)\sqcup r\,SO(n_0)$, since $\det r=-1$. Checked.
4. **Part 1 for $O$.** Used only for the "if" direction of the characterisation in section 3.4, which lies outside H1. Its proof step (ii) is sound as quoted: a complete contraction over $[n]$ of padded factors equals the same contraction over $[n_0]$, because every index sits on a Weyl slot. Taken as given for that direction.
5. **Nothing from Levin and Chandrasekaran enters.** Part 1 and its SO version involve no presentation degree and no Proposition 2.9. The failure below uses only the definitions above.

## 2. The object: $F$ is nonzero, $SO(4)$-invariant, and reflection-odd

### 2.1 Transformation law

For $g\in O(4)$,
$$F(g\cdot W)=\varepsilon_{ijkl}\,g_{a\alpha}g_{b\beta}g_{i\gamma}g_{j\delta}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\;g_{a\alpha'}g_{b\beta'}g_{k\gamma'}g_{l\delta'}W_{\alpha'\beta'\gamma'\delta'}.$$
Orthogonality gives $g_{a\alpha}g_{a\alpha'}=\delta_{\alpha\alpha'}$ and $g_{b\beta}g_{b\beta'}=\delta_{\beta\beta'}$. The determinant identity gives $\varepsilon_{ijkl}g_{i\gamma}g_{j\delta}g_{k\gamma'}g_{l\delta'}=\det(g)\,\varepsilon_{\gamma\delta\gamma'\delta'}$. Hence
$$F(g\cdot W)=\det(g)\,F(W)\qquad(g\in O(4)).$$
So $F$ is $SO(4)$-invariant and $F(r\cdot W)=-F(W)$. It remains to show $F\not\equiv0$ on $\mathcal W_4$.

### 2.2 A Weyl tensor with $F\ne0$

Let $\omega^1,\omega^2$ be the antisymmetric $4\times4$ matrices with $\omega^1_{12}=\omega^1_{34}=1$ and $\omega^2_{13}=1$, $\omega^2_{24}=-1$, other entries above the diagonal zero. Set
$$W_{ijkl}=\omega^1_{ij}\omega^1_{kl}-\omega^2_{ij}\omega^2_{kl}.$$

**Curvature symmetries.** Each $\omega\otimes\omega$ with $\omega$ antisymmetric is antisymmetric in $ij$, antisymmetric in $kl$, and symmetric under $(ij)\leftrightarrow(kl)$. Checked.

**Components.** Write $K_{ab}=W_{abab}$ for $a\ne b$. Then $K_{12}=K_{34}=1$, $K_{13}=K_{24}=-1$, $K_{14}=K_{23}=0$. The components with four distinct indices are $x=W_{1234}=1$, $y=W_{1342}=-\omega^2_{13}\omega^2_{42}=-1$, $z=W_{1423}=0$. Every nonzero component has index pairs $\{i,j\},\{k,l\}$ taken from the supports $\{12\},\{34\}$ of $\omega^1$ or $\{13\},\{24\}$ of $\omega^2$, so the two pairs are either equal or complementary. In particular no component of the form $W_{abac}$ with $b\ne c$ is nonzero.

**Bianchi identity.** Take $B_{ijkl}=W_{ijkl}+W_{iklj}+W_{iljk}$ and sort by the multiset of indices.

- A value occurring three or more times forces a repeated index inside some pair in every term, so every term is zero.
- Three distinct values with one repeated: each term is zero or of the form $W_{abac}$ with $b\ne c$, which vanishes.
- Two values $a,b$, each twice, with $i=a$: the three orderings of $(j,k,l)$ give $W_{aabb}+W_{abba}+W_{abab}=0-K_{ab}+K_{ab}=0$, then $W_{abab}+W_{aabb}+W_{abba}=K_{ab}+0-K_{ab}=0$, then $W_{abba}+W_{abab}+W_{aabb}=-K_{ab}+K_{ab}+0=0$.
- Four distinct values: $B_{1234}=x+y+z$. Reading components back to $x,y,z$ through the pair symmetries, $B_{2134}=W_{2134}+W_{2341}+W_{2413}=-x-z-y$, $B_{3124}=W_{3124}+W_{3241}+W_{3412}=y+z+x$, $B_{4123}=W_{4123}+W_{4231}+W_{4312}=-z-y-x$. $B$ is antisymmetric in its last three slots (a cyclic sum of an object antisymmetric in its last two slots), so these four determine every ordering up to sign. All vanish because $x+y+z=1-1+0=0$.

**Ricci contractions.** $\mathrm{Ric}_{jl}=W_{ajal}$. For $j\ne l$ each summand has pairs $\{a,j\},\{a,l\}$ sharing exactly one index, so it vanishes. On the diagonal, $\mathrm{Ric}_{11}=K_{12}+K_{13}+K_{14}=0$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{22}=K_{12}+K_{23}+K_{24}=0$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{33}=K_{13}+K_{23}+K_{34}=0$, $\mathrm{Ric}_{44}=K_{14}+K_{24}+K_{34}=0$.

So $W\in\mathcal W_4$.

**Value of $F$.** For an antisymmetric $4\times4$ matrix $M$, the terms of $\varepsilon_{ijkl}M_{ij}M_{kl}$ group by the partition $\{\{i,j\},\{k,l\}\}$ of $[4]$. Each partition contributes eight ordered terms of equal value, with sign $\varepsilon_{1234}=+1$, $\varepsilon_{1324}=-1$, $\varepsilon_{1423}=+1$. Hence $\varepsilon_{ijkl}M_{ij}M_{kl}=8\,\mathrm{Pf}(M)$ with $\mathrm{Pf}(M)=M_{12}M_{34}-M_{13}M_{24}+M_{14}M_{23}$. For fixed $(a,b)$ the matrix $M^{ab}_{ij}=W_{abij}$ equals $s\,\omega^1+t\,\omega^2$ with $s=\omega^1_{ab}$ and $t=-\omega^2_{ab}$. Its entries are $M_{12}=M_{34}=s$, $M_{13}=t$, $M_{24}=-t$, $M_{14}=M_{23}=0$, so $\mathrm{Pf}(M^{ab})=s^2+t^2$. Summing over ordered $(a,b)$, and noting that each of $\omega^1,\omega^2$ has four entries equal to $\pm1$,
$$F(W)=8\sum_{a,b}\big((\omega^1_{ab})^2+(\omega^2_{ab})^2\big)=8(4+4)=64.$$
By 2.1, $F(r\cdot W)=-64$.

## 3. The argument

### 3.1 Every $SO(n)$-invariant extension is reflection-even on the padded copy

Let $n>n_0\ge4$, let $G$ be any $SO(n)$-invariant function on $\mathcal W_n$, and put $h=\mathrm{blkdiag}(r,-1,I_{n-n_0-1})$. Then $\det h=(-1)(-1)=1$, so $h\in SO(n)$. By inventory item 2 with $g=r$ and $g'=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)$, $h\cdot\mathrm{pad}(V)=\mathrm{pad}(r\cdot V)$ for every $V\in\mathcal W_{n_0}$. Hence
$$G(\mathrm{pad}(r\cdot V))=G(h\cdot\mathrm{pad}(V))=G(\mathrm{pad}(V)).$$
So $G\circ\mathrm{pad}$ is $r$-invariant. It is also $SO(n_0)$-invariant, through $\mathrm{blkdiag}(g,I)\in SO(n)$. By item 3 it is $O(n_0)$-invariant.

### 3.2 The failure, located and in closed form

Take $n_0=4$, $d=2$, $n=5$ and the supplied $F$. Suppose $G\in\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal W_5^*$ is $SO(5)$-invariant with $G\circ\mathrm{pad}=F$. With $h=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1,-1)$ and the $W$ of 2.2, section 3.1 gives
$$-64=F(r\cdot W)=G(\mathrm{pad}(r\cdot W))=G(h\cdot\mathrm{pad}(W))=G(\mathrm{pad}(W))=F(W)=64,$$
a contradiction. **No $SO(5)$-invariant extension of $F$ exists.** The same computation with $h=\mathrm{blkdiag}(r,-1,I_{n-5})$ excludes every $n\ge5$. Only $n=n_0=4$, where $G=F$, survives. The step asserts existence at every $n\ge n_0$, so it fails at $n=5$.

Neither the degree nor polynomiality enters. The argument excludes any $SO(n)$-invariant function whatever.

### 3.3 Three checks before FAILS

- **Located.** The failure is the extension of the quantifier of part 1 from $O(n_0)$-invariant to $SO(n_0)$-invariant $F$, at the first $n>n_0$. Part 1's proof applies the first fundamental theorem for $O(n_0)$, which yields only $\delta$-contractions. An $SO(n_0)$-invariant form also admits $\varepsilon_{n_0}$-contractions, and an $\varepsilon$ with $n_0$ indices has no counterpart on $\mathcal W_n$ that restricts correctly under padding.
- **Exhibited.** $W=\omega^1\otimes\omega^1-\omega^2\otimes\omega^2\in\mathcal W_4$ with $F(W)=64$ and $F(r\cdot W)=-64$, together with $h=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1,-1)\in SO(5)$ and $h\cdot\mathrm{pad}(W)=\mathrm{pad}(r\cdot W)$.
- **Strongest defence.** The sentence's own reason speaks of "an $SO(n_0)$-invariant form with a $B_n$-invariant extension". Read that way, the SO clause is a remark about part 2 and silently assumes that $F$ has some extension invariant under a group containing $r$. Under that hypothesis the remark is correct: $\mathrm{blkdiag}(r,I)\in B_n$ makes $F$ $r$-invariant, hence $O(n_0)$-invariant, and part 1 then applies unchanged. Two further readings also make H1 true. For odd $n_0$, $-I_{n_0}\in O(n_0)\setminus SO(n_0)$ acts on 4-tensors as $(-1)^4=1$, so every $SO(n_0)$-invariant form is $O(n_0)$-invariant. And H1 holds for any $F$ assumed $O(n_0)$-invariant. Each reading needs a hypothesis the step does not state, whether a $B_n$-invariant extension, odd $n_0$, or $O(n_0)$-invariance, and the step as written quantifies over all $SO(n_0)$-invariant forms at all $n_0$. The brief's range includes $n_0=4$ and the working case $n_0=12$, both even. The defence therefore does not rescue the step as written.

### 3.4 What the step can say

For $n>n_0$ and $SO(n_0)$-invariant $F$: **$F$ has an $SO(n)$-invariant extension if and only if $F$ is $O(n_0)$-invariant.** The "only if" direction is section 3.1. The "if" direction is part 1, since an $O(n)$-invariant extension is $SO(n)$-invariant. The corrected clause for part 1 reads: the same holds with $SO$ in place of $O$ exactly for those $SO(n_0)$-invariant forms that are $r$-invariant, which is all of them when $n_0$ is odd.

A side observation outside H1. The SO reading of part 2 ("exactly one $B_n$-invariant extension") has the same defect, because a reflection-odd $F$ has no $B_n$-invariant extension at all. The quoted reason proves "at most one" and correctly reduces existence to $O(n_0)$-invariance. The supplied $F$ does not itself reach part 2's range, since $k_B(2)\ge8>4$ by Passage A.

**Verdict: FAILS**
