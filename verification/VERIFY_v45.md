## 0. Conventions, fixed once

$n=2m$, $W^*$ the Weyl operator of $S^m\times S^m$ on $\Lambda^2$, self-adjoint with $\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*=\{\lambda_-,\lambda_+\}$, $\lambda_-<0<\lambda_+$. $g_n$ is the **absolute** spectral gap at $W^*$, that is $\min\{\lvert\lambda\rvert:\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*\}$ ([findings/second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md):960 and :1108, where it is $g_n=\tfrac2n\sqrt{\tfrac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}$). $\Gamma=\{\lambda_-+\alpha g_ne^{i\theta}:\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}$. $\rho=\gamma g_n/\lVert H\rVert_{op}$, $W(z)=W^*+zH$. $D=\lvert\lambda_+-\lambda_-\rvert/g_n$, $d=\min(\alpha,D-\alpha)$.

**The spectrum, rebuilt cold, because two of the entry's constants depend on it.** [findings/second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md):1113 gives $W^*_-=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert(\mathrm{id}-P_{\Lambda^2V_1}-P_{\Lambda^2V_2})$, so the negative eigenspace is $V_1\wedge V_2$ with $\dim=m^2$ and the positive is $\Lambda^2V_1\oplus\Lambda^2V_2$ with $\dim=m(m-1)$; these sum to $2m^2-m=\binom{2m}2$. Tracelessness is $\lambda_+\,m(m-1)+\lambda_-m^2=0$, hence $\lambda_+/\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=m/(m-1)$. Since $m/(m-1)>1$ for every $m\ge2$, the smaller modulus is $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$, so $g_n=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$ **is a consequence of the base point and not a definition**, and $\lambda_+=\tfrac m{m-1}g_n$, $\lVert W^*\rVert_{op}=\lambda_+$, and
$$D=1+\tfrac m{m-1}=\tfrac{2m-1}{m-1}.$$
Every one of those is exact and every one is used by the entry.

## 1. The hypothesis inventory

**Stated in the entry.** (H1) $\Gamma$ encloses the negative spectrum only. (H2) at $W^*$ that spectrum is the single eigenvalue $\lambda_-$. (H3) $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$, attributed to "the definition of the gap". (H4) $\Gamma$ is the circle of radius $\alpha g_n$ about $\lambda_-$. (H5) $\rho=\gamma g_n/\lVert H\rVert_{op}$. (H6) $W^*$ is symmetric, in the box only. (H7) $D=\tfrac{2m-1}{m-1}$ and $\lambda_+=\tfrac m{m-1}g_n$.

**Used and never stated.** (U1) the base point is $W^*=S^m\times S^m$, without it neither H2 nor H7 is available, and H3 is not available either, since $g_n=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$ is the multiplicity computation of §0 and not a definition. (U2) $\ker W^*=0$, equivalently $0\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*$, the entry uses this fact at line 1465 while attributing it to "the agreed list above" rather than carrying it as its own hypothesis. (U3) $m\ge2$, without which $D$ is undefined and the positive eigenspace is empty. (U4) $\alpha<D$, so that $\lambda_+$ stays outside $\Gamma$ and $d$ means what C7 says; the entry asserts $\alpha<1$ instead. (U5) $\gamma<d$, the Neumann condition standing behind the $\alpha-\gamma$ in the Consequence, the entry never writes a Neumann step. (U6) $\lVert H\rVert_{op}\le1$ and $W(z)=W^*+zH$ affine, presupposed by H5.

**Which the written argument for the surviving identity actually uses: H3 and H4, and $\alpha>0$. Nothing else.** Not H1, not H2, not H6, not H7, and none of U1–U6. This is the whole of §2 and it is why the clause is safe to export even though its home entry is refuted, the identity is a statement about a circle in $\mathbb C$, and the operator theory around it is inert for it.

**Hypotheses of the imported results, checked at the object.** The entry imports (i) the Riesz representation $W_-=-\tfrac1{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma\zeta(W-\zeta)^{-1}d\zeta$, which needs $\Gamma$ rectifiable, positively oriented, and disjoint from $\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W$ with the negative part inside, satisfied at $W^*$ for $0<\alpha<D$, $\alpha\ne1$ being unnecessary since $0\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*$; (ii) the ML estimate, which needs only continuity of the integrand on $\Gamma$; (iii) the self-adjoint resolvent identity $\lVert(W^*-\zeta)^{-1}\rVert=1/\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(\zeta,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*)$, which needs normality on the complexification, $W^*$ is real symmetric so its complexification is Hermitian and normal, and the step is sound though the entry leaves the complexification implicit.

## 2. Question 1, first part, is the identity proved, and at what generality

**What the entry writes is one sentence** (line 1463): *"THE NUMERATOR IS $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert$, AND ON A CIRCLE ABOUT $\lambda_-$ IT IS $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert+\alpha g_n=(1+\alpha)g_n$."* No justification is attached. The justification is one line and I give it here only to say what the sentence would have to contain, not to supply an argument the entry lacks: for $\zeta=c+re^{i\theta}$, $\lvert\zeta\rvert\le\lvert c\rvert+r$ by the triangle inequality, with equality at $\theta=\arg c$, the point $\zeta^*=c(1+r/\lvert c\rvert)$, which lies on the circle since $\lvert\zeta^*-c\rvert=r$. So the supremum is a maximum and equals $\lvert c\rvert+r$ exactly.

**Generality.** With $c=\lambda_-$ and $r=\alpha g_n$ this is $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert+\alpha g_n$, and the second equality $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert+\alpha g_n=(1+\alpha)g_n$ is H3 and nothing else. Therefore:

- **It does not need $\lambda_-$ real.** The entry's "for any complex centre" is correct and is the right generality claim.
- **It does not need $\lambda_-$ negative**, nor that it be an eigenvalue, nor that $\Gamma$ enclose anything.
- **It does need $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$**, and this is the only hypothesis it needs. Read as a statement about an arbitrary centre with no modulus condition the clause is false: take a self-adjoint operator with $\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}=\{-2g,\,g\}$, so that the absolute gap is $g$ and the negative eigenvalue has modulus $2g$; the circle of radius $\alpha g$ about the negative eigenvalue has $\max\lvert\zeta\rvert=(2+\alpha)g\ne(1+\alpha)g$. **The correct general form is $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=\big(\lvert\lambda_-\rvert/g_n+\alpha\big)g_n$**, of which $(1+\alpha)g_n$ is the case $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$.

**Verdict on C1.** The identity is true and attained; the entry states it correctly and does not write its proof, which is a triangle equality. I record it as `PROVEN, hypotheses corrected`, the correction being that the hypothesis is the centre's **modulus**, not its reality, and that the entry's own attribution of $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$ to "the definition of the gap" is wrong: $g_n$ is defined as the absolute gap and the equality with $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$ is the multiplicity computation of §0, available only at $W^*=S^m\times S^m$.

## 3. Question 1, second part, what it gives when $\Gamma$ encircles $0$

$\Gamma$ encircles $0$ exactly when $\alpha>1$, since $\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(0,\lambda_-)=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$ and the radius is $\alpha g_n$. **The maximum is unaffected.** What encirclement changes is $\min_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=\lvert\alpha-1\rvert g_n$, which drops to $0$ at $\alpha=1$ and rises again beyond, and $\min_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert$ appears nowhere in this entry, in [L156](../findings/second-order.md), or in any factor of either bound. So the identity is exactly as true at $\alpha=\tfrac{11}{10}$ as at $\alpha=\tfrac12$.

**Checked at the value that matters.** At $m=6$, $D=\tfrac{11}5$ and L156's optimum is $\alpha^*=\tfrac{D}2=\tfrac{11}{10}$. Then $\Gamma$ has centre $-g_n$ and radius $\tfrac{11}{10}g_n$, so $0$ is interior at depth $\tfrac1{10}g_n$; $\lambda_+=\tfrac65g_n$ sits at distance $(D-\alpha^*)g_n=\tfrac{11}{10}g_n$ from $\Gamma$, hence outside; and $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=\tfrac{21}{10}g_n=(1+\alpha^*)g_n$. **The identity holds at the point of use.**

## 4. Quantifier order and scope

**Free, and never optimised inside this entry:** $\alpha$, $\gamma$, $m$, and the direction $H$. The entry performs no optimisation and says so at line 1473; the optimisation is [L156](../findings/second-order.md)'s $\min_{(\alpha,\gamma)}J$. **Bound:** $\zeta$, under $\max_\Gamma$; $z$, under $\sup_{\lvert z\rvert=\rho}$ in the $M$ that the Consequence is a constant for.

**One $\max$ is distributed across two factors sharing a variable**, in the display at line 1461: $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert$ and $\max_\Gamma\lVert(W-\zeta)^{-1}\rVert$ are taken separately over the same $\zeta$. This is legitimate (both factors are non-negative and the direction is $\le$) but it is lossy in general, and it is worth recording exactly where it is not:

- **For $\alpha\le D/2$ there is no loss at all.** $\lvert\zeta-\lambda_-\rvert=\alpha g_n$ identically on $\Gamma$, and $\lvert\zeta-\lambda_+\rvert\ge(D-\alpha)g_n\ge\alpha g_n$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(\zeta,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*)\equiv\alpha g_n$ and the resolvent factor is **constant** on $\Gamma$. A constant factor distributes exactly.
- **For $\alpha>D/2$ the loss is real.** The resolvent maximum is attained at the single point $\zeta_1=\lambda_-+\alpha g_n=(\alpha-1)g_n$ nearest $\lambda_+$, and $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert$ at $\zeta_2=\lambda_--\alpha g_n=-(1+\alpha)g_n$, **antipodal points of $\Gamma$**. At $m=6$, $\alpha=\tfrac43$: at $\zeta_1$ the pointwise product $\lvert\zeta\rvert/\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}$ is $\tfrac{1/3}{13/15}=\tfrac5{13}$ and at $\zeta_2$ it is $\tfrac{7/3}{4/3}=\tfrac74$, while the distributed bound is $\tfrac{7/3}{13/15}=\tfrac{35}{13}$. The distributed bound is not attained at either.

Since L156's optimum is exactly $\alpha^*=D/2$, the distribution is lossless where the chain uses it. **No finding: the quantifier order is sound and the one distribution costs nothing at the point of use.**

## 5. The edges of the ranges

**The admissible range of $\alpha$.** The genuine constraints are $\alpha>0$ and $\alpha<D$: below $D$, $\lambda_+$ lies outside $\Gamma$ and the Riesz projection is the one intended; at $\alpha\ge D$ it is a different projection. **$\alpha<1$ is not a constraint**, because $0\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*$ and a contour enclosing a point of the resolvent set adds no spectral projection, this is [L156](../findings/second-order.md)'s ruling and it is right. The entry's own box at line 1465 lists both candidates and declines to rule; its next paragraph (line 1467) rules for $\alpha<1$. See §7, C4.

**The smallest admissible dimension.** The entry states none. $D=\tfrac{2m-1}{m-1}$ and $\lambda_+=\tfrac m{m-1}g_n$ both require $m\ge2$; at $m=1$ the positive eigenspace $\Lambda^2V_1\oplus\Lambda^2V_2$ is empty and $W^*=0$. The programme's own range is $n\ge12$, i.e. $m\ge6$, which the entry also does not state. **The identity itself carries no dimension hypothesis** (it holds for every $\alpha>0$ and every centre of modulus $g_n$) so the omission costs the surviving clause nothing and costs the constant its range, which is what the heading already records.

**$\ker W\ne0$, the record's named witness.** [findings/second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md):1173 carries it: *"$W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is Morse–Bott nondegenerate with $\dim\ker W=3$, so $g_n$, $\rho$, $M$ and branches 2–3 **do not exist there**."* **This entry asserts nothing at a kernel witness, and cannot**: with $0\in\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W$ the absolute gap is $g_n=0$, so $\Gamma$ has radius $0$, $\rho$ is undefined, and $W\mapsto W_-$ is not analytic at the base point. The identity is vacuous there rather than false. The entry's silence is therefore correct in substance but is silence: it never names $\ker W^*=0$ as a hypothesis, and uses $0\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(W^*)$ at line 1465 as a borrowed fact. [L156](../findings/second-order.md) states it explicitly ("*so that $\ker W^*=0$*"), so the citing entry is the one carrying the hypothesis, which is the wrong way round but is not an error of consequence.

**The edge $\alpha=1$ itself.** At $\alpha=1$ the contour passes through $0$. Since $0\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*$ this is admissible for $W^*$ (the identity gives $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=2g_n$ and the resolvent is finite there) and L156's domain $0<\alpha<D$ correctly includes it. **No finding.**

## 6. Question 1, third part, and the round's centre, proven against cited

[L156](../findings/second-order.md)'s proof (line 1425) cites: *"the maximum $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=(1+\alpha)g_n$ (L152's surviving half)"*, as the second of five factors, evaluated on its whole domain $0<\alpha<D$ and in particular at $\alpha^*=D/2>1$.

**What L152 establishes:** $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=(1+\alpha)g_n$ for every $\alpha>0$, given a circle of radius $\alpha g_n$ about a centre of modulus $g_n$.
**What L156 needs:** the same, on $0<\alpha<D$, at the centre $\lambda_-$ of $W^*=S^m\times S^m$, where $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$ holds by §0.

**The two match, and the import is sound.** L156 restates $\Gamma$, $\lambda_-$, $g_n$ and the base point in its own hypothesis line, so the normalisation the clause needs is present at the point of use and is not being smuggled. The three later rounds that take the identity as given are safe on this point.

**But the match is an accident of the clause's robustness, and one thing is genuinely wrong across the citation.** L152's text at line 1467 rules that $\alpha<1$ binds. L156's optimum is $\alpha^*=D/2$, which exceeds $1$ for every $m\ge2$, $D/2=1+\tfrac1{2(m-1)}$, so $\alpha^*=\tfrac{11}{10}$ at $m=6$ and $\alpha^*\downarrow1$ only in the limit. **So L156 evaluates a clause at parameter values whose home entry declares them inadmissible.** The clause survives because its proof uses neither the admissibility ruling nor anything $\alpha<1$ would protect, see §2, where the used hypotheses are H3 and H4 alone. A reader who followed the citation back would find the entry forbidding the very $\alpha$ at which the citation stands, with nothing in the entry's heading to say the prohibition had fallen.

**This is the finding the round was called for, and it is a bookkeeping defect rather than a mathematical one:** what travels without its refutation is not the identity, which needs none, but the entry's $\alpha<1$ ruling, which travels *against* three later rounds without being marked as fallen.

## 7. Question 2, does the refutation reach the whole entry or only the constant

The heading names three things: the identity holds; the constant has no range; the disagreement was never one. **The refutation reaches further than the heading's list, in two more places, and both are about $\alpha$.**

### C4, "the first binding" (line 1467), BROKEN and unlisted

**Located.** *"the contour must separate $\lambda_-$ from both $0$ and $\lambda_+$, so $\alpha<1$ keeps $0$ outside and $\alpha<1+\tfrac m{m-1}$ keeps $\lambda_+$ outside, the first binding."*

**Exhibited.** The premise "must separate $\lambda_-$ from $0$" is false at this base point: $0\notin\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*$ by §0, so the Riesz projection $\tfrac{-1}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma(W^*-\zeta)^{-1}d\zeta$ is unchanged by enclosing $0$, the integrand being holomorphic there. Concretely at $m=6$, $\alpha=\tfrac{11}{10}$: $\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}W^*=\{-g_n,\tfrac65g_n\}$, $\Gamma$ has centre $-g_n$ and radius $\tfrac{11}{10}g_n$, the enclosed spectrum is $\{\lambda_-\}$ exactly, and $\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(\Gamma,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}})=\min(\tfrac{11}{10},\tfrac{11}5-\tfrac{11}{10})g_n=\tfrac{11}{10}g_n>0$. Every object in the bound exists and the projection is the intended one, at an $\alpha$ the sentence forbids. The arithmetic inside the sentence is sound ($1+\tfrac m{m-1}=D=2+\tfrac1{m-1}>1$, so $1$ is indeed the smaller of the two candidates) but the smaller of two candidates binds only if both are constraints, and the first is not.

**Argued the opposite.** The most charitable reading is that the box two lines above (line 1465) has already declared the range unsettled, *"Which one binds decides the optimisation, and the record currently supports both. Owed with L152's own Gate 2"*, so line 1467 is a candidate ruling inside an entry that flags its own uncertainty, and the whole entry is marked `REFUTED AS STATED` in any case. **That reading does not save it**: the box defers between $\alpha<1$ and $\alpha<D$, and the paragraph then rules for $\alpha<1$ without marking itself as the deferred question; and the entry's heading, which is what a reader consults to learn what fell, lists the constant and the straw man and not this. **The finding stands.**

### C5, the closing dichotomy (line 1473), BROKEN

**Located.** *"$\alpha<1$ excludes the optimum [r456](../reviews/REVIEW_r456.md) locates near $\alpha=\tfrac43$, so either that optimum uses a contour enclosing $\lambda_+$ as well, which is a different projection, or it is outside the admissible range."*

**Exhibited.** At $m=6$, $\alpha=\tfrac43$: $\Gamma$ has centre $-g_n$, radius $\tfrac43g_n$, so $0$ is enclosed at depth $\tfrac13g_n$, while $\lambda_+=\tfrac65g_n$ lies at distance $(D-\alpha)g_n=(\tfrac{11}5-\tfrac43)g_n=\tfrac{13}{15}g_n$ outside $\Gamma$. **Neither horn holds:** the contour does not enclose $\lambda_+$, and it is not outside the admissible range, the admissible range being $0<\alpha<D=\tfrac{11}5$. The omitted third case ($0$ enclosed, $\lambda_+$ outside, projection unchanged), is what actually obtains, and it is the case in which L156's whole optimisation lives.

**Argued the opposite.** Charitably, the dichotomy is stated conditionally on the $\alpha<1$ ruling of C4 ("$\alpha<1$ excludes …"), so it inherits rather than compounds that error, and the entry closes by handing the question to a successor. **True, and it still leaves a false disjunction standing in an entry that is cited for a clause three lines above it.** Recorded as a consequence of C4 rather than an independent defect.

### C6, the Consequence has no derivation in this entry, GAP

**Located.** The display at line 1461 has three factors; the Consequence at line 1469 asserts $C(\alpha,\gamma)=\tfrac{1+\alpha}{\alpha-\gamma}$.

**Exhibited.** Multiply the display out, granting the most favourable resolvent bound the entry could intend, $\max_\Gamma\lVert(W-\zeta)^{-1}\rVert\le1/((\alpha-\gamma)g_n)$:
$$\tfrac1{2\pi}\cdot 2\pi\alpha g_n\cdot(1+\alpha)g_n\cdot\tfrac1{(\alpha-\gamma)g_n}=\tfrac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}{\alpha-\gamma}\,g_n .$$
That is a bound on $\lVert W_-\rVert$ of size $\Theta(g_n)$ with no smallness in $\gamma$, whereas the constant is the constant in $M\le C\gamma g_n$ for $M=\sup_{\lvert z\rvert=\rho}\lVert A(z)\rVert$ with $A(0)=\mathrm{Alt}(W^*_-)=0$. At $(\alpha,\gamma)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac14)$ the display gives $\tfrac{(1/2)(3/2)}{1/4}g_n=3g_n$ while the claimed $C\gamma g_n$ is $6\cdot\tfrac14g_n=\tfrac32g_n$, a factor $\alpha/\gamma=2$, and, more to the point, a bound on a different quantity. **The two missing factors are named only in [L156](../findings/second-order.md)** (line 1425): the perturbation $\lVert zH\rVert\le\gamma g_n$ and the second, unperturbed resolvent $1/(dg_n)$, both of which come from the resolvent-**difference** identity that this entry never writes.

**Argued the opposite.** The entry's declared purpose is the numerator alone; the surrounding chain is r456's and is recorded upstream at [findings/second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md):1394 as $M\le C(\alpha,\gamma)\rho$, so the Consequence can be read as quoting a formula rather than deriving one. **Accepted in part**, this is why the verdict is `GAP` and not `BROKEN`, and why it falls inside the heading's second clause rather than extending the refutation. The value $\tfrac{1+\alpha}{\alpha-\gamma}$ is correct on $\alpha\le D/2$, as L156 confirms ($\tfrac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}{d(d-\gamma)}$ with $d=\alpha$). **The defect is that this entry contains no argument for it, not that it is wrong.**

### What the refutation does **not** reach

- **The identity itself.** §2.
- **The resolvent range claim, C7.** For self-adjoint $W^*$, $\lVert(W^*-\zeta)^{-1}\rVert=1/\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(\zeta,\mathop{\mathrm{spec}})$; on $\Gamma$, $\lvert\zeta-\lambda_-\rvert\equiv\alpha g_n$ and $\min_\Gamma\lvert\zeta-\lambda_+\rvert=\lvert\lambda_+-\lambda_-\rvert-\alpha g_n=(D-\alpha)g_n$ for $\alpha<D$, so $\max_\Gamma\lVert(W^*-\zeta)^{-1}\rVert=1/(\min(\alpha,D-\alpha)g_n)=1/(dg_n)$. Exact.
- **The arithmetic, C9, all four values hand-checked.** At $m=6$, $D=\tfrac{11}5$, $(\alpha,\gamma)=(\tfrac43,\tfrac23)$: $d=\min(\tfrac43,\tfrac{11}5-\tfrac43)=\tfrac{13}{15}$, and $\tfrac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}{d(d-\gamma)}=\tfrac{(4/3)(7/3)}{(13/15)(3/15)}=\tfrac{28/9}{39/225}=\tfrac{700}{39}=17.9\overline{48}$, against the recorded $\tfrac{1+\alpha}{\alpha-\gamma}=\tfrac{7/3}{2/3}=\tfrac72=3.5$. The numerator ratio $\tfrac{\alpha+m/(m-1)}{1+\alpha}=\tfrac{\alpha+6/5}{1+\alpha}$ is $\tfrac65$ at $\alpha\to0$, giving $20\%$, and $\tfrac{11/10+6/5}{11/10+1}=\tfrac{23/10}{21/10}=\tfrac{23}{21}$ at $\alpha^*=\tfrac{11}{10}$, giving $\tfrac2{21}=9.52\%$. And $\tfrac{1+\alpha}{\alpha-\gamma}$ at $(\tfrac12,\tfrac14)$ is $\tfrac{3/2}{1/4}=6$ exactly, while $\tfrac{\alpha+6/5}{\alpha-\gamma}=\tfrac{17/10}{1/4}=\tfrac{34}5=6.8$, which reaches $6$ only as $m\to\infty$. **All confirmed.**
- **C8, the straw-man diagnosis, confirmed at source.** [REVIEW_r457](../reviews/REVIEW_r457.md):72 bounds $\lVert\Pi(z)\rVert_{op}\le\tfrac\alpha{\alpha-\gamma}$ and $\lVert\Pi(z)-\Pi(0)\rVert_F\le\tfrac\rho{(\alpha-\gamma)g_n}$, and line 76 assembles $C=\tfrac{\alpha+m/(m-1)}{\alpha-\gamma}$, the $\tfrac m{m-1}$ arriving as $\lVert W^*\rVert_{op}/g_n=\lambda_+/g_n$ multiplying the second term, exactly as L152 says. No quantity of the form $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert$ appears anywhere in r457. Its domain is $0<\gamma<\alpha<1$ (line 126), inside $\alpha<1<D/2$, so it never meets the kink. **The entry's third clause is right on all three counts**, and the two bounds are genuinely different valid routes, of which L152's is the smaller since $1<\tfrac m{m-1}$.

### C10 and C11, two smaller things the heading also does not carry

## 10. What would falsify the surviving identity, and what I did to look for it

**The falsifier has exactly one shape.** On $\Gamma=\{c+\alpha g_ne^{i\theta}\}$, $\max\lvert\zeta\rvert=\lvert c\rvert+\alpha g_n$ is an equality with an exhibited maximiser, so nothing about the circle can break it. The identity $\lvert c\rvert+\alpha g_n=(1+\alpha)g_n$ fails exactly when $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert\ne g_n$, that is, **at a base point where the spectral point of smallest modulus is not $\lambda_-$**.

**Where such a point would have to live.** It requires either (i) $0<\lambda_+<\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$, or (ii) a third spectral value strictly between $0$ and $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$, or (iii) $0\in\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}$.

**What I did.** (i) is impossible on this family: tracelessness gives $\lambda_+\,m(m-1)=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert\,m^2$, so $\lambda_+/\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=m/(m-1)>1$ for every $m\ge2$, the negative eigenvalue is the smaller in modulus **because its eigenspace $V_1\wedge V_2$ is the larger**, $m^2>m(m-1)$, and no dimension can reverse that. (ii) is excluded at $W^*=S^m\times S^m$ by the two-valuedness recorded at [findings/second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md):1108 and rebuilt independently at [REVIEW_r457](../reviews/REVIEW_r457.md):68, which reaches the same $g_n^2=\tfrac{m-1}{m^2(2m-1)}$ from trace, norm and multiplicities. (iii) makes $g_n=0$ and empties the statement, §9.

**So the object that would falsify it is a base point outside the balanced family with three or more $\Lambda^2$ spectral values, at which the absolute gap is set by a positive eigenvalue while the contour is still drawn about $\lambda_-$**, an unbalanced $S^p\times S^q$ with $p\ne q$ is the natural candidate, since its two positive eigenvalues on $\Lambda^2V_1$ and $\Lambda^2V_2$ differ and the smaller of them could in principle fall below $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$. **L152 names no base point at all, so nothing in its text excludes such a point; [L156](../findings/second-order.md) excludes it by hypothesis.** Deciding whether an unbalanced product actually realises it is an eigenvalue computation on $\Lambda^2$ at $S^p\times S^q$, which this round did not perform and which no closed form on the record supplies. **It does not affect the identity as used**, since every use is at the balanced base point, and I record it as the exact shape of the object a successor would have to exhibit.

## Verdict

**`PROVEN, hypotheses corrected`**, for the clause the round exists to test.

- **The surviving identity $\max_\Gamma\lvert\zeta\rvert=(1+\alpha)g_n$ is true, attained, and proved by a triangle equality the entry states without writing.** Its one hypothesis is $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$; it needs neither $\lambda_-$ real nor $\lambda_-$ negative, and it is unaffected by $\Gamma$ encircling $0$. **[L156](../findings/second-order.md)'s import, including at $\alpha^*=D/2>1$, is sound.**
- **The correction:** $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=g_n$ is not "the definition of the gap", $g_n$ is the absolute gap and the equality is the multiplicity computation $\lambda_+/\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=m/(m-1)$, available only at $W^*=S^m\times S^m$. The clause as exported carries no hypothesis at all and is false at a centre of the wrong modulus.
- **The refutation reaches further than the heading says.** Beyond the constant and the straw man, two further clauses fall, both about $\alpha$: the ruling that $\alpha<1$ binds (C4) and the closing dichotomy built on it (C5), the second horn of which excludes the configuration L156's optimum actually occupies. Two smaller items are also unstruck: "three independent computations" (C10) and the body's $20\%$ that its own box withdraws (C11).
- **What is owed:** one line in L152's heading recording that the $\alpha<1$ ruling fell with the rest, and one sentence in [results.md](../results.md)'s L156 block defining $\Gamma$ and $\rho$, so that the normalisation the imported clause depends on is recoverable from the canonical record. **No new L152 block is owed**, the absence from [results.md](../results.md) is correct and matches L150 and L151.
