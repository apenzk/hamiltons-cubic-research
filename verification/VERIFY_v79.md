# VERIFY v79, the $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][SO_5]$ descent offered as the warrant for L87's step (3)

*The brief was cold: the argument was put neutrally, with no author, no history, and no property named to look for. **I ran nothing**, no script, no Sage, no numerics, and none written. Every value below is exact: derived by hand or read verbatim from a file. Files were read as text only, [orbit_span.sage](../scripts/reviewers/r178/orbit_span.sage) included. Log: [agent_logs/verify-L87-v79.log](../agent_logs/verify-L87-v79.log).*

## Verdict

**VALID WITH A NAMED GAP.**

**What is valid.** The inequality the argument exists to supply ($\dim\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_{5,\overline{\mathbb F_p}})\le\dim_{\mathbb C}U$), is correct, and the construction reaches it in the way the argument says: through a single $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$-linear map that is built from $W_0$ and the group scheme alone and mentions no sampled group element. That is precisely what the defect it replaces needed: the Cayley points of [orbit_span.sage](../scripts/reviewers/r178/orbit_span.sage) are elements of $SO(5,\mathbb F_p)$ and lift to nothing, and this argument never asks them to.

**The gap, named exactly.** The sentence *"fix $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$-bases and let $L$ be its matrix"* has no referent as written. The target the argument names, $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][SO_5]$, is not a finitely generated $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$-module, carries no grading in which it would be finite in each degree, and is not shown to be free. Until a finite free $M$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\Lambda\subseteq M\subseteq\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][SO_5]$ is named, neither *"its matrix"* nor $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_k)$ is defined, and *"rank drops on reduction"* has no matrix to drop on. **What would have to be proved:** (i) that such an $M$ exists; and, for the *equality* the argument asserts at $k=\mathbb F_p$, (ii) that $M$ may be chosen with $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12][SO_5]/M$ torsion-free, so that $L_k$ computes $\Lambda_k$ rather than merely dominating it. **I do not supply either proof here.** Obligation (ii) is dispensable, the chain consumes only one direction of that equality, and it is the direction that holds without (ii). Obligation (i) is not dispensable: without it the argument has no $L$.

**Standing, in one line.** The argument is a correct repair of L87's warrant with one unwritten definitional step; it is not a proof of L87's number, and it does not make L87's headline (*"proved rather than measured"*), true.

## The object under adjudication

Fixed throughout: $R=\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$; $N=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3(\mathcal W_5)^*$, free over $R$ of rank $\binom{37}{3}=7770$; $A=R[SO_5]$; $\Lambda\colon N\to A$, $f\mapsto(g\mapsto f(gW_0))$; $\Phi(g)=(gW_0)^3$; $U=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_5)$. The chain the argument serves, in the form the source states it, is

$$3570=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}_{\mathbb F_p}(\mathrm{Gram})\ \overset{(1)}{\le}\ \dim_{\mathbb F_p}\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{(g_aW_0)^{\otimes3}\}\ \overset{(2)}{\le}\ \dim\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_5)_{\overline{\mathbb F_p}}\ \overset{(3)}{\le}\ \dim_{\mathbb C}U\ \overset{(4)}{\le}\ 3570,$$

and the argument is the warrant for $(3)$ alone.

## 1. Is $\Lambda$ well defined, and is $L$ a matrix over $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$?

**$\Lambda$ is well defined. No finding.** $SO_5$ acts on $\Lambda^2$ of the standard module by $\Lambda^2g$, and on operators by conjugation; on $SO_5$ the inverse is the transpose, so every matrix entry of $gW_0$ is a $\mathbb Z$-coefficient polynomial in the entries of $g$. A linear functional on $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3$ of that is a cubic $\mathbb Z$-polynomial in those entries. $W_0$ is integral, [orbit_span.sage](../scripts/reviewers/r178/orbit_span.sage) builds it as $2w_1w_1^{\mathsf T}-w_2w_2^{\mathsf T}-w_3w_3^{\mathsf T}$ on integral $w_i$, with no denominator anywhere, so $\Lambda(f)$ is a regular function on $SO_5$ over $R$, and no factorials are needed if $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3$ is taken as the quotient of $\otimes^3$ and $N$ as its dual: then $v\mapsto v^3$ is integral and $N$ is free on the dual monomial basis.

**$L$ is where the gap is.** Three distinct things are being conflated in *"fix $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$-bases"*:

- **The target is not a free module the argument has exhibited.** $A$ is a finitely generated $R$-*algebra*, of infinite rank as a module. Freeness of $A$ over $R$ is a real statement (it is what a Peter–Weyl or good-filtration theorem over a Dedekind base would give) and the argument asserts nothing of the kind; the hypotheses it does state ($W_0$ integral, $SO_5$ smooth), give flatness, hence torsion-freeness over the domain $R$, and over a general ring torsion-free is not free.
- **"Finitely generated in the relevant degree" has no referent.** The ideal cutting $O_5$ out of the $25$ affine coordinates is generated by $g^{\mathsf T}g-I$, which is inhomogeneous, so the ambient grading does not descend to $A$ and $A$ has no degree decomposition to be finite in. The finiteness that is actually available is of a different kind: $f(gW_0)=\langle f,\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3\rho(g)\,W_0^3\rangle$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\Lambda$ lies in the coefficient space of the rank-$7770$ representation $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3\mathcal W_5$, which is finitely generated. The argument does not say this, and the phrase it uses instead points at nothing.
- **$L$ must reduce to $\Lambda_k$, not merely dominate it, for the stated equality.** Even granted a finite free $M\supseteq\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\Lambda$ with a basis, $L_k$ is the matrix of $N_k\to M_k$; the equality of item 3 additionally needs $M_k\to A_k$ injective, which is a saturation condition on $M$ inside $A$ and is not stated.

**Strongest defence, and how far it carries.** $\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\Lambda$ is finitely generated (it is spanned by the images of $7770$ basis vectors) and torsion-free (it sits inside the flat $R$-module $A$, and $R$ is a domain); $R=\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$ is a principal ideal domain, and a finitely generated torsion-free module over a PID is free of finite rank. So a basis exists, $L$ has finitely many nonzero rows, and its entries lie in $R\subseteq\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ for every odd $p$. **The defence succeeds, and it is one forced line**, there is no competing reading of *"let $L$ be its matrix"*. It is retained as a gap rather than a failure because that line is the one making the argument's central object exist, and it is not on the page; the author's stated hypotheses (integrality, smoothness) are exactly the ones it consumes, which is evidence he had it in mind and did not write it.

**A second reading that also survives.** Take $M$ to be *any* finite free $R$-module with $\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\Lambda\subseteq M\subseteq A$. Then $\Lambda_k$ factors through $M_k$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\Lambda_k\le\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}L_k$ for every $k$, while over $\mathbb Q$ localisation is exact and $M_{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow A_{\mathbb Q}$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}L_{\mathbb Q}=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}$. Both comparisons point the way the chain needs. So the saturation obligation (ii) is genuinely inessential; obligation (i) is not.

## 2. Does "$SO_{5,k}$ is reduced" give what it is used for?

**Yes, and it enters at one step, which I name, but it is load-bearing at $k=\mathbb Q$ and inert at $k=\mathbb F_p$, which is the reverse of how the sentence reads.**

The step is the forward implication of *"$f$ kills the whole image iff $L_kf=0$"*. The converse is trivial. The forward direction is a Nullstellensatz argument: $\Lambda_k(f)$ is a regular function on a scheme of finite type over a field, hence Jacobson, so a function vanishing at every closed point lies in the nilradical; reduced makes the nilradical zero, so $\Lambda_k(f)=0$. Without reducedness $\Lambda_k(f)$ could be a nonzero nilpotent, $\ker\Lambda_k$ would be a proper subspace of the annihilator of $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi$, and $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_k)$ would **over**-state $\dim_k\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_{5,k})$.

That reducedness holds is immediate from the argument's own smoothness hypothesis: $SO_5$ smooth over $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$ gives $SO_{5,k}$ smooth over $k$, hence regular, hence reduced. The hypothesis is sound, for the standard form $\sum_{i=1}^5x_i^2$ the associated bilinear form has determinant $2^5$, a unit in $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]$, so $O_5$ is smooth affine there and $SO_5=\{\det=1\}$ is open and closed in it, char $2$ being exactly where this collapses.

**Where it is used is not where the sentence puts it.** Over-statement is fatal only where the chain consumes $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_k)\le\dim\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi$; the chain consumes that at $k=\mathbb Q$, in the identification $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_{\mathbb Q})=\dim U$. At $k=\mathbb F_p$ the chain consumes the opposite direction, $\dim\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi\le\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_{\mathbb F_p})$, which is the trivial inclusion and needs nothing. So the hypothesis *"characteristic $\ne2$"*, advertised as licensing the $\mathbb F_p$ end, is doing its work at the $\mathbb Q$ end, where it is free, Cartier's theorem making every group scheme over a field of characteristic zero smooth. **This is a mis-attribution of a hypothesis to a step, not an error**: the hypothesis is true where invoked and the step it is actually needed for holds.

## 3. Is $\dim_k\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_{5,k})=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_k)$ correct, at both fields, and does the finite sample reach it?

**Correct under the scheme-theoretic reading; unproved under the rational-points reading; and the chain needs neither at $\mathbb F_p$.**

- **Scheme-theoretic reading**, $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_{5,k})$ means the smallest linear subspace containing the image of the morphism $\Phi$, equivalently the span over $\bar k$-points. Then item 2's argument gives $\ker\Lambda_k=\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}(\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi)$ and the equality follows, modulo item 1's obligation (ii) for $L_k$ to compute $\Lambda_k$. **This holds at $k=\mathbb Q$ and at $k=\mathbb F_p$ alike.**
- **Rational-points reading**, $SO_5(\mathbb F_p)$ is a finite set, and a regular function can vanish on all of it without being zero; $x^p-x$ on $\mathbb A^1$ is the standard witness, and on a $10$-dimensional $SO_{5,\mathbb F_p}$ the ideal of the finite set of rational points is strictly larger than zero. **Under this reading the equality is unproved at $k=\mathbb F_p$**, and no degree-versus-$p$ estimate is offered.
- **The candidate failure is withdrawn.** The source chain writes step $(3)$'s middle term as $\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\Phi(SO_5)_{\overline{\mathbb F_p}}$, so the scheme-theoretic reading is the author's; and the sample sits at step $(2)$, where the $\mathbb F_p$-rational points are a *subset* of the $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$-points, so the inclusion is trivial in the direction used. What survives is a **notation finding**: one symbol carries both readings and the chain silently uses both, at $(2)$ and at $(3)$.

**The argument does not need the full group, and the finite sample is not asked to reach it.** The full group appears only as a container: the sample's span is bounded above by it, and it is bounded above by $\dim U$. Nothing anywhere asks the $3700$ points to be Zariski-dense, to be uniformly distributed, or to be reductions of anything. **This is the argument's real merit**, and it is what the discarded one-liner could not do: the Cayley construction of [orbit_span.sage](../scripts/reviewers/r178/orbit_span.sage) draws $A$ skew over $\mathbb F_p$ and forms $g=(I-A)(I+A)^{-1}$, which is an element of $SO(5,\mathbb F_p)$ (its determinant is $1$ automatically, since $\det(I+A)=\det((I+A)^{\mathsf T})=\det(I-A)$) and of which no integral matrix is the lift. The descent moves the whole burden onto $L$, which the sample never touches.

## 4. "Rank drops on reduction", the condition, and whether the construction supplies it

**The condition:** $L$ has entries in a subring of $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, $L_{\mathbb F_p}$ is its entrywise reduction, and both ranks are read off the same matrix. Then a nonvanishing $r\times r$ minor mod $p$ lifts to a nonzero minor over $\mathbb Q$, so $\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_{\mathbb F_p})\le\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}(L_{\mathbb Q})$.

**Supplied, conditional on item 1.** Entries lie in $\mathbb Z[\tfrac12]\subseteq\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ for $p$ odd, and $46337$ is odd. What is *not* supplied without item 1's obligation (i) is the matrix itself; with it, this step is unconditional. Note that this is the one step whose failure in the original warrant was the whole complaint, there, the matrix whose reduction was invoked was the Gram matrix, which is not the reduction of any integral Gram matrix. Here the reduced object is $L$, defined before any field is chosen, so the objection does not transfer.

## 6. Characteristic $2$, and $p=46337$

- **Char $2$ is the right exclusion for what it is attached to.** It is exactly where $O_5$ for $\sum x_i^2$ stops being smooth over $\mathbb Z$, and where $\{\det=1\}$ stops separating $SO$ from $O$. **No finding on the exclusion itself.**
- **It is not the only small-characteristic caveat the sentence sweeps in.** *"Any field $k$ of characteristic $\ne2$"* includes $\mathbb F_3$, where the identification of $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3(\mathcal W_5)^*$ with cubic polynomial functions, and the $\Gamma^3$-versus-$\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3$ comparison of item 5's link $(1)$, both fail. It also includes $p=5$, where the trace conditions defining the $\mathcal W_5$ lattice may fail to stay saturated. **Both inert at $46337$, and both are reasons the general phrasing over-reaches.**
- **$p$ must be prime**, or $\mathbb F_p$ is not a field and neither *"rank"* nor the minor argument means anything. I checked this by hand rather than by machine: $216^2=46656>46337>46225=215^2$, so trial division to $215$ suffices, and $46337$ leaves a nonzero remainder against every prime up to $211$, the closest call is $181\cdot256=46336$, remainder $1$. **$46337$ is prime.** By the same hand arithmetic $p-1=46336=2^8\cdot181$, so $\gcd(3,p-1)=1$.
- **Size against the dimensions is irrelevant, and this is worth saying plainly.** Nothing in the descent requires $p>7770$, $p>3700$ or $p>3570$. A small $p$ would make attaining the cap *harder*, never spuriously easier, so no false positive can be manufactured by the choice of prime. What a single prime does cost is elsewhere: it is one draw, and a defect landing the rank exactly on the cap is invisible.
- **$p$ divides $|SO(5,\mathbb F_p)|=p^4(p^4-1)(p^2-1)$ for every $p$.** So $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^3\mathcal W_5$ is never semisimple over $\mathbb F_p$ and the isotypic decomposition the cap rests on has no characteristic-$p$ counterpart. **This is not a defect in the argument; it is the reason the argument is needed**, the cap can only be applied after the number has been moved to characteristic zero, which is what $(3)$ does.

## 7. What the argument does NOT establish

- **It does not establish $3570$.** It licenses the transfer of whatever number the run produced. The lower bound remains one machine measurement, at one prime, one seed, one run, with the target `theoretical cap = 3570` hardcoded in the script's header beside the printed rank.
- **It does not make L87 *"proved rather than measured"*.** It repairs the warrant, not the status. A block adopting it and keeping that headline would be carrying the same over-claim with a better proof underneath it.
- **It does not establish the cap.** Step $(4)$ is [L86](../findings/sym3-blocks.md)'s parity rule plus [L83](../findings/sym3-blocks.md)'s table. On the ground the block actually states (*"using $\dim V_\lambda^H\le1$"* over all $24$ blocks), the cap is $5640$ and the squeeze does not close. Nothing here touches that.
- **It does not give the sixteen evaluation maps individually.** Those come out of the squeeze as a package; the per-block check [L86](../findings/sym3-blocks.md) names (evaluating the $41$ highest-weight vectors at orbit points), is a different and stronger statement, and it remains unrun.
- **It concludes nothing about characteristic $p$ representation theory.** Only $\le$ crosses from $\mathbb F_p$ to $\mathbb Q$; the argument's own equality at $\mathbb F_p$ is neither needed nor, as written, proved.
- **It uses two links it does not name.** That $U$, defined as a span over the real group, has the dimension of the span over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-points needs the Zariski density of the compact real form in $SO_{5,\mathbb C}$; and that $U$'s cap may be read off $O(5)$-blocks while $\Lambda$ runs over $SO_5$ needs $-\mathrm{Id}$ acting trivially on $\Lambda^2$, which [L86](../findings/sym3-blocks.md) supplies four lines away and this argument does not cite.
- **It does not extend past its hypotheses**: characteristic $2$, a non-integral $W_0$, or a $W_0$ whose denominators meet $p$ all break it, the last silently.
