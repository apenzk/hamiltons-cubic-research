## 0. Conventions, fixed once

Quoted from the brief, Definitions:

- "$V=\mathbb R^n$ with the standard action of $O(n)$, and $V^{\otimes m}$ with the diagonal action $g\cdot(v_1\otimes\dots\otimes v_m)=gv_1\otimes\dots\otimes gv_m$ and the induced inner product."
- "A **block label** is a partition $\lambda$ with $\lvert\lambda\rvert\in\{0,2,\dots,12\}$. $\lambda'$ is the conjugate partition, so $\lambda'_1$ is the number of nonzero parts of $\lambda$ and $\lambda'_2$ the number of parts of size at least $2$. $\lambda$ is **$O(n)$-admissible** when $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2\le n$."
- "$V_\lambda$ is the $O(n)$-representation labelled by $\lambda$, and $M_\lambda=\operatorname{Hom}_{O(n)}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$."

From Passage A: "let $\iota_\lambda:V_\lambda\hookrightarrow V^{\otimes\lvert\lambda\rvert}$ be the inclusion given by Weyl's construction of $V_\lambda$ as the traceless tensors of symmetry type $\lambda$ (classical, unfiled), and let $\mathrm{cup}=\sum_a e_a\otimes e_a\in V\otimes V$ be the invariant metric element", with $k=(12-\lvert\lambda\rvert)/2$.

The step under adjudication: "for every block label covered by the lemma, the seed $c_0=\iota_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$ is given in closed form and is obtained without a search, and this follows from the passages."

## 1. Hypothesis inventory

- **H1. Model of $V_\lambda$.** The step needs $V_\lambda$ to be realised as a concrete subspace of $V^{\otimes\lvert\lambda\rvert}$, so that $\iota_\lambda$ is the literal inclusion. Passage A states this ("the inclusion given by Weyl's construction"). Passages B and C say "the inclusion", which presupposes a model, and the brief's Definitions speak of "the representation labelled by $\lambda$", an isomorphism class. Checked: the strongest reading adopts Passage A's model throughout, and nothing in B or C contradicts it. A fixed tableau must be chosen for the Young symmetrizer (say the row-reading tableau of shape $\lambda$), since different tableaux give isomorphic but distinct subspaces. This is a convention, and it is harmless: any choice yields a valid $\iota_\lambda$.
- **H2. Weyl's theorem for $O(n)$ (standard, Weyl, *The Classical Groups*, and Fulton and Harris, *Representation Theory*, Theorem 19.19).** With $d=\lvert\lambda\rvert$, $V^{[d]}\subseteq V^{\otimes d}$ the traceless tensors (the common kernel of all $\binom d2$ contraction maps) and $c_\lambda$ the Young symmetrizer, the space $V_{[\lambda]}=c_\lambda V^{[d]}$ is an irreducible $O(n)$-representation, nonzero exactly when $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2\le n$. Checked: this is the classical statement over $\mathbb R$ (or any field of characteristic zero). It supplies both that $\iota_\lambda$ is $O(n)$-equivariant and that $V_\lambda\neq0$ under admissibility.
- **H3. $n\ge1$ and the ground field $\mathbb R$.** Needed for $\mathrm{cup}\neq0$ and for "a tensor product of nonzero vectors is nonzero", which holds over any field. Stated in the lemma.
- **H4. Admissibility of $\lambda$.** Needed so that $V_\lambda\neq0$ and a nonzero $x$ exists in the nonvanishing argument. Stated in the lemma. The auxiliary claim that $\lvert\lambda\rvert\le n$ implies admissibility is correct by hand: $\lambda'_1+\lambda'_2=\sum_i\min(\lambda_i,2)\le\sum_i\lambda_i=\lvert\lambda\rvert$. It is used by the step only as a sufficient condition.
- **H5. Meaning of "search".** The step needs "search" in the sense Passage A sets up: looking for a nonzero element of $\operatorname{Hom}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$ as an open construction problem, once per block. Checked against Passage A's own framing ("if the seed must be found by search, finding a nonzero element of $\operatorname{Hom}(V_\lambda,V^{\otimes12})$ is the same construction problem the item started with"). Under this sense, a formula for the element together with a uniform proof that it is nonzero removes the search.
- **H6. The seed role.** The passages say the realisation theorem needs "one nonzero seed per block". The step needs only that nonvanishing in $M_\lambda$ qualifies an element as a seed. Checked: this is how Passage A uses the theorem, and it is consistent with standard double-commutant theory, where $M_\lambda$ is a simple module over the commutant of $O(n)$ on $V^{\otimes12}$, so any nonzero element generates it.

## 2. The argument

**Membership and nonvanishing (J1a).** Invariance of $\mathrm{cup}$: $g\cdot\mathrm{cup}=\sum_a ge_a\otimes ge_a=\sum_{b,c}(\sum_a g_{ba}g_{ca})\,e_b\otimes e_c$, whose coefficient matrix is $gg^{\mathsf T}=I$ for every $g\in O(n)$. So $\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$ is an invariant vector of $V^{\otimes2k}$. For $x\in V_\lambda$ and $g\in O(n)$, $c_0(gx)=\iota_\lambda(gx)\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}=g\iota_\lambda(x)\otimes g\cdot\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}=g\cdot c_0(x)$, using H2 for the equivariance of $\iota_\lambda$ and the diagonal action on $V^{\otimes12}=V^{\otimes\lvert\lambda\rvert}\otimes V^{\otimes2k}$. Linearity in $x$ is clear. By H4 and H2 there is $x\neq0$ in $V_\lambda$, then $\iota_\lambda(x)\neq0$ since $\iota_\lambda$ is injective, $\mathrm{cup}\neq0$ by H3, and a tensor product of nonzero vectors is nonzero. So $c_0\neq0$ in $M_\lambda$. The degenerate cases hold: at $\lvert\lambda\rvert=12$, $k=0$ and $c_0=\iota_\lambda$, and at $\lambda=\varnothing$, $V_\varnothing$ is the trivial representation, $\iota_\varnothing$ is the identity of $\mathbb R=V^{\otimes0}$, and $c_0$ sends $1$ to $\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes6}$. The contraction check in Passage A is also correct: contracting the two legs of one cup gives $\sum_a\langle e_a,e_a\rangle=n$, so contracting all $k$ cups returns $n^k\iota_\lambda$, nonzero since $n\ge1$.

**Closed form (J1b).** Under H1 and H2, $V_\lambda=c_\lambda V^{[d]}$ is cut out by explicit data: the linear equations defining $V^{[d]}$ (all contractions vanish) and the explicit operator $c_\lambda=\sum_{p\in R_\lambda}\sum_{q\in C_\lambda}\operatorname{sgn}(q)\,pq$ acting by permuting tensor factors. The inclusion $\iota_\lambda$ is the literal inclusion of this subspace, and $c_0(x)=x\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$ is a formula in named objects. No choice depending on $n$ or $\lambda$ beyond the tableau convention enters. So $c_0$ is in closed form relative to $\iota_\lambda$, and $\iota_\lambda$ is in closed form relative to Weyl's construction. This is precisely Passage A's own wording ("every block has a seed in closed form relative to $\iota_\lambda$") and its concession that at $\lvert\lambda\rvert=12$ "the content is Weyl's construction".

**No search (J1c).** The one place where something could still look like a search is producing a concrete nonzero vector of $V_\lambda$ or a basis of it, which a certificate pipeline might evaluate. This is a bounded deterministic computation with a guaranteed outcome, and the map $c_0$ is defined without it. Standard facts make it explicit: $S_d$ acts orthogonally on $V^{\otimes d}$ and preserves $V^{[d]}$ (a permutation carries each contraction to another contraction), so the orthogonal projection $P$ onto $V^{[d]}$, which exists for every $n\ge1$ because the inner product is positive definite, commutes with $c_\lambda$. Hence $V_\lambda=c_\lambda P\,V^{\otimes d}$ is spanned by the finitely many vectors $c_\lambda P(e_{i_1}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{i_d})$, at least one of which is nonzero whenever $V_\lambda\neq0$. Nothing here is a search over $M_\lambda$ for an unknown nonzero element, which is the sense H5 fixes. So the step's "obtained without a search" holds.

**Follows from the passages (J1d).** Passage A supplies the model (H1), the definition of $c_0$, and the proof of nonvanishing and equivariance. Standard mathematics supplies H2. Passages B and C restate the lemma with the conclusion "so the seed of every block is given in closed form and need not be searched for" and "so it is constructed rather than searched for". Both conclusions are licensed once H1 is read in, and "every block" in Passage B is covered for all labels that carry a block: a non-admissible label has $V_\lambda=0$, hence $M_\lambda=0$ and no block to seed, so the restriction to admissible labels loses nothing.

**Strongest attack and why it does not land.**

- *Attack 1, the closed form is borrowed.* Passages B and C drop Passage A's qualifier "relative to $\iota_\lambda$", and at $\lvert\lambda\rvert=12$ the lemma's content is Weyl's construction itself, so "closed form" could be read as claiming more than the lemma proves. Defence: the step's words say $c_0$ is given in closed form, and it is, as a formula in $\iota_\lambda$ and $\mathrm{cup}$, where $\iota_\lambda$ is a classical explicit construction. Passage C's closing sentence ("removes a search from the certificate pipeline and adds no certificate") shows the author claims no more. This is a presentational point: B and C would read more precisely with "relative to Weyl's construction of $V_\lambda$" restored, and the step stands without it.
- *Attack 2, model mismatch.* If the pipeline fixes some other model of $V_\lambda$ (highest weight, Gelfand and Tsetlin), then a seed on that model would need an isomorphism to Weyl's model, which is a construction in its own right. Defence: Passage A fixes Weyl's model explicitly, and the object the realisation theorem acts on is the block inside $V^{\otimes12}$, whose image $c_0(V_\lambda)=V_{[\lambda]}\otimes\mathrm{cup}^{\otimes k}$ is independent of any abstract model. The reading needs H1, which the passages supply.
- *Attack 3, admissibility at small $n$.* A label with $\lvert\lambda\rvert>n$ may be admissible, and the traceless tensors of that type then cannot be written with distinct basis indices. Defence: Weyl's theorem (H2) covers all admissible labels at every $n\ge1$, and the projection description above is valid at every $n$, so no case is left to search.

No location of failure survives the three checks: none of the attacks exhibits a label, an $n$, or a sentence of the passages at which $c_0$ fails to be explicit, fails to be nonzero, or requires a search over $M_\lambda$.

**Verdict: VALID**
