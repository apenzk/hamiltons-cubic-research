## Passage A, the heading and the argument ([findings/sym3-blocks.md](../findings/sym3-blocks.md) line 38, and lines 54-60)

#### Lemma L84, Casimir separation is forced, not measured, the PAIR $(C_2,C_4)$ separates all $24$

## Separation of the blocks, which is a theorem

The degree-two Casimir takes **$21$** distinct values across the $24$ blocks, $24$ less the three colliding pairs. *Reviewer 178; the $22$ this file carried came from a run made before $\rho$ was corrected to $(3/2,1/2)$.* Three pairs collide: $(4,1)$ with $(3,3)$ at $30$, $(4,3)$ with $(5,0)$ at $40$, and $(5,4)$ with $(6,2)$ at $60$.

**The quartic generator separates them, and separation is forced rather than measured.** The higher Casimirs have Harish-Chandra images whose leading terms are the power sums $p_{2k}(\ell)$, $\ell_i=\lambda_i+\rho_i$. For a dominant $B_2$ weight $\ell_1>\ell_2>0$, so $e_1=p_2$ and $e_2=(p_2^2-p_4)/2$ recover $\{\ell_1^2,\ell_2^2\}$ as a multiset and hence recover $\lambda$. Equivalently, $Z(U(\mathfrak{so}(5)))$ is the ring of $W(B_2)$-invariants, generated by $p^2+q^2$ and $p^2q^2$. On the three pairs $p_4$ reads $7361/8$ against $4481/8$, $8521/8$ against $14281/8$, and $17561/8$ against $25625/8$.

**The generator is what separates, not the degree.** $C_2^2$ is a legal degree-four central element and separates nothing.
