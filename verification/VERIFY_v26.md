## 2. C1, the premise: what makes simultaneous diagonality legitimate, and why the entry's phrasing hides it

**Two tensors each "diagonal on coordinate $2$-planes" are in general diagonal in different frames, and then their sum is diagonal in neither.** The statement asserts simultaneity flatly. What makes it true here is one fact about $W^*$ and one about $H$, neither of them in the entry.

**$W^*$ is diagonal in every block-adapted frame.** [Lemma L145](../findings/second-order.md) records the spectrum as two-valued with no kernel, and r446's form $W^*_-=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert(\mathrm{id}-P_{\Lambda^2V_1}-P_{\Lambda^2V_2})$ exhibits the eigenspaces: $\Lambda^2V_1\oplus\Lambda^2V_2$ and $V_1\wedge V_2$. So $W^*=\lambda_+(P_{\Lambda^2V_1}+P_{\Lambda^2V_2})+\lambda_-(\mathrm{id}-P_{\Lambda^2V_1}-P_{\Lambda^2V_2})$ is **scalar on each of the three blocks**, and is therefore diagonal on coordinate $2$-planes in *any* orthonormal frame whose vectors lie in $V_1\cup V_2$, the frame inside each factor is free. **This freedom is the whole of the transfer, and the statement does not mention it.**

**$H$ is diagonal in the eigenframe of its own $h$, and that frame is block-adapted.** The record's embedding is $h\mapsto$ Weyl projection of $h\owedge g_q$, with $h$ supported on $B_1$. Re-deriving it from the record's own projector, with $(A\owedge B)_{ijij}=A_{ii}B_{jj}+A_{jj}B_{ii}$ for $A,B$ diagonal in the frame:

- $\mathrm{Ric}(h\owedge g_q)_{aa}=\sum_{j\ne a}\big(h_a(g_q)_{jj}+h_{jj}(g_q)_{aa}\big)=m\,h_a$ for $a\in B_1$, and $\mathrm{Ric}_{bb}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}h=0$ for $b\in B_2$. So $\rho_0=mh$, traceless, and the scalar-curvature term of the projector drops.
- $\mathrm{Alt}(h\owedge g_q)=0$, both factors being diagonal, so $Y=X$ and the projector's first term is inert.
- $(h\owedge g_q)_{abab}=h_a$ on a cross plane, $0$ on both in-block families; $(h\owedge g)_{ijij}=h_{ii}+h_{jj}$.
- Hence the Weyl image is $h\owedge g_q-\tfrac{m}{n-2}(h\owedge g)$, with eigenvalues $h_a\big(1-\tfrac m{2m-2}\big)=\alpha h_a$ on $e_a\wedge e_b$; $-\tfrac m{2(m-1)}(h_a+h_{a'})=-\beta(h_a+h_{a'})$ on $e_a\wedge e_{a'}$; and $0$ on $e_b\wedge e_{b'}$.

**This reproduces [second-order.md:1131](../findings/second-order.md) exactly**, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ included, and every entry of it is a coordinate-$2$-plane eigenvalue, so the image is diagonal in the $h$-eigenframe. The map is linear, $O(m)\times O(m)$-equivariant and nonzero, so by L27's multiplicity-freeness its image **is** the summand $\Sigma_1$: every element of $\Sigma_1$ is the image of exactly one $h$, and every symmetric $h$ has an orthonormal eigenframe inside $B_1$. **C1 is therefore true for every element of one copy, with no exceptional element.**

**Finding 1 (scope of the argument, not of the mathematics).** *Locate*: "the tensors $W^*$ and $H$ are simultaneously diagonal on coordinate $2$-planes", [second-order.md:1187](../findings/second-order.md), asserted with no argument and no cross-reference. *Exhibit*: the assertion is not self-evident from the two tensors being separately diagonal; it needs $W^*$'s block-scalar spectrum, without which a frame diagonalising $h$ need not diagonalise $W^*$, and the entry supplies neither that fact nor a pointer to it, two entries after the record had to correct this same embedding once already ([second-order.md:1133](../findings/second-order.md)). *Argue the opposite*: L148 sits eight lines below [Lemma L146](../findings/second-order.md), whose Gate 2 box states in its own words that "the Weyl image is diagonal on coordinate $2$-planes", and whose Setting paragraph fixes $W^*$; on the most charitable reading L148 imports both silently from the entry immediately above it and its premise is an audited fact rather than a new one. **The candidate stands as a citation defect, not a mathematical one**: the premise is true, and I confirm it independently above.

## 3. C3 and C4, the two implications the entry states and does not argue

**C3.** $W(t)$ is symmetric on $\Lambda^2$, so $\lvert W(t)\rvert$ is computed by functional calculus; on a matrix diagonal in the basis $\{e_i\wedge e_j\}$ this acts entrywise, so $\lvert W(t)\rvert$ and $W(t)_-=\tfrac12(\lvert W(t)\rvert-W(t))$ are diagonal in the same basis. **No hypothesis is needed**, in particular no eigenvalue gap and no analyticity, which is why the step survives eigenvalue collisions and sign changes as $t$ runs over $\mathbb R$. The entry writes only "and so is $W(t)_-$".

**C4.** $\mathrm{Alt}$ is the projection onto $\Lambda^4$ ([problem.md:191](../findings/problem.md)), so $\mathrm{Alt}(R)_{ijkl}=\tfrac13(R_{ijkl}+R_{iklj}+R_{iljk})$ vanishes identically unless $i,j,k,l$ are four **distinct** indices. For four distinct indices each of the three terms pairs disjoint index sets ($\{i,j\}\ne\{k,l\}$, $\{i,k\}\ne\{l,j\}$, $\{i,l\}\ne\{j,k\}$) and a coordinate-diagonal tensor has every such entry zero. Hence $\mathrm{Alt}(W(t)_-)=0$. **This is the only step in the lemma with mathematical content beyond bookkeeping, and the entry states it as "Therefore".**

*This is also the record's own reason elsewhere for "every sphere product, in any frame, has defect zero" ([problem.md:191](../findings/problem.md)); L148 is that observation applied to a segment rather than a point, and it does not say so.*

**No finding beyond the citation one**: both implications are valid as written, and I record them as unargued rather than as gaps, because each is an implication the entry asserts and whose validity is checkable in one line.

## 4. The edges of every range

**$m=3$ ($n=6$).** L27's excluded case. Computing $\mu$ from L27's own display at $p=q=m$: $\tfrac12-\mu=\frac{(m-2)^2-1}{2(m-1)^2}$, so $\mu=\frac{(m-1)^2-(m-2)^2+1}{2(m-1)^2}=\frac{2m-2}{2(m-1)^2}=\frac1{m-1}$. At $m=3$: $\mu=\tfrac12$ and $\lambda_3=\tfrac14$. **The $S^2_0$ summand is not the margin summand there**, its margin $3\theta_0(\tfrac12-\lambda_3)=\tfrac34\theta_0$ is four times the $\Lambda^2\!\otimes\!\Lambda^2$ margin $3\theta_0(\tfrac12-\mu)=0$. C1–C4 still hold verbatim; the **title** does not.

**$m=4$ ($n=8$).** $\lambda_4=\tfrac13=\mu$. The two summands **tie**, so $S^2_0$ is *a* minimal-margin summand and not *the* one. $\lambda_m>\mu\iff\tfrac{m-2}2>1\iff m\ge5$, which is exactly the range [second-order.md:1147](../findings/second-order.md) states for the margin sitting on this summand, and exactly what the title of L148 needs.

**Finding 2 (a hypothesis the title uses and the statement omits).** *Locate*: the heading "the defect vanishes identically on **the margin summand**", [second-order.md:1183](../findings/second-order.md). *Exhibit*: at $m=3$ the margin is on $\Lambda^2\!\otimes\!\Lambda^2$ with value $0$, not on $S^2_0$ with value $\tfrac34\theta_0$; at $m=4$ the two are equal at $3\theta_0/(n-2)=\theta_0/2$, so the definite article is wrong at both. *Argue the opposite*: the naming convention of this file attaches "the margin summand" to $S^2_0(\mathbb R^m)$ throughout ([Lemma L146](../findings/second-order.md)'s Setting), the whole chain runs at $n\ge12$, and no downstream user reads L148 below $m=6$. **The candidate stands, at reduced weight**: it costs nothing downstream and it is a missing hypothesis on the face of a statement that carries none.

**Odd $n$.** The base point $S^m\times S^m$ forces $n=2m$ even, so the statement is empty at odd $n$ rather than false there. The mechanism would transfer verbatim to $S^p\times S^q$ with $p\ne q$ and to either of $S^2_0(\mathbb R^p)$, $S^2_0(\mathbb R^q)$ ($W_{p,q}$ is still block-scalar and $h\owedge g_q$ still diagonal) but **the statement makes no claim there**, and the margin question at $p\ne q$ is priced nowhere on file.

**$\ker W\ne0$, and the named witness.** $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is Morse–Bott nondegenerate with $\dim\ker W=3$ ([Lemma L147](../findings/second-order.md)'s hypothesis box). **L148 asserts nothing there**: its object is a segment based at $W^*=S^m\times S^m$, and $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is a different point. It does assert something *about* it by implication, and the implication is consistent: every point of $W^*+\Sigma$ is coordinate-diagonal in some frame and so has defect $0$, while padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ has $\delta^2=\tfrac19$ in every ambient dimension ([problem.md:347](../findings/problem.md)); hence $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\notin W^*+\Sigma$, which is what the record independently says by putting it on a different orbit. **And unlike its neighbours, L148 would survive a kernel** (see §1) so a successor working at a degenerate base point may carry L148's mechanism and must drop L147's and L149's branches 2 and 3.

## 5. Pre-registered question 1, one copy, as written; both, in every use

**The words give one.** "For $H$ in $S^2_0(\mathbb R^m)$" is singular, and the frame the argument builds comes from a single $h$ diagonalised inside $B_1$. At $p=q=m$ there are two such summands, $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$, exchanged by the factor swap, which fixes $W^*$ and commutes with $L_e$ ([second-order.md:1199](../findings/second-order.md)).

**Every downstream use needs both.** [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md) cites L148 three times and each citation quantifies over $\Sigma_1\oplus\Sigma_2$: "makes $a=0$ on **both** $S^2_0$ copies" ([second-order.md:1212](../findings/second-order.md)); "at $w=0$ the defect vanishes identically", where $w=0$ means $H\in\Sigma_1\oplus\Sigma_2$ by that entry's own corrected definition of $w$ ([second-order.md:1199](../findings/second-order.md), [second-order.md:1233](../findings/second-order.md)); and "$A'(0)$ … vanishes on the margin subspace by [Lemma L148](../findings/second-order.md), $u\le w$" ([second-order.md:1235](../findings/second-order.md)), which is worthless unless $A'(0)$ kills the whole of $\Sigma$, since $u\le\lVert A'(0)\rVert\,w$ needs $A'(0)H=A'(0)H_r$.

**The two-copy fact is true, and the record holds it elsewhere.** For $H=\Phi_1(h)+\Phi_2(k)$, diagonalise $h$ by a frame $\{e_a\}$ of $V_1$ and $k$ by a frame $\{f_b\}$ of $V_2$; the two frames live in **orthogonal blocks**, so they combine into one block-adapted orthonormal frame of $\mathbb R^n$. In it, $\Phi_1(h)$ is diagonal by §2, and $\Phi_2(k)$ is diagonal by the same computation under the swap, $g_p$ is the identity on $B_1$ in every orthonormal frame of $B_1$, so $\Phi_2(k)$ does not care which frame $B_1$ carries. The joint spectrum is $\alpha(h_a+k_b)$ on $e_a\wedge f_b$, $-\beta(h_a+h_{a'})$ on $e_a\wedge e_{a'}$, $-\beta(k_b+k_{b'})$ on $f_b\wedge f_{b'}$. **The cross-plane value $\alpha(h_a+k_b)$ is precisely the "third, cross-plane branch" that [second-order.md:1137](../findings/second-order.md) records for a pair $(h,k)$**, so the record has already computed this joint diagonal spectrum, in the entry above, and L148 neither states nor cites it.

**Finding 3 (the answer to the pre-registered question).** *Locate*: "For $H$ in $S^2_0(\mathbb R^m)$ at $W^*$", [second-order.md:1187](../findings/second-order.md), against "makes $a=0$ on **both** $S^2_0$ copies", [second-order.md:1212](../findings/second-order.md). *Exhibit*: a unit $H=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\Phi_1(h)+\Phi_2(k))$ with $h\ne0\ne k$ lies in the $w=0$ set that L149 and [ROUND_L147](ROUND_L147.md) apply L148 to, and is in **neither** $\Sigma_1$ nor $\Sigma_2$; the statement as written says nothing about it. ## 6. Pre-registered question 2, what "identically" quantifies

**The proof establishes**: for each fixed $H$ in the summand, $\mathrm{Alt}\big((W^*+tH)_-\big)=0$ for **every real $t$**, with a frame that depends on $H$ through the eigenframe of $h$. Ranging over $H$ and rescaling $tH$, this is equivalent to: $\mathrm{Alt}\big((W^*+X)_-\big)=0$ for **every** $X\in\Sigma_1$, the vanishing holds on the whole affine slice $W^*+\Sigma_1$, one frame per point.

**The words claim**: "for every $t$" and "identically zero along the whole segment" claim the $t$-quantifier, which is proved; the heading "vanishes identically **on the margin summand**" claims the direction-quantifier as well, which is also proved, for one copy. **So both quantifiers are supported, and there is no finding here**, with three exclusions worth stating because the chain's uses sit near each of them:

- **not a neighbourhood.** $\Sigma_1$ has codimension $\Theta(n^4)$ in $\mathcal W_n$; nothing here gives the defect vanishing on a ball, and [Lemma L145](../findings/second-order.md)'s box records the per-direction-to-neighbourhood trap as the distinguishing feature of a refuted family of claims. Every downstream use is an infimum over directions, so none of them commits it.
- **not one frame.** The frame varies with $H$; the summand is not simultaneously diagonalisable, since $h$ and $h'$ need not commute. Any successor statement quantified as "there is a frame in which all of $\Sigma$ is diagonal" would be false.
- **not directions with any weight outside $\Sigma$.** $\mathrm{Alt}$ is linear but $W\mapsto W_-$ is not, so vanishing on $\Sigma$ transfers to no $H$ with $w>0$; the entry's own closing box says as much, "the question lives on the mixed directions".

## 7. The gap between what is proven and what is used

**(a) Setting $a=0$ in branch 1, $f(u)=(c_p+u^2)/(S+au)$.** What L148 delivers is $A\equiv0$, hence $u=\lVert A'(0)\rVert=0$, a constraint on the **variable**, pinning its admissible range to $\{0\}$. The coefficient $a$ is **defined nowhere in the record**: it is used at [second-order.md:1179](../findings/second-order.md), [second-order.md:1208](../findings/second-order.md), [second-order.md:1212](../findings/second-order.md) and [second-order.md:1290](../findings/second-order.md), and the only characterisation on file is "$a\propto(1-\eta)^{-2}$" ([second-order.md:1262](../findings/second-order.md)), the Cauchy-shrinkage factor. Under the reading in which $a$ carries the defect's own second-order size, $A\equiv0$ gives $a=0$ literally; under the reading in which $a$ is a positive geometric constant, $a$ never vanishes and what L148 gives is $au=0$. **Both readings deliver the same value of branch 1 at $w=0$, namely $f=c_p/S$**, which is what the citation is for, so the downstream use is supported in substance and its wording, "L148 makes $a=0$", is exact only under a reading the record does not fix. *That the two readings agree here is not a general fact: $f$'s minimiser $u^*=(\sqrt{S^2+a^2c_p}-S)/a$ depends on $a$ and not only on $au$, so any use of L148 that ranges over $u>0$ would separate them.*

**A limit of the support, stated positively.** L148 constrains $u$ **on the set it covers**, that is at $w=0$. [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md)'s clause "the first is minimised at $w=0$" quantifies over $w\in[0,1]$ and so needs a statement about $u$ at $w>0$ as well; the only such statement on file is $u\le w$, which is an upper bound and cannot show that $f$ is larger there. **L148 supports the parenthetical it is cited for (that at $w=0$ the minimum is at $u=0$ with value $c_p/S$) and not the comparison across $w$ that the sentence around it makes.**

**(b) Making a defect branch unbounded at $w=0$.** $A\equiv0$ on the segment gives $A''\equiv0$ directly, with no Cauchy estimate and no analyticity needed, so branch $2=\sqrt{c_p+w^2}/\lVert A''\rVert$ is $\sqrt{c_p}/0=+\infty$ at $w=0$. **The written statement supports this**, subject to §5's two-copy correction, and it is the one use where L148's own strength is what is wanted. Two riders: the validity branch $\rho(H)/2=\Theta(n^{-1/2})$ is untouched and stays finite, and branch 1 stays at $c_p/S$, so the **minimum** over the three branches at $w=0$ is unchanged at $\Theta(n^{-1-\beta})$. *The "slack" [second-order.md:1233](../findings/second-order.md) calls a live resource is real as slack in one branch and does not by itself move the chain.*

**Finding 4, the last clause of the statement.** *Locate*: "and both defect branches are absent there", [second-order.md:1187](../findings/second-order.md). *Exhibit*: read against the three-branch display at [second-order.md:1208](../findings/second-order.md), branch 1 at $u=0$ is $c_p/S$, finite, positive and **binding**, it is precisely the branch [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md) evaluates at $w=0$ to state $r_n\gtrsim n^{-1-\beta}$, and it is the branch whose cap $c_p/S\le c_p/\theta_0=3/(n-2)$ [Lemma L147](../findings/second-order.md) calls the one proved thing in the entry. A branch that both survives and decides the headline is not absent. Worse, the phrasing collides with [second-order.md:1179](../findings/second-order.md), which quotes the very words "both defect branches moved out of the way" and calls that outcome **impossible in principle**. *Argue the opposite*: "defect branches" can mean the two places the defect $\delta$ enters the chain, the penalty $-\kappa_n\delta^2$ against the margin ([second-order.md:1235](../findings/second-order.md)) and the defect-tail term through $\lVert A''\rVert$, and **both of those do vanish identically** at $\delta\equiv0$, while $c_p/S$ is the cubic branch, which contains no defect and so is not a defect branch at all. Under that reading the clause is true and the collision with [second-order.md:1179](../findings/second-order.md) dissolves, L148 pinning $u$ rather than minimising over it. **The candidate stands as REFUTED-AS-WRITTEN with a true reading available**: the clause is ambiguous exactly where the record has already lost a cycle to the same ambiguity, and the two readings differ on whether the term that decides the chain's headline is present. **The repair is one word**: "and both defect **terms** vanish there, branch 1 reading $c_p/S$".

## 8. What would falsify the statement, and what I did to look for it

**The object that would break C1–C4** is an $H$ in the summand (or, for the version every user needs, in $\Sigma_1\oplus\Sigma_2$) for which no orthonormal frame diagonalises $W^*$ and $H$ together on coordinate $2$-planes; equivalently, a point of $W^*+\Sigma$ with $\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)\ne0$.

**What I did.** Three closed-form searches, no computation.

1. **Over the summand's elements.** The embedding $h\mapsto$ Weyl proj $(h\owedge g_q)$ is linear, equivariant and nonzero, so by L27's multiplicity-freeness it is a bijection onto $\Sigma_1$, **every** element is $\Phi(h)$ for exactly one symmetric traceless $h$, and every symmetric $h$ has an orthonormal eigenframe. No exceptional element exists, and there is no element of $\Sigma_1$ built from two non-commuting $h$'s to test.
2. **Over the joint eigenspace.** $\Phi_1(h)+\Phi_2(k)$ with $[h,k]$ undefined across orthogonal blocks, the obstruction that would exist for two forms on the same $\mathbb R^m$ is absent, and §5 exhibits the joint frame and its spectrum, which matches the record's independently computed cross branch $\alpha(h_a+k_b)$.
3. **Against a named non-diagonal object.** Padded $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$, $\delta^2=\tfrac19\ne0$, is the record's standard witness for a point off the diagonal locus; it is **not** of the form $W^*+X$ with $X\in\Sigma$, since every such point has defect $0$. It falsifies nothing here and confirms the slice is a proper subset of $\{\delta=0\}$, itself strictly larger than the diagonal locus ([problem.md:183](../findings/problem.md)).

**No falsifier found.** The one place a falsifier does exist is the title: at $m=3$, $\Lambda^2\!\otimes\!\Lambda^2$ carries margin $0$ against $S^2_0$'s $\tfrac34\theta_0$, so "the margin summand" names the wrong summand, a false naming with a true theorem under it.

## Verdict

**`PROVEN, hypotheses corrected`**, for C1–C4, with C5 refuted as written.

The corrected statement the record should carry: *for $W^*=S^m\times S^m$ at $n=2m$ and $m\ge3$, and for $H$ in **either or both** $S^2_0(\mathbb R^m)$ summands (that is, for every $H$ in the joint $\lambda_m$-eigenspace $\Sigma_1\oplus\Sigma_2$), a single block-adapted orthonormal frame diagonalises $W^*$ and $H$ simultaneously on coordinate $2$-planes, because $W^*$ is scalar on each of $\Lambda^2V_1$, $\Lambda^2V_2$, $V_1\wedge V_2$ and the eigenframes of the two forms live in orthogonal blocks. Hence $W(t)=W^*+tH$ and, by functional calculus, $W(t)_-$ are diagonal in it, and $\mathrm{Alt}$ being the projection onto $\Lambda^4$, $\mathrm{Alt}(W(t)_-)=0$ for every real $t$, with no analyticity and no $\ker W^*=0$ used. **Both defect terms vanish there (the $\delta^2$ penalty and the defect tail through $\lVert A''\rVert$) while branch 1 survives at $u=0$ as $c_p/S$.** The summand is the margin summand only for $m\ge5$.*
