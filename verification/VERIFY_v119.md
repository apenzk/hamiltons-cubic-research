## 0. Conventions, fixed once

## 2. The cubic expansion, CORRECT, re-derived without the block's algebra

Set $W^*=cu+sV$ with $c=\cos\varphi$, $s=\sin\varphi$, $\lVert V\rVert=1$, $V\perp u$. Then $\lVert W^*\rVert^2=c^2+s^2=1$, so $W^*$ is on the unit sphere and $m=\sqrt{2/3}P(W^*)=\sqrt{2/3}\,T(W^*,W^*,W^*)$. Expanding the trilinear symmetric $T$:

$$T(W^*,W^*,W^*)=c^3T(u,u,u)+3c^2s\,T(u,u,V)+3cs^2\,T(u,V,V)+s^3T(V,V,V).$$

Multiply by $\sqrt{2/3}$ term by term. First, $\sqrt{2/3}\,T(u,u,u)=\sqrt{2/3}\,P(u)=\sqrt{2/3}\cdot\sqrt{3/2}=1$, so the leading term is $c^3$ exactly. Second, $T(u,u,V)=\langle Q(u),V\rangle=\theta^*\langle u,V\rangle=0$ by E and $V\perp u$, this is the vanishing of the linear term, and it uses **criticality of $u$**, not maximality. Third, $\sqrt{2/3}\,T(u,V,V)=\sqrt{2/3}\langle F_uV,V\rangle=\alpha$ by F. Fourth, $\sqrt{2/3}\,T(V,V,V)=\sqrt{2/3}\,P(V)=\gamma_V$, $V$ being a unit vector. Hence

$$m=c^3+3cs^2\alpha+s^3\gamma_V,$$

which is the block's display, term for term. **The word "exact" is earned**: this is an identity, not a truncation, because $P$ is a cubic *form* and the sphere geodesic is affine in $(u,V)$. The same identity is Nienhaus's $f(\varphi)$ (thesis p. 73), with his $\alpha=\sqrt{2/3}\langle Q(W),W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\rangle$ equal to the block's $\sqrt{2/3}\langle F_uV,V\rangle$ by full symmetry of $\mathrm{tri}$, so the two normalisations agree and no factor is lost in the import.

## 3. The first-variation step, CORRECT, with three hypotheses used and not stated

Quoting: *"The vector $V$ is a unit vector orthogonal to $u$, and orthogonal also to $T_u(SO(n).u)$ by the first-variation condition for a nearest point on the orbit."*

**This is what a nearest point gives.** Let $\delta(x)=d(x,W^*)$ on $\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n$ and let $u$ minimise $\delta$ over $\mathcal O$. On the unit sphere with the round metric, $\delta(x)=\arccos\langle x,W^*\rangle$, so for $x$ in a neighbourhood of $u$ with $\varphi=\delta(u)\in(0,\pi)$ the function $\tfrac12\delta^2$ is smooth and its gradient at $u$ is $-\varphi\,V$, where $V$ is the unit initial velocity at $u$ of the minimising geodesic to $W^*$. The parametrisation $W^*=\cos\varphi\,u+\sin\varphi\,V$ *is* that geodesic, at arc length $\varphi$. Stationarity of $\delta^2|_{\mathcal O}$ at $u$ therefore reads $\langle-\varphi V,X\rangle=0$ for every $X\in T_u\mathcal O$, i.e. $V\perp T_u\mathcal O$. Correct.

**What the step needs and the block does not say.** (i) $\mathcal O$ must be a *submanifold* for $T_u\mathcal O$ to exist, true, $\mathcal O$ being the orbit of the compact group $SO(n)$, hence diffeomorphic to $SO(n)/\mathrm{Stab}(u)$ and compactly embedded. (ii) $\mathcal O$ must be *compact* for the infimum to be attained at all, same reason. (iii) $\varphi>0$, else $V$ is undefined and $\tfrac12\delta^2$ is not differentiable at the minimiser. None of the three is stated; all three hold.

**And the step is load-bearing, not decoration.** Orthogonality to $u$ alone is not enough. Differentiating the equivariance $Q(g.u)=g.Q(u)$ along the orbit gives $2F_u([X,u])=[X,Q(u)]=\theta^*[X,u]$, so $T_u\mathcal O$ is an eigenspace of $F_u$ at $\theta^*/2$; Nienhaus records exactly this at Remark 4.16 (*"we have $F(W)=\tfrac12\sqrt{\tfrac32}W$"* for $W\in T_{W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}}SO(n).W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$). Had $V$ been allowed a component there, the available bound would be $\alpha\le\sqrt{2/3}\cdot\tfrac12\theta^*=\tfrac12$, and at $\alpha\le\tfrac12$ the theorem is **vacuous**: writing $\psi_a(\varphi)=(c^3+3acs^2)/(1-s^3)$ for the bound with $\alpha\le a$, the expansions $c^3+3acs^2=1+(3a-\tfrac32)\varphi^2+O(\varphi^4)$ and $1-s^3=1-\varphi^3+O(\varphi^5)$ give $\psi_a(\varphi)=1+(3a-\tfrac32)\varphi^2+\varphi^3+O(\varphi^4)$. The quadratic coefficient is negative exactly for $a<\tfrac12$ and vanishes at $a=\tfrac12$, where $\psi_a>1$ immediately and no interval survives.

## 4. The $\alpha\le\tfrac13$ import, the citation names a clause that does not carry the claim

**Located.** *"The largest eigenvalue of $F_u$ on $u^\perp\cap T_u(SO(n).u)^\perp$ is $\tfrac13\theta^*$, so $\alpha\le1/3$; this is Nienhaus Theorem 4.14(c), valid for $n\ge9$ and reverified here from scratch."*

**Exhibited, at the source on file.** Nienhaus Theorem 4.14 (thesis p. 68 = PDF p. 72) is a seven-clause list. Clause (c) reads, in full: *"$W\in S^2_1\oplus(S^2_1\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4})\oplus(\Lambda^2_{-,1}(\mathbb R^4)\otimes\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^{n-4}))$ is an eigenvector of eigenvalue $\tfrac13\sqrt{\tfrac32}$."* That is an eigenvalue **on one subspace**. It contains no maximality claim, and it cannot: clause (b) gives the strictly larger eigenvalue $\tfrac12\sqrt{\tfrac32}$ on $\mathrm{Weyl}_4^{-,1}\oplus(X_4^{-,2}\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4})$, and clause (a) gives $\sqrt{\tfrac32}$ on $\langle W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\rangle$. The statement the block needs is that $\tfrac13\theta^*$ is the **largest** eigenvalue *after* (a) and (b) are removed, which is (a)–(g) taken together, plus the identification of (b)'s space with $T_u\mathcal O$. That identification is in the source but at a different place, Remark 4.16, *"the tangent space above is given by $\mathrm{Weyl}_4^{-,1}\oplus X_4^{-,2}\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4}$"*, which the block never cites. So the sentence as written is under-cited by two references, one of them the one that removes the $\tfrac12\theta^*$ eigenvalue that would make the theorem vacuous.

**A dimension check confirms the identification is the right one.** At $n=12$: $\dim\mathrm{Weyl}_4^{-,1}=2$ (Nienhaus's generator lists put $\Lambda^2_{-,1}=\langle i_-\rangle$ and $\Lambda^2_{-,2}=\langle j_-,k_-\rangle$, and his $W_1$ lies in the $2$-dimensional $i_-$–$\{j_-,k_-\}$ off-diagonal piece of $S^2_0(\Lambda^2_-)$) and $\dim X_4^{-,2}=4$ from his four listed generators, giving $2+4\cdot 8=34$; and $\dim T_u\mathcal O=\dim\mathfrak{so}(12)-\dim\mathrm{Stab}=66-(3+1+28)=34$. They agree.

**Argue the opposite.** The most charitable reading available is that "Theorem 4.14(c)" is shorthand for "the clause of Theorem 4.14 that supplies the operative value", with the rest of the theorem understood, which is how Nienhaus himself cites it at Theorem 4.17, writing only *"Proposition 4.14 tells us that $\alpha\le\tfrac13$"* for a $W$ perpendicular to $\mathbb RW_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\oplus T_{W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}}(SO(n).W_{\mathbb{CP}^2})$. Under that reading the block is imprecise rather than wrong, and the mathematics is Nienhaus's. **The candidate stands as a citation defect and not as a mathematical error**, but §5 shows the parent theorem does not close on its own either.

## 5. Theorem 4.14 does not exhaust $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ as printed, one summand is unassigned

**Located.** Proposition 4.11 (thesis p. 66), for $n\ge9$, decomposes $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ orthogonally into nineteen summands, the first being $\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$, and asserts *"The decomposition is irreducible under the action of $\mathrm{Pin}(2)\cdot SU(2)_+\times SO(n-4)$."*

**Exhibited, by matching the two lists.** Theorem 4.14's clauses account for eighteen of them: (a) $\langle W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\rangle$; (b) $\mathrm{Weyl}_4^{-,1}$, $X_4^{-,2}\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4}$; (c) $S^2_1$, $S^2_1\otimes S^2_0(\mathbb R^{n-4})$, $\Lambda^2_{-,1}(\mathbb R^4)\otimes\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^{n-4})$; (d) $\mathrm{Weyl}_4^+$, $S^2_0(\mathbb R^{n-4})$, $\Lambda^2_+(\mathbb R^4)\otimes\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^{n-4})$, $\mathrm{Weyl}_{n-4}$, $T_{W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}}SO(n).W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}$, $\mathbb R^4\otimes X_{n-4}$, $X_4^+\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4}$; (e) $S^2_2$, $S^2_2\otimes S^2_0(\mathbb R^{n-4})$, $\Lambda^2_{-,2}(\mathbb R^4)\otimes\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^{n-4})$; (f) $X_4^{-,1}\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4}$; (g) $\mathrm{Weyl}_4^{-,2}$. **The summand $\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$ appears in no clause.** It is a one-dimensional subspace of $u^\perp\cap T_u\mathcal O^\perp$, so it is inside the very space over which the maximum is being claimed, and Theorem 4.14 as printed says nothing about $F_u$ there.

**And the multiplicity-free question bites exactly here.** $\langle W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\rangle$ and $\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$ are both one-dimensional and both fixed by $\mathrm{Pin}(2)\cdot SU(2)_+\times SO(n-4)$, the first because that group is inside $\mathrm{Stab}(W_{\mathbb{CP}^2})$, the second because $W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}$ is $SO(4)\times SO(n-4)$-invariant. So the trivial isotypic component of $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ has multiplicity at least two, Lemma 4.13 does not apply to it, and the preamble sentence *"any $\mathrm{Pin}(2)\cdot SU(2)_+\times SO(n-4)$-irreducible subspace is an eigenspace of $F_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$"* is not available for those two lines. This is a concrete instance of the multiplicity-free gap [route-a.md](../findings/route-a.md) records in the abstract, sitting in the clause list the block cites.

**Argue the opposite.** The charitable reading is that the omission is a typographical one in clause (d): $\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$ is $SU(2)_-$-invariant, so Lemma 4.15 gives $\operatorname{tr}(F|_V)=0$ on it, and if it is an eigenline its eigenvalue is $0$ and it belongs in the kernel. Its being an eigenline is also available without Lemma 4.13: $F_u$ is self-adjoint and fixes $\langle u\rangle$ by criticality, so it preserves $u^\perp$, and if the trivial isotypic is exactly $\langle u\rangle\oplus\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$ the second line is forced to be invariant too. **I record that repair as available and do not adopt it**: it is not on file, it needs the isotypic to be exactly two-dimensional, and supplying it here would put a fresh unaudited argument into the record under a verifier's letterhead. The finding is that the block's stated authority does not carry the claim, and that the record's own replacement, [Lemma L21](../findings/route-a.md), still has an open obligation ([INDEX-results.md](../findings/INDEX-results.md):36, *"which reduction is correct is open"*). **This is the debt.**

**Minor, at the same clause.** Proposition 4.11's summand is $S^2_1\otimes S^2_0(\mathbb R^{n-4})$; Theorem 4.14(c) prints $S^2_1\otimes\mathbb R^{n-4}$, while the parallel clause (e) prints $S^2_2\otimes S^2_0(\mathbb R^{n-4})$. One of the two is a slip in the source, and it is in the clause the block names.

## 6. The bootstrap $\gamma_V\le m$, CORRECT, and it closes

**$V$ is a unit Weyl operator.** $u\in\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ and $W^*\in\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, and $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$ is a linear subspace, so $V=(W^*-\cos\varphi\,u)/\sin\varphi$ lies in $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$; it has $\lVert V\rVert=1$ by construction. Hence $\gamma_V=\sqrt{2/3}P(V)\le\sqrt{2/3}\max P=m$. Checked.

**It is not circular.** $m$ is defined before the supposition, as the value of an attained maximum of a continuous function on a compact set. No value is assumed for it, and no bound on $\max P$ is used to obtain it, which is what distinguishes this from Nienhaus's Theorem 4.17, whose corresponding estimate $\gamma\le\sqrt{1-2\alpha}(1+\alpha)$ is derived from *"The fact that $f(\varphi)\le1$ on $[0,\pi]$"*, i.e. from the a priori cap $P\le\theta^*$, which is available only where $W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ is already known to be the global maximiser. The block says this in its closing paragraph and it is right.

**The substitution preserves the inequality direction.** Under the supposition $\varphi\in[0,0.5748]\subset[0,\pi/2)$ we have $c>0$ and $s\ge0$, so $3cs^2\ge0$ and $s^3\ge0$, and substituting the upper bounds $\alpha\le\tfrac13$ and $\gamma_V\le m$ into $m=c^3+3cs^2\alpha+s^3\gamma_V$ gives

$$m\le c^3+cs^2+ms^3=c(c^2+s^2)+ms^3=c+ms^3,$$

using the block's identity $c^3+cs^2=c$. Then $m(1-s^3)\le c$, and since $s\le\sin(0.5748)<1$ we have $1-s^3>0$, so division is legitimate and $m\le\psi(\varphi)$. Checked. **The closing needs $m$ to appear on both sides with coefficient $s^3<1$, and it does**, the bootstrap closes rather than looping, because $s^3<1$ strictly on the interval.

## 7. $\psi\le1$ on $[0,0.5748]$, CORRECT, with the endpoint margin computed by hand

Write $u(\varphi)=\cos\varphi+\sin^3\varphi-1$, so that $\psi(\varphi)\le1\iff u(\varphi)\le0$ (the denominator being positive).

**The shape.** $u(0)=0$ and $u'(\varphi)=-\sin\varphi+3\sin^2\varphi\cos\varphi=\sin\varphi\big(\tfrac32\sin2\varphi-1\big)$. On $(0,\pi/2)$ the first factor is positive, and the second is negative exactly while $\sin2\varphi<\tfrac23$. So $u$ decreases on $(0,\tfrac12\arcsin\tfrac23)$ and increases after, and it returns to $0$ at a single point $\varphi_c$ in $(0,\pi/2)$. Hence $u\le0$, i.e. $\psi\le\psi(0)=1$, on all of $[0,\varphi_c]$, with equality only at the two ends.

**Where $\varphi_c$ is, exactly.** $u=0$ with $s=\sin\varphi$, $c=1-s^3$ and $s^2+c^2=1$ gives $s^2-2s^3+s^6=0$, i.e. $s^2(s^4-2s+1)=0$, and $s^4-2s+1=(s-1)(s^3+s^2+s-1)$, expand: $s^4+s^3+s^2-s-s^3-s^2-s+1=s^4-2s+1$. The root $s=1$ is spurious (it is $\varphi=\pi/2$, introduced by using $c=1-s^3$ with $c\ge0$). So $\sin\varphi_c=s^*$, the real root of $s^3+s^2+s=1$, which is unique in $(0,1)$ since the cubic is strictly increasing there. **Bracketing $s^*$ by hand**: at $s=0.54368$, $s^2=0.295588$ and $s^3=0.160707$, so $s^3+s^2+s-1=-0.000025$; at $s=0.54370$, $s^2=0.295610$ and $s^3=0.160725$, so the value is $+0.000035$. Hence $s^*\in(0.54368,0.54370)$.

**And $0.5748$ is inside.** $\sin(0.5748)=2\sin(0.2874)\cos(0.2874)$. From the alternating series, $\sin(0.2874)=0.2874-0.0039565+0.0000163-\dots=0.2834598$ to within $3\times10^{-8}$, so $\cos(0.2874)=\sqrt{1-0.0803494}=0.9589844$ and $\sin(0.5748)=0.5436700$. That is **below** $s^*$, so $0.5748<\varphi_c$ and the closed interval $[0,0.5748]$ lies strictly inside $\{u\le0\}$. The block's *"first exceeding $1$ at $\varphi=0.5748$"* is therefore false as an equality and true as a safe truncation.

**The margin, quantified.** $u(0.5748)\approx u'(\varphi_c)\cdot(0.5748-\varphi_c)$. With $\varphi_c-0.5748=(s^*-0.54367)/\cos\varphi_c\approx 0.000018/0.83930=2.1\times10^{-5}$ and $u'(\varphi_c)=s^*\big(\tfrac32\cdot 2s^*c-1\big)=0.54369\cdot(1.36893-1)=0.2006$, we get $u(0.5748)\approx-4.2\times10^{-6}$ and

$$\psi(0.5748)=1-\frac{|u(0.5748)|}{1-s^3}\approx1-\frac{4.2\times10^{-6}}{0.8393}\approx1-5\times10^{-6}.$$

**So the stated radius is safe by about five parts in a million, and $0.5749$ would not be.** The block prints four decimals of a degree-three algebraic number and never states the cubic; a consumer who re-rounded the number in the other direction would break the proof.

**The trough is misreported, harmlessly.** *"$\psi$ falls to $0.9787$ near $\varphi=0.3927$."* Differentiating, $\psi'$ vanishes where $-s+3s^2-2s^4=0$, i.e. $2s^3-3s+1=0$, i.e. $(s-1)(2s^2+2s-1)=0$, so the interior minimiser is $s=(\sqrt3-1)/2=0.3660254$ (a closed form the record never states), at $\varphi=\arcsin\!\big((\sqrt3-1)/2\big)=0.37472$, where $c=\sqrt{1-\tfrac{4-2\sqrt3}{4}}=\sqrt{0.866025}=0.930604$ and $s^3=0.049038$, giving $\psi=0.930604/0.950962=0.978589$. The quoted pair $(0.3927,0.9787)$ is $\psi$ at $\pi/8$, which is $0.923880/0.943949=0.978739$, a point near the trough, not the trough. Nothing downstream uses either number; only $\psi\le1$ is used. **Recorded as an inaccuracy, not a defect.**

## 8. What is $d$?, geodesic angular distance, forced by the proof, stated nowhere in this record

**What the expansion commits the proof to.** $W^*=\cos\varphi\,u+\sin\varphi\,V$ with $\lVert u\rVert=\lVert V\rVert=\lVert W^*\rVert=1$ and $V\perp u$ is the unit-speed great-circle geodesic through $u$ with initial velocity $V$, evaluated at arc length $\varphi$. Equivalently $\langle W^*,u\rangle=\cos\varphi$. So $\varphi=\arccos\langle W^*,u\rangle$: **the geodesic angular distance in the unit sphere of $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, in the record's inner product.** No other reading makes the display an identity, under a chordal $d$ the same letter $\varphi$ would appear both as a chord length and as an angle in the same line.

**Whether that is stated anywhere.** In this record, no. [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md) has no row for $d$ or for a metric on $\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n$; the string "angular" occurs in [results.md](../results.md) at :80 and :673, in [problem.md](../findings/problem.md):467 and in [route-a.md](../findings/route-a.md)'s commentary, never with a definition, and **never inside the proof block itself**. It *is* stated in the imported source, and that is the only place on file where it is defined: Nienhaus Theorem 4.19 introduces $U_{W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}}=\{W\in\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n\mid d_{\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n}(W,SO(n).W_{\mathbb{CP}^2})<\gamma_n\}$ with *"The distance function $d_{\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n}$ denotes the inner metric on $\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n$"*, and immediately after Remark 4.20: *"since $\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n$ is a sphere in the vector space $\mathrm{Weyl}_n$, the inner metric on $\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n$ is given by $d_{\mathrm{Weyl}^1_n}(W_1,W_2)=\sphericalangle(W_1,W_2)$"* (thesis p. 75). His own radii, $\gamma_{11}=0.13$ and $\gamma_{10}=0.26$ with the alternatives $\pi/24$ and $\pi/12$, are angles.

**How the radius changes under the other reasonable reading.** Read $0.5748$ as a chord, $\lVert W^*-u\rVert=0.5748$. Then $\sin(\varphi/2)=0.2874$ and $\varphi=2\arcsin(0.2874)$. By the arcsine series at $x=0.2874$, terms $x$, $x^3/6=0.00395655$, $3x^5/40=0.00014706$, $15x^7/336=0.00000723$, $105x^9/3456=0.00000041$, this is $\varphi=0.5830225$. There $\cos\varphi=1-2(0.2874)^2=0.8348025$ and $\sin\varphi=0.5748\cos(0.2874)=0.5748\cdot0.9578107=0.5505490$, so $\sin^3\varphi=0.1668730$ and

$$\psi=\frac{0.8348025}{1-0.1668730}=\frac{0.8348025}{0.8331270}=1.00201.$$

**Under the chord reading the proof does not reach its own stated radius**: $\psi>1$ there, so the interval $[0,\varphi]$ is no longer inside $\{\psi\le1\}$ and the contradiction is not obtained at the far end. The two readings are not close: the chord reading claims a tube $1.4\%$ wider in angle than the proof supports, and the proof's own $\varphi_c$ corresponds to a chord of only $2\sin(\varphi_c/2)\approx0.5669$. **The metric is load-bearing and undefined in this record.**

## 9. Quantifiers, scope, and the three edges

**Quantifier order.** $n\ge12$ is fixed first; then $m=m(n)$ and $W^*=W^*(n)$; then the supposition is on that $W^*$. Since the argument only uses that *some* maximiser satisfies $\varphi\le0.5748$, its negation gives $\varphi>0.5748$ for **every** maximiser, which is what the canonical form claims. Correct. $V$ and $\alpha$ depend on $W^*$; $\tfrac13$ does not; $m$ does not depend on $W^*$. No quantifier is inverted.

**$n=12$ exactly.** $\theta_0(12)^2=2\cdot11\cdot10/144=220/144=55/36=1.5\overline{27}>3/2$, so the witness clears with margin $1/36$ in the square. Equivalently $\theta_0(n)>\theta^*\iff 4(n-1)(n-2)>3n^2\iff n^2-12n+8>0$, which is $8>0$ at $n=12$ and $-11<0$ at $n=11$. Correct, and the threshold $n>6+2\sqrt7=11.2915\dots$ is as stated.

**$\varphi=0$.** The quoted proof divides by $\sin\varphi$ implicitly, in that $V$ is only defined for $\varphi>0$; at $\varphi=0$ there is no $V$ and no first-variation condition. The branch is covered in [route-a.md](../findings/route-a.md), in a paragraph placed **after** the $\square$: *"there $W^*$ lies on the orbit, $\max P=\sqrt{3/2}$, and the same contradiction applies"*, which is right, since $\varphi=0$ forces $W^*\in\mathcal O$, hence $\max P=P(u)=\theta^*$, contradicting $\max P\ge\theta_0(n)>\theta^*$. **The branch is absent from the brief as dispatched and absent from [results.md](../results.md):673**, so a consumer reading the canonical entry alone gets a proof with an uncovered endpoint.

**Odd $n$, the final step is empty as written.** Quoting: *"But the balanced sphere product is a unit Weyl operator with $P=\theta_0(n)$, which exceeds $\sqrt{3/2}$ for $n\ge12$, a contradiction."* The object the last inequality needs is **a unit Weyl operator whose $P$ exceeds $\theta^*$**. The object named is the balanced sphere product $S^{n/2}\times S^{n/2}$, which exists only at even $n$; [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md):18 says so in terms (*"$\theta_0(n)$ … It is the value at the balanced sphere product **at even $n$ only**"*) and adds that at odd $n$ the balanced split gives $\Theta_n<\theta_0(n)$, the gap being $\theta_0^2-\Theta_n^2=8(n-1)/(n^2(n+1)(n-2))$. So at $n=13,15,17,\dots$ the proof's final sentence names a nonexistent witness and asserts for it a value nothing attains. **The statement is quantified over every $n\ge12$; at odd $n$ its proof stops one object short.**

**What would have to be true instead, and whether it is on the record.** The record's own diagonal maximum $\Theta_n$ is the natural candidate, and it clears: $\Theta_n^2>3/2$ reads $2(n-1)(n-3)/((n+1)(n-2))>3/2$, i.e. $4(n-1)(n-3)>3(n+1)(n-2)$, i.e. $4n^2-16n+12>3n^2-3n-6$, i.e. $n^2-13n+18>0$, whose positive root is $(13+\sqrt{97})/2=11.4244\dots$, so it holds at every $n\ge12$ and fails at $n=11$, matching $\Theta_{11}=\sqrt{160/108}=1.21716<\theta^*<\Theta_{13}=\sqrt{240/154}=1.24838$. **That repair is on the record**, [route-a.md](../findings/route-a.md)'s commentary states it, and [lean/INDEX.md](../lean/INDEX.md):228 carries it as a kernel-checked row, [OddWitness.lean](../lean/HamiltonsCubic/Results/OddWitness.lean), **but it is nowhere in the proof, and nowhere in [results.md](../results.md):673.** I record the debt rather than performing the substitution: what the block owes at odd $n$ is a named unit Weyl operator attaining $\Theta_n$, with its value, cited from wherever the record proves it.

## 12. What would falsify the statement, and what I did to look for it

**The object that would break it.** A unit Weyl operator $W$ with $P(W)=M(n)$ for some $n\ge12$ and $\arccos\langle W,g.W_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\rangle\le0.5748$ for some $g\in SO(n)$. By the chain above, such a $W$ exists only if one of three things fails: (i) $\lambda_{\max}(F_u|_{u^\perp\cap T_u\mathcal O^\perp})>\tfrac13\theta^*$, which is where I would look first, since it is the only unproved input and since the neighbouring eigenvalue $\tfrac12\theta^*$ on $T_u\mathcal O$ would already destroy the theorem; (ii) $M(n)\le\theta^*$ at some $n\ge12$, i.e. the witness fails, which is exactly the odd-$n$ hole; or (iii) an arithmetic failure in $\psi\le1$ on the interval, which §7 rules out with margin $5\times10^{-6}$ at the endpoint.

**What I did to look for it.** For (i): read Nienhaus Proposition 4.11 and Theorem 4.14 in the PDF on file, matched the nineteen summands of the decomposition against the seven clauses of the eigenvalue theorem one by one, and found $\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$ unassigned, a one-dimensional subspace of the transverse space on which the source states nothing. That is the nearest thing to a falsifier I found, and it is a hole in the *authority*, not a witness: I did not establish that $F_u$ is large there, and I did not close it either. For (ii): evaluated $\theta_0(n)^2$ and $\Theta_n^2$ against $3/2$ in closed form, getting $n^2-12n+8>0$ and $n^2-13n+18>0$; both hold at $n\ge12$, so no dimension in range lacks a value-witness, though at odd $n$ the block names the wrong one. For (iii): re-derived $\psi\le1$ from $u'=\sin\varphi(\tfrac32\sin2\varphi-1)$, factored the radius cubic by hand, and bracketed its root. **No falsifier found. No numerical work was done, and no script was run.**

## 13. Verdict

**`HYPOTHESIS-DEBT`.**

The mathematics of L20 is sound wherever it is written out. The expansion is exact and I rebuilt it from the polarization identity; the first-variation step is what a nearest point gives; the bootstrap is not circular and closes because $\sin^3\varphi<1$; $\psi\le1$ holds on $[0,0.5748]$, and the constant is a truncation of $\arcsin s^*$ in the safe direction, with about five parts in a million to spare.

**Two things it consumes have no argument on file.**

1. **$\lambda_{\max}\big(F_u|_{u^\perp\cap T_u(SO(n).u)^\perp}\big)\le\tfrac13\theta^*$.** The block cites Nienhaus Theorem 4.14(c), which states an eigenvalue on one subspace and no maximum; the maximality needs (a)–(g) together with Remark 4.16, and (a)–(g) as printed leaves the Proposition 4.11 summand $\langle W_{S^4\times S^{n-4}}\rangle$ unassigned, inside the very space at issue, at a place where the multiplicity-free hypothesis behind the parent theorem's Schur step provably fails, the trivial isotypic containing at least two one-dimensional summands. The record's replacement, [Lemma L21](../findings/route-a.md), has an open obligation of its own.
2. **A unit Weyl operator with $P>\theta^*$ at odd $n$.** The proof names the balanced sphere product, which does not exist at odd $n$; [conventions.md](../findings/conventions.md):18 says so directly. The repair under $\Theta_n$ exists on the record and in Lean, and is not in the block or in [results.md](../results.md):673.

**And one word is unsupported in three places.** *"Unconditional"* at [results.md](../results.md):44, :48 and :232 is stated with no qualifier, while input 1 above is unproved; :44 additionally attributes unconditionality to a bundle containing an Observation the same file calls circular.

**What would have to be shown to reach `PROVEN`:** input 1, in the form $\langle F_uV,V\rangle\le\tfrac13\theta^*$ for every unit $V\in u^\perp\cap T_u\mathcal O^\perp$ at every $n\ge12$, with an exhaustion that accounts for every summand; and input 2, a named unit Weyl operator with $P>\sqrt{3/2}$ at each odd $n\ge13$. Both are then to be written **inside** the proof block, not beside it.
