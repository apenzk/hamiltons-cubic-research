## 0. Conventions, fixed once

$\mathcal W_n$ is the space of algebraic Weyl tensors, read as symmetric operators on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^n$ with the Frobenius inner product; $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ without a subscript is that norm, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{op}$ the operator norm. $S=\{\lVert W\rVert=1\}$. $P(W)=\langle W,Q(W)\rangle$, $\theta_0(n)=\sqrt{2(n-1)(n-2)}/n$, $\kappa_n=9(\sqrt{3/2}-\theta_0(n))$, $\Phi=P-\kappa_n\delta^2$ with $\delta^2(W)=\lVert\mathrm{Alt}(W_-)\rVert^2$ and $W_-$ the negative spectral part. $W^*$ is the unit-norm Weyl tensor of $S^m\times S^m$, $n=2m$, $\mathcal O=SO(n)\cdot W^*$. Along a direction $H$, $A(t):=\mathrm{Alt}\big(W(t)_-\big)$ with $W(t)=W^*+tH$ (equivalently the great circle; the distinction is second order and does not enter below), $M:=\sup_{\lvert z\rvert=\rho}\lVert A(z)\rVert$ on the complexified disc of radius $\rho$. $c_n$ and $c_p$ both name [Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md)(b)'s margin constant $3\theta_0(n)/(n-2)$; $C$ and $C_\delta$ name third-order constants, $C_\delta$ the defect half. "Tube radius" is used by [plan.md](../plan.md) I2 and by [Lemma L133](../findings/second-order.md) for *the radius of a tube about $\mathcal O$ on which [Conjecture L16](../findings/problem.md) holds*, and by [Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md) for *the derived quantity $r_n=c_n/C$*. **L145 does not say which it means, and section 1 shows the boxed claim fails under both.**

## 1. C1, the boxed $\Theta$ is refuted inside its own entry, and fails under every reading of "tube radius"

**Locate.** [findings/second-order.md](../findings/second-order.md):1102, "> **LEMMA L145.** *At the balanced product $W^*=S^m\times S^m$, $n=2m$, the penalised functional's tube radius about $SO(n)\cdot W^*$ is $r_n=\Theta(n^{-3/2})$.*", and the heading one line above, "the balanced-orbit tube radius is $\Theta(n^{-3/2})$".

**Exhibit the failure.** A $\Theta$ is two statements. **Nothing in the entry bounds $r_n$ above.** Every estimate it carries runs the other way: $C_\delta=O(n)$ is an upper bound on a constant that sits in a denominator, and $r_n\gtrsim c/C$ is what an upper bound on $C$ buys. The one sentence that touches an upper bound denies it, step 4's row reads "**CONFIRMED as a lower bound.** *The equality reading is withdrawn: [Lemma L146] removes its support, and $\Theta(n^{-1})$ is reachable*" (line 1111). "$\Theta(n^{-1})$ is reachable" and "$r_n=\Theta(n^{-3/2})$" cannot both hold: $n^{-1}/n^{-3/2}=\sqrt n\to\infty$. **The box and the table of the same entry state contradictory things, and the box was never amended.**

Take the three readings of "tube radius" the record supplies, and the box fails under each.

- **Reading A, the record's primary one** ([plan.md](../plan.md) I2: "*corollary, [Conjecture L16](../findings/problem.md) holds on a tube of explicit radius about the balanced orbit*"): $r_n:=\sup\{r:\Phi\le\theta_0$ on the $r$-tube$\}$. Then the upper half of $\Theta$ asserts that there is a constant $K$ and, at each even $n\ge12$, a tensor $V$ with $d(V,\mathcal O)\le Kn^{-3/2}$ and $\Phi(V)>\theta_0(n)$. **That is a refutation of [Conjecture L16](../findings/problem.md)**, the statement this entire route exists to prove, and which [findings/problem.md](../findings/problem.md):347 reports as surviving a full adversarial battery at $n=12$ and $n=14$. Under reading A the box is not merely unproved; it asserts the opposite of the programme's target.
- **Reading B, [Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md)'s** ("*the radius is $r_n=c_n/C$*"): with the entry's own constants, $c_n=3\theta_0/(n-2)=\Theta(1/n)$ and $C=O(n)$, so $c_n/C=\Omega(n^{-2})$, and the entry itself says so, calling $n^{-2}$ "the direction-uniform quotient". So under reading B the entry's own arithmetic gives $n^{-2}$, not $\Theta(n^{-3/2})$.
- **Reading C, the entry's own** ("$n^{-3/2}$ is $\inf_H c(H)/C(H)$, which is what a tube radius is", line 1115): $\inf_H c(H)/C(H)$ is a *lower* bound on the radius of validity, since $c$ is a lower bound on the margin and $C$ an upper bound on the remainder. The entry has already recorded the shape of that error one page earlier, [post F60](../findings/second-order.md): "*dividing a $\min$ by a $\max$ is a lower bound on the radius, and a fence needs the opposite direction*". Even taken as an equality about that expression, the value depends on which estimates $c$ and $C$ are fixed, and [Lemma L147](../findings/second-order.md) puts the method's own ceiling at $\Theta(n^{-1})$, so the expression is bracketed in $[n^{-3/2},n^{-1}]$, which is not $\Theta(n^{-3/2})$.

**Argue the opposite.** The most charitable reading available is that "is $\Theta(n^{-3/2})$" was written as shorthand for "*the chain returns the exponent $-3/2$*" (a claim about the output of one calculation rather than about $r_n$) and that step 4's withdrawal is the entry correcting itself in place, as this record does elsewhere. That reading is exactly what [Lemma L147](../findings/second-order.md) enforces on itself: "**$\gtrsim$, not $\Theta$.** *The first version wrote $\Theta$, the exact error L145 step 4 had been corrected for two entries earlier.*" **It rescues the intent and not the text.** The heading and the box are the two lines a reader takes, [findings/INDEX-results.md](../findings/INDEX-results.md):164 reproduces the $\Theta$ verbatim under "Gate 2 passed", and [plan.md](../plan.md):66 records the sub-step as DONE with "the radius is $\Theta(n^{-3/2})$". The withdrawal is in a table cell inside the entry and has propagated to neither index.

**Verdict C1: BROKEN.** The correct statement of what the entry supports is $r_n\gtrsim n^{-3/2}$, with the caveats of §3.

## 2. C2–C6, C8, every numeral I could reproduce, and the two I could not

**C2, reproduced exactly.** From [Lemma L133](../findings/second-order.md) step 1, $W^*\propto R-\tfrac{m-1}{2m-1}\mathrm{id}$ has eigenvalue $\tfrac m{2m-1}$ with multiplicity $m(m-1)$ on $\Lambda^2V_1\oplus\Lambda^2V_2$ and $-\tfrac{m-1}{2m-1}$ with multiplicity $m^2$ on the mixed planes. Unnormalised squared norm:
$$m(m-1)\frac{m^2}{(2m-1)^2}+m^2\frac{(m-1)^2}{(2m-1)^2}=\frac{m^2(m-1)\big[m+(m-1)\big]}{(2m-1)^2}=\frac{m^2(m-1)}{2m-1}.$$
Dividing by its square root, $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=\dfrac{m-1}{2m-1}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{2m-1}}{m\sqrt{m-1}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{m-1}}{m\sqrt{2m-1}}$, and at $n=2m$,
$$\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=\frac{\sqrt{(n-2)/2}}{(n/2)\sqrt{n-1}}=\frac2n\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}},$$
**the entry's $g_n$ character for character**, $\sim\sqrt2/n$. The trace check $m(m-1)\cdot m-m^2(m-1)=0$ confirms tracelessness, so the spectrum is two-valued as claimed. That $g_n$ is the gap at $0$ and not merely the negative eigenvalue needs $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert\le\lambda_+$, which is $\tfrac{m-1}{2m-1}\le\tfrac m{2m-1}$, i.e. $(m-1)/m<1$, **true at every $m$**, so the identification $g_n=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$ is unconditional and the entry is right to write it without a range. **CONFIRMED.**

**C3.** With $\lVert E\rVert_{op}\le\lVert E\rVert_F$, a real Frobenius perturbation of norm $<g_n$ moves no eigenvalue across $0$, so the spectral projection and hence $W\mapsto W_-$ is real-analytic on that ball. For the complexified disc, a contour separating the negative group must sit at distance $\asymp g_n/2$ from $0$ and the perturbation must stay strictly inside it, which is the entry's "$\rho<g_n/2$, not $\rho=g_n/2$". **CONFIRMED as stated.** The entry supplies no proof of either half; both are standard and I record that I checked the correction rather than a derivation.

**C5, exact.** $\delta^2=\langle A,A\rangle$ gives $(\delta^2)'=2\langle A',A\rangle$, $(\delta^2)''=2\langle A'',A\rangle+2\langle A',A'\rangle$, and
$$(\delta^2)'''=2\langle A''',A\rangle+2\langle A'',A'\rangle+4\langle A'',A'\rangle=2\langle A''',A\rangle+6\langle A'',A'\rangle.$$
**CONFIRMED.**

**C4, the bound holds, the attribution does not.** Cauchy on the disc of radius $\rho$ gives $\lVert A^{(k)}\rVert\le k!\,M/\rho^k$. With $\rho=\Theta(g_n)=\Theta(1/n)$:

| $M$ | $\lVert A\rVert$ | $\lVert A'\rVert$ | $\lVert A''\rVert$ | $\lVert A'''\rVert$ | $2\langle A''',A\rangle$ | $6\langle A'',A'\rangle$ | $C_\delta$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $O(1)$ | $O(1)$ | $O(n)$ | $O(n^2)$ | $O(n^3)$ | $O(n^3)$ | $O(n^3)$ | $O(n^3)$ |
| $O(g_n)$ | $O(n^{-1})$ | $O(1)$ | $O(n)$ | $O(n^2)$ | $O(n)$ | $O(n)$ | $O(n)$ |

Reading the table settles the entry's own account of itself. **The $M=O(g_n)$ correction alone carries $O(n^3)$ to $O(n)$**: in the second row *both* terms are $O(n)$, so the vanishing of $\langle A''',A\rangle$ at $t=0$ changes nothing. And in the first row *both* terms are $O(n^3)$, so killing only the first leaves $O(n^3)$. **The entry's "two errors compounded" is one error; the half it emphasises in bold is order-inert.** Worse, the two are not independent: $M=O(g_n)$ *is* the statement $\mathrm{Alt}(W^*_-)=0$ read through the Lipschitz bound, since $x\mapsto x_-$ is $1$-Lipschitz in Frobenius norm and $\lVert\mathrm{Alt}\rVert\le1$ give $\lVert A(z)\rVert=\lVert A(z)-A(0)\rVert\le\rho$. So the entry offers one fact twice and calls the two halves independent.

**Argue the opposite.** Charitably, the entry may intend the pointwise third derivative *at* $t=0$, where $A(0)=0$ genuinely removes a term, and may intend the two errors as errors of the *previous* derivation rather than as two independent contributions to the correction. That reading survives, and it is why the verdict on the bound is CONFIRMED and only the attribution is BROKEN. It also exposes a smaller gap the entry does not mention: a Taylor remainder needs $(\delta^2)'''$ on the whole segment, where $A(t)\ne0$; the sup is still $O(n)$, but by $\lVert A\rVert\le M=O(g_n)$, again the first correction, again not the vanishing at $0$.

**C6, a sign error against the entry's own import.** Line 1113 writes $W^*_-=\lvert\lambda_-\rvert(\mathrm{id}-P_{\Lambda^2V_1}-P_{\Lambda^2V_2})$. The bracket is $0$ on $\Lambda^2V_1\oplus\Lambda^2V_2$ and $\mathrm{id}$ on the $m^2$ mixed planes, so as written $W^*_-$ has eigenvalue $+\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$ there and is positive semidefinite, impossible for a negative spectral part. At $n=12$ the written form gives $+0.1123666$ on each of the $36$ mixed planes where $W^*_-$ has $-0.1123666$. [Lemma L133](../findings/second-order.md) states it correctly: "*$W_-^*$ is a **negative** multiple of the projector onto the mixed planes*". **Charitably**, $\lvert\lambda_-\rvert$ may be shorthand for the magnitude with the sign carried by convention, and the conclusion the sentence is making, $\mathrm{Alt}(W^*_-)=0$, because each of the three summands is a curvature tensor satisfying first Bianchi, is unaffected, $\mathrm{Alt}$ being linear. **BROKEN in sign, inert in consequence**, recorded because the same line is the entry's structural justification and is quoted downstream.

**C8, the three exponents.** $n^{-4}=c_n/C_\delta^{\text{old}}=\Theta(n^{-1})/\Theta(n^3)$: **reproduces**. $n^{-2}=c_n/\sup_HC(H)=\Theta(n^{-1})/\Theta(n)$: **reproduces**. The supporting sentence also reproduces: $\lVert W^*_-\rVert_{op}=g_n$ while $\lVert W^*_-\rVert_F=m\lvert\lambda_-\rvert=\Theta(1)$, and the rank conversion factor $\sqrt{\binom n2}=\sqrt{n(n-1)/2}\sim n/\sqrt2$ against $1/g_n\sim n/\sqrt2$, **$\asymp$ confirmed**, and at $n=12$ the two read $\sqrt{66}$ and $1/0.1123666$, a ratio of $1.095$, so the "$\asymp$" and not "$=$" is the right symbol. The third exponent is C7 and is not reproduced, because nothing is displayed to reproduce.

## 3. C7, pre-registered question 1: which of the two directions the constant chain establishes

**Answer: the lower one only, and even that is asserted rather than derived here.** §1 disposes of the upper. This section is about the lower.

**Locate.** Line 1115: "*$n^{-3/2}$ is $\inf_H c(H)/C(H)$, **which is what a tube radius is***." That is the whole of the entry's account of its own exponent. There is no display of $c(H)$, no display of $C(H)$, no domain for $H$, and no minimisation.

**Exhibit what is missing, concretely.** The entry's own uniform ingredients are $c(H)\ge c_n=\Theta(1/n)$ and $C(H)=O(n)$, and it says in the same sentence that they give $n^{-2}$. To reach $n^{-3/2}$ one needs the *coupling*: that a direction with $C(H)=\Theta(n)$ cannot also have $c(H)=\Theta(1/n)$. Write the trade-off in the form the block uses, $f(u)=(c_p+u^2)/(S+au)$ with $S=\sup\lvert P\rvert$ and $a=\Theta(n)$ the defect coefficient. Then $f'(0)=-ac_p/S^2<0$, the minimum is interior at $u^*=(\sqrt{S^2+a^2c_p}-S)/a$, and $f(u^*)=2u^*/a$. With $c_p=\Theta(1/n)$ and $a=\Theta(n)$ one has $a^2c_p=\Theta(n)$, so

- $S=\Theta(1)$: $u^*\asymp\sqrt n/n=n^{-1/2}$ and $f(u^*)=2u^*/a=\Theta(n^{-3/2})$;
- $S=\Theta(\sqrt n)$: $\sqrt{S^2+a^2c_p}=\Theta(\sqrt n)$ likewise, so again $u^*=\Theta(n^{-1/2})$ and $f(u^*)=\Theta(n^{-3/2})$;
- $S=\Theta(n)$: $\sqrt{n^2+n}-n\to\tfrac12$, so $u^*\asymp1/n$ and $f(u^*)=\Theta(n^{-2})$.

**So the exponent $-3/2$ holds exactly when $\sup\lvert P\rvert=O(\sqrt n)$, and collapses to $-2$ at the record's older $\Theta(n)$ bounds.** The bound that puts it in the first regime is [Lemma L143](../findings/bounds.md), $\sup\lvert P\rvert\le1+\sqrt{n-2}$, whose own text says so in terms: "*Substituting $\lVert P\rVert_\infty=C\sqrt n$ … returns $r_n=\Omega(n^{-3/2})$ … So $\sqrt n$ is exactly the threshold*". **L145 cites L143 nowhere and never mentions $\sup\lvert P\rvert$ except inside the reviewers' disagreement.** [Post F60](../findings/second-order.md), one page above L145 in the same file, states the same dichotomy with the same two outcomes and adds "*so the exponent is not fixed at all*". The exponent in L145's box therefore rests on an uncited import that the record was, at the time, explicitly unsure of.

**Argue the opposite.** A reader could say the chain is a single object migrated wholesale from [I2](../plan.md)'s ingredient (2), that L143 is numbered below L145 and was available, and that "$\inf_H c(H)/C(H)$" is a pointer to a calculation the plan already contained. **That defence concedes the finding**: the entry is a pointer to a calculation, and the calculation is not in it. The entry's own provenance line says as much, the load-bearing step "*lived only in [r441]*", a review, when the lemma was written.

**Verdict C7: HYPOTHESIS-DEBT.** What would have to be proved, stated precisely: *for every $H\in T_{W^*}S\cap T_{W^*}\mathcal O^\perp$ with $\lVert H\rVert=1$, the margin $c(H)$ and the third-order constant $C(H)$ satisfy $c(H)/C(H)\ge Kn^{-3/2}$ with $K>0$ absolute*, the joint minimisation over the direction's decomposition, together with the substitution $\sup\lvert P\rvert=O(\sqrt n)$ from [Lemma L143](../findings/bounds.md).

## 4. C10, the quantifier, and the object as literally written is zero

**Locate.** Line 1115, "$\inf_H c(H)/C(H)$", and line 1119, "*The object is an **infimum** over directions … every ingredient is uniform in $H$, Cauchy using only $\lVert H\rVert=1$*". The only restriction placed on $H$ anywhere in the entry is $\lVert H\rVert=1$.

**Exhibit the failure, in closed form at named objects.** [Lemma L27](../findings/nondegeneracy.md) makes $L_e$ a scalar on each of ten summands, and the margin is $c=3\theta_0(\tfrac12-\lambda)$ per summand ([Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md)(b), [Lemma L146](../findings/second-order.md)):

| summand | $L_e$ | margin $3\theta_0(\tfrac12-\lambda)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\langle W\rangle$ | $1$ | $-\tfrac32\theta_0$ |
| $\mathbb R^m\otimes\mathbb R^m$ (the orbit tangent) | $\tfrac12$ | $0$ |
| $S^2_0(\mathbb R^m)$, two copies | $\lambda_m=\tfrac{m-2}{2(m-1)}$ | $\tfrac{3\theta_0}{n-2}=c_p$ |
| $\Lambda^2\otimes\Lambda^2$ | $+\mu=\tfrac2{n-2}$ | $\tfrac{3\theta_0(n-6)}{2(n-2)}$ |
| $S^2_0\otimes S^2_0$ | $-\mu$ | $\tfrac{3\theta_0(n+2)}{2(n-2)}$ |
| $\mathrm{Weyl}_m$ (twice), $X_m\otimes\mathbb R^m$ (twice) | $0$ | $\tfrac32\theta_0$ |

Take $H$ a unit vector in $\mathbb R^m\otimes\mathbb R^m$, a direction tangent to the orbit, which is a perfectly good unit direction and the only restriction the entry imposes. Then $c(H)=0$, so $c(H)/C(H)=0$ and **the displayed infimum is $0$: the entry's own formula returns $r_n=0$ at every $n$.** Take $H=W^*$ instead: $L_e=1$, so $c(H)=-\tfrac32\theta_0$, negative, at $n=12$, $-\tfrac32(1.236033)=-1.854$, and the quotient is negative. [Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md) flags exactly this second one: "***The radial direction must be excluded by hand***", and its domain is $T_{W^*}S\cap T_{W^*}\mathcal O^\perp$, the intersection load-bearing.

**Argue the opposite.** The intended domain is unmistakable from context, the tube is *about the orbit*, $\Phi$ is constant along it, and line 1119's "*the geodesic segments from the orbit **fill** the tube by invariance*" is precisely the sentence that makes orbit directions irrelevant. Every successor entry reads it that way: [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md) writes the domain out as $T_{W^*}S\cap T_{W^*}\mathcal O^\perp$ and says the two exclusions are "*not bookkeeping: at ten the margin floor would be $-\tfrac32\theta_0$*", the exact number above. **So the reading is recoverable and the mathematics is not wrong; the statement is.** The finding is that the entry states a quantifier over all unit $H$ and needs a quantifier over a subspace, and that the difference between them is the difference between $r_n>0$ and $r_n=0$.

**Verdict C10: GAP.** The repair is one clause, restrict $H$ to $T_{W^*}S\cap T_{W^*}\mathcal O^\perp$, citing [Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md)(b), which L145 does not cite at all.

## 5. C11, the edge of every range

**The smallest admissible dimension.** L145 states none. It is $n\ge12$, on two independent grounds the entry uses silently. *First*, $c_n=3\theta_0/(n-2)$ is [Lemma L141](../findings/second-order.md)(b), stated at $n=2m\ge12$. *Second*, and sharper, the whole construction is about $\Phi=P-\kappa_n\delta^2$ being a *penalised* functional, which needs $\kappa_n\le0$, i.e. $\theta_0(n)\ge\sqrt{3/2}$. **That is the crossover $n=6+2\sqrt7=11.2915\ldots$**, equivalently $n^2-12n+8>0$: at $\Theta_{11}^2=1.4815$ against $\Theta_{12}^2=1.5278$ the sign flips, and below it the penalty becomes a bonus and the balanced orbit is no longer where [Conjecture L16](../findings/problem.md) is an equality. The entry mentions neither the floor nor the crossover. Both [Lemma L147](../findings/second-order.md) and [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md) later write the floor into their own hypothesis boxes, L147 explicitly "*$n\ge12$, which the imports force through $n^2-12n+8>0$ … **all four used and none stated until [ROUND_L147]***". **L145 is the entry those two inherited the omission from.**

**Odd $n$.** L145 says nothing, and correctly cannot: its base point is $S^m\times S^m$, which exists only at even $n$. ## 9. What would break it, and what I did to look

**For the lower bound.** A single direction $H\in T_{W^*}S\cap T_{W^*}\mathcal O^\perp$, $\lVert H\rVert_F=1$, with $c(H)=\Theta(1/n)$ and third-order constant $C(H)=\omega(\sqrt n)$ (margin at the floor, remainder above the square root), would push $\inf_Hc(H)/C(H)$ below $n^{-3/2}$ and refute the surviving claim. **I looked in the only place such a direction can live.** [Lemma L27](../findings/nondegeneracy.md)'s ten summands are enumerated with their margins in §4: two are removed by the domain ($\langle W\rangle$ at $-\tfrac32\theta_0$, the orbit at $0$); of the eight remaining, the unique margin floor is $c_p=3\theta_0/(n-2)$ on the two $S^2_0(\mathbb R^m)$ copies, and it is strictly below the next one, $\tfrac{3\theta_0(n-6)}{2(n-2)}$ on $\Lambda^2\otimes\Lambda^2$, at every $n>8$. **On the floor summand the defect is identically zero** ([Lemma L148](../findings/second-order.md), which needs no gap and holds for every real $t$), so $C(H)$ there carries the $P$-side alone and is $O(\sqrt n)$ by [Lemma L143](../findings/bounds.md), the falsifier is not on any single summand. It can therefore only be a mixed direction, which is exactly the object [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md) sweeps and [I33](../plan.md) was opened for. **I did not find one, and L145 contains no argument that none exists.**

**For the upper bound.** A tensor $V$ with $d(V,\mathcal O)\le Kn^{-3/2}$ and $\Phi(V)>\theta_0(n)$ at some even $n\ge12$ would confirm the $\Theta$'s upper half, **and would refute [Conjecture L16](../findings/problem.md)**. I searched the record for any such object or any argument toward one: [findings/problem.md](../findings/problem.md) reports the opposite at $n=12$ and $n=14$, and no entry in the $145$–$158$ block attempts an upper bound on $r_n$ at all. The $\Theta(n^{-1})$ that L147 and L149 call a ceiling is a ceiling *of the method*, not of $r_n$, [Lemma L149](../findings/second-order.md) says so: "*a ceiling on a statement this local is a limit on the method rather than on the problem*". **There is no upper bound on the tube radius anywhere in this record, and the box claims one.**
